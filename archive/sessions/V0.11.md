## The theme: a recorded cost is a hypothesis about a number

V0.10 closed by naming the pattern *a recommendation written from a summary is a
hypothesis about a document, and this project's own evidence standard applies to
it.* This session extends it one step, and the extension is sharper because it
cost more.

The containment question had sat in REPORTS.md since V0.10 in these words: *if a
series and the release containing it are both nodes, authority accruing to each
double-counts one programme.* It was stated as fact. It gated a node —
*"the System of Macroeconomic Accounts should not be built until this is settled,
because that one would triple-count rather than double-count."* Two of the three
options offered were framed around managing an overstatement, and the third was
"accept the double-count as the price of finer granularity."

**Nobody had ever run it.** So it was run: merge `statcan-hfce` into
`statcan-national-accounts`, re-rank, and difference the two builds.

| | authority | rank |
|---|---|---|
| child, `statcan-hfce`, alone | 0.2702 | #18 |
| parent, `statcan-national-accounts`, alone | 0.4604 | #6 |
| arithmetic sum of the parts | 0.7305 | — |
| **merged into one node** | **0.8412** | **#2** |

**Splitting divides, and then loses another 15.2% on top of the division.** The
containing release reads at 55% of its rolled-up weight and sits four positions
too low. The 15.2% is the interesting part: it is the gap between the merge and
the arithmetic sum, and it exists because rank compounds — a merged node passes
more of it upstream and receives more of it back. A sum cannot do that. The
upstreams confirm it from the other side, `statcan-sut` **+34.7%** and `sna-2008`
**+16.5%** under the merge, against a median of **−3.3%** for everything else.

So the third option — leave it implicit, accept the double-count — was accepting
an error whose *direction* had never been checked, and it is the largest single
distortion in the ranking. And the reason to build the System of Macroeconomic
Accounts node was, if anything, stronger than the reason to hold it.

**The general form is worth writing down, because this is the third instance.**
V0.9 wrote a recommendation from a remembered reading and got both the location
and the content wrong. V0.10 wrote a `_dropped` note from a summary and got the
chapter wrong (below). V0.10 wrote a *cost* from an intuition and got the sign
wrong. In each case the artefact carried no `evidence_url` and nothing in the
process could catch it. **Prose in this project's own documents is not
load-bearing until something has measured it**, and the three artefact types that
routinely escape — recommendations, dropped notes, and stated costs — are exactly
the three that look like conclusions and are not.

## The decision, and why it is not one of the three recorded options

Full reasoning is in REPORTS.md and the `part_of` comment in `types.ts` and is
not repeated here. The shape of it:

**`component_of` as a `relationship_type` is refused on the model's own terms.**
`RelationshipType` is defined as *how one report depends on another* and every
value feeds `RELATIONSHIP_WEIGHT`. Containment is not a dependency. Weighting it
would convert a bounded 15.2% understatement into an unbounded overstatement,
which is the sink-leak family arriving from a third direction.

**A nullable `part_of` on the Report, with three constraints** that answer the
objection it was originally rejected on — that it "invites a hierarchy the
position rule forbids rendering":

1. It never touches `authority`, `size_score`, the degree counts, or position.
   The position rule survives because that rule exists on the ground that
   jurisdiction strata are a *fiction*. Containment between two named releases is
   not a fiction; it is in the documents.
2. It carries an edge's evidence burden. Here both nodes cite IMDB record 1901.
3. **A dependency edge between a node and its container is a validator error.**
   That rule is what stops `component_of` creeping back in wearing a new hat.

**It is rendered**, in the hover card and in the validator, because `country` was
wrong on nine nodes for five sessions for exactly the reason that nothing drew
it. The hover card tells a child node it is a series inside its parent and that
its authority is its own; it tells a parent what it also publishes and what
everything under the masthead comes to.

`rolledUpAuthority()` is offered as a reading aid and never as a score, and the
docstring says why in the only terms that are honest: it understates a true merge
(0.729 against 0.841) while overstating a bare reading. What it answers is *how
much sits under this masthead*, which is a real question with a real answer.

### The rules were fired before they were trusted

A validator rule nobody has watched fire is a rule nobody knows works. All five
were run against deliberately doctored data:

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| baseline, real data | nothing fires |
| `part_of` → unknown id | error |
| `part_of` → itself | error |
| containment cycle, two nodes each inside the other | error, both ends named |
| dependency edge parent → child | error |
| dependency edge child → parent | error |

The baseline line matters as much as the others: a rule that fires on correct
data is worse than no rule.

## The Industrial Product Price Index

`statcan-ippi`, record 2318. Monthly, Prices Division — a sibling of the CPI from
the same shop.

V0.10 called it "the best-evidenced missing node in the corpus" and it was, by
some distance. The monthly GDP by industry source table names it **by title and
IMDB record number in 64 rows**, against **27** for the CPI and **37** for SEPH.
Representative row, NAICS 3111 Animal food manufacturing, under Gross output:
*"Sales and inventory change in constant prices, Monthly Survey of Manufacturing,
Record no. 2101. Industrial product price indexes, Record no. 2318."*

And the deflation guide, unhedged, under *Sources of deflators*: *"The main data
source of deflators for manufactured goods is the Industrial Product Price
Indexes (IPPI) produced by Statistics Canada's Prices Division."*

**Why the accounts need it as well as the CPI**, which is the fact that makes the
node more than an addition: the supply and use tables are built at *basic
prices*, and the CPI measures *purchasers'* prices. Record 2318 states the
difference plainly — IPPI prices are collected at the factory gate and *"exclude
all indirect taxes, such as sales taxes, customs duties and tariffs as this money
does not go to the factors of production"*, along with common-carrier transport
and retail or wholesale distribution beyond the gate. Two price indexes from one
division measuring two different points in the same transaction, and the accounts
use both, for different cells.

**The uncomfortable part.** Nothing about this evidence was hard, or new, or
newly published. The 64 rows sit in the same document as the 27 and the 37, both
of which became edges in V0.10. The IPPI was invisible for one reason: nobody had
grepped for it. There is no methodological lesson in that beyond the obvious one,
and the obvious one is worth stating anyway — **when a document turns out to name
one source at release level, sweep it for all of them before closing it.**

Note also that record 1301's own IMDB page never mentions the IPPI at all. The
release-level disclosure lives entirely in an attached table. That is the
disclosure asymmetry doctrine operating *within a single statistical programme*,
which is a tighter instance than the municipal one it was written from.

### The second mutual pair, and it is better evidenced than the first

`statcan-ippi -> statcan-sut` runs the other way. Record 2318, Estimation: *"For
the IPPI, commodity and industry weights are derived from a combination of the
2019 Supply and Use Tables that closely reflect actual economic transactions by
industry and the sales of goods manufactured by companies as reported for the
2019 ASML."*

It clears the V0.10 mutuality rule on every count — each direction stated by a
*different* document in its own words, each with its own `evidence_url`, and the
two asymmetric in kind rather than restatements of one relationship. The tables
take the index as a price measure; the index takes the tables as a weighting
frame. `relationship_type` records the asymmetry as required:
`methodology_depends_on` outbound from the tables against `uses_data_from` here.

**The loop closes across vintages, not within a calculation**, and that is worth
knowing before anyone reads the cycle as circular reasoning. The 2019 tables
weight today's index; today's index deflates a later vintage of the tables.
Nothing circular happens in any single computation — they are five years apart,
which is why the edge carries a `reference_period` of 0.2 readings a year.

## The mutual-pair correction: V0.10 generalised from one instance

V0.10 measured its cycle and reported a **+7.2% median** across the corpus,
explaining it as *"a cycle recirculates rank instead of letting it terminate."*
That reads as a property of cycles. It is not.

| | first pair (V0.10) | second pair (V0.11) |
|---|---|---|
| the pair | `statcan-sut` ↔ `statcan-national-accounts` | `statcan-sut` ↔ `statcan-ippi` |
| larger member | +31.9% | +40.0% |
| smaller member | +16.9% | +10.7% |
| **median, all scored nodes** | **+7.2%** | **−2.4%** |

**Opposite sign.** The mechanism is not the cycle, it is where the pair points.
The first pair's members both point at the international standards, so
recirculated rank propagated onward and lifted the corpus. The IPPI's only other
outgoing edge is to `naics`, so the recirculated rank stays in the pair and
everyone else is diluted.

This sharpens rather than weakens V0.10's warning. The incentive it identified —
*recording a relationship as mutual pays more than recording it one way* — pays
the **pair**, and can charge everyone else. The guard is unchanged and is still
the evidence standard, which is the only thing standing between the incentive and
the ranking. Recorded in REPORTS.md as an open watch item rather than a managed
one, because two pairs is not a pattern.

## Structure, re-measured after three stale sessions

`BACKLOG.md` prioritises tiers against the bridge fraction and had been applying
V0.8's numbers across twelve added edges and two added nodes.

| | V0.8 | V0.11 |
|---|---|---|
| bridges, documented | 40 of 187 (21%) | **38 of 192 links (20%)** |
| articulation points | 32 of 121 (26%) | **30 of 122 (25%)** |
| components, strict standard | 1 | **1, 0 orphans** |

**The criterion was stale and was never wrong.** Both fractions fell slightly,
nothing in the tier ordering needs to change, and the work of the last three
sessions moved the number in the direction `BACKLOG.md` says to want.

Two things about the denominator, stated once so the next re-measure compares
like with like:

- **It counts undirected links, not directed edges.** The two mutual pairs
  collapse to one link each, so 194 documented edges make 192 links. This did not
  matter before V0.10, when there were no cycles and the two counts were equal.
  Comparing 192 against V0.8's 187 is legitimate; comparing a future directed
  count against it would not be.
- **The bridge count did not move at all** across everything added since V0.8 —
  38 before this session's node and 38 after. New edges that leave the bridge
  count flat while raising the link count are the ideal shape: they thickened the
  graph instead of extending it.

The highest-authority articulation points are the CPI, the US CPI, the Census,
SEPH, the Regulated Property Guidelines and the quarterly accounts — the same
list the ranking gives, which is expected and slightly disappointing. The measure
is not currently telling us anything the authority score does not.

## The chapter was wrong

The `_dropped` lead that created this session's node placed its decisive
quotation *"unhedged, in chapter 3 under the heading 'Sources of deflators'"*. It
is in **chapter 2**. The table of contents lists *Sources of deflators* at page 28
under chapter 2; chapter 3 begins at page 31. In the extracted text the heading
is at line 1537 and `CHAPTER 3 DEFLATORS FOR GOODS` at line 1775.

The quotation is verbatim and the edge stands. It is recorded because it is the
third citation-location error in three sessions, and all three share a shape:
**the substance right, the address wrong, and the address written from a summary
rather than from the document.**

**One thing gained from re-reading the same passage.** V0.10 recorded two hedges
on `statcan-sut -> statcan-cpi`, one being that the guide says CPIs *"can be
used"* to deflate rather than *are* used. Four sentences later the same passage
says it outright: *"the purchaser value, deflated with the appropriate Consumer
Price Index (CPI)"*. That hedge is retired. The edge was under-rated by a first
reading that stopped at the first mention — the mirror image of the error above,
and from the same cause.

## A submitted artifact was adjudicated, and two of its twenty-four edges survived

A large consolidated research artifact arrived mid-session: 17 reports and 24
edges covering Equalization, the supply and use tables, Ontario's OMPF and MPAC,
Calgary, and the Alberta education requisition, plus a province-comparison table.
It was adjudicated under the V0.9 rule rather than merged. Counts after taking
only what verifies: **124 reports / 205 dependencies**.

**What was taken.** Two nodes — `mpac-assessment` and `on-ompf` — and three
edges, one of which supersedes a thinner existing copy.

The find is the OMPF Technical Guide's **Appendix F, headed "Data sources"**: a
literal table of every data element the allocation formula consumes and where
each comes from. Documents that name their own inputs in a table are the
strongest evidence class this project recognises and they are rare outside
statute. It names MPAC in three of twelve rows, with a named product —
*"Number of Households | 2025 | MPAC (Municipal Property Assessment Corporation)
Returned Roll."*

**Six rows say "Statistics Canada" and name nothing.** Median household income,
low-income threshold, working-age ratio, employment rate, municipal land area,
and the rural measure are all attributed to the agency alone, and Appendix G's
definitions repeat the pattern in prose. That is the H.15 finding in a provincial
grant formula — a thoroughly-disclosing document whose disclosure stops one level
short of a title, five times out of six.

**The sixth is the whole reason Ontario is connected to anything.** Appendix G,
defining the Rural and Small Community Measure: *"The measure is based on
Statistics Canada data from the 2021 Census."* One named release, in one clause,
in an appendix definition. Measured: with that edge the graph is **1 component**;
without it, **2**. An invariant held since V0.8 rests on a single sentence that
the submitting artifact did not quote.

### The bridge fraction rose, which is BACKLOG's stop signal

| | before Ontario | after |
|---|---|---|
| links | 192 | 194 |
| bridges | 38 (20%) | **40 (21%)** |
| articulation points | 30 of 122 (25%) | 31 of 124 (25%) |

`BACKLOG.md` says: *"If a tier adds nodes and the bridge fraction rises, the tier
added territory rather than structure, and that is the signal to stop and bridge
instead."* **Both new edges are bridges**, including the Census one. The criterion
was re-measured earlier in this same session and then immediately fired on the
first thing added after it. That is the check working exactly as designed, and it
says: do not add more Ontario until Ontario is bridged.

### Why twenty-two edges were refused

Recorded at length in the slice's `_dropped` so none is re-proposed. The four that
generalise:

- **A duplicate node under a different id.** `statcan-supply-use-tables` had the
  same title, publisher and IMDB URL as `statcan-sut` — record 1401, added in
  V0.10. This is the `statcan-hfce` failure arriving a second time, and the loader
  still cannot see it. One of its two edges also had its basis attached to the
  wrong node: the quotation describes records 1301 and 1303, and the edge pointed
  at 1901.
- **The uncorrected section numbers.** The Equalization edges cite s. 8(3) and
  s. 8(5). V0.10 caught the second reader on precisely these and corrected them to
  8(4) and 8(6) after reading the sections. Their reappearance is a fact about the
  artifact, not the regulation.
- **`FullText.html` as evidence for twelve edges.** V0.10 measured that page
  truncating at ~126 kB against a 525 kB document. Most of what was cited is not
  visible at the URL offered as proof of it.
- **A substitution presented as a reading.** `fiscal-equalization-program ->
  statcan-hfce` was justified by a quotation naming the *Provincial and
  Territorial Economic Accounts* — a different release, and one V0.10 recorded as
  a missing node. It would now compound, because `statcan-hfce` is `part_of` the
  national quarterly accounts as of this session.

### The format regression, which is the process finding

V0.9 settled this: a second reader extracts, this project adjudicates — no verdict
field, verbatim quotes, `AGENCY ONLY` markers. Three rounds under that format in
V0.10 produced zero adjudication errors. This artifact arrived in the pre-V0.9
shape, as finished verdicts with `basis` written as paraphrase.

**The paraphrasing is what did the damage.** Not one of the twelve Equalization
bases contains a quoted string, so there was nothing to check except the section
numbers, and the section numbers were the ones already known to be wrong. Where
the artifact *did* quote — the Appendix F rows, the Assessment Act current-value
definition — it was accurate, and those are exactly the parts that became edges.
**A verbatim quote is checkable in minutes; a paraphrase is not checkable at all.**

It also under-read its own best source: Appendix F names twelve data elements, the
artifact used three, ignored Municipal FIR, OPTA and Ontario Parcel entirely, and
spent its effort on the six rows that name no release. The Ontario FIR is the
sharpest loss — `ab-municipalaffairs-fir` is already a node, so that one is a
symmetry the corpus is visibly missing.

### A third statute that anticipates its own suspension

The artifact said MPAC's valuation date is frozen at 1 January 2016 "because of
successive postponements", citing nothing. The Act says something narrower.
Assessment Act s. 19.2(1) para 4 assigns 1 January 2016 to *"the four taxation
years from 2017 to 2020"*, and para 5 puts each later four-year period two years
before itself. Read alone, the Act does not put 2016 anywhere near 2026.

The bridge is s. 19.2(5): *"Subsection (1) does not apply … if the Minister
prescribes a different day as of which land is valued for that year."* MPAC
confirms a regulation was filed on 16 August 2023 doing exactly that. So a statute
contains a clause permitting its own timetable to be set aside, and that clause
has held a valuation date six years past the schedule it was written into.

With the CPP Act's splice clause and the Oil Sands Royalty Regulation's provision
for the day the Bank of Canada stops publishing an exchange rate, that is **three
instances, which is a pattern rather than a curiosity**: legislation about numbers
routinely writes in what to do when the number stops behaving, and the graph has
no way to show it.

case where the gap between "how well evidenced" and "how much rests on it" is
this wide, and `BACKLOG.md`'s bridge criterion will not see it either.
