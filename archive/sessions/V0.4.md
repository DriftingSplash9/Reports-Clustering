## The authority metric had a sink leak

**The bug.** Adding one node — the Alberta Regulated Property Assessment
Minister's Guidelines, on two incoming edges — put it at #1 weighted authority
above the US CPI. Textbook PageRank transfers *all* of a node's rank along its
outgoing edges. Both assessment rolls pointing at the guidelines had exactly one
outgoing edge each, so they drained everything they had into it.

**The deeper problem, which matters more than the instance.** Out-degree in this
graph is a fact about *our research*, not about the world. An assessment roll
really rests on the assessment regulations, the quality guidelines and market
sales data; the graph knew about one of those because one had been documented.
Full transfer therefore lets whichever dependency happened to be researched first
inherit an entire node's authority — so **the least complete part of the graph
produces the loudest result**. That will recur with every terminal standard
added, which is why the fix had to address the class and not the case.

**The fix: `SELF_RETENTION = 1.0` in `graph.ts`.** A lazy random walk — some of
the walk stays put at each step. Expressed as a weight competing with the real
edges, so at 1.0 a single `calculated_from` edge, the strongest relationship in
the model, transfers half a node's authority and no more.

Applied **only to nodes that have outgoing edges**. Giving a true sink a
self-loop lets it hoard: with damping at 0.85 a pure sink that keeps everything
settles at over five times its inflow, which is the original bug made worse.
Sinks keep the existing even redistribution.

**What the fix revealed underneath.** With the leak closed, the two Grande
Prairie assessment rolls went to #1 and #2 and the US CPI fell to #8. That was
not a second bug — it was the honest report of a **curation imbalance**. At that
moment 24 of 47 nodes were Alberta municipal or provincial documents, densely
interlinked, while the federal statistical cluster was sparse. The metric had
stopped hiding it. The cure was data, not tuning: researching what the national
indices actually rest on moved them back up on their merits.

The lesson worth keeping: **when the ranking looks wrong, first ask whether it is
accurately describing a graph that is wrong.**

## The municipal template, tested

**Grande Prairie was mapped in full** — tax rate bylaw, budget, assessment roll,
municipal census, audited financial statements — then **the County of Grande
Prairie was mapped to test whether that was a template or a one-off.**

**It replicated.** The same six documents exist, published under the same
statute, naming their inputs in the same recitals. The tax rate bylaw is the
keystone in both: by statute it must state the assessment it levies against and
every requisition it collects for another body, so it names its own inputs in a
way most municipal documents do not. Six edges out of one PDF, twice.

A rural municipality **adds** two things rather than departing from the pattern:

- **Regulated property.** Nearly 40% of the County's $12 billion tax base is
  machinery, equipment and linear property, which the local assessor does not
  value at market at all — the province prescribes it through Minister's
  Guidelines. The city has the same classes at trivial scale.
- **A seniors housing requisition** large enough to be its own line, which also
  rescued a node the city slice had to drop.

Where they differed was **disclosure, not reality**: the city's statements say
outright that property tax revenues are based on assessment values; the County's
carry no equivalent sentence, so the same edge is not available on the same
terms. That difference is why the graph is uneven, and it is worth expecting
everywhere.

**~330 municipalities remain a replication job with per-instance URL
verification**, which was the whole point of doing two properly.

## Research agents, and the failure that did not repeat

V0.2 records five agents, ~900,000 tokens, all killed by a session limit before
any wrote a file. **Eight agents ran this session across two batches. Nothing was
lost.** Three things made the difference:

1. **The instruction, stated first and with its reason.** Every prompt opened
   with the incremental-write rule and the story of why it exists. Every agent
   reported creating its file after the first confirmed edge and appending
   thereafter.
2. **One file per agent**, named in the prompt. No coordination, no conflicts, no
   agent waiting on another.
3. **The full list of existing node ids in every prompt.** This is what stopped
   the batch producing a pile of disconnected islands — 45 new nodes across five
   agents, **zero dangling edges**. Agents attached to `statcan-cpi` and
   `ab-tbf-fiscal-plan` instead of inventing their own.

What still went wrong, and the fix: **two agents independently defined
`cpp-pension-index`.** The loader caught it (first definition wins, logged) and
nothing broke, but converging node definitions is the signature of overlapping
slice boundaries. Batch prompts should state not just what an agent owns but
what it does *not*.

**Give agents permission to report negatives.** The most valuable results
included "Basel appears zero times in Call Report Schedule RC-R instructions" and
"CUDGC's 220KB standards never say Basel, OSFI or Superintendent". Both were
checked deliberately and both are findings.

## The reconciliation pass

Run before the second batch, and worth doing every time:

- **Removed the duplicate `cpp-pension-index`** from the provincial slice; the
  federal slice owns it. Edges from the provincial slice still resolve.
- **Removed `bls-cpi -> coicop`**, on the researching agent's own flag. BLS
  publishes an R-COICOP series monthly for the OECD, but it is a research series
  and an alternative aggregation — COICOP does not structure the headline CPI.
  The graph's edges describe what a release rests on, not every classification an
  agency also maps to. The COICOP node went with its only edge.

Both fixes were deletions. An agent flagging its own weakest edge for review is
the behaviour to encourage.

## Two islands, and how they were closed

At one point the graph had three components: the main graph, plus
`osfi-car-guideline`↔`bis-basel-framework` and `fed-h8`↔`ffiec-call-report`. Bank
regulation had no documented path into the macroeconomic releases.

Rather than accept it, one agent of the second batch was spent entirely on
**structural repair** — no new territory, just bridges. Both closed:

- **Canadian:** CMHC's Residential Mortgage Industry Report cites StatCan Table
  36-10-0639-01 for household credit liabilities, and defines its market
  segmentation by "the stress test introduced in January 2018 under OSFI's B-20
  Guideline"; B-20 in turn states it "should be read in conjunction with the
  Capital Adequacy Requirements Guideline". A second independent path runs
  through CDIC's Differential Premiums Manual, which instructs members to report
  the CET1 ratio "in accordance with the Capital Adequacy Requirements
  guideline".
- **American:** the Z.1 Financial Accounts Technical Q&A states "Z.1 depository
  institution data are aggregated from Call Reports and do not involve estimation
  from a sample", and that Z.1 tables "group Call Report line items into
  instrument categories that better align with the *System of National Accounts*
  (SNA 2008)". Z.1's June 2026 release renumbered every table onto SNA sector
  codes, so that edge is stronger now than it would have been a year ago.

**Spending an agent on connectivity rather than volume was the highest-value
call of the session.** A disconnected cluster is worth much less than its node
count suggests.

## The population estimate nobody expected

The Census of Population is now the highest-authority node in the graph, and the
reason is a detail nobody would guess.

Federal transfers do **not** run on the census directly. The Canada Health
Transfer regulation prescribes "Statistics Canada's official estimate of the
population of the province on **July 1** of that fiscal year" — moved from June 1
by SOR/2023-230, a quiet 2023 amendment that changed the reference date for
every major transfer. That is the postcensal Quarterly Demographic Estimates
programme, which is the census adjusted for net undercoverage plus births,
deaths and migration.

So the chain is Census → population estimates → CHT/CST/Equalization, with the
census one hop further back than expected and carrying more weight for it.
StatCan confirms it from its own side: the QDE programme page states the
estimates "play a vital role under the Federal-Provincial Fiscal Arrangements
... in determining the amounts of federal-provincial-territorial transfers."

## Chains now traceable end to end

- **AISH → SNA 2008 in four hops.** Alberta's disability benefit rests on 27
  reports. Bill 32 (2024) replaced the direct Alberta-CPI rules in the AISH,
  Income Support and Seniors Benefit statutes with one "Alberta escalator"
  defined in s. 44.2 of the *Alberta Personal Income Tax Act* — the lesser of 2%
  and Alberta CPI change to September 30. **The income tax bracket indexation
  factor now sets AISH payments**, and the 2% cap bound in both 2025 and 2026.
- **A line of credit → a UN standard.** Prime rate → policy rate → CPI, LFS and
  the national accounts → SNA 2008.
- **Grande Prairie's tax bylaw rests on 41 reports**, reaching international
  accounting standards one way and StatCan's CPI the other.
- **Alberta's largest revenue line is set by a Chicago exchange.** Oil Sands
  Royalty Regulation s.29: `RG% = 1% + [FG (A – B)]` where A is "the settlement
  price ... of West Texas Intermediate crude futures as traded on NYMEX",
  converted at the Bank of Canada's published rate, with a statutory fallback for
  the day the Bank stops publishing.
- **A statute containing a correction procedure for a statistical revision.** CPP
  s.18(7) requires the Minister, *on the advice of the Chief Statistician*, to
  splice StatCan's Industrial Aggregate average weekly earnings back onto its
  former basis if the definition changes by more than 1%. Legislation
  anticipating a methodological break upstream.

## What we learned about the corpus

Facts about the source material that will shape every future research pass:

- **Municipal reporting is markedly more machine-readable than post-secondary
  reporting.** Northwestern Polytechnic publishes its annual report, strategic
  plan and academic plan *only* through Issuu, a page-flipping viewer, with no
  text-extractable copy on its own domain. Public and unreadable at once. Expect
  this across Alberta's 26 post-secondary institutions.
- **The evidence standard bites hardest at the commercial boundary.** All CPI
  used-car prices come from J.D. Power; Alberta's bitumen valuation regulation
  names ICE Brent and Argus Mexico Maya; CORRA's real input is a confidential
  CIRO regulatory feed; the H.8's largest input is a confidential reporting form.
  Headline public numbers demonstrably rest on private and confidential data that
  the graph's scope excludes. **This needs a decision** — see below.
- **Documented non-dependencies are worth as much as edges.** BLS states the CPI
  item structure "does not correspond to" NAICS. Alberta's Credit Union Deposit
  Guarantee Corporation standards — 220KB, two editions — contain "Basel",
  "OSFI" and "Superintendent" exactly zero times. Canada's provincial credit
  union supervisory chain is genuinely disconnected from the federal one in
  public documents.
- **Statutes are better evidence than webpages** and are usually available. The
  strongest edges in the graph come from regulations quoting their inputs by
  name.

## Renderer and schema changes

- **Default view now matches how the graph is actually used**: pulses, edges,
  arrows, ground grid, platform, glow and both focus cones on; bounding box,
  horizon, drop lines and distance haze off. Distance haze is off because it
  fights the 24° lens — the long lens exists so a node reads as large because it
  *is* large, and fog adds a second depth cue on top of the one being
  deliberately suppressed.
- **Room clearance is proportional** — `MARGIN_RATIO = 0.16` of the node cloud
  radius with a floor, replacing a fixed 55 units chosen when the graph was 30
  nodes. A constant gap under a growing slab reads as the platform drifting away.
- **Two new domains**: `banking` and `financial-regulation`.

## Known problems

**The cadence enum is now worse than V0.3 described, and still blocks milestone
3.** New arrivals that fit none of the five buckets: a school year ending August
31 (offset from every other document by four months), a discretionary municipal
census with no cycle at all, a decennial US census (annual is off by 10×), and —
the most interesting — the chartered bank prime rate, which is *published* weekly
but *changes* only when banks move it. **Publication cadence and change cadence
are different things and the model cannot express the difference.** That is a
modelling gap, not just an enum gap.

**`last_updated` is null everywhere.** Unchanged, and now across 118 nodes.

**No search, no find-by-name.** At 118 nodes this is the sharpest usability gap
in the project. Click-to-focus works, but selecting a node you cannot locate is
not much use.

**One orphan report remains** — LGFF Operating, dropped every build because no
document names its inputs. The standard working.

**The Alberta health delivery layer is mid-restructure.** The Provincial Health
Agencies Act split AHS into four sector agencies in 2024-25; the 2024-25 audit
opinion is addressed to the Minister of Primary and Preventative Health. Two
nodes describe entities in transition and will need revisiting.

## Two decisions taken at the end of this session

Both were open problems at the start of the session and are now settled in
REPORTS.md under Decisions. **Neither is implemented** — that is V0.5 work.

**Cadence is three quantities, and the third belongs on the edge.** Publication
rate, change rate, and transmission rate are different things: the prime rate is
published weekly, changes up to eight times a year, and is read by a mortgage
product at yet another rhythm. Since the pulse represents influence propagating,
transmission is what it should show — and transmission is a property of the edge,
because the same CPI release reaches the Monetary Policy Report monthly and the
Alberta escalator annually.

This is cheap because we are already collecting the data and discarding it: six
distinct documented reference periods are sitting as prose in `basis` fields
(September 30, October 31, third quarter, August 31, rolling three-month, July
1). Plan: drop `update_frequency`, keep `releases_per_year`, add an optional
reference period to `Dependency`.

**Published commercial sources are in; confidential ones stay out.** This was
never an evidence problem — the BLS Handbook states outright that all CPI
used-car prices come from J.D. Power, which is better documented than many
existing edges. The node rule was what failed, so the node rule bends. Commercial
providers become nodes marked non-official and **excluded from the authority
calculation**, since a terminal commercial sink accruing rank is exactly the
shape that caused the sink-leak bug. Confidential feeds (FR 2644, CIRO MTRS, W-2
records) stay out and become a note on the dependent report.

## Recommended next steps

1. **Search / find-by-name (milestone 6).** The navigation gap is the binding
   constraint at 118 nodes. Focus works; locating the node to focus does not.
2. **Implement the cadence decision.** Drop `update_frequency`, add an optional
   reference period to `Dependency`, and backfill it from the `basis` fields that
   already state one. Then milestone 3 becomes reachable.
3. **Implement the commercial-source decision.** Add the non-official marker,
   exclude marked nodes from `pagerank`, and add the J.D. Power, ICE Brent and
   Argus edges that are currently sitting in `_dropped` blocks. Ship it with a
   view toggle — off gives the official-only reading, on gives the real one, and
   because the marked nodes are outside the authority calculation, flicking it
   changes no size and no ranking. Build the toggle as a filter predicate over
   nodes; it is the first piece of milestone 6, and domain and jurisdiction
   filters should reuse it.
4. **Remaining research batch**: advanced education and student aid; housing
   statistics (CMHC data, New Housing Price Index, the CPI shelter component).
5. **The municipal fan-out**, now that the template is proven at n=2.

Note that 2 and 3 both involve touching every existing slice, so they are
cheaper now than after another research batch.
