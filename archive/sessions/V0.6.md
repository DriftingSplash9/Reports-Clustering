# V0.6 — Cadence, and the edges that were being thrown away in silence
Session date: 2026-07-29
Supersedes V0.5. Node and edge counts are unchanged at 121 and 187, and **every
authority number is unchanged** — a cadence change must not move a score, and it
did not.

**The first rollup, `V1.5.md`, is still owed.** It was due at V0.5 and has now
been skipped twice. See the two-thread protocol in REPORTS.md.

---

## What was built

The cadence decision from V0.4, deferred through V0.5, is now implemented. Scope
was schema plus backfill; the calendar (milestone 3) was deliberately left for
later.

`update_frequency` is gone. In its place:

| Quantity | Where it lives | Field |
|---|---|---|
| **Publication rate** — how often the document appears | Report | `releases_per_year` |
| **Change rate** — how often the number moves | Report | `changes_per_year?` |
| **Transmission rate** — when a dependent reads it | **Dependency** | `reference_period?` |

Transmission belongs on the edge, and that is the part that took longest to see.
The same CPI release reaches the Monetary Policy Report every month and the
Alberta escalator once a year. No field on the CPI can express that, because it
is not a fact about the CPI.

## The enum was worse than V0.4 thought

V0.4 said the cadence enum was failing on a handful of awkward new arrivals. The
actual state, measured before deleting it:

- **It disagreed with `releases_per_year` on 15 of 104 researched nodes.**
- **77 of 104 were filed as `annual`** — a single bucket doing duty for a
  decennial census (0.1 a year), SNA 2008 (0.05, revised roughly once a
  generation), a five-yearly Canadian census (0.2), and a discretionary
  municipal count with no cycle at all (0.25).
- **65 nodes carried a `cadence_note`.** More than half the corpus needed a
  prose correction attached.

Several of those notes said outright that the enum had no bucket for the case.
A filter dimension that has to be corrected in prose on every second node is not
a filter dimension, and nothing was lost by deleting it: in all 15 disagreements
the number was right and the bucket was wrong.

**Fractional publication rates are the replacement, and they are worth keeping
fractional.** `describeRate` renders them as "about every 20 years" rather than
"0.05× a year", because a number can say *once a generation* and a bucket
cannot.

## The backfill

34 of 187 edges (18%) now carry a documented reference period, extracted from
what the `basis` prose already stated. **No source documents were re-read** —
same evidence standard as the edges themselves, so a period exists only where a
document says it.

The distribution is more varied than V0.4's "six reference periods" suggested:

```
  5  point at no fixed date        5  12mo to 09-30       4  point at 01-01
  4  point at 07-01               4  1mo to rolling       2  3mo to rolling
  1  12mo to 06-30                1  3mo to 09-30         1  point at 09-30
  1  point at 08-31               1  3mo to 03-31         1  12mo to 04-30
  1  12mo to 12-31                1  12mo to 08-31        1  point at 12-01
  1  point at 10-31
```

Fourteen distinct shapes across 34 edges, and **September 30 is the single
busiest date in Canadian statutory indexation** — the CRA indexation factor, the
Canada Child Benefit, the Alberta escalator, AISH and Income Support all read a
12-month CPI window closing on it. That is a genuine structural fact the graph
can now state, and it is exactly the sort of thing prose in a `basis` field
cannot be queried for.

The two Canadian earnings-based programmes turn out **not** to share a date:
EI's maximum insurable earnings reads 12 months to April 30, CPP's YMPE reads 12
months to June 30. Both off the same SEPH series, two months apart. V0.4 had
recorded CPP as October 31, which is the pension *index*, not the YMPE — the
backfill separated two things that had been conflated.

Only one node needed a change rate: **the chartered bank prime rate**, published
weekly and moving eight times a year. Fifty-two of those fifty-two publications
carry no news. An earlier pass also set a change rate on the posted chartered
bank rates on the same reasoning; it was removed, because nothing documents it
there and the pattern was being assumed rather than read.

## Six edges were being discarded silently, and three of them were this session's work

This is the finding worth carrying forward.

The backfill wrote 34 reference periods. The validator then reported 31. Nothing
was dangling, no ids were duplicated, and no error was raised anywhere.

**The loader deduplicates edges and says nothing about it.** Six source→target
pairs are defined twice — once thinly in the hand-written seed set, once again in
a research slice with a better `basis`, an `evidence_url`, and now a reference
period. `if (seen.has(key)) continue` keeps the first, which is always the seed.
The richer version loses, every build, invisibly.

The loader has always reported dropped dangling edges and duplicate *node* ids.
Duplicate *edges* were the one case it swallowed, and it swallowed them in the
direction that discards research.

Fixed two ways:

- **`loadIssues.duplicateEdges` now exists** and is printed by `npm run validate`
  and warned in the browser console. The six are visible.
- The three affected reference periods were added to the seed edges directly, so
  the data is correct today.

**What is not fixed is the underlying rule.** For nodes, "first wins, the seed
set is authoritative" is a deliberate decision. For edges it is an accident with
the wrong sign: the seed edges were written first and are uniformly worse
evidenced — three of these six have no `evidence_url` at all, which by the
project's own standard means they should not exist. Options are to make later
definitions win for edges, to merge field-by-field preferring the more
evidenced, or to delete the seed edges that research has superseded. That is a
decision, not a bug fix, and it is listed below rather than taken here.

## Known problems

**Milestone 3 is now reachable but not built.** Pulse rate still runs on
publication rate, which is the wrong quantity — the pulse represents influence
propagating, and influence propagates when the dependent reads. Switching it to
transmission is the next step and was deliberately out of scope.

**Transmission coverage is 18%.** Absent means no document read so far says when
the reading happens, not that the dependency is continuous. This is a research
backlog and `npm run validate` now prints it so it can be watched climb.

**Six duplicate edges resolve the wrong way** — see above. Surfaced, not settled.

**The default view is still wrong at 121 nodes.** Unchanged from V0.5: the camera
fits the room rather than the node cloud, and distance haze is off in a scene
that now has no other depth cue.

**`last_updated` is null everywhere.** Unchanged. Worth noting this is the field
that would let the new cadence model do anything predictive — a rate without a
last observation cannot say when the next one is due.

**One orphan report remains** — LGFF Operating. The standard working.

## Recommended next steps

1. **Write `V1.5.md`.** Owed since V0.5, skipped twice. The rollup protocol
   exists precisely to stop this, and it is not working.
2. **Decide the duplicate-edge rule.** Cheap to settle, and it is currently
   discarding the better-evidenced half of six edges on every build.
3. **Milestone 3.** The data is now shaped for it: 34 edges can say when they
   transmit, and the graph can be asked which pulses are in flight on a date.
   `last_updated` becomes the blocker the moment this starts.
4. **Fix the default view** — fit to the node cloud, restore a low fog value.
   Both are small and both answer something observed rather than guessed.
5. **Remaining research batch**: advanced education and student aid; housing
   statistics (CMHC data, New Housing Price Index, CPI shelter component).

## Verification performed

- `npx tsc --noEmit` — clean under `strict` with `noUnusedLocals`.
- `npx vite build` — succeeds.
- `npm run validate` — 121 reports, 187 dependencies, no structural issues.
- **Authority unchanged**: top six identical to V0.4 and V0.5 (Census 1.000,
  StatCan CPI 0.872, SEPH 0.690, Regulated Property Assessment 0.683, US CPI
  0.660, NAICS 0.494). A cadence change must not move a score.
- Commercial-source invariant still holds — official scores identical with and
  without the four commercial nodes.
- New validator checks: `changes_per_year` may not exceed `releases_per_year`;
  `readings_per_year` positive; `window_months` non-negative; `ends` is `MM-DD`
  or null; `stated_as` present.
- **`describeRate` and `describePeriod` exercised over the whole corpus** — all
  16 distinct publication rates and all 34 reference periods rendered and read
  by eye. This caught two grammar bugs ("1 months, rolling" and "1 node publish")
  that a typecheck cannot.

**Not verified: the hover card on screen.** The dev server stopped part-way
through the session and the cadence lines in the detail panel have not been seen
rendered. The display logic was exercised directly against the real data instead,
which covers the strings but not the layout — the new lines could still be
crowding the card.
