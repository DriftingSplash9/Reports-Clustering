# V0.2 — Real research, a room to see it in, and the removal of a fiction

Session date: 2026-07-28
Supersedes V0.1 entirely. **V0.1 is now wrong** about the renderer — it describes
always-on labels, domain-based node colour, and horizontal level bands. None of
those exist. Read this file instead.

Covers roadmap milestones **0, 1**, most of **4**, and the start of **5**.

---

## Where the graph is

**30 reports, 41 documented dependencies.** One connected component of 28, plus
a detached pair (Alberta Education funding manual and student enrolment). The
graph spans international statistical standards down to Alberta municipal
funding formulas, with every edge carrying a URL to the document that
establishes it.

Top of the ranking, and both methods agree on the shape:

| | Weighted authority | Raw in-degree |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | BLS Consumer Price Index — 1.00 | StatCan CPI — 5 |
| 2 | Census of Population — 0.80 | BLS CPI — 4 |
| 3 | StatCan Consumer Price Index — 0.69 | BEA Personal Income — 3 |

Consumer price measurement and population counting come out as the foundation
of everything, without anything being tuned to produce that.

### The chain that works end to end

Grande Prairie's capital funding allocation → Municipal Affairs Population List
→ federal Census of Population → Labour Force Survey population controls →
the unemployment rate. Every step has an evidence URL.

And since this session, a second one: Statistics Canada's National Economic
Accounts and BEA's NIPAs both rest on **SNA 2008**. That is the first
structural link between the Canadian and US clusters — before it, they touched
only through two soft "cites" edges on the Bank of Canada's Monetary Policy
Report.

## What changed since V0.1

### Data architecture

Research now lands as **individual JSON files in `src/data/research/`**, merged
by a loader in `src/data/index.ts`. This was a direct response to a failure —
see "The agent failure" below. Slices can be added in any order; edges pointing
at reports that have not been researched yet are dropped and logged rather than
breaking the render. To add a slice: drop the JSON in, import it, add it to the
`slices` array.

Three slices exist:

- `alberta-municipal.json` — LGFF, equalized assessment, FIR, population lists
- `population-and-education.json` — census, K-12 funding manual, enrolment
- `international-standards.json` — SNA 2008, ILO ICLS resolution

### The evidence standard

**Documented citations only.** Every dependency carries `evidence_url` pointing
at a document that explicitly names its source. If no document says it, the edge
does not exist. This is enforced by convention, not by code, and it is the most
important rule in the project.

Consequences visible in the data: `LGFF Operating Funding` is real and receives
$60 million a year, but no document names its inputs, so the loader drops it
every build and logs why. That is the standard working, not a bug.

The LGFF Capital fact sheet turned out to be exceptional evidence — it names
every input dataset with links, which is why the Alberta municipal cluster is
the densest part of the graph.

### Schema changes

- `jurisdiction_level` — international | federal | provincial | municipal |
  institutional. Drives colour and, later, node style. **Never position.**
- `region` — human-readable area.
- `size_score` — authority rescaled across the observed range. PageRank has a
  floor, so raw authority only spanned 0.20–1.00, giving a radius ratio under
  2×. Rescaling gets it to 4.2×, which is the difference between readable and
  not.
- `evidence_url` on every dependency.

### Renderer

Rewritten substantially. Current state:

- **Field of view 24°.** The single most important number. At a wide angle
  apparent size is dominated by distance from camera, which inverts the size =
  authority rule. A long lens compresses depth so a node reads as large because
  it is large.
- **No labels.** They competed with size and turned the centre into grey mush.
  Identity is on hover instead, with publisher, cadence, authority, and the
  actual lists of what feeds in and what is built from it.
- **Pulses** travel each edge at the publishing rate of the upstream report,
  logarithmically scaled so annual crawls and daily streams.
- **Direction is influence, not dependency.** Arrows and pulses both flow from
  the foundational report outward to its dependents. The data model is
  unchanged; only rendering is reversed.
- **A room** — platform slab sized to the data with a bright rim, wireframe
  box, infinite fixed-cell ground grid, sky gradient horizon, optional drop
  lines. Nothing in the room encodes data. It is scale reference and depth cue.
- **View panel** with eleven toggles and a two-way zoom slider.

### The fiction that was removed

The renderer previously pulled nodes to fixed heights by jurisdiction level,
with horizontal grids marking each band. This was wrong on its own terms and
has been deleted.

There is no real sense in which one release sits above another, and the fiction
actively misleads at the edges. International bodies like the IMF and OECD
*consume* national statistics — they are downstream in influence — yet any
stack puts them on top, pointing the opposite way to the flow the graph exists
to show. Provinces and territories have the mirror problem: thirteen peers
forced into a rank they do not have.

Position now encodes only what the dependency edges say. Level is colour.

If a meaningful vertical axis is wanted later, the honest version is **depth in
the dependency graph** — how many hops a report sits from a pure source.
Computed from edges rather than asserted, and it inverts correctly for the
international case.

## Bugs found and fixed this session

**The room was never rendering.** `three-forcegraph` builds its objects and
starts its simulation asynchronously after `graphData()` is set, so at React
effect time every node still had `x === undefined`. The camera fit hit its
"no positions yet" guard and returned early, so `onBounds` was never called —
and App gates the entire room on `bounds`. Platform, grid, box, bands and
horizon were all unmounted. The fit now runs in the frame loop and waits for
real coordinates.

This cost two rounds of adjusting colours and opacity on objects that were not
in the scene. **The lesson: when a toggle has no visible effect, check whether
the thing is mounted before adjusting how it looks.**

**Glow was doing the horizon's job.** Mounting and unmounting `EffectComposer`
re-routes the render through an offscreen buffer with different colour
handling, shifting the background tint of the whole scene. The composer now
stays mounted permanently and the toggle only changes bloom intensity.

**OrbitControls target was lost** in an edit, so orbit pivoted around the world
origin instead of the graph. Restored.

**Bloom was blown out** at intensity 0.9 / threshold 0.22 with emissive up to
1.7 — every node clipped to white and the halo added apparent size uniformly,
destroying the encoding. Now 0.6 / 0.5 with emissive capped below 1.

## The agent failure

Five research subagents were launched in parallel. Each ran roughly twenty
minutes and 80–90 tool calls — around 900,000 tokens of real research between
them. Two were within a step of finishing. A session limit then killed all five
simultaneously, before any had written a file. Nothing survived.

The cause was the instruction, not the limit: each agent was told to research
everything and write one file at the end, creating a single point of failure at
the last step. **Any future agent work must write incrementally**, appending
findings as they are confirmed.

## Known problems

**The cadence enum is broken and now blocks milestone 3.** `update_frequency`
has five buckets; four important nodes do not fit any of them:

| Report | Reality | Bucket used |
|---|---|---|
| Bank of Canada policy rate | 8 per year | monthly |
| FOMC statement | 8 per year | monthly |
| Census of Population | every 5 years | annual |
| SNA 2008 | once a generation | annual |

`releases_per_year` carries the truth and drives the pulses, so nothing is
visibly wrong yet. But the pulse simulation cannot be wired to real release
schedules until this is settled. Three options: keep the split, extend the
enum, or drop the enum and derive buckets from `releases_per_year`.

**`last_updated` is null everywhere.** Not wired to any source. Left null
rather than filled with invented dates, which would corrupt milestone 3.

**SEPH still over-ranks** — 5th weighted against 12th raw. It sits at the end of
a short statutory chain where every link is `calculated_from` and each node has
one outgoing edge, so the chain's full weight concentrates on it. The maths is
right; the graph is thin there. Resolves as data grows.

**Two detached nodes** — the Alberta Education funding manual and student
enrolment pair. The link from education funding to the property tax requisition
that pays for it is presumably documented; it has not been found yet.

**No search, no filtering, no click-to-focus.** Fine at 30 nodes. Not fine at
300, which is the stated target.

## Recommended next steps

1. **Alberta provincial spine** — budget and fiscal plan, royalties, health,
   advanced education. This is the hub layer every Alberta municipality and
   school board will attach to, and it closes the detached education pair.
   Doing it before the fan-out is what makes the fan-out affordable.
2. **Milestone 2 — click-to-focus.** Select a node, highlight its dependents
   and dependencies, dim the rest. This is what makes the graph answer
   questions rather than merely display. It becomes necessary, not optional,
   past about a hundred nodes.
3. **Settle the cadence model**, then wire pulses to real schedules.
4. **Municipal fan-out by template.** Map Grande Prairie fully to establish the
   pattern — budget, financial statements, FIR, tax rate bylaw, assessment —
   then replicate structurally across other municipalities, verifying URLs per
   instance. Alberta has ~330 municipalities, ~60 school authorities and 26
   post-secondary institutions; only templating makes that tractable.
5. **CPI Manual and COICOP** at the international layer. The manual is real and
   jointly published by Eurostat, ILO, IMF, OECD, UNECE and the World Bank, but
   no document has yet been found where StatCan or BLS names it as their basis.
   If found, it puts a second manual under the two highest-authority nodes in
   the graph.
