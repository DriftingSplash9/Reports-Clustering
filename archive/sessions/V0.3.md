Session date: 2026-07-28
Supersedes V0.2. **V0.2 is now wrong** about the size of the graph, the number
of research slices, and the detached education pair — and its description of the
flagship dependency chain reverses direction halfway through. Its account of the

The `alberta-provincial.json` slice exists and is wired into the loader, but
V0.2 was written without it. It is three nodes — the Budget Fiscal Plan, the
Fiscal Update and Economic Statement, and the Alberta Economic Outlook — and
five edges, and it does two things V0.2 lists as future work:

**It closes the detached pair.** The K-12 Funding Manual is `calculated_from`
the Fiscal Plan that sets its grant rates. The education cluster is no longer
floating; the graph is one component of 33.

**It makes the LGFF chain statutory.** Grande Prairie's capital allocation now
runs back through the Local Government Fiscal Framework Act to Alberta's own
revenue position three years prior — a documented link from a provincial fiscal
number to a municipal road-repair budget.

So recommended next step 1 in V0.2 is substantially done. The provincial spine
still lacks royalties, health, and advanced education.

### The flagship chain is a V, not a line

V0.2 describes the end-to-end example as:

> Grande Prairie's capital funding allocation → Municipal Affairs Population
> List → federal Census of Population → Labour Force Survey population controls
> → the unemployment rate

The first half is right. The second half reverses. The Labour Force Survey
*depends on* the Census for its population controls, so the Census is upstream
of the LFS, not downstream. Walking dependencies from the LGFF allocation
reaches the Census and stops there; the LFS is a sibling consumer of the Census,
not a further step.

It is still a documented path connecting a municipal funding formula to the
national unemployment rate, and it still spans four levels of government. It is
just a **V meeting at the Census**, not a single directed chain, and describing
it as a chain claims something the edges do not say. Worth stating correctly
because it is the example the project leads with.

The confirmed upstream cone of the LGFF capital allocation is ten reports:
municipal population estimates and the MAPL, the FIR and the financial and
statistical data, equalized assessment, the education property tax requisition,
the Fiscal Plan, the Fiscal Update, the Economic Outlook, and the Census.

## Milestone 2 — click-to-focus

Click a node and it stays selected; its cone stays lit and everything else
drops back. Click it again, click empty space, or press Escape to clear.
Selection survives orbiting, which hover never could.

**Full cone, not one hop.** The choice was between highlighting direct
neighbours and highlighting everything transitively reachable. One hop is
guaranteed legible; the full cone answers the question the graph exists to ask —
what does this ultimately rest on, and what would an update ultimately reach.
The objection to the full cone is that it lights too much of the graph, and on
the current 33 nodes that objection is weakest where it matters: cone sizes run
from 2 to 14 nodes, **median 5**. As the graph grows toward 300, a municipal or
provincial cone becomes a *smaller* fraction of the whole, not a larger one. The
worry inverts with scale.

**Two independent toggles**, `focusBuiltFrom` and `focusFeedsInto`, worded to
match the hover card exactly. Four usable states rather than two:

| Built from | Feeds into | Answers |
|---|---|---|
| on | on | The whole chain this release sits in |
| on | off | What would have to be wrong for this to be wrong |
| off | on | What breaks if this release changes |
| off | off | Where is this one node — the selection alone, lit in place |

The last state is deliberate, not a degenerate case. Finding one report in a
crowded scene is a real need and the toggles already express it.

**Dimmed, never hidden.** Out-of-focus nodes drop to 0.14 opacity and near-zero
emissive; out-of-focus links go to a dark slate and their pulses are switched
off entirely. Hiding them would leave a small diagram floating in a large empty
room — the surrounding structure is the context that makes a cone mean anything.
Pulses are suppressed rather than dimmed because motion outdraws brightness: a
dim moving dot still pulls the eye harder than a bright stationary one.

**The HUD states the counts** — rests on *n*, feeds *n*, counted through the
whole chain. The scene shows where they are; the number is what gets quoted.

### Implementation notes worth keeping

- `src/lib/selection.ts` is a plain BFS with no rendering knowledge. Adjacency
  is built once per graph, so a click costs a walk rather than a rebuild.
- Edges carry a `key` in the **data model's** direction, computed once at build
  time. Links are rendered reversed, so deriving the key at lookup time means
  flipping an already-flipped pair — the kind of double negative that lights the
  right nodes and the wrong lines.
- three-forcegraph's accessors are callbacks on an object built once, so they
  close over the first render's props permanently. Focus is read through a ref;
  nothing else works.
- Node materials are mutated in place through a mesh map rather than by
  re-running `nodeThreeObject`, which would rebuild every sphere and its
  geometry on every click.
- `transparent: true` is set at construction with opacity 1. Flipping it on a
  live material forces a shader recompile at the exact moment of the click.
- Link colour and particle count are owned by the focus effect alone. They were
  briefly set in two effects, which meant toggling Edges could silently undo the
  dimming — whichever effect ran last won.
- Clicks that moved more than 4px since pointer-down are ignored, because
  orbiting ends in a click event and would otherwise destroy the selection every
  time you tried to look at it.

## Edges: gradient lines and teardrop pulses

Two changes to how an edge draws, both hooking into three-forcegraph rather
than replacing it. The library keeps ownership of positioning, which is the
part that has to stay correct while the simulation moves nodes 60 times a
second.

**Lines now run from the upstream node's colour to the downstream node's.**
Previously an edge took the upstream colour along its whole length, which said
"this line belongs to that report" — true, but it left the far end
unattributed. A gradient states both facts: what this is, and what it reaches.
Federal blue running into provincial green is the jurisdictional boundary being
crossed, drawn.

This costs nothing per frame. A link's cylinder is built as length 1 along
local +Z and then scaled to reach its target, so the `position.z` a vertex
shader sees still runs 0 → 1 from source to target no matter how long the link
is. Local z *is* the interpolation factor. The shader is four lines, supplied
through the `linkMaterial` accessor, and the library's own geometry and
positioning are untouched.

The ramp is a smoothstep rather than linear. A linear mix between two saturated
hues spends most of the line in a muddy middle; easing holds each end long
enough to be read as that node's colour.

**Pulses are teardrops, round end leading.** three-forcegraph orients any
particle whose geometry is not a `SphereGeometry` with `lookAt` toward its next
position, and `lookAt` aims an object's +Z axis — so a lathed profile built
around +Z arrives pointing the way it travels, with no orientation code of our
own. Direction was already carried by the arrowheads; it is now also on the
moving element, which is the one the eye actually tracks.

Consequences worth knowing:

- Supplying `linkDirectionalParticleThreeObject` makes
  `linkDirectionalParticleWidth` and `...ParticleColor` inert. Both are baked
  into the geometry and material instead.
- Supplying `linkMaterial` makes `linkColor` and `linkOpacity` inert for the
  lines. Focus dimming on edges is now a uniform write rather than a forced
  colour refresh, which is both cheaper and harder to get wrong.
- Pulse material is `MeshBasicMaterial`, unlit on purpose. A pulse should read
  as a signal travelling, not as a small object being lit — Lambert would let
  it darken on the side facing away from the lights, so the same pulse would
  mean different things at different points in an orbit.
- Teardrop geometries are cached in buckets of 0.25 width. A distinct geometry
  per link would be dozens of near-identical objects for a difference no one
  can see.

### Pulses are independent of edges

Turning Edges off used to take the pulses with it. `linkVisibility(false)` looks
like the obvious way to hide a line, but the particle digest runs over
`visibleLinks`, so the photons go too. The lines are now hidden through their
own material's `visible` flag and stay visible to the library.

Worth having as a view in its own right: it reduces each edge to the only part
that is actually moving, which is also the only part carrying cadence.

### Tuning constants live in one place

`view.ts` now holds every number governing focus appearance — node dim opacity
and emissive, lit and dim line opacity — because they are only ever tuned
against each other and against a screenshot. First pass was wrong in both
directions at once: out-of-focus nodes disappeared rather than receding, and lit
lines read as bright solid strokes, turning a cone into a diagram of pipes with
beads on it. Nodes are the subject; edges are the relationship between them and
should stay quieter than they were.

| | First pass | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Dim node opacity | 0.14 | 0.34 |
| Dim node emissive | 0.02 | 0.10 |
| Lit line opacity | 0.34 | 0.22 |
| Dim line opacity | 0.16 | 0.07 |

The contrast between the two line values is what marks the cone, not the
absolute brightness of either.

### Node dimming was probably not landing

Screenshots of the first focus build showed the cone's *edges* dimming
correctly while every node stayed at full brightness. Unconfirmed, but the
likely cause is that the library rebuilds node objects at times we do not
control, and a rebuilt sphere is born at full opacity — discarding mutations
made from a React effect.

Two defences, since the failure is silent either way:

1. `nodeThreeObject` now reads the current focus when it builds a mesh, so a
   rebuilt sphere is born already dimmed if it belongs outside the cone.
2. The frame loop watches the mesh map's size and re-applies the focus when it
   changes, which catches a rebuild without polling materials every frame.

**This one genuinely needs eyes.** If nodes still fail to dim, the cause is
something else and the next thing to check is whether `applyFocus` is running
at all with a populated mesh map.

## Still true from V0.2

Carried forward so this file stands alone.

**The cadence enum is broken and blocks milestone 3.** Four nodes fit none of
the five buckets: both policy-rate announcements are eight per year filed as
monthly, the Census is quinquennial filed as annual, SNA 2008 is generational
filed as annual. `releases_per_year` carries the truth and drives the pulses, so
nothing looks wrong yet.

**`last_updated` is null everywhere.** Not wired to a source. Left null rather
than invented.

**SEPH over-ranks** — 7th weighted, 13th raw. Same thin-chain cause as the
Economic Outlook above.

**No search and no filtering.** Click-to-focus is done, but there is still no
way to *find* a node without spotting it by eye, which is the natural companion
to focus and the next thing to bite at scale.

**Field of view stays at 24°.** At a wide angle apparent size is dominated by
distance from the camera, which inverts the size = authority rule.

**The lesson from the unmounted room:** when a toggle has no visible effect,
check whether the thing is mounted before adjusting how it looks. Two rounds of
colour tuning were spent on objects that were not in the scene.

## Recommended next steps

1. **Search, or at least find-by-name.** Focus is only half of navigation.
   Selecting a node you cannot locate is not much use, and this is the point at
   which the graph stops being small enough to scan.
2. **Finish the provincial spine** — royalties, health, advanced education. The
   fiscal core is in; these are the remaining hubs that municipalities, school
   authorities and post-secondaries will attach to.
3. **Settle the cadence model**, then wire pulses to real schedules.
4. **Municipal fan-out by template.** Map Grande Prairie fully to establish the
   pattern, then replicate structurally, verifying URLs per instance. ~330
   municipalities, ~60 school authorities, 26 post-secondaries — only templating
   makes that tractable.
5. **CPI Manual and COICOP.** Still no document found where StatCan or BLS names
   the manual as its basis. Without one, the edge does not get created.
