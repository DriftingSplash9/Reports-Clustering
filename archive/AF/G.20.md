## Findings

### 21. Guinea (round 21, `af-guinea-commune-finance.json`)

Resolved a lead flagged-but-not-pursued in the earlier Guinea round (AF/G.17's `gn-labe-pdl-2025-2029`, which showed zero dotations 2020-2023): Commune Urbaine de Labé's own council deliberation (N°007/CU/L/2021, independently re-fetched from communelabe.org) shows a FY2021 adopted budget of 7,510,760,123 GNF and FY2020 execution figures with dotations/transferts appearing as a real revenue line — a partial, not full, resolution of the earlier tension. ANAFIC's own FNDL procedures manual states its allocation formula in capitals for emphasis: population-indexed with a fixed per-inhabitant amount for every commune EXCEPT Conakry, which is treated separately (mechanism not detailed in the excerpt reviewed). The Ministry of Budget's own 2021 citizen's budget guide gives the national aggregate: 402.63 billion GNF to ANAFIC for local-investment support, plus a separate 13.83 billion GNF split between the 7 administrative regions (3.79bn) and all decentralized collectivités (10.04bn). Conakry's own city site remains entirely unreachable; no primary decree for its 2024 commune restructuring was located.

### 22. Tunisia (round 22, `af-tunisia-commune-finance.json`)

The 2018 Code des Collectivités Locales (Art. 131-132) commits the State to progressively making ressources propres the "part déterminante" of each collectivité's resources, and creates (Art. 61) the Haute Instance des Finances Locales (HIFL). HIFL's own second annual report (2020) quantifies the gap directly: transfers to local governments have not exceeded 3% of the state budget's own resources "depuis plusieurs années," while HIFL's own composite "autonomy index" for all local governments stood at 37.3% and its "decentralization index" fell to 4% in 2020. A Ministry of Interior-adjacent diagnostic (hosted on Tunisia's own official collectivités portal, in partnership with SKL International) on the distressed commune of Ezzouhour (Kasserine) is one of the starkest single-commune dependency ratios found in this branch: state transfers "plus de 92%" of Title I operating resources, own resources never exceeding 7.27% across 2017-2020. CPSCL's own 2024 comparative balance sheet confirms an active ~300 million-TND loan portfolio to Tunisian local governments. A Sfax 2024 budget document and a Bizerte 2017 Cour des Comptes report were both confirmed to exist but deliberately not minted — Sfax's interior pages weren't OCR'd, and Bizerte's OCR output scrambled its numeric tables badly enough to fail the verbatim standard.

### 23. Togo (round 23, `af-togo-commune-finance.json`)

Two independent Cour des Comptes du Togo per-commune control reports (Amou 1, exercices 2020-2023; Blitta 1, exercices 2020-2021) both confirm real FACT (Fonds d'Appui aux Collectivités Territoriales) dotations with a genuine execution gap: Amou 1 received 75,675,903 FCFA in FACT investment dotations but only disbursed 25.48% of it as actual payment. Both reports independently quote Togo's 2019 decentralization law (Art. 82-84 on compétences propres/partagées/transférées; Art. 336 on commune investment resources) as their legal framework, and Blitta 1's own analysis concludes the commune has a "faible capacité d'investissement" despite the legal apparatus being in place. The Cour des Comptes' own report-listing page confirms 51 per-commune reports published as of November 2025 — this is a large, still-growing series with many more named communes available for a future round.

### 24. Benin (round 24, `af-benin-commune-finance.json`)

Benin's 2020 FADeC per-commune allocation arrêté (verified directly from budgetbenin.bj) names every one of Bénin's 77 communes individually with fonctionnement/investissement/total FCFA figures — Cotonou 1,186,298,000 FCFA, Porto-Novo 625,201,000 FCFA, Parakou 626,423,000 FCFA among them — and its own "Vu" clauses directly cite the 2008 FADeC founding decree by number. The FY2024 Loi de Finances annex ("Note analytique sur les finances locales") quantifies a real year-on-year decline the document flags itself: FADeC resources falling from 71,610.4 million FCFA (2023) to 53,748.4 million FCFA (2024), a stated 24.9% regression. The CURRENT governing law (Loi n°2021-14, Code de l'administration territoriale) has a reproducible corrupted-font problem across every mirror re-checked this session — logged honestly as unreadable rather than quoted from garbled OCR text; the 2008 decree (which cites the three 1999 organic laws it superseded-into-practice) serves as the citable legal-power baseline instead.

### 25. Sierra Leone (round 25, `af-sierra-leone-municipal.json` — final country of this batch)

The Local Government Act, 2022 (verified directly from mof.gov.sl) states in its own preamble that its purpose is "to repeal and replace the Local Government Act 2004" — minted with a `cites` edge to the now-superseded 2004 Act (which received an UPDATE note in `af-sierra-leone.json` flagging the supersession), matching the Mali/Burkina Faso statutory-lineage pattern established earlier in this push. The 2022 Act's own Section 53 sets a real grant-adequacy floor: "Parliament shall appropriate to a Local Council as a tied grant for each devolved service at least the amount necessary to continue the operation and maintenance of that service." A Sierra Leone Gazette (Ministry of Finance, FY2022) names Freetown/Makeni/Koidu New Sembehun/Kenema/Bo City Councils as a weighted-formula Category with real sector totals (health Le 42.19bn, education Le 37.06bn of a Le 150.85bn total local-council allocation) — per-council breakdowns exist in later pages of the same document but weren't re-extracted this session. **Correction to this round's own earlier compacted summary**: independent re-fetch of the Auditor-General's 2024 report found only qualitative findings for Bonthe and Karene District Councils (a modified audit opinion; a late grant-disbursement date), no quantitative figures for either, contrary to what the compacted summary had characterized; and the "Transform Freetown One Year Report" was found to contain no specific transfer/grant amount at all. Both were minted honestly reflecting what re-verification actually found, and the discrepancy is logged in the file's own `_meta`.

## Secondary observations

- **A compaction-survival gap is a real, distinct failure mode from an access failure, and needs its own handling.** Two of this round's five country dispatches (Guinea, Sierra Leone) had their raw agent output compacted down to a narrative summary with real-looking figures but no URLs, before synthesis happened. The safe response was to treat every figure in that summary as an unverified lead, not a citable fact, and re-derive sourcing from scratch — which caught one real discrepancy (the Sierra Leone ASSL/Transform-Freetown figures) that would otherwise have been minted as false verbatim quotes. Worth normalizing as a standing check: when synthesis happens in a different session/context than dispatch, re-verify before minting, don't just trust the summary's specificity as a proxy for its accuracy.
- **The dispatched Agent-tool subagents share this session's own `/tmp` filesystem** — a genuinely useful recovery path discovered this round. When raw findings are lost to compaction but the underlying agent used Bash/curl/pdftotext (rather than only WebFetch's own internal summarization), the extracted text files often still exist on disk and can be grepped/read directly, without needing to re-fetch from the internet at all. This worked for Togo/Tunisia/Benin this round (recovered via file timestamps) but not Guinea/Sierra Leone (those two agents apparently used WebFetch exclusively, which doesn't write to the shared filesystem).
- **Togo's Cour des Comptes per-commune report series (51 published as of Nov 2025) is large and still growing** — a strong candidate for a dedicated future round if Thomas wants deeper single-country coverage rather than continuing to spread across new countries.
