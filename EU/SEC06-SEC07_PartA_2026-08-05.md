# SEC06 + SEC07 — Part A extraction record

**Section VI — European Economic and Social Committee** and **Section VII —
European Committee of the Regions**, Draft Budget 2027. Extracted 2026-08-05
under `Research.1.md` §6. Priority A2 in `G.21.md` / `G.22.md`.

**Why one file for two sections.** The two committees share their Title 2
services, and the central finding here is an asymmetry *between* them (S67-01).
Splitting them would put the two halves of one measurement in different files.
Records are prefixed `S06-`, `S07-` or `S67-` (spanning both).

**Source.** `EU/SEC06.pdf` (39 pp) and `EU/SEC07.pdf` (35 pp), read in full from
disk, text extracted with `pypdf`. Quotes are copy-pasted from extracted text.

**Retrieval URLs backfilled 2026-08-05** from the pattern established and
hash-verified at `G.24.md` finding 1:

```
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/budget/data/DB/2027/en/SEC06.pdf
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/budget/data/DB/2027/en/SEC07.pdf
```

**Neither of these two has been fetched.** Only `SEC05.pdf` was hash-verified
against its URL; SEC06 and SEC07 are **constructed by pattern**. The pattern is
well evidenced — it reproduced SEC05 byte-for-byte and the 2026 siblings are
indexed under the same scheme — but this branch has been wrong about a filename
convention before. **Verify by fetch before importing anything built on these.**

### S06-01 — a quantified obligation from a named regulation

```
URL:       https://eur-lex.europa.eu/budget/data/DB/2027/en/SEC06.pdf
LOCATION:  p. 4, §2 "PRIORITIES IN THE EESC BUDGET", second priority
           ("Financing of the digital transformation processes")
QUOTE:     "The Cybersecurity Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2023/2841 sets clear
           expectations for the budget to be allocated to cybersecurity across
           the EU institutions. Each EU entity must allocate a sufficient portion
           of its ICT budget to cybersecurity, with a long-term indicative target
           of at least 10% of the total ICT budget. Despite the repeated requests
           and the support from the rapporteur of the European Parliament, only
           3.95% of the DIIT budget was allocated by the budgetary authority to
           cybersecurity in 2025."
NAMES:     Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2023/2841
TENSE:     PRESENT
NOTES:     **The best-evidenced instrument-to-budget-line relationship found in
           any institutional section so far**, and better than anything in
           SEC05: a named regulation, a quantified target (10 % of total ICT
           budget), and the institution's own measured performance against it
           (3.95 %). That is a documented obligation *and* a documented
           shortfall in one provision.
           `relationship_type` is arguable and **not picked**: the regulation
           does not supply data, so `uses_data_from` is wrong, but "long-term
           indicative target" is weaker than a method. Closest is
           `methodology_depends_on`; flagged rather than chosen, per §6.
           A near-duplicate statement appears at p. 2 with a different number —
           "Even with the economies of scale generated through joint IT services
           with the Committee of the Regions, only 4% of their budgets could be
           earmarked for cybersecurity". 4 % vs 3.95 % is rounding, and the two
           sentences have different subjects (both committees vs the DIIT
           budget). Recorded so the pair is not mistaken for a conflict.
           **Regulation 2023/2841 is not in SEC07 at all** — zero hits for
           "Cybersecurity Regulation" and "2023/2841" across SEC07's full text,
           despite SEC06 saying the investment is a joint approach and SEC07
           being the other party to it. Second instance of the S67-01 asymmetry.
```

### S06-02 — an allowance set by a dated Council decision

```
URL:       https://eur-lex.europa.eu/budget/data/DB/2027/en/SEC06.pdf
LOCATION:  p. 4, §2, first priority ("Empowering the political work of the
           EESC")
QUOTE:     "Appointed by the Council from all EU Member States, EESC members
           receive an allowance based on Art. 301 TFEU for the provision of their
           expertise. […] They are reimbursed for actual travel expenses within a
           set limit, receive modest compensation for travel time and distance,
           and are entitled to an allowance of EUR 367 for accommodation and
           living expenses per meeting day.
           This allowance was updated by the Council on 21 June 2024 with
           Decision (EU) 2024/1809, the first adjustment in 11 years. This
           represents a 26.55% increase in the daily allowance compared to 2013,
           intended to compensate for the rising costs over the years."
NAMES:     Council Decision (EU) 2024/1809
           Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (Article 301)
TENSE:     PRESENT as to the entitlement; PAST as to the update
NOTES:     A named, dated instrument setting a specific figure that drives a
           budget line — the strongest evidence class in `Research.1.md` §7
           ("a regulation naming a series" territory, applied to a rate rather
           than a series).
           **Directly comparable to the Canadian indexation material** and worth
           flagging for `Research.1.md` §8 item 1a, which exists to make exactly
           these comparisons: a benefit rate frozen for 11 years, then adjusted
           once by 26,55 %, by a dated instrument. That is the *opposite* of an
           indexed rate, and §8 item 1b says explicitly that a provision showing
           there is **no formula** is a finding. This is one, for the EU layer.
           No index is named. The adjustment is discretionary and retrospective.
```

### S67-02 — the request is not the Draft Budget, and the gap is measurable

```
URL:       https://eur-lex.europa.eu/budget/data/DB/2027/en/SEC06.pdf
           https://eur-lex.europa.eu/budget/data/DB/2027/en/SEC05.pdf
LOCATION:  SEC06 p. 2 (narrative total) and p. 14 (EXPENDITURE summary table).
           SEC05 p. 2 (narrative table "Total Budget request") and p. 19
           (EXPENDITURE summary table).
QUOTE:     SEC06 narrative: "The total budget requested for 2027 amounts to EUR
           187 651 416." and "— A total increase of 4.47% compared to the year
           2026"
           SEC06 table: "Total 183 122 221 179 626 652 172 645 707,04"
           SEC05 narrative: "Total Budget request 206 168 000 200 193 000 2,98 %"
           SEC05 table: "Total 205 670 000 200 193 000 190 836 452,83"
NAMES:     Draft Budget (of the European Union)
           statement of estimates (of each Institution)
TENSE:     PRESENT
NOTES:     **This is the worked instance of S05-01's boilerplate warning**, which
           until now had no numbers behind it. That paragraph — identical at the
           head of every section — says the Commission "adjusted the estimates of
           all Institutions" and that introduction figures may differ from "those
           integrated into the Draft Budget". Here is the difference, measured:

           | Section | Narrative request | Draft Budget table | Gap |
           |---|---|---|---|
           | SEC05 (ECA) | 206 168 000 | 205 670 000 | 498 000 |
           | SEC06 (EESC) | 187 651 416 | 183 122 221 | 4 529 195 |

           **The headline growth rates diverge more than the totals do**, because
           they are ratios of a reduced numerator to an unchanged base. Verified
           by calculation against the sections' own 2026 figures:
           — SEC06: request is +4,47 % on 2026 (as printed); the Draft Budget
             table is **+1,95 %**. Less than half the advertised increase.
           — SEC05: request is +2,98 % (as printed); the table is **+2,74 %**.
           2026 comparatives are identical in both places in both sections, so
           the divergence is entirely in the 2027 column.
           **No verdict.** The document states both numbers and explains the
           mechanism in its own boilerplate; it simply never reconciles them at
           the point of use. Same shape as `sc-46`, but **inside a single
           document** rather than between two. Anyone quoting an institution's
           "2027 increase" needs to say which of the two they mean.
```
