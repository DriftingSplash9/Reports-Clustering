## 2. The two questions step 5b requires answering

**Should this sweep happen more or less often than every 20?** **Neither — the interval is not the
problem, and the evidence says so.** The previous sweep ran 2026-09-07, seven handoffs ago, not
twenty; it was off-cycle. In those seven handoffs three defects appeared, and **none of them
predates that sweep** — the oldest, the 172 figure, went stale immediately after it. So the
interval is not letting rot sit: it is catching it about as fast as it arrives. Sweeping more
often would find the same defects sooner at a real cost per pass; sweeping less often would let
roughly nine accumulate instead of three, which is still recoverable.

**RULED by Thomas, 2026-09-09, and it supersedes the recommendation below — which he improved on.**
*"the one true count is going to be the handoff as it actually looks and keeps count, it will always
supercede other counts."* **HANDOFF §2 is the count; every other figure is a dated snapshot that §2
overrides without argument.** This is stronger than the "don't write counts" rule proposed here,
because it makes staleness HARMLESS rather than trying to prevent it — a reader who knows §2 wins
cannot be misled by a stale figure elsewhere, and nobody has to police how files are written. Now
in `PLAYBOOK.md` §2 rule 4, with the two consequences the sweep's own evidence forces: `validate`
is what §2 is refreshed from (so the chain is validate → §2 → everything else), and **for a count
§2 does not carry there is nothing to supersede it**, so the file must name the recount command
instead of a figure. That last point is the real lesson from `"172 edges are stamped"` — it sat
wrong for seven handoffs precisely because no higher number existed to correct it.

**The original recommendation, kept as the reasoning that led there:** **two of the three** defects
are the same species — a live count typed into prose — and that species is mechanically preventable without a
human sweep. **Recommendation for Thomas (in §3): a slow-layer file may name a count only as a
dated observation with the command that produced it, or not at all.** Both stale counts either
pointed at `validate` already or should have; the phrasing deliberately still permits a number
where the number is the warning (e.g. "the GNI inventory PDF backs 35 edges — select every live
edge on the URL or destroy the others' windows"), because a dated, sourced figure is exactly what
that needs.

**What the rule would NOT have caught is the third defect**, and that is the honest limit of it. A
status word — `parked`, `open`, `blocked`, `settled` — goes stale when a DECISION lands, not when
the corpus grows, and nothing mechanical notices. That class is what steps 4 and 5 exist for, and
it is the one to watch on the next sweep now that the counting half is closed off.

**Does Thomas want a recap of past sweeps across the last 100 handoffs?** **Not asked this time.**
The procedure requires it only on a 5b where the archive has crossed a new hundred; the archive
stands at 80. Handoff 079 anticipated this correctly. The next occasion is handoff 100.
