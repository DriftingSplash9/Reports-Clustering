## The theme: the build output cannot make a visual judgement, and this session
## proved it four times in a row

V2.10 already records the rule — *shipping visuals nobody has looked at costs
about a session per unlooked-at change* — as a lesson learned from V0.1, V0.3,
V0.4 and V0.9. This session is the first one where looking happened *inside* the
work rather than after it, and the result is worth writing down precisely,
because it is an argument for a workflow rather than a list of bugs.

**Four faults, each revealed only by rendering, each created by the fix before it:**

1. **The hover card's real bug was not the arithmetic.** Thomas reported the card
   being cut off. The obvious cause is the positioning rule, which flipped the
   card to the other side of the cursor without clamping, so a tall card got
   negative coordinates. That was fixed — and the screenshot still showed the
   card 92px off the bottom. The actual cause is an *ordering* problem: `place()`
   measures `offsetHeight`, but the card's content comes from React state, so on
   the pointermove that first hovers a node the height being clamped against
   belongs to the previous card. React then renders the real content and no
   further pointermove arrives, because the cursor is sitting still on the node.
   **Measured: the CPI's card is 552px tall and sat at top 440 in a 900px
   window,** with a correct clamp operating on a stale number.
2. **The fix for that was also wrong, in a way only rendering shows.**
   `useEffect` fires after paint, so the reposition happened a frame late — the
   card would visibly flinch on every hover, and in the headless capture it had
   not run at all. `useLayoutEffect` is the correct hook and the screenshot is
   what said so.
3. **Fixing the clamp created a new occlusion.** Clamped to y=18, a tall card sits
   directly underneath the search box, which has a higher z-index. The title —
   the one thing a card must show — went from cut off to hidden. Raising the card
   above the search panel is safe rather than merely convenient: the card is only
   visible while the pointer is over a node on the canvas, and the pointer cannot
   be in the search field at the same time.
4. **Shelving isolated nodes re-introduced the oldest framing bug in the
   project.** Placing them at 1.3× the cloud radius plus 60 units meant the camera
   fit framed cloud-*plus*-shelf: the graph shrank to about half the frame and
   slid off-centre. **This is "fit to the subject, not the scenery" arriving for
   the third time** — V0.5 found the camera fitting a platform's diagonal, V0.7
   fixed it by deleting the platform, and V0.12 recreated it within an hour of
   adding the first new scenery since. The fit now covers connected nodes only.

**And a fifth, on the flag**, which is the same class from a different angle: the
first version used 🇨🇦 / 🇺🇸. Regional-indicator pairs render as flags only if
the font supports it, and **Windows does not** — Segoe UI Emoji ships no flag
glyphs, so Thomas would have seen the bare letters "CA" and "US". The headless
browser had no emoji font at all and drew two empty boxes, which is how it was
caught. The second attempt hand-rolled an eleven-point maple leaf as a short
polygon, and Thomas's verdict — *"the cad flag is ugly"* — was correct. The third
uses the official geometry fetched from the public-domain flag file, 9600×4800,
leaf stem curves and all. **A flag is a specified object; there was never a reason
to invent one.**

**The transferable rule is narrower than "look at it".** It is: *a change to
anything positional, and any glyph that depends on the reader's font stack,
cannot be verified from source or from a passing build.* Both categories were
represented here and both were wrong in the first implementation.

### The palette was the sixth, and it is the one the harness could not have caught

Reported by Thomas from a real display: *"The color gradients are a nice idea but
it is too hard to distinguish them for me."*

He was right and the numbers say why. The four Canadian shades ran gold → orange
→ vermilion → brick across **roughly 20° of hue** — a legible gradient on a
swatch card, and four indistinguishable dots at fit zoom, which is the only place
it matters. V0.7 introduced the scope recolouring and V0.8 called it *the single
biggest legibility win the project has*; both were judging the **families**
against each other, where the separation is real. Nobody had checked the steps
*within* a family, and Canada is 97 of 133 nodes.

**Each family now spans two or three anchor hues rather than one:**

| Family | Was | Now | Internal span |
|---|---|---|---|
| Canada | gold → brick | gold → orange → red → crimson | 43° → 338°, ~65° |
| United States | cyan → mid blue | cyan → azure → indigo → deep blue | 188° → 231°, ~43° |
| International | violet → pink | violet → magenta-violet | 268° → 291°, ~23° |

**Adjacent levels now differ by more than the old family's entire range**, while
the gaps *between* families stay as large as the spans — roughly 48° from Canadian
crimson to international violet, 40° from international to US indigo, 50° from US
cyan back round to Canadian gold. Value moves with hue rather than instead of it,
broader publisher lighter, so the levels stay separable a second way at sizes where
hue starts to fail.

Two constraints are written into `palette.ts` because they are easy to close by
accident: Canadian institutional (338°) and international commercial (291°) are the
closest pair in the palette and are held apart by value as well as hue, so the
Canadian one must not be lightened; and US institutional is a dark blue rather than
an indigo specifically so it does not drift toward the international violet.

**Why this one is not in the same class as the other five.** The harness caught
geometry — positions, clipping, glyph presence — because those are true or false in
a screenshot. It cannot catch *discriminability*, because the headless capture is a
software-rendered composite and the question is what a person can tell apart on
their own monitor. V2.10 already lists "four Canadian shades never judged on a real
display" as an open item across five sessions. **It was open because only Thomas
could close it**, and the answer when he finally looked was that the encoding did
not work.

### The harness, recorded so the next session does not rebuild it

`node_modules` in the project is a Windows build and esbuild refuses to run under
Linux, so neither `tsc` nor `npm run validate` will run in an agent sandbox
against the checked-out tree. The working arrangement:

- Copy `src`, `scripts` and the configs to a scratch directory and `npm install`
  there. `tsc --noEmit`, `npm run validate` and `vite build` all then work.
- For rendering: `npx puppeteer browsers install chrome-headless-shell`, plus
  `apt-get download` of `libxdamage1 libxkbcommon0 libxcomposite1 libxrandr2
  libxfixes3 libgbm1 libxext6 libxcb1 libx11-6`, unpacked with `dpkg-deb -x` and
  reached through `LD_LIBRARY_PATH` — there is no root in the sandbox. Launch with
  `--use-angle=swiftshader --enable-unsafe-swiftshader`.
- Serve the built `dist` over `python3 -m http.server`. **A dev server does not
  survive between tool calls**, so the server and the capture have to run in one
  call.
- **CSS transitions do not advance in the headless shell.** Computed style freezes
  at the start value indefinitely. This cost a wrong diagnosis: the panel tab
  measured as stuck in its expanded position while its inline style read the
  collapsed one. Inject `* { transition: none !important }` before measuring end
  states.

## The scope decision

Full reasoning is in REPORTS.md. The shape of it: **the sentence "this is a tool
for economic/statistical reports, not a general knowledge graph of everything" was
doing no work in its first half and all of its work in the second.**

What has actually kept the corpus from sprawling is three rules, none of which
mentions economics — the evidence standard, the requirement that a node be
published on a cadence, and (until this session) that every node connect. Those
are why the graph is a hundred-odd nodes of real material rather than two thousand
plausible ones. "Economic" never had to bind, because documented derivation chains
are rare everywhere.

It also described the project badly. **Municipal tax rate bylaws and provincial
assessment guidelines are not economic reports in any ordinary sense**, and they
are among the best-evidenced material in the corpus.

So: a node is a recurrently published document that another document names as an
input to itself. This admits health, environment and emissions, justice, education
and the occupational and trade classifications. It still excludes anything whose
"dependency" is influence rather than derivation, and anything published once.

### And the plan that proposed it was immediately half-refuted

`EXPANSION-V1.md`, written the day before, argued for entering new territory
**through its classification hub**, on the reasoning that a classification is a
hub by construction and would attach a new cluster at a hub instead of dangling.
The first attempt tested it:

| | result |
|---|---|
| NAICS | +2 incoming edges — **both from surveys**, neither from a classification |
| NOC read directly | 1 weak edge, 1 documented non-dependency |
| NAPCS read directly | **0 edges**, 2 documented non-dependencies |

**A classification document describes alignment, comparability and concordance,
and none of those is derivation.** NAPCS states outright that it is *"not fully
compatible with the CPC"*. NOC is *"comparable to"* ISCO and the same passage
records that *"certain conceptual differences… limit comparability"*. The hub is a
hub because other documents name it, not because it names things.

**The corrected move: find the programmes coded on a standard and read their
methodology pages.** That is what produced every NAICS edge in the corpus,
including both of this session's.

NAPCS therefore entered the graph with no edges at all, which under the old loader
would have meant it never appeared. Which is the next decision.

## Isolated nodes are kept

Reports with no surviving edge were dropped since V0.1, on the reasoning that a
disconnected node carries no information in a dependency graph.

The cost was specific. `fed-h15` is among the most thoroughly researched nodes in
the corpus — every source it names is a reporting form, private transaction data,
unnamed banks or a bare agency — and V2.10 calls it *the worked example the whole
disclosure decision was waiting for.* **It has never once appeared on screen.**
Three logs described dropping it as "the evidence standard working." It was not.
The evidence standard working is showing it and showing why it is alone.

An isolated node is not an absence of information. It is the statement *this
programme exists and nothing published names its inputs.*

**Dangling edges are still dropped** — an edge pointing at an id that does not
exist is a data error, not an island, and tolerating those is what lets slices
arrive in any order.

**They are shelved in a margin**, pinned, in a column at a distance measured from
the connected cloud so it stays outside as the graph grows. Thomas chose this over
the alternative (a distinct hollow rendering) on the ground that a dimmed or
outlined sphere is indistinguishable from what distance haze already does to the
far side of the cloud. That was the right call and it was not the recommendation.

**This does not breach the position rule.** That rule forbids coordinates
asserting a hierarchy the dependency data does not contain; isolation is read
directly off the edge list rather than asserted, the same escape that makes
depth-in-the-graph a legitimate vertical axis. Having no edges is a fact about the
edges.

## Termini

`terminal_reason`: `unpublishable` | `unidentified` | `redistributed` |
`confidential`. Full argument in REPORTS.md.

The prompting question was Thomas's: *what could I say that would allow Haver
Analytics and the Minister's unnamed commodity brokers?* The answer is that they
fail for two different reasons and **neither is fixed by loosening the evidence
standard** — Haver publishes nothing of its own, and the BVM Regulation names a
*slot* whose occupant is set by ministerial order. What was missing was a way for
the graph to say *why a chain stops*.

The argument is the one that admitted commercial sources in V0.4: **the node rule
was excluding real facts about fragility.** The graph exists to answer *what would
break if this changed*, and for a whole class of named, real, load-bearing inputs
it was answering nothing at all.

**Excluded from authority by subtraction**, exactly as commercial nodes are and
for the same sink-leak reason. Three nodes built from this session's quotes: the
CRA PD7 accounts behind SEPH, the GST files behind the Monthly Survey of
Manufacturing, and the T1/T2/T3010/T5013 data behind the annual manufacturing
survey.

**Both validator rules were fired against doctored data before being trusted:**

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| baseline, real corpus | nothing fires |
| a terminus with outgoing edges | error, one per edge |
| a node both commercial and a terminus | error |
| a well-formed terminus with an inbound edge | nothing fires |
| **subtraction** | adding a terminus + its edge moved **0** ranked scores, max delta **0.00e+0** |

## The manufacturing slice, and what was refused

Extraction came from a parallel Grok track under the Brief IV format. **Of six
proposed relationships, four were refused**, and the refusals are more interesting
than the takes.

**Taken:** MSM (record 2101) and ASML (record 2103) as nodes; `statcan-gdp-monthly
-> statcan-msm`; `statcan-msm -> statcan-asml`; MSM and ASML each to `naics`; a
weak `statcan-lfs -> noc`.

**Refused, with the quote that refuses it:**

- **`statcan-seph -> statcan-lfs`.** Record 2612: *"comparisons with independent
  sources such as the Labour Force Survey are performed."* The word *independent*
  is doing the work. Third instance of the "comparable with is not a dependency
  claim" family.
- **`statcan-asml -> statcan-msm`.** Record 2103 analyses its estimates against
  the MSM as a related series — a validation check, not an input. Reading it as an
  edge would have created a **false mutual pair** with the real ASML edge, which
  matters more than usual now that mutual pairs are known to move the ranking.
- **`noc -> isco-08`** and **`napcs -> cpc`** and **`napcs -> naics`**, all on the
  comparability wording above.

### The tense trap, and it is a new error class

Record 2101 says: *"Up to and including 2003, the MSM was benchmarked to the
Annual Survey of Manufactures and Logging (ASML)."*

**Verbatim, correctly located, in the methodology section, and describing an
arrangement that ended twenty-two years ago.** Nothing in the extraction format
catches it — every check the project has is about whether the document says it,
not about when. The edge exists on record 2103's present-tense *"the data are used
by"*, which is the other half of the lesson: when a relationship is described in
the past tense, look for the live statement from the other end before either
taking it or dropping it.

Recorded in REPORTS.md, because it will recur across hundreds of IMDB pages —
these are living documents with historical notes folded in and no visual
separation between the two.

### The weakest edge in the slice, flagged rather than buried

`statcan-lfs -> noc` rests on *"the redesign of the NOC can have significant
implications for several Surveys, such as the Labour Force Survey (LFS)"* — a
statement that a revision *reaches* the LFS, not that the LFS *uses* the NOC. It
is taken on the precedent of `boc-prime-rate -> boc-policy-rate`, which rests on
the Bank's word "affects". Thomas's instruction was to keep it and close it
properly; the LFS-side passage is recorded as a lead. **It is also the session's
one new bridge.**

## Two things the validator caught that reasoning did not

Worth recording because both were mine and both were caught by a rule someone
else wrote.

**NOC's cadence clocks were reversed.** I filed structural revision (decennial) as
`releases_per_year` and content updates (annual) as `changes_per_year`, which
asserts a document changing ten times more often than it is published. The
changes-cannot-exceed-releases rule fired on it — **a rule earning its keep on a
node type it was not written for.** Corrected: a new version appears about
annually, the structure moves about once a decade.

**Two `_dropped` notes described live edges.** The V0.8 check — added after two
documents recommended already-finished work off stale notes — fired within minutes
of the notes being written. Both were commentary about an edge that exists rather
than a dropped edge; the commentary belonged in the edge's `basis`.

## Recommended next steps

**The next session is the debt audit — Tier −1 in BACKLOG.md, in full, before any
expansion.** That is Thomas's instruction and it is not a suggestion.

**Expect a backlog of extraction to adjudicate before starting it.** The parallel
track is running over a long gap and may return several items' worth of material.
Adjudicate it, merge what verifies, and **measure the bridge fraction after each
merge rather than at the end** — V0.11's Ontario episode is the precedent: two
edges, both bridges, and BACKLOG's stop signal fired on the first thing added
after the criterion was re-measured. Do not let a weekend of material land in one
undifferentiated batch.

Then, in order:

1. **`programme_id` and its validator rule.** Two incidents already. Backfill is
   ~20 nodes now and a session after an IMDB sweep. Note that URL equality does
   *not* work: six node groups legitimately share a URL and both real duplicates
   had different URLs.
2. **Re-audit `_dropped`.** Re-read every `unpublishable-source` and `no-document`
   note against `terminal_reason` — three were converted this session, roughly
   forty were not. Resolve the classifications V0.9 flagged. Rebuild the lead list
   in BACKLOG.
3. **`determination` and the disclosure ratio.** Four logs old each. The ratio has
   a deadline rather than a preference: shipping it after a volume sweep means
   shipping it into a graph where it is mostly null.
4. **Glob-import the slices**, fix the stale numbers in BACKLOG, and take one pass
   at the small renderer defects.
5. Only then: the classification-hub entry done the *corrected* way, the SUPC node,
   and BACKLOG Tier 2.

**A parallel extraction track is running and has a fixed brief.** `Research.1.md`
is a standing document — pasted whole into every thread, never edited between
threads, and deliberately not one of the `V*.md` files, so that the research side
cannot drift the way the project documents legitimately do. It carries the
evidence standard, the extract-don't-adjudicate split and the measurements behind
it, both traps in §5 (the comparability phrases and the tense problem), the
`terminal_reason` kinds, the JSON schema, and a four-item work queue in priority
order: **interprovincial first**, then business/banking/industry, then
international standards entered the corrected way, then US material last — the
last on the measured ground that CA↔US direct documented dependencies number
**one**, so American material adds territory rather than structure.

Its output is two parts, and the second is deliberately fenced: Part A is the
extraction record with verbatim quotes and no verdicts, Part B is optional draft
slice JSON, and **any claim in Part B not backed by a quote in Part A is deleted
without being read.** That is the V0.11 lesson turned into a rule the format
enforces rather than a hope.

**JSON rather than charts**, asked and answered: research lands as
`src/data/research/*.json` and is merged and validated by the loader. A chart is a
picture of data that would have to be re-keyed by hand, and re-keying is where
transcription errors enter.

**Sub-clusters and collapsible clusters are decided in principle and not built.**
Community detection on the edge set for position, domain as a colour overlay,
spacing plus hover labels rather than drawn hulls. That is the change that makes
500 nodes legible and it is its own session.
