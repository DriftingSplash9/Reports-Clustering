## The theme: Tier 0 closed by deletion, not by research

V0.8 left 21 seed edges with no supporting document and called them "the only
thing still gating expansion". The expectation, stated in `BACKLOG.md`, was that
most would be backfilled with a URL.

**That is not what happened.** Of the 21: four were documented, four were deleted,
and ten were left untouched. The structural gain came almost entirely from four
documents and four deletions — not from a research campaign.

This is the standard behaving as designed and it is worth stating plainly, because
the instinct when an edge fails the standard is to go hunting for a URL to justify
it. V0.8 already warned against that (*"finding a document is not the default
outcome"*). Acting on the warning turned out to mean removing things.

### The ten left alone, and why that was the right call

The tempting move was to demote the remaining ten to `implied` and declare Tier 0
finished. **That would have recorded a shortage of research budget as a finding
about the world**, which is exactly the confusion the `no-document` /
`deferred` split in the reason vocabulary exists to prevent.

They are not "searched and not found". They are "not searched". A later pass must
not read them as the former, which is the whole reason this paragraph exists.

## What was deleted, and what each deletion cost

**`fed-h15 -> fed-fomc-statement`.** The premise was false, not merely unevidenced.
The H.15 release carries **no target-range series at all**; its only federal funds
line is "Federal funds (effective)", which footnote 1 sources to the **FR 2420**
reporting form — an administrative collection, the same shape as FR 2644 behind
H.8. The seed basis had asserted H.15 "reports the current federal funds target
range set by the FOMC", and the release simply does not.

**This deletion cost a node.** It was H.15's only edge, so `fed-h15` now joins LGFF
Operating as a report dropped on every build. **The first federal release to fall
out of the graph.** That is the standard working on the LGFF precedent, and it is
also the sharpest illustration yet of what the standard costs.

There is a cheap way back, recorded as a `no-node-yet` lead: H.15's footnotes 9 and
10 both read *"Source: U.S. Treasury"* for the constant maturity series, and
describe the yields as *"interpolated by the U.S. Treasury from the daily yield
curve."* The evidence exists; the Treasury release has never been researched as a
node. One research pass restores H.15.

**`fed-fomc-statement -> bls-cpi`.** The basis hedged in its own wording, which
V0.8 had flagged as disqualifying. The Committee's longer-run goals statement names
PCE, and `fed-fomc-statement -> bea-pce` already exists with evidence.

**`bea-gdp -> bls-cpi`.** A two-hop path recorded as one edge. With
`bea-pce -> bls-cpi` documented and `bea-gdp -> bea-pce` now documented from NIPA
Handbook Chapter 5, the relationship is fully represented as GDP → PCE → CPI. The
direct edge double-counted the CPI's authority through the same mechanism and
stated it more weakly than the path it duplicated.

**`boc-mpr -> statcan-national-accounts`.** See below — this one came from the
second reader and was deleted after checking.

## The first cross-border edge

V0.8 measured it precisely: **zero standard-compliant direct official CA↔US
edges**, with the only compliant cross-border link in the entire graph being a
commodity futures price.

That count is now one. The July 2026 *Monetary Policy Report* attributes its US
activity charts to:

> *"Sources: US Bureau of Economic Analysis via Haver Analytics and Bank of Canada
> calculations, estimates and projections"*

The publishing agency is named and BEA publishes one GDP release, so
`boc-mpr -> bea-gdp` stands as documented.

**A new category surfaced with it.** The Bank reaches BEA *via Haver Analytics*, a
commercial redistributor. Haver publishes nothing of its own on a cadence, so it
cannot become a node even under the V0.5 rule that admits J.D. Power and ICE
Brent — those publish something; Haver republishes other people's data. Filed as
`unpublishable-source`.

This is the first time the corpus has caught an **intermediary sitting between two
official statistical agencies**, and it is worth settling before the cross-border
cluster is built out, because that cluster will likely be full of them. The open
question: does a redistributor in the path weaken the claim that one release
depends on another? Current answer, taken but not argued at length: no — the
underlying source is official, named, and unambiguous.

## Working with a second reader

The most transferable thing in this session is not an edge. It is a measured
finding about what a second AI is and is not good for.

**The setup.** A detailed brief was written as a PDF and fed to Grok by hand: the
evidence standard with its reasoning, worked examples of what passes and fails, the
full node-id list, an explicit statement that negative results are valuable, and a
mandatory verbatim-quote requirement for anything claimed as documented. Each of
the 21 edges was stated as **a hypothesis to be disproven, not a fact to confirm.**

**The result.** Seven DOCUMENTED verdicts came back. Every quote was checked
against the URL it was offered with. **Four held. One was refuted by its own
evidence. Two remain unverified.**

### What it is good at

Two of the four are finds that ordinary searching would not have produced.
Footnote 4 of a 40-page PDF:

> *"The LFS-Micro is a measure of underlying wage growth derived from the Labour
> Force Survey (LFS) microdata."*

That settles `boc-mpr -> statcan-lfs` and had been open for four sessions. The
BEA/Haver source line above is the other. **Document-finding and quotation are
reliable.**

### What it is bad at, and the direction it fails in

`boc-mpr -> statcan-national-accounts` came back DOCUMENTED, with this offered as
proof: *"Sources: Statistics Canada and Bank of Canada calculations and
estimates."* That is agency-level attribution — which the brief specifically warned
fails, and which **the same reply had said, two paragraphs earlier, it was treating
as a failure.**

The document was then searched directly. **The phrase "national accounts" does not
occur in the July 2026 MPR once.** Neither does "by industry".

It also noticed, wrote down, and then overrode a direction problem: its own quote
for `statcan-national-accounts -> statcan-gdp-monthly` says the *monthly* estimates
are reconciled to the *quarterly*, which is the reverse of the edge as recorded.
It marked the edge DOCUMENTED anyway.

**The pattern: extraction is reliable, adjudication is not, and adjudication fails
in one consistent direction — toward yes.**

### The fix, and it worked

A second brief removed the verdict field from most rounds. Instead of "is this
documented", the ask became "quote every passage, name every release it names, and
write `AGENCY ONLY` where it names only an agency."

**The behaviour change was immediate and large.** The next round used `AGENCY ONLY`
correctly four times, attempted no adjudication anywhere, and did not repeat the
error. **Structure the task so the weak step is not required, and the weakness
stops mattering.**

### The rule this buys

**A second reader extracts; this project adjudicates.** Delegating the judgement is
what breaks the evidence standard, and delegating the reading is what makes it
affordable. The verbatim-quote requirement is what makes the split enforceable — it
turned a permanent silent error into a five-minute check.

## Equalization: confirmed from the primary source, and unusable as delivered

The largest expansion available was put to the second reader, and the substance
came back confirmed. Reading SOR/2007-303 s.3.1 directly verifies it: the
Equalization revenue-base **definitions** pin themselves to named Statistics Canada
products, repeatedly and verbatim —

> *"household final consumption expenditures as determined by Statistics Canada for
> the purpose of its **Provincial and Territorial Economic Accounts**"*
> *"gross fixed capital formation in machinery and equipment, as determined by
> Statistics Canada on the basis of data for or from its **System of Macroeconomic
> Accounts**"*
> *"any of the commodities as defined by Statistics Canada for the purpose of its
> **Provincial Supply and Use Tables**"*

Counts in the retrieved text: System of Macroeconomic Accounts 11, Provincial and
Territorial Economic Accounts 11, Provincial Supply and Use Tables throughout.

**This is Tier 2's thesis confirmed, and the prize is larger than assumed.** These
are *definitions*, so every province's revenue base runs through the same handful
of named products.

**Nothing from it was added to the graph**, for one reason: the citations were
wrong. The definitions were filed under "s. 1(1) and s. 8"; they are in **s. 3.1**,
and s. 8 is the *Nova Scotia* revenue base in a different Division. Fourteen quotes
were also bundled under one heading marked "illustrative cluster", which cannot be
cited because nothing in it carries its own section number.

Good research, unusable packaging. Re-requested with one-provision-per-entry.

**One claim is outstanding and matters more than the rest.** The Regulations are
reported to name the **Survey of Employment, Payrolls and Hours** as the basis of a
payroll revenue base. SEPH is already a node and ranks **6th by authority**, so a
documented edge into it from the Equalization formula would be high-value. The
phrase was not found in the sections retrieved — but retrieval truncated before the
Revenue Base sections where it would sit, so it is **unverified, not disproven.**

## The disclosure ratio, and the trap inside it

Built as `disclosureByReport()` in `graph.ts`. It returns counts rather than one
number — `documented`, `implied`, `undisclosed`, `unpublishable`, `denied`,
`leads` — because the `_dropped` reason vocabulary draws distinctions a lone ratio
throws away, and the card can discard what it does not want but cannot recover what
the function already merged.

**The obvious rule is wrong, and wrong in an inverting way.** "Ratio is null when
the denominator is zero" gives **94 of 121 nodes a ratio, nearly all of them 100%**,
because a node with three documented edges and no dropped notes scores perfectly.
Under that rule a report nobody has ever examined presents as the most transparent
thing on screen.

The rule is now **null unless something was searched for and not found**, so a
non-null ratio always means a search happened and came up short. That takes it to
**22 of 120 nodes**.

Which answers V0.8's open question — *whether the denominator is useful as opposed
to merely available*. It is useful for about a sixth of the graph, and the honest
answer for the rest is "nobody asked". **The disclosure ratio is a sparse
annotation, not a universal one**, and the hover card must not render "not asked"
and "asked, fully disclosed" the same way.

**The card layout is not built.** It needs a judgement made in front of a screen.

## The V0.8 validator check caught a V0.9 error

Worth recording as a small vindication. The `_dropped` contradiction check — added
last session so that a note describing a live edge fails loudly — fired during
final verification:

```
✗ 1 note(s) describe an edge that IS in the graph — resolve or delete:
    boc-mpr -> statcan-national-accounts (no-document)
```

The refutation note had been written and the edge had **not** been deleted. Exactly
the failure mode that misled two documents before V0.8, caught automatically within
one session of the check existing. The edge was then removed, taking dependencies
from 192 to 191.
