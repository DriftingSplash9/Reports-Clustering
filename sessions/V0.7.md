## Four features that had never worked

This is the theme of the session, and it is worth stating as a pattern rather
than four bugs. Each of these had been shipped, listed in the view panel, and
described in a session log. None of them had ever done anything, and in three of
the four cases the reason was that **nobody had looked at the thing at the size
and state where it would fail.**

**Arrows were inside the spheres.** Drawn at 94% along each link, and links run
centre to centre, so on a typical 40-unit link the arrowhead sat about 2.4 units
from the target's centre — inside a sphere of radius 2.2 to 8. They had been
buried since V0.2. Deleted rather than repositioned: the pulses already carry
direction on teardrops that point the way they travel, and they carry it on the
element the eye actually follows. The accepted cost is that with Pulses off there
is now no direction cue at all.

**Glow lit nothing.** Bloom threshold was 0.5. The brightest node in the graph
reaches 0.36. Zero of 121 nodes crossed it, on any graph, ever. The irony is
recorded in V0.2: bloom had once been cranked so far that it destroyed the size
encoding, and the correction overshot into doing nothing — and because "Glow" was
a checkbox, the failure looked like a preference.

**Distance haze was broken twice over.** The fog planes were computed once at
load from the opening camera distance and never updated, so the first zoom put
every node nearer than the near plane and the effect switched itself off
silently. Separately, the link material is a custom shader and receives none of
three.js's automatic fog chunks, so 187 edges would never have fogged even when
it worked — the one part of the scene most in need of a depth cue was the only
part not getting one. Fog is now recomputed every frame against the node cloud
as seen from the current camera position, and the shader has fog code written by
hand.

**The `country` field was wrong on 11 nodes.** Typed `'CA' | 'US'` with no value
for "neither", so every international body — IMF, BIS, ILO, UN, and the joint
standards — was recorded as Canadian. ICE Futures Europe and Argus Media, both
London, were recorded as American. Wrong for five sessions and invisible the
whole time, because nothing rendered the field.

**The lesson worth keeping: an attribute nothing reads is an attribute nobody
checks, and a boolean toggle disguises a broken feature as a taste.** Two of
these four became visible only when the control was changed from a checkbox to a
slider and the value still did nothing. Both are now sliders.

## Implied references

New edge kind. `evidence: 'documented' | 'implied'`, absent meaning documented.

An implied edge means the relationship is believed on strong grounds and **no
document states it**. Not a guess about mechanism — the standing example is
Grande Prairie's financial statements and the tax rate bylaw that produced the
levy they report, where by statute no levy can exist without the bylaw and the
statements simply never name it.

Eight were promoted out of `_dropped` blocks, all with both endpoints already in
the graph. They are drawn **dashed**, **never pulsed** — a pulse asserts that
influence actually propagates, which is the exact claim these cannot make — and
**excluded from the authority calculation** by the same subtraction used for
commercial sources.

That exclusion is doing more work here than it does for commercial nodes. If an
undocumented edge could move a score, the ranking would depend on what somebody
found plausible, and the evidence standard — the most important rule in the
project — would quietly stop being load-bearing. `npm run validate` proves it
holds on every run.

**Two toggles, four states**, shaped like the focus cones: documented only (the
default), both, implied only, and neither. Implied-only is the striking one — 121
nodes frozen exactly in place with eight dashed threads between them, which is
the research backlog drawn.

The clearest finding: **the city of Grande Prairie's statements say outright that
property tax revenue is based on assessment values, and the county's do not.**
Same statute, same mechanism, one sentence of difference in disclosure. That
asymmetry was already recorded in V0.4 as prose; it is now a visible dashed line.

Also: the Alberta bitumen valuation cone goes from 5 nodes to 2 when commercial
sources are hidden. Three of the five things Alberta's bitumen floor price rests
on are private price feeds.

## Publisher scope was hiding the thing the graph is for

The change with the largest effect on legibility, and it came from looking at a
filtered view rather than the whole graph.

Colour was keyed to jurisdiction level alone. **62 of 121 nodes were "federal /
national", all the same blue** — so the largest single group on screen silently
merged Statistics Canada with the Bureau of Labor Statistics, which are the two
systems the graph most exists to tell apart. Country was carried only by a rim,
and a rim cannot separate sixty dots at fit zoom.

Scope is now the pair **(country, jurisdiction level)**:

```
Everything                121
International              11      violet
  International bodies      9
  Commercial / other        2
Canada                     85      warm — gold, orange, vermilion, brick
  Federal                  39
  Provincial               28
  Municipal                10
  Institutional             8
United States              25      cool — cyan, azure, indigo
  Federal                  23
  Institutional             2
```

**Hue family says country, position within the family says level.** Families read
in peripheral vision; shades within a family still read as one family, which is
the point — "all the Canadian ones" is a question people ask, and "all the
provincial ones across both countries" mostly is not.

Every level toggles separately, each country header toggles its group, and an
"Everything" row sits on top. `US:provincial` has a colour reserved with no nodes
behind it, because state-level releases are the obvious next expansion and that
colour should not be picked in a hurry.

**What this made visible:** the US cluster is a distinct cyan region hanging off
the Canadian mass, and the violet international bodies sit between them. The two
national systems connect mainly through shared standards rather than directly.
That was arguable from the data before and is now simply the picture.

## Platform deleted, and the camera fit fixed by accident

The platform slab was sized to the data, so it grew with the graph until it was a
wall: from most angles it either occluded the lower half of the network or forced
the camera to look down to clear it. Deleted, not defaulted off. The infinite
ground grid does the same job better — fixed cell size, so it reports scale by
how many cells the graph spans, and it is never between you and a node.

Removing it also fixed the V0.5 framing finding for free. The auto-fit was
measuring `frame.extent * 1.42` — the slab's diagonal — rather than the node
cloud, which is why the graph always opened occupying about a fifth of the frame
above a large empty floor. It now fits the nodes.

## The rim, and a mistake worth recording

Country is a second channel: fill says scope, a fresnel rim says which national
system. A sphere in a rotating scene has no border; the equivalent is brightness
rising where the surface turns away, which follows the silhouette from every
angle for free.

**The first implementation added the rim colour to the fragment and failed for
exactly the reason flag colours would have.** Amber light on a blue fill resolves
to near-white at the silhouette, so all three countries converged on the same
pale edge, and bloom then amplified it. Mixing toward the rim colour instead of
adding preserves the hue and adds no luminance, so the glow pass leaves it alone.

Rim sharpness scales with node radius. A fresnel band occupies a fixed *fraction*
of a silhouette, so at a constant exponent the rim on a radius-2.2 node would be
a quarter the width of one on a radius-8 node — thinnest exactly where the fill is
smallest and the cue matters most.

The rim is now redundant with the fill, and kept deliberately: redundant encoding
is the cheapest discriminability available, and at fit zoom a node is three pixels
across.

## Documents and protocol

**`V1.5.md`** — the first rollup, covering V0.1 through V0.5. 11,900 words of
session logs down to 2,900.

**Two session logs had been written that nobody asked for.** V0.5 and V0.6 were
both produced off a standing instruction in REPORTS.md — "at the end of a
meaningful session, write the next-numbered log" — which an agent read as
authority to act once it judged a session meaningful. The judgement was fine;
acting on it silently was the error. The first attempt at a fix removed the
agent's ability to propose at all, which threw away the useful half. The rule is
now **offer, then write**: notice, say "now's probably a good time to wrap up
V0.8, want me to?", and wait.

**The rollup now rides along with the fifth session log** rather than being its
own deferrable task. Rollup 1 was due at V0.5 and written after V0.6, having been
deferred twice in favour of more interesting work; attaching it to the log
removes the choice. `npm run logs` answers which is due.

**Corpus guidance moved from V1.5 into REPORTS.md**, under *Working with the
corpus*. Adding and editing nodes never stops being the job, so the guidance for
it should not sit in a document a later rollup will consolidate away. V1.5 keeps
the incidents that explain why the rules exist.

**`START-HERE.md`** — a plain-language description for anyone, technical or not.
Leads with what the thing is and why it is interesting; running it is an
appendix.

**`BACKLOG.md`** — twelve expansion clusters, each rated by evidence outlook
rather than by interest, because "does a document say so" is the actual
constraint. Not prioritised; that is the next conversation.

## Known problems

**Four Canadian shades in a narrow warm band.** Federal gold against provincial
orange should be clear; **municipal vermilion against institutional brick** is
the pair most likely to be marginal. Both are small groups, so it may not matter,
but it has not been judged on a real display.

**No reset-camera button.** Orbit somewhere odd and reloading is the only way
back. The most obvious remaining gap in the panel.

**Drop lines fall to a floor that is no longer drawn.** They still compute
against the deleted platform's geometry. Re-anchor to the grid or drop the
toggle.

**Out-of-focus node dimming is still too weak.** Unchanged from V0.5: selecting a
node visibly changes the edges, not the spheres.

**Six duplicate edges still resolve to the thinner seed version.** Surfaced in
V0.6, decision recorded in REPORTS.md, not taken.

**`last_updated` is null across all 121 nodes.** Every log since V0.1. It is the
blocker for milestone 3, which the V0.6 cadence work otherwise unblocked.

**Milestone 3 pulse rate still runs on publication rate**, which is the wrong
quantity — transmission is what a pulse represents.

**One orphan remains** — LGFF Operating. The standard working.
