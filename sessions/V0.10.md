## The theme: a recommendation list that was mostly wrong, and usefully so

V0.9 closed with six recommended next steps. Four were attempted. **Three of the
four turned out to rest on something false**, and the falsity was the finding in
each case.

**Recommendation 1 — "verify and resolve the two held StatCan edges. Cheapest real
work available."** Neither edge survived. One was deleted outright and one demoted
to `implied`. The recommendation was written expecting verification; what it got
was refutation.

**Recommendation 2 — "settle the SEPH provision, then build the cluster. Best value
in the project right now."** The provision is real and confirmed. But it sits in
**DIVISION 2 — Nova Scotia**, a legacy calculation, not in the all-provinces
formula, and the cluster it was supposed to unlock turned out to be blocked on
missing nodes rather than on missing evidence.

**Recommendation 3 — "the Treasury yield curve node, restoring `fed-h15`. One
research pass restores H.15."** This does not work at all, and the note it rested
on was wrong twice over. See below.

**Recommendation 5 and 6 were not attempted.** Recommendation 4, the
`determination` backfill, was not either.

The pattern is worth stating because it will recur: **a recommendation written
from a summary is a hypothesis about a document, and this project's own evidence
standard applies to it.** V0.9 wrote recommendation 3 from a remembered reading of
two footnotes and got both the location and the content wrong. Nothing in the
process catches that, because a recommendation carries no `evidence_url`.

## What was deleted, demoted, and why

**`statcan-national-accounts -> statcan-gdp-monthly` — deleted.** The seed basis
asserted that "the quarterly expenditure-based accounts are reconciled against the
monthly by-industry GDP estimates." Both programmes' IMDB methodology records were
read in full and neither states it. Record 1901 refutes it by enumeration: its
Estimation section lists what the sub-annual estimates are built from — "the
Quarterly Survey of Financial Statements, the Monthly Survey of Manufacturing and
the monthly Survey of Employment, Payrolls and Hours" applied to annual benchmarks
— and monthly GDP by industry is not among them, nor in its Data sources list.
Record 1301 points the anchoring elsewhere: "GDP by industry depends on the Supply
and Use tables to which it is anchored."

**The only relation the two documents assert between the quarterly and monthly
measures is comparability**: "This makes the monthly GDP by industry estimates
more comparable with the expenditure-based GDP data, which are chained quarterly."
That phrasing is now a known trap and it caught something else this session — see
the H.15 commercial-paper footnote below.

The external reader had returned this edge as documented, on a quote
("their ongoing reconciliation with the quarterly estimates") that describes the
*reverse* relationship, which it noticed, wrote down, and overrode. That string
does not occur in the 2021 methods document — `reconcil` returns zero occurrences
there — and it was not searched for in Catalogue 15-547-X, which was not opened.
So the quote is unlocated as well as pointing the wrong way.

**`statcan-national-accounts -> statcan-cpi` — demoted to `implied`.** The
accounts' own User Guide, ch. 5 §5.5, names the CPI but only as an illustration of
what a price index is: *"For example, the Consumer Price Index does precisely that
for various classes of consumer products."* It never says which indexes do the
deflating, and record 1901 says only that "certain series (mainly expenditure
series) are deflated." Strong grounds, no document stating it — which is the
standing definition of `implied`, and the first time that kind has been used for a
demotion rather than a promotion out of `_dropped`.

## The seed edge had the right mechanism and the wrong node

Both deletions were productive, because the relationships they described are real
and documented one level down.

The monthly programme publishes an industry-by-industry source table which names
the **CPI by title and record number in 27 rows** and **SEPH in 37**. Typical row,
NAICS 441: *"Deflated sales and margins, Retail trade survey (monthly), Record no.
2406, Consumer price indexes adjusted for sales tax changes, Record no. 2301,
Retail commodity survey, Record no. 2008, Retail trade survey (annual), Record no.
2422."* Record 2301 is the CPI.

So `statcan-gdp-monthly -> statcan-cpi` and `-> statcan-seph` are properly
evidenced, and record 1901 names SEPH twice, giving
`statcan-national-accounts -> statcan-seph`.

**This is REPORTS.md's disclosure asymmetry doctrine in a federal statistical
setting.** The doctrine was written from municipalities — a city states that
property tax revenue is based on assessment values, the neighbouring county does
not, and the dependency is identical. Here the monthly by-industry programme
discloses its deflators at release level with record numbers, and the quarterly
accounts do not, and the two almost certainly deflate the same way. *Where the
graph looks uneven, suspect disclosure before reality* now has an instance at the
top of the corpus rather than the bottom.

## `fed-h15` cannot be restored from its own release

The sharpest correction in the session, and it inverts a recommendation.

V0.9 recorded a cheap way back: footnotes 9 and 10 "both read 'Source: U.S.
Treasury' … and describe the yields as 'interpolated by the U.S. Treasury from the
daily yield curve.' The evidence exists; the Treasury release has never been
researched as a node. One research pass restores H.15."

**Two errors.** The quotation is not in footnotes 9 or 10 — those cover the
30-year series discontinuation and the TIPS constant maturities. It is in a
separate unnumbered section at the foot of the release headed "Description of the
Treasury Nominal and Inflation-Indexed Constant Maturity Series." And neither
location **names a Treasury publication**. "Source: U.S. Treasury" is agency-level
attribution, which is the exact test that killed
`boc-mpr -> statcan-national-accounts` last session, and "the daily yield curve
for non-inflation-indexed Treasury securities" describes a curve, not a titled
release.

Following it through the whole release: **every source H.15 names falls outside the
node rule.**

| Footnote | Named source | Category |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Report of Selected Money Market Rates (FR 2420) | reporting form |
| 5 | commercial paper trades settled by The Depository Trust Company | private transaction data |
| 7 | "a majority of top 25 … insured U.S.-chartered commercial banks" | unnamed banks |
| 9, 10 | "Source: U.S. Treasury" | agency only |
| Description | "composites of quotations obtained by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York" | agency only, OTC quotes |

**So H.15 is not an under-researched node. It is a thoroughly-disclosing release
whose entire input set is unpublishable** — which is precisely the distinction the
`determination` field and the disclosure ratio exist to make visible, and one of
the few nodes where that denominator is now genuinely populated rather than
hypothetical.

The practical consequence: H.15 can only be reconnected **from downstream**, by a
document that cites H.15 as its own source. US financial regulation references the
series routinely and none of it has been researched. That is a larger job than
"one research pass", and the Treasury yield curve node — still worth having — will
not do it.

**One trap avoided.** Footnote 5 says H.15's CP rates "are equivalent to the 30-,
60-, and 90-day dates reported on the Board's Commercial Paper Web page." That
reads like an edge to a real Fed release and is not one: *equivalent to* is
comparability, both series deriving from the same DTCC data. Identical
construction to the monthly/quarterly GDP case deleted the same day. Two instances
in one session is enough to name the pattern: **"comparable with", "equivalent to"
and "consistent with" are not dependency claims**, and all three appear in
methodology prose exactly where a dependency claim would sit.

## Equalization: confirmed, narrower than it looked, and blocked on nodes

The SEPH claim V0.9 left unverified is **confirmed**. SOR/2007-303 s. 8(1)(y)
makes the survey a term in a statutory formula: *"P is the gross dollar value,
before deductions, of the payrolls of all employers in the province … as
determined by Statistics Canada on the basis of its Survey of Employment, Payrolls
and Hours, other than the payrolls of (i) employers not included in that survey,
and (ii) employers in the following industry classifications, as defined by the
North American Industry Classification System …"* Taken as `calculated_from`, with
a documented reference period.

**But the section number hides the scope, and this matters.** s. 8 sits in
DIVISION 2 — Nova Scotia, defining revenue base "For the purpose of sections 3.71
to 3.9 of the Act", and retains the pre-2007 thirty-three-source structure with
paragraphs running (a) to (z.5). SEPH does **not** appear in the DIVISION 1
all-provinces revenue base at s. 5 — `payrolls`, `gross dollar value` and
`military pay and allowances` all return zero across ss. 1 to 8(1)(j), because the
2007 reform folded payroll taxes into the personal-income base. The edge stands;
its `basis` says so explicitly, because "s. 8(1)(y) of the Equalization
Regulations" reads like the main formula and is not.

Of the 24 provisions harvested, **twelve are in the Nova Scotia division, ten are
in DIVISION 1 (ss. 3.1 and 5), two in the general Interpretation.** The ten are
the valuable ones and they are the minority — s. 3.1 in particular is *definitions*,
so those do apply to every province at once, which is what makes Tier 2's thesis
true as originally stated.

**No edges were created from any of it, and the reason is the finding.** The three
most-cited products have no node: *Provincial and Territorial Economic Accounts*,
*Provincial Supply and Use Tables*, and the *System of Macroeconomic Accounts*
itself. **The bottleneck has moved from evidence to node coverage.** Recorded as
twelve properly-cited leads.

Two finds that were not in the returned research. s. 8(1)(i) names *"the R.L. Polk
& Co. publication Canadian and International Registration Manual"* — a titled
commercial publication inside a federal funding formula, the third after J.D.
Power in the US CPI and ICE Brent in Alberta's bitumen valuation, and admissible
under the V0.5 rule where Haver Analytics was not. And s. 8(1)(h) provides for the
Minister to substitute "if Statistics Canada does not make the determination", the
second statutory fallback-for-a-discontinued-source in the corpus after the Oil
Sands Royalty Regulation's provision for the day the Bank of Canada stops
publishing an exchange rate.

**A vintage split worth knowing before these nodes are built.** The same
instrument cites StatCan tables under two incompatible numbering systems, divided
by division: DIVISION 2 uses retired CANSIM numbers (183-0024, 002-0020,
405-0004), while Part 1.1 and s. 1(1) use current product identifiers
(10-10-0011-01, 23-10-0066-01) for the same subject matter. Any node built from a
CANSIM citation needs its modern successor matched by hand, and that match is a
judgement, not a quotation.

## The Supply, Use and Input-Output Tables, and what they explain

`statcan-sut`, record 1401. Annual, calendar-year reference, and unusually
delayed: *"They are available about two and half years after the end of the
reference year."*

Two `calculated_from` edges that had nowhere to point until now. Record 1301: *"The
monthly estimates are benchmarked to annually chained Fisher volume indexes of GDP
obtained from the constant-price supply and use tables."* Record 1901: *"The annual
benchmarks for GDP by the income approach and by the expenditure approach are
generated through the construction of annual supply and use tables which balance
the supply of each product in the economy against its use."*

**This is what makes the day's first deletion legible rather than merely correct.**
The quarterly and monthly programmes do not read each other. They are two
projections off one annual benchmark, and the graph had no way to say so.

Also `statcan-sut -> statcan-cpi`, from Catalogue 15F0077GIE, the deflation guide
record 1301 cites for its double-deflation method. Chapter 2: *"Consumer price
indexes can be used to deflate the purchaser value of commodities sold for personal
expenditure. Similarly, industry product price indexes are used to deflate the
basic values of the same commodities."* Two hedges recorded in the `basis`: "can be
used" rather than "is used", and the guide is a 2001 edition marked ARCHIVED.

**The IPPI evidence in that document is stronger than the CPI evidence acted on**,
and the IPPI is not a node. Chapter 3, unhedged: *"The main data source of
deflators for manufactured goods is the Industrial Product Price Indexes (IPPI)
produced by Statistics Canada's Prices Division."* It is a monthly Prices Division
release, sibling to the CPI. Best-evidenced missing node in the corpus.

## The node decision, and the first cycle

The question arrived four separate times in one session, which is what forced it:
`statcan-hfce` duplicating record 1901; `bea-gdp -> bea-pce`; the System of
Macroeconomic Accounts as a framework named by four Division 1 revenue-base
components; and `statcan-sut` and `statcan-national-accounts` each stating a
dependency on the other.

**Decided: a node may be a named series, a framework, or a mutual definitional
partner — not only a standalone release.** Recorded in REPORTS.md, superseding the
open question that used to read "whether within-system structure needs its own
relationship kind." Full reasoning is there and is not repeated here.

### The cycle was measured before it was written down

A cycle in a PageRank variant is the sink-leak bug's family, so this was checked
rather than argued. **The V8.0 proportional-retention rule is what makes it safe**:
every node with outgoing edges keeps a flat 50% however many edges it has, so two
nodes pointing at each other each pass half and keep half, and the series
converges. The retention invariant still reports exactly 50.0% across all 88
disclosing reports.

**But mutual pairs are not neutral, and the cost is real.** Measured on
`statcan-sut` ↔ `statcan-national-accounts`, comparing the graph with and without
the reverse edge:

| | before | after | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| National Economic Accounts | 0.3489 | 0.4604 | **+31.9%** |
| Supply, Use and I-O Tables | 0.1998 | 0.2335 | **+16.9%** |
| `sna-2008` | 0.1149 | 0.1287 | +12.0% |
| `imf-bpm6`, `imf-gfsm` | 0.0789 | 0.0902 | +14.2% |
| median, all 121 nodes | | | **+7.2%** |

The median is non-zero because a cycle recirculates rank instead of letting it
terminate. The gain propagates correctly downstream to the standards the accounts
cite, so it is not a leak. It is still a **self-reinforcing pair**, bounded but
real, and it creates an incentive: recording a relationship as mutual pays more
than recording it one way.

**The guard is the evidence standard and nothing else**, so the constraint is
written into the decision: mutuality only where *both* documents state it in their
own words, each with its own `evidence_url`; one document mentioning both
directions is one edge; and where the directions differ in strength,
`relationship_type` must record the asymmetry. In this pair, `calculated_from` from
1901, which discloses an input, against `methodology_depends_on` from 1401, which
describes a validation check.

**What the decision does not solve is containment**, and that is now the open
question in REPORTS.md with three options and none taken. It needs deciding before
the System of Macroeconomic Accounts node is built, because that one would
triple-count rather than double-count.

## Working with the second reader: the format is proven

V0.9 established the rule (*a second reader extracts; this project adjudicates*)
and the mechanism (no verdict field, verbatim quotes, `AGENCY ONLY` where only an
institution is named). Three rounds ran this session against a written brief,
`Grok-Research-Brief-IV.md`, and every quote was checked against its own URL.

| Round | Returned | Verified verbatim | Errors |
|---|---|---|---|
| SEPH in SOR/2007-303 | 5 provisions | **5** | under-counted its own find: SEPH occurs 3× not 2× |
| Equalization products | 24 provisions | **24** | two section numbers one subsection out (8(3)/8(5) → 8(4)/8(6)) |
| H.15 + deflation guide + LFS | 4 + 5 + 1 negative | 4 + 2, **2 unreachable** | none |

**Zero adjudication errors across three rounds**, against one refuted verdict and
one overridden direction warning in V0.9's single verdict-carrying round. The
`AGENCY ONLY` marker was used correctly and unprompted on H.15's footnotes 9 and
10, which is the call that killed recommendation 3 — the reader got that right and
the project's own prior note had it wrong.

**One clean negative result, and it was acted on.** Asked for a confirming document
for Labour Force Survey use in monthly GDP by industry, the round returned nothing
and listed what it searched. The lead was reclassified `deferred` → `no-document`.
That is the first time the reason vocabulary's central distinction has been used to
move an entry toward "answer", and it is what the "negative results are valuable"
instruction is for.

**What still requires the checker.** Both remaining error classes were citation
errors, not fabrications: a section number one level off, and an under-count.
Neither would have been caught without reading the primary source, and both would
have been silently absorbed. The verbatim-quote requirement is what makes the check
a five-minute job rather than a re-run.

## `vite build` ran, and the sandbox workaround is simpler than recorded

V0.8 recorded that `npm run validate` cannot run in a Linux sandbox because
`node_modules` holds Windows `esbuild` binaries, with the workaround being a
scratch copy of `src`, `scripts` and the configs. V0.8 and V0.9 both listed
`vite build` as not run.

**It runs.** A full copy of the tree outside the project folder with a fresh Linux
install builds clean: 950 modules, 1,648 kB bundle, 460 kB gzipped, in 8.4s. The
only warning is the chunk-size advisory, which is expected for a three.js app and
is not new.

Two refinements to the recorded workaround:

- **`tsc` needs no scratch install at all.** `node node_modules/typescript/bin/tsc
  --noEmit` runs against the project's own Windows `node_modules` and is clean —
  TypeScript is pure JavaScript and has no platform binary. Only `tsx` and `vite`
  need the scratch copy.
- The scratch copy needs to be the **whole tree**, not the lib/data/scripts subset,
  if `vite build` is wanted — react and three are required.

## Looked at, after a session of debt

V0.9 recorded that nothing had been looked at in a browser while the ranking moved
and two nodes left the graph. That debt is paid, and the harness is worth recording
because "nobody has seen any of it" has appeared in five session logs.

A headless Chromium can be driven inside the sandbox. `playwright install chromium`
succeeds but `--with-deps` fails without root; the missing X libraries can be
fetched as `.deb` packages with `apt-get download` and unpacked with `dpkg-deb -x`
into a local sysroot, with `LD_LIBRARY_PATH` pointed at it. Software rendering
(`--use-angle=swiftshader --enable-unsafe-swiftshader`) then renders the WebGL
scene. **Background processes do not survive between tool calls**, so the server
and the screenshot have to run in one invocation.

What the default view shows, at haze 35% / glow 55%:

- **The data changes are live.** Legend reads the current counts, top three reads
  CPI 1.00 / Census 0.69 / US CPI 0.69, evidence panel reads `Implied · 9`.
- **V0.8's colour claim holds up, checked independently.** The cyan US cluster
  reads as a distinct region hanging off the warm Canadian mass, with the violet
  international bodies between them.
- **The default framing is still wrong.** The node cloud uses roughly a third of
  the frame with dead space left, right and below, above an empty grid floor. This
  is the V0.5 finding *surviving the V0.7 platform deletion* — V0.7 recorded that
  removing the slab fixed the auto-fit "for free", and at 121 nodes it is visibly
  not fixed. Whether the cause is the same measurement bug or something new has not
  been diagnosed.
- Occlusion in the dense centre-bottom, unchanged and known.
