Written 2026-07-29 in the same pass as `V0.10.md`, which is what the protocol
requires and the reason it exists — rollup 1 was due at V0.5, deferred twice, and
written after V0.6.

**The window shares V0.5 with rollup 1 on purpose.** Anything V0.5 decided that a
later log revised is described here from the revised side. Where `V1.5.md` and this
file disagree, this one is current.

This replaces the six session logs as *reading*. They are not deleted and the human
still audits them; a new agent reads REPORTS.md, this file, and the latest session

## Decisions that still bind

The rules are in REPORTS.md. What is here is why they are not arbitrary.

### Retention is proportional to outgoing weight

The single most consequential change in the window, and the one most likely to be
"simplified" by someone who has not seen the numbers.

A node keeps `retention / (retention + total outgoing weight)`. Retention used to
be a fixed 1.0 while outgoing weight varied, so **how much of its own authority a
node kept was set by how many inputs it disclosed, and how vaguely.** One `cites`
edge kept 80%; three well-documented edges kept 30%.

Measured on one node, disclosing inputs one at a time:
`0.165 → 0.303 → 0.236 → 0.214 → 0.203 → 0.195`. **The peak is at exactly one
disclosed input.** And describing that one input vaguely paid: as `cites` it scored
**0.515**, as `calculated_from` **0.277** — the vaguest available characterisation
worth 1.9× the most precise.

It reached the top four. **The Census outranked the CPI on five incoming edges
against thirteen**, because the Census disclosed one input weakly and the CPI
honestly documented three.

**This was the sink-leak bug's mirror image.** Retention existed because full
PageRank transfer let a one-edge node drain everything into its target — the
*receiving* side. The *transmitting* side was left, where having one edge became
the best place to be. Both have the same root cause, already written down: **out-
degree in this graph is a fact about our research, not about the world.** A fixed
retention competing with variable outgoing weight reintroduces exactly the
dependency retention existed to remove.

Scaling retention *with* outgoing weight removes it: every disclosing node keeps a
flat 50%. `npm run validate` asserts the constant fraction on every run, recomputed
from the edge data rather than read back out of the graph — a property nothing
checks is a property that quietly stops being true.

One discontinuity is accepted rather than papered over: a node with no outgoing
edges gets no retention, because retention is now a multiple of a weight it does
not have. Giving true sinks a self-loop lets them hoard — at damping 0.85 a pure
sink settles above five times its inflow, the original bug made worse.

### Later definition wins for edges; first still wins for reports

Deliberately opposite rules. For reports, "first wins, the seed set is
authoritative" is a curation choice. For edges the same rule was **an accident with
the wrong sign**: seed edges were written first and are uniformly worse evidenced.

**All six duplicates resolved to the seed copy, and in all six the losing research
copy carried an `evidence_url` while the winning seed copy did not.** One pair also
disagreed on `relationship_type`, so the rule was moving authority and not only
prose. Reversing it moved 3 of 117 rank positions, left the top four untouched, and
took the strict-standard graph from three components to two and six orphans to two.

### Cadence is three quantities, and the third lives on the edge

Publication rate and change rate on the report; **transmission on the dependency**,
because the same CPI release reaches the Monetary Policy Report monthly and the
Alberta escalator annually, and no field on the CPI can say that.

What deleting the old enum revealed, measured before it went: it **disagreed with
`releases_per_year` on 15 of 104 researched nodes**, **77 of 104 were filed
`annual`** — one bucket doing duty for a decennial census, a
revised-once-a-generation standard and a discretionary municipal count — and **65
nodes carried a prose correction**. A filter dimension needing a prose correction on
every second node is not a filter dimension. In all 15 disagreements the number was
right and the bucket was wrong.

Publication rates stay fractional on purpose: 0.05 is "about once a generation",
and that is a fact a bucket cannot hold.

### Implied edges are quarantined, not admitted

`evidence: 'documented' | 'implied'`. An implied edge means the relationship is
believed on strong grounds and **no document states it**. Drawn dashed, never
pulsed — a pulse asserts influence actually propagates, which is the exact claim
these cannot make — and **excluded from the authority calculation by subtraction**.

That exclusion carries more weight here than for commercial nodes: if an
undocumented edge could move a score, the ranking would depend on what somebody
found plausible, and the evidence standard would quietly stop being load-bearing.

V0.10 added a use nobody had anticipated: `implied` is also where an **unevidenced
seed edge goes when its mechanism is right and no document states it**, which is a
demotion rather than a promotion out of `_dropped`.

### Published commercial sources are in; redistributors and unidentifiable ones are not

Sources that are published but not official become nodes, drawn grey, excluded from
authority **by subtraction** so the toggle provably reshuffles nothing. Two
refinements bought in this window:

- **A redistributor is not a publisher.** The Bank of Canada reaches BEA data *via
  Haver Analytics*. Haver republishes other people's data on no cadence of its own,
  so it cannot be a node even under the rule that admits J.D. Power and ICE Brent —
  those publish something. Whether an intermediary weakens the underlying claim was
  answered no: the underlying source is official, named and unambiguous.
- **Commercial is allowed; unidentifiable is not.** Alberta's BVM Regulation sets
  the WCS index from "the commodity brokers specified" in a list the Minister sets
  by order, so there is no stable publication to point at. No rule change fixes
  that.

### Disclosure is a property of the source, not a gap in the research

A statutory formula with four enumerated inputs and a central bank that deliberates
are indistinguishable in the data, and the second reads as under-researched when it
is correctly characterised. Two instruments were decided: **`determination`
(`formulaic` | `discretionary`)** and a **disclosure ratio**.

**The disclosure ratio's denominator trap is the part worth carrying.** The obvious
rule — null when nothing was dropped — gives 94 of 121 nodes a ratio, nearly all of
them 100%, because a node nobody has examined scores perfectly. **Under that rule a
report nobody has looked at presents as the most transparent thing on screen.** The
rule is now null unless something was searched for and not found, which takes it to
about a sixth of the graph. **The disclosure ratio is a sparse annotation, not a
universal one**, and "not asked" must not render like "asked and clean".

`fed-h15` is the worked example the decision was waiting for. Every source it names
is a reporting form, private transaction data, unnamed banks, or an agency without a
publication. It is not under-researched; it is a thoroughly-disclosing release whose
entire input set is unpublishable, and nothing in the graph could say that.

### A node may be a named series, a framework, or a mutual definitional partner

Decided V0.10, after the question arrived four separate times in one session. The
first cycle in the graph came with it, and it was **measured before it was written
down**, because a cycle in a PageRank variant is the sink-leak bug's family.

**Proportional retention is what makes it safe** — two nodes pointing at each other
each pass half and keep half, so the series converges. **But mutual pairs are not
neutral.** On the first one the two nodes gained **31.9%** and **16.9%** against a
median of **7.2%** across all 121 nodes, the median being non-zero because a cycle
recirculates rank rather than letting it terminate. The gain propagated correctly
downstream, so it is not a leak — but recording a relationship as mutual pays more
than recording it one way, and **the only guard on that incentive is the evidence
standard.** Mutuality only where both documents state it, each with its own
`evidence_url`.

### A second reader extracts; this project adjudicates

The most transferable method finding in the window.

An external AI was briefed with the evidence standard, worked examples, the node-id
list, and a mandatory verbatim-quote requirement. Round one returned seven
verdicts: **four held, one was refuted by the very quote offered as its proof, and
one had a direction error the reader noticed, wrote down, and then overrode.**

**Extraction is reliable; adjudication is not, and adjudication fails in one
consistent direction — toward yes.**

The fix was to remove the verdict field entirely and ask instead for: every passage
quoted verbatim, every release it names listed, and `AGENCY ONLY` written wherever
the passage names only an institution. **Three subsequent rounds returned 33
provisions with zero adjudication errors.** The two residual error classes were both
citation errors — a section number one subsection out, and an under-count of its own
find — neither of which would have been caught without reading the primary source.

Two rules follow. **Structure the task so the weak step is not required, and the
weakness stops mattering.** And **the verbatim-quote requirement is what makes the
split enforceable** — it turns a permanent silent error into a five-minute check.

One further discipline, bought expensively: **one provision per entry.** A round
returned fourteen good quotes bundled under a heading marked "illustrative cluster";
none carried its own section number, so none could be cited, so all fourteen were
discarded. Good research, unusable packaging.

## Mistakes worth not repeating

### Four features that had never worked

Arrows were drawn at 94% along links that run centre-to-centre, so every arrowhead
sat *inside* the target sphere — buried since V0.2. Bloom threshold sat at 0.5 while
the brightest node in the graph reached 0.36, so **zero of 121 nodes ever crossed
it, on any graph, ever.** Fog planes were computed once at load and never updated,
so the first zoom switched the effect off silently — and the link shader received
none of three.js's automatic fog chunks, so the edges would never have fogged even
when it worked. The `country` field was typed `'CA' | 'US'` with no value for
"neither", so every international body was recorded as Canadian for five sessions.

Each had been shipped, listed in the view panel, and described in a session log.
**An attribute nothing reads is an attribute nobody checks, and a boolean toggle
disguises a broken feature as a taste.** Two of the four became visible only when
the control changed from a checkbox to a slider and the value still did nothing.

### The loader discarded research in silence

A backfill wrote 34 reference periods; the validator reported 31. Nothing dangled,
no ids duplicated, no error was raised. The loader deduplicated edges and said
nothing about it, keeping the first definition — always the thinner seed copy.
**It swallowed exactly one case, and it swallowed it in the direction that discards
research.** Fixed by reporting duplicates and then by reversing the rule.

### Documents drift from the data, and nothing was checking

`_dropped` was write-only: entries went in and nothing read them again. So notes
were never removed when the edge was later built, and **two documents recommended
finished work off stale notes** — `BACKLOG.md` called an edge "the most obvious
missing edge in the entire graph" when it already existed and had been built better
than either document proposed. One note rejected `bea-gdp -> bea-pce` on evidence
grounds while that edge sat in the graph marked documented.

The fix was a validator check that fails loudly if any note describes a live edge.
**It caught a real error within one session of existing** — a refutation note
written while the edge it refuted was still in the graph.

**Then V0.8 shipped a stale recommendation of exactly the kind V0.8 was about.** It
recommended setting the slider defaults to values found legible; the defaults had
held those values since hours before the log was written. The theme was documents
drifting from data and it added one more instance while saying so.

**And V0.9 did it again in a new way**: it recommended restoring `fed-h15` from two
H.15 footnotes, and got both the location and the content of the quotation wrong.
**A recommendation is a hypothesis about a document, and it carries no
`evidence_url`.** Nothing in the process checks one.

### Citing to the wrong location

Three instances in two sessions, all in material that was otherwise correct. The
Equalization definitions filed under "s. 1(1) and s. 8" when they are in s. 3.1 —
and s. 8 is a different division entirely. A quotation attributed to H.15 footnotes
9 and 10 that is in an unnumbered section elsewhere on the page. Two statutory
definitions cited to s. 8(3) and s. 8(5) when they are in s. 8(4) and s. 8(6).

None was a fabrication and all three were caught only by opening the document.
**A verified quote with an unverified location is half-verified.**

### Declaring absence from a truncated retrieval

V0.9 could not confirm a claim because its retrieval had truncated before the
relevant sections, and correctly recorded it as **unverified, not disproven.** V0.10
nearly made the opposite error: a whole-document grep returned zero occurrences of
the phrase, from a retrieval that turned out to cover a quarter of the instrument.

Retrieval caps sit at roughly 126 KB against documents of 525 KB and more.
**Whole-file greps on a truncated fetch prove nothing about absence.** Section-by-
section URLs (`/section-N.html` on laws-lois) work where `FullText.html` does not.

### "Comparable with" is not a dependency claim

Two instances in one session. Record 1301 says the monthly GDP estimates are made
"more comparable with the expenditure-based GDP data" — which had been recorded as a
dependency for several sessions and was deleted. H.15's footnote 5 says its
commercial paper rates "are equivalent to" those on the Board's own Commercial Paper
page — which looks like an edge to a real Fed release and is not one; both series
derive from the same private trade data.

**"Comparable with", "equivalent to" and "consistent with" appear in methodology
prose exactly where a dependency claim would sit.** They are statements about
agreement between outputs, not about inputs.

### Shipping visuals nobody has looked at

V0.1, V0.3 and V0.4 all closed with "not verified: how any of it looks". V0.5
looked and found three expired visual decisions. V0.8 looked and found the usable
haze and glow bands. **V0.9 looked at nothing while the ranking moved and two nodes
left the graph**, and V0.10 had to pay that debt one session later.

**The build output cannot make a visual judgement**, and the logs show it costs
about a session per unlooked-at change. A headless browser can be driven inside the
sandbox — see V0.10 for the specifics — so the excuse is now weaker than it was.

### Writing a log nobody asked for

Two were produced off a standing instruction that an agent reasonably read as
authority once it judged a session meaningful. The judgement was fine; acting
silently was the error, and the first attempt at a fix removed the agent's ability
to propose at all, throwing away the useful half. The rule is **offer, then write**.

Relatedly, the rollup now **rides with the fifth session log** rather than being its
own deferrable task, because a rollup that competes with whatever the human wants
next loses every time. `npm run logs` answers which is due.

## Findings about the subject matter

- **September 30 is the busiest date in Canadian statutory indexation.** The CRA
  indexation factor, the Canada Child Benefit, the Alberta escalator, AISH and
  Income Support all read a 12-month CPI window closing on it. Fourteen distinct
  reference-period shapes were found across the first 34 edges backfilled.
- **Two Canadian earnings-based programmes read the same SEPH series two months
  apart.** EI's maximum insurable earnings uses 12 months to April 30; CPP's YMPE
  uses 12 months to June 30. An earlier pass had recorded CPP as October 31, which
  is the pension *index* — the backfill separated two things that had been
  conflated.
- **The real shape of the Canadian national accounts is a shared benchmark, not a
  chain.** The monthly GDP-by-industry programme and the quarterly income and
  expenditure accounts do not read each other. Both are projections off the annual
  Supply, Use and Input-Output Tables, which arrive about two and a half years after
  the reference year. Two documents were misread as saying otherwise before this was
  established.
- **The Equalization formula pins itself to named Statistics Canada products by
  definition, not by measurement** — which is why one provision affects every
  province at once. But **which division a provision sits in decides how much it is
  worth**: s. 3.1 and s. 5 are the live all-provinces formula, while s. 8 is a
  legacy Nova Scotia calculation retaining the pre-2007 thirty-three-source
  structure, and the two are easy to confuse because the section numbers give no
  hint.
- **The same regulation cites StatCan tables under two incompatible numbering
  systems, split by division** — retired CANSIM numbers in the Nova Scotia division,
  current product identifiers elsewhere, for the same subject matter. Matching a
  CANSIM citation to its modern successor is a judgement, not a quotation.
- **Statistics Canada documents miscite their own record numbers.** A methodology
  table cites "Labour Force Survey, Record no. 3401"; record 3401 is the Field Crop
  Reporting Series. Record numbers in these tables are corroboration, not proof.
- **Commercial publications sit inside government funding formulas more often than
  expected.** J.D. Power supplies every used-car price in the US CPI; ICE Brent and
  Argus Mexico Maya are named verbatim in Alberta's bitumen valuation regulation;
  "the R.L. Polk & Co. publication *Canadian and International Registration
  Manual*" is named in the federal Equalization Regulations. Each was found while
  looking for something else.
- **Direct official cross-border dependencies are nearly absent.** V0.8 measured the
  count of standard-compliant direct official CA↔US edges at **zero**, with the only
  compliant cross-border link in the graph being a futures price. V0.9 took it to
  **one**. The two national systems connect mainly through shared standards.
- **Disclosure asymmetry runs from the bottom of the corpus to the top.** A city
  states that property tax revenue is based on assessment values and the
  neighbouring county does not; the monthly GDP programme names its deflators with
  record numbers and the quarterly accounts do not. Same dependency, different
  wording. *Where the graph looks uneven, suspect disclosure before reality.*
- **A release can disclose thoroughly and still have nothing citable behind it.**
  H.15 names its sources in eleven footnotes plus a methodological description, and
  every one is a reporting form, private transaction data, unnamed banks, or a bare
  agency. Such a node can only be reconnected from downstream.

## Live problems

**One decision blocks the best-evidenced node available: containment.** If a series
and its containing release are both nodes — now permitted — authority accruing to
each double-counts one programme, and the model cannot say one is part of the other.
Three options are recorded in REPORTS.md and none is taken. The System of
Macroeconomic Accounts should not be built until it is settled, because that one
would triple-count.

**Two decided features have never been built, across three logs each.** The
`determination` field, and the disclosure ratio in the hover card. Neither is
blocked; the data both need exists. The claim that `determination` is mostly obvious
to classify remains an expectation, because it has not been attempted on a single
node.

**Eight seed edges still fail the evidence standard**, down from 27 and then 21.
They are named in REPORTS.md. **They have had no research at all** — they are not
"searched and not found", and a later pass must not read them as the former. The
structural argument for doing them is gone: the graph is one component with zero
orphans under the strict standard, so they now buy completeness, not connectivity.

**Two real programmes are absent from every build** — `fed-h15` and LGFF Operating —
because no document names their inputs. That is the standard working, and it now has
a worked explanation rather than an apology.

**The renderer's open items are unchanged and all were observed rather than
guessed.** The default framing still shows the node cloud in about a third of the
frame above an empty grid floor, which is the V0.5 finding *surviving* V0.7's claim
to have fixed it for free — not diagnosed. Out-of-focus node dimming is weaker than
the code believes: selecting a node visibly changes the edges, not the spheres, and
this has been open since V0.5 and is still unlooked-at because a focused-state
capture could not be taken. No reset-camera button. Drop lines fall to a floor that
is no longer drawn. Four Canadian shades have never been judged on a real display,
municipal vermilion against institutional brick being the marginal pair.

**Haze and glow have a usable band and a named trade-off.** ~35% haze and ~55% glow
is legible; both at maximum flattens the scene and **collapses the country hue
families toward the same grey-brown**, costing the single biggest legibility win the
project has. Haze buys depth cue and spends colour discriminability.

**`last_updated` is null on every node, in every log since V0.1.** Never wired to a
source, left null rather than invented because fabricated dates would corrupt the
cadence work. It is the blocker for milestone 3: a rate without a last observation
cannot say when the next one is due. Milestone 3's pulse still runs on publication
rate, which is the wrong quantity.

**One connected component oversells the structure.** V0.8 measured 40 of 187 edges
as bridges and 32 of 121 nodes as articulation points — much of the periphery is a
tree hanging off a few hubs. That became the ranking criterion in `BACKLOG.md`: *if
a tier of work raises the bridge fraction, it added territory rather than
structure.* **The measurement is now three sessions stale** and twelve edges plus a
node have been added against it.

**Some `_dropped` classifications are one reader's judgement and two are probably
wrong.** They were identified in V0.9 and deliberately left, because reclassifying
moves nodes in and out of the disclosure-ratio denominator. A spot-check of the ten
`denied` entries found six sound and four on the line.

**The Alberta health delivery layer is still mid-restructure**, two nodes describing
entities in transition since the 2024-25 split of AHS into four sector agencies.
