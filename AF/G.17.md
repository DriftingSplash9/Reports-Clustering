## Headline result

**All 54 sovereign African states now have at least one node in this corpus.** The 15 countries with zero prior presence — in population order, Sudan, Angola, Mozambique, Madagascar, Guinea, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Eritrea, The Gambia, Mauritius, Djibouti, Comoros, Cabo Verde, São Tomé & Príncipe, Seychelles — were each researched at the national-CPI, international/regional-bloc, and provincial/state tiers (not municipal, per Thomas's instruction) via a dedicated dispatched agent, then synthesized, validated, and committed in three waves of five. A fourth wave went back and deepened five countries that an earlier Grok-synthesis round had left with only a bare CPI node (Somalia, Burundi, South Sudan, Guinea-Bissau, DR Congo), adding provincial/federal fiscal-tier and regional-bloc citations to each. A fifth, smaller wave checked Egypt and Algeria's provincial tier — Egypt turned out to already be well-covered from an earlier session (only the Constitution itself was missing), while Algeria's wilaya tier was genuinely new ground. Coverage is real but uneven by design: several of the smallest/most opaque states (Eritrea, Djibouti, Liberia, Seychelles) got 1-3 nodes and multiple honest NOT FOUND results rather than forced or padded entries — thinness is itself a documented finding for those countries, not a gap in the research.

## Findings

### 1. Grok's 14-item follow-up response — processed at the start of this session

Nine of 14 items resolved and minted (Ethiopia's Proclamation 1250/2021 constitutional basis is Article 55(1), not 62(7); Rivers State's and Kano's 2026 budgets; Kenya's Constitution Article 204 in full; Morocco's organic-law promulgation date resolved to 7 July 2015). Five remained NOT FOUND even after Grok's pass (Oromia's CPI agency, Mombasa's CFSP, DRC's CBMT/Article 175 link, Kinshasa's primary budget, DRC's INS primary CPI, Zanzibar's CPI methodology, Rwanda's 2006 reform primary source — several of these recur in this round's Grok prompt below). Grok's own unsourced "bonus material" on Nigeria's FAAC formula presented a superseded 15/50/35 VAT-sharing split as current; independent verification found the actual split changed to 10/55/35 on 1 January 2026 — caught and corrected in `af-grok-provincial-followup.json`, which also flagged (not resolved) a real discrepancy between the Allocation of Revenue Act's stated 56/24/20 federal/state/local split and the actually-applied 52.68/26.72/20.60 split.

### 2. The import pass — `palette.ts` and 12 files fixed

Ran the full tar-to-sandbox `npm install` / `check` / `validate` / `build` pass (see the `npm-validate-procedure` memory) against every DRAFT file sitting unimported since G.15/G.16. Two real, recurring bugs: `releases_per_year: null` on one-off instruments (must OMIT the key — `null <= 0` evaluates true in JS, this exact mistake had already been made and fixed once before in a different branch) across roughly 15 nodes in 12 files, and an invented `"fiscal-federalism"` domain tag used across 9 nodes where the existing `"fiscal-transfers"` tag was the correct, already-established choice. Also resolved a stale `_dropped` note in an earlier file that contradicted a live edge minted later in the same G.16 session. Two pre-existing, unrelated validation notes (`rw-nisr-cpi` and `ug-ubos-cpi`, both vs. `eac-hcpi-regulations`) were confirmed still present and left untouched — out of scope, flagged for a session with the original research context.

### 3. Wave 1 — Sudan, Angola, Mozambique, Madagascar, Guinea

Five parallel agents, the top five missing countries by population. Minted a new shared node this session, `comesa-treaty-establishing` (COMESA's founding treaty, Article 140 on statistical harmonisation), now reused by three countries. Sudan: CBS's national CPI (disrupted by war — 2 of 18 states now use estimates rather than field collection) and a 2021 national budget's aggregate transfers-to-states line; no state-level budget document exists online for any of Sudan's 18 states, a genuine gap not a search failure. Angola: INE's own CPI and a 2018 activity report explicitly naming SADC; one agent caught and corrected a wrong premise in my own research brief — Angola did NOT hold provincial elections in 2025 as I had assumed, governors remain presidentially appointed. Mozambique: INE's methodology note names the UN CPI Manual 2009 AND COICOP explicitly, one of the strongest methodology citations in the whole branch; a 2023 mining-revenue decree sets a real 7.25%/2.75% province/local split. Madagascar: INSTAT's own strategy document credits COMESA support for the 2019 national CPI-coverage extension. Guinea: INS explicitly copies UEMOA's CPI methodology despite not being a UEMOA member, and separately names AFRISTAT in its national statistics strategy — reused the existing `afristat-ihpc-guide-2014` node rather than minting a duplicate, matching the Mauritania precedent.

### 4. Wave 2 — Sierra Leone, Liberia, Eritrea, The Gambia, Mauritius

Sierra Leone: the Local Government Act 2004 routes transfers to the district/chiefdom tier, not the "provinces" — provinces are purely administrative groupings with no elected government or budget of their own, a real structural finding. Liberia: deliberately the thinnest file of this wave — only the national CPI was mintable; the Layer 2 and Layer 3 leads that turned up (a World Bank document naming a planned future WAEMU-model reform, a CSO's paraphrase of the 2018 Local Government Act) were both judged too weak or too secondary to mint, per this branch's standing rule against treating NGO paraphrase as a citable primary source. Eritrea: the thinnest country in the whole session — no live statistics-agency website; the best CPI evidence is a 2003 IMF report OCR-extracted from a scanned PDF; a UN Commission of Inquiry report quotes Eritrea's own 1996 proclamation stating its regional zobas "do not have fiscal authority," a genuine structural negative finding, not a research gap. The Gambia: reused BOTH `cpi-manual` and `african-charter-statistics-2009`, sourced from third-party IMF/AU assessments rather than GBoS's own methodology note — disclosed explicitly in each edge's basis text. Mauritius: the Rodrigues Regional Assembly Act 2001 is the closest thing to a provincial tier for this unitary state; SADC/COMESA/COI triple membership produced only near-misses, none specific enough to force-link.

### 5. Wave 3 — Djibouti, Comoros, Cabo Verde, São Tomé & Príncipe, Seychelles

The final five, all small/island states, deliberately researched last. Djibouti: INSTAD's live CPI methodology pages are JavaScript-rendered and inaccessible to this branch's tooling — Layer 1 is genuinely thin — but its own 2011-2015 national strategy names both AFRISTAT and COMESA. Comoros: a genuinely federal three-island union (Grande Comore, Anjouan, Mohéli), Constitution Articles 105-106 on island fiscal transfers is a strong Layer-3 finding, and an AU peer review hosted on INSEED's own site states directly that "INSEED adapte les nomenclatures développées par AFRISTAT" — the most direct AFRISTAT methodology claim found all session. Cabo Verde: AFRISTAT appeared only in an acronym glossary, too weak to force into an edge; the constitution's Article 233 covers municipal fiscal autonomy (no intermediate provincial tier exists in this unitary state). São Tomé and Príncipe: a strong pair of findings for such a small country — a 2018-2021 national strategy directly credits AFRISTAT with capacity-building on the harmonised CPI specifically, and Príncipe's Autonomous Region statute (Article 101) is a genuinely federal-like fiscal relationship. Seychelles, last of the 15: a solid national CPI (COICOP, 2020 base) but a genuine structural absence at Layer 3 — no sub-national fiscal tier has ever been implemented despite a permissive constitutional clause (Article 167) dating to 1993; and Seychelles is the fourth and last of the COI/IOC's founding members (after Comoros, Madagascar, Mauritius) researched this session without producing a single citable COI/IOC document from any of their own statistics agencies — that specific gap should probably be attacked from the COI's own site in a future session, not by continuing to search member states individually.

### 6. Thin-country deepening — Somalia, Burundi, South Sudan, Guinea-Bissau, DR Congo

Five countries an earlier Grok-synthesis round had left with only a bare national-CPI node. Somalia: its current 2024-2029 national statistics strategy cites the African Charter on Statistics directly, and the 2012 Provisional Constitution's Articles 50 and 54 establish the federal/member-state fiscal-negotiation framework. Burundi: found the organic law governing state-to-commune resource transfers (Loi n°1/16 du 25 mai 2015) — provinces themselves are deconcentrated, not a distinct fiscal tier, the same structural pattern as Sierra Leone; confirmed an existing `bi-insbu-cpi -> eac-hcpi-regulations` edge was already correctly wired from an earlier round, not duplicated. South Sudan: the Transitional Constitution's Articles 168 and 176-179 include a specific, quantified 2%-of-oil-revenue rule for producing states and establish the National Revenue Fund and the Fiscal and Financial Allocation and Monitoring Commission — corrected a research-brief error along the way (there is no "National Revenue Authority" in this Constitution, contrary to what I'd assumed going in). Guinea-Bissau: found a direct, strong methodology citation — INE's own fuller technical note states its CPI methodology comes from "the UEMOA working group and AFRISTAT" — but its fiscal-transfer law covers municipal autarquias only, not the 9 regions (which are deconcentrated with centrally-appointed governors), so that finding is recorded as a municipal lead, not minted as provincial-tier. DR Congo already had 4 solid nodes (national CPI, Constitution, Kinshasa's own budget, a national medium-term framework) but zero international/bloc citation despite 4 overlapping bloc memberships — closed that gap with a newer (2022) SADC HCPI bulletin edition naming DRC directly, and a distinct AFRISTAT bulletin that extends coverage to non-member DRC by name.

### 7. Egypt and Algeria's provincial tier

Checked last, per Thomas's "consider it" framing rather than a firm instruction. Egypt turned out to already be well-covered from an earlier, unlogged session — `eg-cairo-citizen-budget-2024-2025`, `eg-local-administration-law-43-1979`, and `eg-real-estate-tax-law-196-2008` already existed; this round added only the Constitution's own Articles 176-178 (not previously minted) and a new negative finding (no CAPMAS-COMESA statistical citation exists, despite Egypt being a COMESA member — only trade-statistics mentions were found). Algeria's wilaya tier was genuinely new: the 2012 Wilaya Code (Loi n°12-07) has explicit equalisation-fund language (Articles 176-179, "fonds de solidarité des collectivités locales" and "fonds de garantie des collectivités locales") paying wilayas an "attribution annuelle de péréquation."

## Secondary observations

- **Parallel agent dispatch is the right tool at this scale.** Every prior AF round used sequential WebSearch/WebFetch from the main conversation loop; that approach does not scale to "cover every remaining country." Dispatching 4-5 agents at once, each with a self-contained research brief covering all three tiers, worked cleanly across all 21 dispatches this session with no coordination failures — the only real risk (duplicated node ids across parallel agents working on genuinely unrelated countries) never materialised because each agent was scoped to one country's own code prefix.
- **Shared hub-node reuse compounded nicely this session.** `afristat-ihpc-guide-2014` grew from 1 to 7 countries, `cpi-manual` to 9, `african-charter-statistics-2009` from 1 (Tanzania) to 4, and a brand-new `comesa-treaty-establishing` node (minted in the Sudan file) immediately got reused by 2 more countries in the same session. The direction/relationship_type conventions established early (hub `uses_data_from` national CPI; national CPI `methodology_depends_on` or `cites` a methodology guide, depending on how directly the country's own document states the dependency) held up cleanly across every new country without needing revision.
- **The "note describes an edge that IS in the graph" validator check caught a real mistake before it shipped**, not just a hypothetical one: while deepening South Sudan, I nearly recorded a `_dropped` note claiming no document links South Sudan's CPI to the EAC's harmonised-CPI regulation — but that edge already existed in the corpus from an earlier round, and a parallel agent working DR Congo's file independently confirmed the EAC regulation's own text does name South Sudan. Caught and fixed before commit; worth treating this validator check as load-bearing, not just a formality, in every future session.
- **The COI/Indian Ocean Commission remains uncited by any country's own statistics agency**, despite this session deliberately researching all four of its founding African members (Comoros, Madagascar, Mauritius, Seychelles) with the COI as an explicit research target each time. Worth trying the COI's own site directly in a future round rather than continuing to search from the member-state side.
- **Two research-brief errors were caught by the dispatched agents themselves, not by a later check** — Angola's assumed-but-nonexistent 2025 provincial elections, and South Sudan's assumed-but-nonexistent "National Revenue Authority." Both are recorded as corrections in the relevant files' own descriptions. Worth remembering that a research brief's premise is itself a hypothesis, not a given, even when I write it.

## How to write the next hand-off

(Copied verbatim per branch convention.)

Required sections, in this order: Orientation, Session conditions, Headline result, Findings (numbered `###` subsections, one per country/topic), Secondary observations, Corrections to prior sessions (never omit this section — even "none this session" is itself a claim, state it explicitly), Thomas's stated priority for the remaining work, Cheap checks still outstanding, What to pass at the start of next thread, and this spec block itself, copied verbatim into every hand-off.

Never edit a predecessor file. Corrections to earlier sessions go in the new file's Corrections section, dated and attributable to this session.

Every claim needs a document behind it — a URL/location and, wherever practical, a verbatim quote. "Comparable with"-type language is not a dependency. Tense matters: a PAST-tense arrangement is not a live dependency; flag it if a source's tense is ambiguous. AGENCY ONLY and NOT FOUND are legitimate research outcomes, not failures — record them in `_dropped` with an honest reason, don't leave the question unanswered and don't force an edge that isn't there.
