## Headline result

**AFRISTAT is real, findable, and substantively citable in this branch for the first time — but always as an institution, never yet as a titled document a session could actually open and mint.** Three of the four countries' own primary documents name AFRISTAT directly: Senegal's SDDS metadata twice, in two separate DQAF sub-sections ("This activity is coordinated within the Union by... AFRISTAT" / "This function... is performed by AFRISTAT"); Cameroon's own CPI methodology note ("La méthodologie de l'IPC résulte des travaux et des arbitrages des experts nationaux et des organismes internationaux (AFRISTAT, CEMAC)"); and Mali's own site plus two independent UEMOA workshop records place AFRISTAT's headquarters in Bamako and its staff in the same room as INSTAT Mali's Director General validating draft CPI regulations. None of this clears this corpus's own node bar — "Statistics Canada is not a node," per `Research.1.md` §4 — because AFRISTAT itself has no title, and the specific titled documents it publishes (a differently-titled, apparently ~2008-vintage AFRISTAT guide; a CEMAC regulation named by number but not independently opened) keep landing one step short of a mintable edge. What did clear that bar, for the first time in this branch: a real UEMOA Commission bloc-level document (`uemoa-ihpc-note-2023`) and a real CEMAC Commission bloc-level document (`cemac-inflation-note`), both independently opened, quoted, and wired into the graph — this branch's first actual answer, rather than an open question, to what "research a regional institution directly" looks like.

## Findings

### 1. Senegal's own SDDS metadata is the strongest AFRISTAT citation found anywhere in this branch

`sn-ansd-ihpc`, VERIFIED DIRECTLY via ANSD's own December 2025 bulletin: base 2023, COICOP 2018 named explicitly via the regional NCOA 2018 adaptation, EHCVM 2021-2022 weights, a Young (not Laspeyres) formula. Independently corroborated, quote-for-quote, by Senegal's own IMF DQAF metadata submission (`dsbb.imf.org` — expected by this branch to be an unreadable JS-rendered SPA, but which rendered fully via a live browser session this time). That same metadata page states, in Senegal's own submitted words: "This activity is coordinated within the Union by the Economic and Statistical Observatory for Sub-Saharan Africa (AFRISTAT)," and separately names "a methodological guide, published by the WAEMU Commission and entitled 'Harmonized Index of Consumer Prices in the WAEMU Member Countries – Theory and Practice.'" Neither AFRISTAT nor that exact guide could be minted — the guide's title does not match a differently-titled AFRISTAT document independently found (evidenced base year ~2008, very likely a superseded earlier edition) — but the citation itself is the cleanest, most direct one this branch has produced. On social protection: IPRES's and the FNR's pension mechanisms both read as points/wage-based, not CPI-indexed, but the supporting quotes trace to secondary sources (CLEISS, a French government liaison body; a Senegalese news article) because Senegal's own Journal Officiel and IPRES's own site could not be reached this session — flagged as leads, not settled denials. PNBSF, Senegal's flagship cash transfer, pays a flat CFAF 25,000/quarter with no CPI-escalation clause found in its World Bank project document.

### 2. Côte d'Ivoire rebased mid-branch, and so did its statistics institute

`ci-anstat-ihpc`, VERIFIED DIRECTLY: base 2023 (replacing base 2014 from the January 2025 publication), COICOP 2018 named explicitly for the first time — the prior base-2014 series used a WAEMU-derived nomenclature ("NCOA-IHPC... dérivée de la COICOP"), not COICOP itself, a real methodological change rather than a relabeling. ANStat's own bulletin names a specific, dated UEMOA Council of Ministers decision (4th ordinary session 2024, Bamako, 20 December 2024) adopting the guide governing the new series — a genuine, Côte-d'Ivoire-specific citation of a UEMOA act, though the guide document itself remains unlocated (see Cheap checks). Institutionally: Côte d'Ivoire's statistics institute was dissolved and rebuilt inside this branch's own working period — INS (Institut National de la Statistique) was dissolved by Council of Ministers decree on 12 June 2024 and replaced by ANStat, so `ins.ci` is now dead and `anstat.ci` is the live publisher going forward. On social protection: CNPS's own founding ordinance legally requires pension, minimum-wage and family-benefit revaluation to consider "l'évolution du coût de la vie" (cost-of-living evolution) across three separate articles — a real, repeated statutory linkage to a *concept* — but the mechanism is always a ministerial decree and no titled CPI report is ever named as the trigger, so no edge was minted; this is exactly the near-miss this corpus's evidentiary standard exists to catch rather than paper over. CGRAE, the parallel civil-servant scheme, could not be checked at all — its founding ordinance exists only as a non-OCR'd scanned PDF.

### 3. Cameroon's own CPI documentation names both AFRISTAT and a specific numbered CEMAC regulation, and a real CEMAC-level document corroborates it from the other side

`cm-ins-cpi`, VERIFIED DIRECTLY: base 2011 ("2ème génération," official since Jan 2012) shown mid-rebasing to base 2022 per the CEMAC Commission's own 2024 regional inflation note. INS Cameroon's own methodology text states plainly: "La méthodologie de l'IPC résulte des travaux et des arbitrages des experts nationaux et des organismes internationaux (AFRISTAT, CEMAC). Un règlement de la CEMAC définit très précisément la manière de calculer l'IPC" — and the CEMAC Commission's own 2024 inflation note (`cemac-inflation-note`, independently opened and quoted, `cemac.int`) names the same regulation by number and date ("Règlement N° 03/21-CEMAC-UEAC-CM-36 du 27 janvier 2021") and names INS-Cameroun directly as a contributing national institute, reporting Cameroon-specific IHPC statistics "Source : INS des États membres." This is the first bidirectional, both-sides-opened citation this branch has achieved between a country's CPI and a regional bloc document — stronger than the ECOWAS HCPI guide has ever managed. Separately, an IMF program review (`cm-imf-program-review-2025`) documents a Fund-monitoring requirement for monthly disaggregated CPI data from INS. On social protection, the standout finding is a denial stated by the statistics agency about its own economy: INS's own note says outright, "les salaires ne sont pas indexés sur les prix ; ce qui est une cause de l'érosion du pouvoir d'achat des populations concernées." CNPS pension, family-allowance and occupational-injury benefits are all wage-linked or flat-decree, not CPI-linked, confirmed across four separate legal texts in CNPS's own 2021 compendium of base texts, plus a regional CIPRES directive naming "cost of living" as only one of several non-exclusive discretionary factors.

### 4. Mali: AFRISTAT's own headquarters, a real (if superseded) UEMOA regulation citation, and a closed cash-transfer programme

`ml-instat-ihpc`, VERIFIED DIRECTLY: base 2023 (replacing base 2014, 2008, and the original 1996 rollout), weights from EHCVM 2021, formula changed from Laspeyres to a chained Young index at the 2023 rebasing. AFRISTAT's own site states plainly it is headquartered in Bamako, Mali, and two independent UEMOA news items place AFRISTAT staff, BCEAO staff and IMF staff in Bamako workshops chaired by INSTAT Mali's own Director General — once in November 2021 (training), once in November 2024 (validating the draft base-2023 regulation ahead of Council-of-Ministers submission). This session also independently opened and quoted the UEMOA Commission's own descriptive note on the Union-wide Harmonised CPI (`uemoa-ihpc-note-2023`, dated April/May 2023) — this branch's first genuinely bloc-level document read directly rather than only cited — which names a specific numbered regulation (Règlement N° 03/2017/CM/UEMOA) governing the *base-2014* generation. Because Mali (and Côte d'Ivoire) have since moved to base 2023, and the base-2023 successor regulation was still in draft as of the November 2024 workshop, the edge from `ml-instat-ihpc` to this document is recorded as `cites`, not `methodology_depends_on`, and flagged explicitly as weak/composite evidence — kept rather than dropped, per this branch's own `au-cgc-gst-relativities` precedent for flagged low-confidence findings. On social protection, Mali's flagship cash-transfer programme, Jigisémèjiri, is the sharpest finding of the batch: two independent World Bank documents show a flat monthly amount raised once, in 2018, explicitly "on account of inflation and currency devaluation" but with no formula — and the programme formally closed 30 June 2023, with no confirmed successor located.

## How to write the next hand-off

Adopted wholesale for this branch 2026-08-10 (G.1), following `AU`'s and `NZ`'s precedent of adopting the EU spec verbatim — copy this whole section into every successor, so the chain never depends on one file surviving. It is the spec, not an example. When Thomas says "write the next handoff", "write the next G file", "wrap this thread up" or anything close, this is what he is asking for. Do not ask which format.

Mechanics

* Filename: `G.<n>.md`, where `<n>` is one higher than the highest-numbered `G.*` file in `AF/`. Take the highest number, not the count.
* Write it as `.md`, plain text, in `AF/`.
* Then write the JSON sidecar. Every hand-off has a machine-readable twin at `AF/G.<n>.json`. Do not hand-write it — run:

```
python3 scripts/handoff-to-json.py AF/G.<n>.md
```

The Markdown stays the document of record; the JSON is a structured index of it (date, predecessor, findings, corrections, priorities, cheap checks, and which required sections are missing). `python3 scripts/handoff-to-json.py` with no arguments rebuilds every sidecar across all branches (`BRANCHES` in the script now includes `"AF"`); `--check` reports which are stale without writing. If you are ever unsure whether the sidecar is current, just re-run it — it is idempotent.

* Never edit a predecessor. Corrections to earlier sessions go in this file's Corrections section, where they are dated and attributable. The one exception is this spec block, which is copied forward unchanged.
* This branch's priority lists are plain-numbered, not the EU's lettered A-G convention — following `AU`'s and `NZ`'s precedent. The sidecar script's priority parser falls back to numbered/bullet lists automatically when no lettered blocks are found.

What each section is for

Orientation — carried forward and updated, not rewritten each time. A new agent must be able to read this section alone and know what to read next. Session conditions — what constrained the work: session type, what tooling was available, what did not arrive, what was left untouched by instruction. State plainly which sources you read in full, because everything downstream inherits that limit. Headline result — the single most important thing established, and how strongly. If the session established nothing, say that; a session that only refutes is still a result. Findings — numbered `###` subsections, one per finding. Each states what was checked, what was found, and what it rests on. Quote verbatim; `Research.1.md` §2/§3 apply here exactly as they do to research output. Secondary observations — real but low-priority. Keep them short. Corrections to prior sessions — numbered, each naming the file and the claim being corrected, and whether it is confirmed, refuted, overstated or resolved. A session that finds a predecessor wrong and does not record it here has actively damaged the corpus. Thomas's stated priority for the remaining work — the numbered list carried forward from the predecessor, edited to reflect what moved. Mark items no longer needed explicitly and say why, rather than deleting them silently. Cheap checks still outstanding — ordered by value per unit effort, each one a single lookup. What to pass at the start of next thread — the packing list. If the next agent has filesystem access, say so and keep the list anyway; it doubles as an index of what matters.

Conventions that make these files worth reading

* Say what you did not do. Every one of these files carries an explicit not-read / not-verified statement.
* Distinguish inference from documented fact, and say which narrow respect is still inference.
* A refuted hypothesis is a good outcome. Report both sides of a conflict and pick neither; `Research.1.md` §3 is explicit that adjudication is not the research role.
* Do not pad. These files are dense because every line earns its place.
