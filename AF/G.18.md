## Headline result

Ten countries researched this round; the "collapse of structure" pattern showed up, but not as a clean population cliff — it tracks each country's actual sub-national institutional design more than population rank alone. **Genuine, rich municipal-tier fiscal documents were found for**: Nigeria (state-hosted per-LGA budgets and Auditor-General-for-Local-Governments reports naming individual LGAs), Tanzania (Kinondoni's own signed financial statement, Dar es Salaam City Council's own budget page, a concrete 0.3%-of-turnover service-levy formula). **A genuine "collapse to provincial/national" was confirmed, not just assumed, for**: Egypt (a second governorate, Luxor, plus direct statutory text showing markaz/city "resources" are governorate-allocated transfers, not independent budgets) and Ethiopia (no ordinary municipal tier at all — instead two federally-chartered city administrations, Addis Ababa and Dire Dawa, that sit alongside the 11 regional states as named line items in the federal budget, not beneath them). **The richest single finding of the round was Algeria** — an annual Finance-Law annex table (État E) giving exact tax-by-tax percentage splits and real currency figures for commune-level revenue, recurring across two budget years, something no other country's file in this branch has this level of quantitative specificity. **Two countries (Angola, Mozambique) turned out to have real, detailed ENABLING law for municipal fiscal autonomy — but confirmed non-operative or barely-operative in practice**: Angola's 2017/2020 autarquias framework (direct elections, a 20%-of-five-taxes FEAL transfer fund) has never actually been activated (a "gradualism" clause defers implementation to further state action that hasn't come); Mozambique's 1997-era municipalities have a concrete 1.5%-of-fiscal-revenue transfer formula on paper but almost no discoverable individual-municipality budget publication online (one compromised .gov.mz domain found serving spam). **DR Congo and Madagascar landed in between**: both have real, distinct commune-level fiscal-transfer LAWS (DRC's Loi Organique 08/016 Art. 115, a second 40% cascade below the province-level 40%; Madagascar's Impôt Synthétique 50/30/10/10 split and a National Equalisation Fund with an honest documented ZERO disbursement in late 2025 due to political unrest) but no individual city/commune self-published budget document for either. **Sudan's national tier was deepened instead of searched for municipal documents**, per the fallback instruction — the war has genuinely collapsed state/municipal fiscal transparency, confirmed rather than assumed this round (Red Sea State's own finance-ministry domain doesn't resolve, though a federal article confirms it still submits accounts).

## Findings

### 1. Nigeria (round 1, `af-nigeria-lga-municipal.json`)

Kano State hosts a genuine, LGA-specific 2025 budget spreadsheet for Kano Municipal Council (not a state aggregate) among 44 per-LGA files. Lagos and Osun States' own Auditor-General-for-Local-Governments annual reports name individual LGAs with Statutory Allocation, Grant, and IGR figures — a document TYPE that may repeat across other Nigerian states, worth checking further. The Constitution's own Section 162 and Fourth Schedule were independently verified from a federal `.gov.ng` host (a genuine `.gov.ng` copy had not previously been located in this branch). No individual LGA operates its own website publishing a budget (checked Ikeja, Eti-Osa, Port Harcourt City, AMAC) — the real LGA-level documents that exist are all state-hosted, not LGA-operated.

### 2. Ethiopia (round 2, `af-ethiopia-chartered-cities.json`)

Resolved this branch's own open question about whether Ethiopia's Property Tax Proclamation 1365/2025 names Addis Ababa specifically — it does, directly, in its own Article 9(9) definitions. The federal FY2023/24 Budget Proclamation (1297/2023) lists Addis Ababa and Dire Dawa as separate, numbered subsidy line items structurally parallel to (not nested under) the 11 regional states, with real Birr figures. Addis Ababa's own Bureau of Finance reports a specific approved city budget (140.3bn Birr) via a news-style page on its own site, though verification was limited by a TLS access issue. Dire Dawa's own finance bureau site exists but publishes no discoverable documents.

### 3. Egypt (round 3, `af-egypt-sub-governorate.json` + update to `eg-cairo-municipal-finance.json`)

A second governorate (Luxor) confirms the same pattern already found for Cairo. Independently re-verified the Local Administration Law 43/1979's actual article text (not previously quoted at this level of detail in this branch): Article 1 confirms markaz/city/hay/village units DO have legal personality, but Articles 43 and 51 show their "resources" are statutorily governorate-ALLOCATED transfers, not independent budgets — the constitutional promise of "independent financial budgets" (Article 178, corroborated this round by Egypt's own State Information Service in Arabic) has had no operative body to exercise it since Local Popular Councils were dissolved in 2011. Egypt's long-stalled new Local Administration Law remains an unpassed draft as of the latest evidence (30 June 2026).

### 4. DR Congo (round 4, `af-drc-commune-etd.json`)

Found a second, textually distinct 40%-cascade rule one tier below the existing province-level Constitution Article 175 finding: the 2008 Organic Law on Entités Territoriales Décentralisées entitles communes/secteurs/chefferies to 40% of the share of national revenue already allocated to provinces — legally separate even though numerically identical to the provincial rule. No individual commune or city (Kinshasa's own communes, Lubumbashi, Mbuji-Mayi) publishes a self-authored budget with figures; several DRC-domain access attempts (Lubumbashi's own government sites, the central bank's CPI pages) were blocked at a technical/network level rather than confirmed absent — flagged for retry.

### 5. Tanzania (round 5, `af-tanzania-lga-municipal.json` + update to `tz-zanzibar-union.json`)

The strongest genuine municipal-document round: Kinondoni Municipal Council's own signed audited financial statement, Dar es Salaam City Council's own budget-plan page with a real 48%-own-source-revenue figure, and a concrete national own-source-revenue formula (0.3%-of-turnover service levy) in the Local Government Finance Act — with the added nuance that property-rate collection was statutorily reassigned from LGAs to the national Tanzania Revenue Authority in a later amendment. Separately, re-accessed the OCGS Zanzibar CPI page (previously robots-blocked in this branch) via a different fetch path and found it does name COICOP directly, updating that node's own description; it still does not name EAC or SADC, now confirmed via full-text check rather than left as inference from Tanzania's constitutional structure alone.

### 6. Sudan (round 6, update to `af-sudan.json`)

Per the fallback instruction, deepened the national tier instead of forcing a municipal search. MoFEP's own site names a specific "Resource-Sharing Law" governing FY2026 state transfers (more specific than the prior aggregate lump-sum figure), and the central bank continues publishing CPI data sourced from CBS through the war (Q4 2024 edition, base year 2007=100). The single strongest sub-national lead — Red Sea State (the wartime interim capital) submitting full FY2024 closing accounts to the federal Chamber of Accounts — is evidenced only by a federal article ABOUT the submission; the state's own finance-ministry domain does not resolve.

### 7. Algeria (round 7, `af-algeria-commune-finance.json`)

The richest quantitative finding of the round. Algeria's annual Finance Law publishes an official "État E" annex giving exact percentage splits (e.g. 10% of domestic VAT, 66% of the petroleum-products tax) between État/Commune/Wilaya/a local-solidarity fund, WITH real DA-denominated commune-level revenue projections, recurring identically in structure across the 2025 and 2026 editions. The Communal Code (2011) independently confirms the enabling fund mechanism by name, though under an inconsistent name across different primary texts over time (FCCL / CSGCL / FSGCL — flagged as likely the same Treasury account, not three funds). No individual commune's own budget document was found; the national Finance Law is where the real figures live.

### 8. Angola (round 8, `af-angola-autarquias.json`)

Found a second, genuinely distinct legal track alongside the corpus's existing (deconcentrated, appointed-governor) local-administration law: a real decentralized "poder local" (autarquias) framework — Lei 15/17 (2017, direct elections, financial autonomy) and Lei 13/20 (2020, a specific 20%-of-five-central-taxes transfer fund called FEAL with a 50/30/20 internal split). Both are textually rich and real, but Lei 15/17's own "gradualism" clause makes actual activation contingent on further state action that has not come — corrected this round's own research-brief premise that autárquicas elections had already occurred; they have not, as of the latest evidence found.

### 9. Mozambique (round 9, `af-mozambique-autarquias.json`)

Found a concrete, quantitative transfer formula in the 2008 municipal finance law: the Fundo de Compensação Autárquica is set at 1.5% of projected state fiscal revenue, distributed 75%-by-population/25%-by-area across municipalities. The current 2023 organic law confirms full financial-autonomy powers on paper. In practice, individual-municipality budget publication is thin: Maputo's own "Documentos" page 404s, Beira has no discoverable budget page, and Matola's official budget URL was found serving unrelated compromised spam content (flagged for corpus-integrity purposes, not cited). Only a smaller municipality (Pemba) was found publishing real applied tax rates, with a caveat about likely-misattributed article citations on its own page.

### 10. Madagascar (round 10, `af-madagascar-commune-finance.json` + update to `af-madagascar.json`)

Found explicit commune-level revenue-sharing formulas (Impôt Synthétique: 50% commune / 30% région / 10% province / 10% national equalisation fund) and the National Equalisation Fund's (FNP) own legal basis and funding sources — plus an honest, primary-sourced admission that ZERO commune-level FNP disbursement occurred as of 30 November 2025, attributed explicitly to political unrest beginning 25 September 2025. This branch's own existing `mg-loi-organique-2014-018` node is updated: its previously-generalized description of "Articles 25/45" is now anchored to the specific FNP-establishment text those articles actually contain. Antananarivo's special-status law splits commune-tier resources 70% to Mairies d'Arrondissement / 30% to the Commune Urbaine itself. No individual commune (including the capital) publishes its own budget document online.

## Secondary observations

- **The "collapse of structure" pattern is real but not a population cliff** — it tracks institutional design, not population rank. Tanzania (5th) and Algeria (10th) produced richer municipal-tier documents than Egypt (3rd) or Ethiopia (2nd), because Egypt and Ethiopia's real sub-national fiscal architecture genuinely doesn't include an ordinary elected-municipal tier the way Tanzania's and Algeria's do — this is a structural finding, not a search-effort gap, and worth stating plainly to Thomas rather than smoothing over.
- **"Enabling law without operative practice" is now a recurring, distinct category**, not a one-off (as Egypt's dormant-since-2011 Local Popular Councils might have suggested it was) — Angola's autarquias and, to a lesser extent, Mozambique's municipalities (rich law, thin practice) both fit this same shape. Worth treating as a named pattern in this branch's own vocabulary going forward: a country can have a textually rich, fully-detailed fiscal-transfer law that is legislated but not actually running.
- **State/provincial-government-hosted documents about LGAs are a distinct, valuable document type from LGA-self-published documents** — Nigeria's Auditor-General-for-Local-Governments reports and Kano's per-LGA budget files are all STATE government products, not LGA-operated sites, yet they are the richest LGA-level primary sources found. Worth explicitly looking for this document type (a higher tier of government reporting ON named lower-tier units) in future municipal-tier rounds rather than only searching from the lower tier's own web presence.
- **A genuine corpus-integrity hazard was caught, not just a dead link**: Mozambique's Matola municipal council domain (a live `.gov.mz` government domain) is currently serving unrelated compromised spam content. Flagged in the file rather than silently skipped, since a future session (or Grok) could otherwise stumble on it and mistake spam-injected content for something real.
- **Technical access failures (TLS/certificate issues, Cloudflare gates, proxy-level 502s) accounted for a meaningfully large share of this round's NOT FOUND results** — DRC's central bank and one INS domain, Algeria's ONS, Mozambique's INE methodology PDF, several DRC city-government domains. These are flagged distinctly from confirmed content-absence NOT FOUNDs in every file this round, and several are carried into this round's Grok follow-up prompt on the theory that a differently-configured fetch path might succeed where this session's tooling didn't.
