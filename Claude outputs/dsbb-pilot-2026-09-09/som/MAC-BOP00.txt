I. Analytical Framework, Concepts, Definitions, and Classifications
 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Since 2013 (data for the reference year of 2012), the balance of payments (BOP) statistics of Macao SAR have been compiled in accordance with the concepts, definitions, and classifications in the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual, sixth edition (BPM6). BOP data for 2002-2011 were originally compiled in the BPM5 format and converted to the BPM6 format. For foreign direct investment statistics prior to the reference year of 2009, the BPM5 and OECD Benchmark Definition of Foreign Direct Investment, third edition are generally applied. For data for the reference years of 2009 and after, the Coordinated Direct Investment Survey Guide and OECD Benchmark Definition of Foreign Direct Investment, fourth edition are in use. 
 
 The residency criterion of the BPM6 is adopted, while the BOP covers transactions of Macao SAR residents with non-residents. Transactions with Mainland China, Hong Kong SAR and Chinese Taiwan are included in the BOP as external transactions. 
 
 Macao SAR reports BOP statistics in the domestic currency, Pataca. 
 
 Macao SAR adopts currency board arrangements under which the legal tender, Pataca, is linked to the Hong Kong dollar. Although the pataca is not a widely traded international currency, the absence of foreign exchange controls under the fixed exchange rate regime ensures that the domestic currency can be freely converted into any other foreign currencies. Hence, the BOP surplus or deficit is largely reflected in the change in international reserves held by the Monetary Authority of Macao (AMCM). 
 
 Reserve assets in the BOP refer to international reserves held by the Monetary Authority of Macao. Macao SAR does not possess any amount of monetary gold, SDRs and unconditional drawing rights with the IMF, and hence foreign exchange reserves are identical to international reserves. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification system is largely in accordance with the BPM6 with a few exceptions. Due to confidentiality considerations, Construction services (credit) are included in Other business services . Moreover, breakdown by type of transport (by sea, air or others) is not available. 

II. Scope of the data
 2.2.1 Scope The classification system is largely in accordance with the BPM6 with a few exceptions. Due to confidentiality considerations, Construction services (credit) are included in Other business services . Moreover, breakdown by type of transport (by sea, air or others) is not available. Scope of the data The data cover the entire territory of Macao SAR. The BOP statistics cover, in principle, all resident organizations in private and public sectors that are engaged in transactions with nonresident units, and to a certain extent, resident individuals’ transactions with nonresidents. Exceptions to coverage Current account : 
 
 Government n.i.e. does not cover technical assistance of foreign government and international organizations. 
 
 Compensation of employees (credit) is missing because local workers employed temporarily abroad do not need to report to the Macao Government. In addition, income data are not available for resident employees who work for foreign embassies and international organizations located in Macao. However, with the limited number of such organizations in Macao, data gaps should not be significant. 
 
 Direct investment income earned from abroad under individual status is missing because no survey can be devised effectively and efficiently to target this area. 
 
 Portfolio investment income earned from abroad by individuals not transacted through local authorized financial institutions are not included. 
 
 For Other investment income , interest income from deposits abroad and interest expenses on external loans are enhanced by data models based on data of the BIS (Bank for International Settlements). 
 
 Financial account : 
 
 Direct investment made abroad under individual status is not covered. Since 2015 (data for the reference year of 2014), personal outward remittances of Macao residents through local banks for external real estate investment have been collected in the enterprise survey of the financial sector. Household financial investment abroad (portfolio and other investments, except deposits abroad that can be obtained from the BIS data) only covers those transactions going through local authorized financial institutions, and hence, transactions outside the local financial channel are not recorded. Unrecorded activity The following transactions are not recorded: 
 
 - unreported imports and exports of goods, which are imputed through the equilibrium analysis of supply and use in the national accounts (see Part 3.3: Statistical techniques below); 
 
 - compensation of Macao SAR employees earned from abroad; 
 
 - direct investment income earned from abroad by individuals; 
 
 - portfolio and other investment incomes earned from abroad by households for transactions that are not done through local authorized financial institutions, though interest income from deposits abroad and interest expenses on external loans are enhanced by data models using BIS data; 
 
 - direct investment made abroad by individuals (investment in external real estate made by personal outward remittances through local banks have been collected since data for the reference year of 2014); 
 
 - household financial investments abroad (portfolio and other investments except deposits) that are not made through local authorized financial institutions. 

III. Accounting Conventions
 2.4.1 Valuation Both exports and imports of goods are valued on an f.o.b. basis. 
 
 The principle of market valuation specified in the BPM6 is used to value transactions. Transactions denominated in foreign currencies are converted into Pataca values using, if possible, the exchange rates prevailing at the time of the transactions, or otherwise, the annual average exchange rates. 2.4.2 Recording basis Recording is generally done on an accrual basis. However, interest income for financial institutions, other than for the AMCM, is sometimes recorded on a due-for-payment basis. 

IV. Nature of the Basic Data Sources
 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Source data mainly come from international trade statistics, national accounts statistics and enterprise surveys, supplemented by Coordinated Portfolio Investment Survey (CPIS), annual economic activity surveys, some smaller-scaled/ad-hoc surveys, and administrative data from various government bodies. Estimations from data models are used to bridge data gaps where raw data could not be obtained directly from surveys. 
 
 Enterprise surveys are carried out independently for the financial and non-financial sectors. All authorized financial institutions (including banks, insurance companies and financial intermediaries) are required to respond to the enterprise survey of the financial sector. For the enterprise survey of the non-financial sector, only enterprises with foreign direct investment are covered. 
 
 Since 2010, in order to cope with the earlier time schedule of releasing BOP statistics in August, the preliminary BOP statistics of the non-financial sector, which is an integral component of the entire BOP statistics, have been based on sample data from the enterprise survey and other estimations, which are subject to revision later on. In the sample of the non-financial sector, all gaming enterprises are selected while sampling is applied to the other non-financial enterprises. 
 
 1. Current Account 
 
 Goods General merchandise trade statistics and Non-monetary gold are obtained mainly from the merchandise trade statistics compiled by the Statistics and Census Service (DSEC). 
 
 Services 
 
 Transport : Data are obtained from statistics of various annual economic activity surveys and national accounts. 
 
 Travel : Credit entries for travel are mainly derived from national account statistics that utilize data of the Visitor Expenditure Survey, Hotel and Similar Establishment Survey, information from hotels, and administrative records on gaming receipts. The split between business and personal travel is based on purpose of visit reported by interviewed visitors. Debit entries of business travel are mainly derived from the annual enterprise surveys and economic activity surveys, whereas those for personal travel are principally extracted from national account statistics that are mainly estimated from the Household Budget Survey and other statistics. 
 
 Financial services: The explicitly charged and other financial services are collected from the enterprise surveys and annual economic activity surveys, whereas FISIM is estimated from data models. 
 
 Government n.i.e.: Data are collected from the Government Expenditures Abroad Survey conducted by the AMCM with assistance from the Financial Services Bureau. 
 
 Other services: Data are collected from the enterprise surveys and annual economic activity surveys, supplemented with national account statistics and administrative data if necessary. 
 
 Primary Income 
 
 Compensation of Employees: Debit entries are compiled based on enterprise surveys and the Government Expenditures Abroad Survey. Credit entries are missing. 
 
 Investment Income : The enterprise survey serves as the major source of investment income data, supplemented by the CPIS. 
 
 (i) Direct investment income : The basic source of information is enterprise surveys. The DSEC enterprise survey collects information on “undistributed earnings/losses” by the all-inclusive method, which does not exclude realized/unrealized capital gains and losses; meanwhile, realized/unrealized capital gains and losses are excluded from “undistributed earnings/losses” in the AMCM enterprise survey. 
 
 (ii) Portfolio investment income : The main data source is the CPIS for the financial sector, which actually collects portfolio investment income data for both financial and non-financial sectors (as long as the non-financial sector makes portfolio investments abroad via local authorized financial institutions). The CPIS for the non-financial sector augments the portfolio investment income data by incorporating the data for those non-financial enterprises that directly make portfolio investments abroad by themselves. 
 
 (iii) Other investment income : The basic source of information is enterprise surveys. The debit entries of the Withdrawals from income of quasi-corporations are primarily estimated from the dividend payout rate of the Direct Investment income, while the interest incomes (both credit and debit entries) are enhanced by using data models from BIS data. 
 
 (iv) Other primary income: Source of information is the administrative records on the rent paid by the Macao Government for the use of land owned by Mainland China. 
 
 Secondary Income 
 
 General Government : Debit entries are obtained from the Government Expenditures Abroad Survey, as well as the amount paid as social benefits that are mainly estimated from administrative records. The Macao SAR government rarely receives current transfers from abroad according to government accounts so that there is no credit entry. 
 
 Other sectors : Personal transfer outflows (which covers workers’ remittances and others) are collected from banks through the enterprise survey whereas personal transfer inflows include mainly workers’ remittances. Workers’ remittances are estimated on the basis of the number of foreign workers in Macao SAR (debit) and the number of Macao people working abroad (credit), on which assumed rates on remittance out of their wages are applied. The number of Macao residents working abroad is estimated by extrapolating the unpublished data of the Population Census while the earnings of Macao residents working abroad are mainly estimated with reference to the median salaries published by the respective official statistical units. Miscellaneous current transfers include mainly complimentary services such as hotel accommodation, food and beverages, etc. that are provided free of charge by gaming enterprises in Macao to nonresident clients for the purposes of business promotion, supplemented by current transfers to NPISHs that are obtained from a survey aimed at major nonprofit and charitable organizations selected from the file containing registered associations updated by the DSEC. 
 
 2. Financial Account 
 
 Direct Investment: Data are obtained from enterprise surveys as well as administrative records, covering real estate bought by nonresidents, from the Financial Services Bureau. Financial press review (clipping service) and administrative information from the Macao Trade and Investment Promotion Institute are augmented to improve coverage of outward direct investment. Since 2015 (data for the reference year of 2014), personal outward remittances of Macao residents through local banks for external real estate investment have been collected in the enterprise survey of the financial sector. For the years from 2010 to 2017, data from administrative records on capital invested by the Macao Government into the Hongkong-Zhuhai-Macao Bridge are also included. 
 
 Portfolio Investment: Financial institutions report both portfolio assets and liabilities as well as those investments made through them by residents. Data from the CPIS are also fed into this category for those non-financial enterprises that directly make portfolio investments abroad by themselves. 
 
 Financial Derivatives: Enterprise surveys and the CPIS are used to collect the data. 
 
 Other Investment: The flow data are derived from differences between beginning and closing stocks reported in the enterprise surveys, except currency and deposits (assets) of other sectors where exchange-rate adjusted flow data from the BIS are applied. No adjustments are made for both price and exchange rate changes for other items in the Other Investment category. Since 2015 (data for the reference year of 2014), personal outward remittances of Macao residents through local banks for other cross-border investment and for deposit transfers to bank accounts in Mainland China have been collected in the enterprise survey of the financial sector. Nevertheless, with the inclusion of Mainland China in the locational international banking statistics compiled by the BIS towards the end of 2015, the deposit transfers to Mainland bank accounts made through personal outward remittances are no longer used in the BOP compilation from the reference year of 2016 to avoid double counting. 
 
 Reserve Assets: Data are provided by the AMCM where valuation and other changes are eliminated. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Data requirements of different surveys are indicated in the explanatory notes attached to the survey forms. Source data drawn from surveys are consistent with the definitions, scope and classifications, and time of recording of the BOP statistics. 
 
 Administrative records used to compile BOP statistics provide reasonable approximation of the methodological requirements of the BOP. However, certain degree of bias may exist in purchases of properties by nonresidents based on the record of stamp duty provided by the Financial Services Bureau. Since a piece of property can be transacted more than once during the year and the selling of the previously recorded property by nonresidents to residents will not be reflected in BOP statistics, this part of direct investment for the year 2002-2008 could be overstated. This problem has been resolved with the Financial Services Bureau and the bias has been eliminated for the data for the reference years of 2009 and after. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Annual merchandise trade statistics and preliminary national accounts statistics (which are subject to revisions) are compiled within 3 months after the end of the reference period. 
 
 The data from the enterprise survey for the financial sector as well as those from nonprofit and charitable organizations on current transfer are received within 5 months after the end of the reference period. Data of the enterprise survey for the non-financial sector and various economic activity surveys are generally available within 10 months after the end of the reference period. However, all enterprises in the gaming industry and sample companies in other industries are requested to submit the enterprise survey within 6 months after the end of the reference period. 
 
 Supplementary data from the CPIS are collected within 3 months after the end of the reference period. 
 
 Upon receiving all the completed questionnaires of the Government Expenditures Abroad Survey, the Financial Services Bureau returns them to the AMCM. Normally, this collection would be done no longer than 6 months after the end of the reference period. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The data collected from the enterprise survey of the financial sector are cross-validated with the financial statements submitted to the AMCM. Respondents will be asked to confirm the accuracy of data with abnormal variation. 
 
 Administrative data sources are checked for temporal consistency and for consistency with other related data sources. 

V. Compilation Practices
 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Current Account 
 
 Goods 
 
 For General merchandise , adjustments are first made to imports and exports of goods through the equilibrium analysis of supply and use in the national accounts for the improperly declared goods, for example, mobile phones, food and beverages, jewellery and watches, cosmetics, raw materials, semi-manufactures, construction materials, etc. In addition, fuels that are purchased in non-local ports are also counted as imports of goods. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures 1. Current Account 
 
 Goods 
 
 To convert the value of imports of General merchandise from a c.i.f. basis to an f.o.b. basis, the freight and insurance services are estimated according to assumed rates on the c.i.f. import value of goods. The “i” and “f” components are then separated to reach an f.o.b. value of merchandise imports and subsequently booked, respectively, as debit entries in Insurance and pension services and Transport services. 
 
 Services 
 
 Insurance and pension services include life, nonlife and reinsurance services with same treatments. Gross premiums and claims are collected separately. On the credit side, gross premiums after deduction of gross claims are recorded as insurance services; gross claims are debited and credited in secondary income as offsetting entries. On the debit side, since the insurance service charge per unit of premium is not known, gross premiums are booked as insurance services whereas gross claims from abroad go into secondary income. 
 
 Compensation of Employees and Investment Income 
 
 Compensation of employees and investment income of the non-financial, non-gaming sector for the preliminary BOP statistics are generally estimated from their respective growth rates, while no estimations are made to those of the gaming sector as all gaming enterprises are included. 
 
 Secondary Income 
 
 Other sectors : Workers’ remittances are estimated on the basis of the number of foreign workers in Macao SAR (debit) and the number of Macao people working abroad (credit), on which assumed rates on remittance out of their wages are applied. 
 
 2. Financial Account 
 
 The financial account statistics of the non-financial, non-gaming sector for the preliminary BOP statistics are generally estimated from their respective growth rates, while no estimations are made to those of the gaming sector as all gaming enterprises are included. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results There is no procedure to validate intermediate results against other data sources. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data There is no procedure in place for regularly assessing and investigating the behavior of statistical data series against related series. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Errors and omissions remain large. 

VI. Other Aspects
 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses No routine revision studies and analyses are carried out at present. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request In addition to the statistics routinely disseminated, other general statistics and customized tabulations are made available upon request on the discretion of the AMCM and subject to the confidentiality principle.
