I. Analytical Framework, Concepts, Definitions, and Classifications
 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Merchandise trade statistics are compiled in accordance with recommendations on International Trade Statistics Concepts and Definitions of the United Nations Statistical Papers Series M No.52 of 1970. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Data are available by commodity classification, quantity, value, and country of origin/destination. Goods are classified using both the Harmonised System (HS) and Standard Industrial Trade Classification (SITC) Rev. 3, however data is published only in SITC Rev. 3 format. Data in HS format is available upon request. 

II. Scope of the data
 Scope of the data Merchandise trade covers the movement of goods to and from Grenada and the rest of the world via air and sea and parcel post (goods passing through the post office). Items traded include the value of improvements and repairs to goods, 
 

III. Accounting Conventions
 2.4.1 Valuation Imports are recorded inclusive of insurance and freight charges (c.i.f.) and domestic exports and re-exports are recorded at the f.o.b. value, i.e. exclusive of insurance and freight charges. 2.4.2 Recording basis The special trade system is used to record trade statistics. Therefore goods are only recorded as imports when they have been cleared by the Customs and Excise Department. Imports exclude all goods brought into the country but which remain under the control of Customs in bonded (customs) warehouses. Goods re-exported from customs warehouses are not included in the trade system. 

IV. Nature of the Basic Data Sources
 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The data are compiled primarily from extractions from the ASYCUDA system from the Customs and Excise Department, however information on banana exports , nutmeg and mace, fish and cocoa exports are obtained directly from the exporter. These extractions are processed using the EUROTRACE computer program.
