I. Analytical Framework, Concepts, Definitions, and Classifications
 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The overall conceptual framework for the balance of payments of Aruba is in broad conformity with the guidelines in BPM5 . The transition to conform with the guidelines in BPM6 is foreseen in 2017. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification of transactions is in accordance with the list of standard components in BPM5, but services transactions are not provided in the same detail. The services account distinguishes, transportation services, travel, construction, 'other government services, n.i.e.' and includes all other services transactions in the item 'other services, n.i.e.' 
 The sectorization in the balance of payments is in accordance with BPM5 . For example, government-controlled enterprises that are public enterprises are excluded from general government and are included as public enterprises in the appropriate nonfinancial or financial corporations sector; examples are the Water and Electricity Company (WEB) and the telecommunications enterprise SETAR. 

II. Scope of the data
 2.2.1 Scope The classification of transactions is in accordance with the list of standard components in BPM5, but services transactions are not provided in the same detail. The services account distinguishes, transportation services, travel, construction, 'other government services, n.i.e.' and includes all other services transactions in the item 'other services, n.i.e.' 
 The sectorization in the balance of payments is in accordance with BPM5 . For example, government-controlled enterprises that are public enterprises are excluded from general government and are included as public enterprises in the appropriate nonfinancial or financial corporations sector; examples are the Water and Electricity Company (WEB) and the telecommunications enterprise SETAR. Scope of the data The balance of payments covers all transactions between residents and nonresidents of Aruba. The economic territory of Aruba coincides with the island of Aruba and the neighboring continental shelf. The residency of institutional units is determined on the basis of the criterion of center of economic interest in accordance with the guidelines in BPM5 . For example, unincorporated branches of nonresident construction enterprises active in Aruba are considered residents. Bonded warehouses and factories established in the Free Zone are considered residents, too. The “Arubahuis”, representative office of the government of Aruba in The Hague, The Netherlands is considered resident. So is the Representative Office in Washington D.C. The Dutch army base in Aruba and its staff are considered nonresidents. 
 The balance of payments of Aruba is published in Aruban florin. Exceptions to coverage In deviation from the guidelines in BPM5, financial offshore entities legally established in Aruba are not considered residents. 
 In deviation from the guidelines in BPM5 , the balance of payments records merchandise trade predominantly on a payments basis. Accordingly, the entry on trade credits has poor coverage. Another deviation is that interest is mainly recorded on a cash basis instead of an accrual basis. No entries are made for debt arrears (arrears on interest payments and loan redemptions), which is in line with BPM6. 

III. Accounting Conventions
 2.4.1 Valuation Transactions are valued at market value. Invoice data on merchandise imports are adjusted to FOB basis using fixed coefficients. Transportation services and insurance premiums are adjusted accordingly. 
 In the ITRS, all transactions are reported in their original currency. They are converted into Aruban florin at the monthly average of the daily exchange rates. 2.4.2 Recording basis Accrual accounting is not consistently applied to interest payments, in deviation from the guidelines in BPM5 . Interest transactions reported by affiliated enterprises in their reports on foreign intercompany accounts are recorded on an accrual basis. Interest transactions related to foreign bank accounts are recorded on a cash basis. So are interest on private loans and government debt. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures In accordance with the guidelines in BPM5 , current account transactions are recorded on a gross basis and capital and financial account transactions are recorded on a net basis, separately for the individual asset and liability components. 

IV. Nature of the Basic Data Sources
 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The core data sources - in the case of Aruba, the international transactions reporting system (ITRS) - are broadly sufficient to compile the balance of payments. 
 The core data sources are the banks’ foreign exchange reports on international transactions, and reports on foreign bank accounts (FBA), foreign intercompany transactions (FIA) and portfolio investment report (PIR). They are supplemented by reports on free zone transactions, money transfers, sub-report tourism and imputed transactions. 
 Information from other available sources supplement the core data sources, for example, licenses for foreign exchange transactions. Data on reserve assets are provided by the central bank. Data on the government’s external transactions and positions (e.g., transactions in the government's external debt and data on representative offices' expenditures abroad) are provided by the central bank, acting as bank for the government, and by the Department of Finance. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data are consistent with the definitions, scope and classification of balance of payments statistics. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data collection and processing timetables are adequate to meet timeliness and periodicity for the dissemination of the balance of payments. Respondents are regularly reminded of the deadlines set for reporting. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The completeness and consistency of the ITRS reports by the banks are regularly assessed. In particular, settlements in ITRS reports that are not relevant to the balance of payments (so-called neutral transactions) are carefully analyzed to ensure that they cancel out. 

V. Compilation Practices
 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The CBA uses sound data compilation procedures benefiting from specific characteristics of the ITRS. The foreign exchange statements by the domestic banks and the reports on foreign bank accounts and foreign intercompany accounts by enterprises are reported in the original currency. The change between the beginning position and the end position of the accounts has to be fully explained by the settlements in the reports. The beginning position must be equal to the end position of the previous period. The plausibility of the codes used in the reports is manually checked for all settlements above the threshold (currently 100.000 Aruban Guilders (AWG)). 
 Adjustments are occasionally made for banking positions on the basis of monetary data and for the debt service by government on the basis of information from the Department of Finance. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Banking transactions are derived from stocks (for correspondence with monetary statistics). The same holds true for central bank transactions (including transactions in official reserves). In theory, the value of transactions would, therefore, include valuation changes. However, as foreign assets of banks and the central bank are predominantly denominated in US dollars, a currency to which the Aruban florin is pegged, this procedure produces outcomes that are approximately correct. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results High value transactions are cross checked with information from other sources, for example financial statements of financial and nonfinancial enterprises.
