I. Analytical Framework, Concepts, Definitions, and Classifications
 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The Central Bureau of Statistics in Aruba compiles and disseminates merchandise trade statistics in accordance with the guidelines provided in the International Merchandise Trade Statistics: Concepts and Definitions, Rev2 ( IMTS) of the United Nations Statistics Division. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System is used for collection, compilation, and dissemination of international merchandise trade statistics. The statistics are presented in the following international classifications, which have different levels of detail and which are based on different classification criteria: "International Standard Industrial Classification of all Economic Activities, third revision" (ISIC Rev. 3). 
 The country classification is based on the coding proposed by the United Nations Statistics Division Standard Country and Area Codes Classifications. 

II. Scope of the data
 2.2.1 Scope The Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System is used for collection, compilation, and dissemination of international merchandise trade statistics. The statistics are presented in the following international classifications, which have different levels of detail and which are based on different classification criteria: "International Standard Industrial Classification of all Economic Activities, third revision" (ISIC Rev. 3). 
 The country classification is based on the coding proposed by the United Nations Statistics Division Standard Country and Area Codes Classifications. Scope of the data Trade statistics are compiled in accordance with the General Trade System. Total imports and total exports comprise all goods entering and leaving the economic territory of Aruba, respectively. The economic territory of Aruba consists of the island of Aruba (the free circulation area) and the Free Zone Aruba. Exceptions to coverage In accordance with IMTS, monetary gold, securities, banknotes, coins in circulation are excluded from merchandise trade statistics, because they are not considered goods. Goods consigned to the Dutch armed forces on Aruba and to the Representative Office in the Hague, The Netherlands (Arubahuis) are also excluded from merchandise trade statistics, because they do not represent transactions with nonresidents. All postal items (not courier services) are excluded from merchandise trade statistics. Transactions in mineral fuels are excluded to a large extent because of confidentiality reasons. This relates to imports of crude oil and butane, and to (re-)exports of crude oil, kerosene, gasoline, gas, propane and butane. Unrecorded activity No estimates are made for non-reporting or under-reporting. 

III. Accounting Conventions
 2.4.1 Valuation The value of the goods equals the value of the commodity at the place and time it crosses the border. The basis for valuation is cost, insurance & freight (c.i.f.) for imports and free on board (f.o.b.) for exports. The value of all merchandise transactions is in thousands of Aruban Florins (Vx1000 Afl.). The Aruban florin (AFL) is pegged to the US dollar at the rate of USD 1=AFL 1.79. Quantities are expressed as net weight in thousands of kilograms (Wx1000 Kg.). Blank cells indicate that there was no transaction for that commodity code. 2.4.2 Recording basis Timing of recording transactions: imports and exports are digitally recorded on the date of registration by the company and later assessed by the Custom department. 
 They are processed monthly. The data are provisional and are reprocessed on quarterly and annual basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Merchandise transactions are recorded on a gross basis. 

IV. Nature of the Basic Data Sources
 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The Customs clearance process uses "ASYCUDA" (Automated SYstem for CUstoms DAtay). This is a computerized customs management system designed by UNCTAD to assist customs authorities all over the world to automate and control their core processes. This system takes into account the international Harmonized Commodity Description, coding System (generally referred to as "Harmonized System or simply "HS") and standards developed by ISO (International Organization for Standardization), WCO (World Customs Organization) and the United Nations. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The Harmonized contributes to the harmonization of Customs and trade procedures for Aruba, and covers around 5916 eight digit commodity codes. The first two digits of the code are universally recognizing product codes. The remaining six digits further divide the categories into the specific commodities. For publication purposes, Aruba uses 21 sections to clearly organize these product groups. Imports and exports are published by C.I.F and f.o.b. appraisals, respectively. Goods are valued at the date recorded by the Customs, import reported in foreign currencies are translated at the official exchange rate at that time to Aruban Florins. 
 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Every fifteenth of the month The Central Bureau of Statistics receives a digital excel file from the Custom department of Aruba. The data in this file covers all transactions processed by the Custom department of the previous month. Every year the Central Bureau of Statistics receives an update of the data to compare and exclude any Cancelled document. 3.2.1 Source data assessment When the Central Bureau of Statistics receives monthly data from the Custom department a completeness test is executed to determine any missing document and any document which may be processed more than once. Also checks are done to determine if any new codifications have been used in order to properly process these new codifications. The Central Bureau of Statistics is entirely dependent on the Custom department for thorough and correct data collection and recording. The Central Bureau of Statistics in close cooperation with the Custom department is assessing coverage and providing feedback on the experienced problems. 

V. Compilation Practices
 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The data processing of the Customs trade databases consists o f two-stages. Stage 1 consists of macro-editing, series-editing, and small-count automated system analysis utilizing techniques based on transaction groups and time-series. Stage 2 or final control is the verification of problematic data when checked against historical data. 
 
 The macro-editing Program incorporates univariate and bivariate exploratory data-analysis techniques on the basis of transaction groups (declaration series, Customs office and commodity code). It provides a rapid means of checking value and weight measures. Macro-edit works by estimating the constant relationship between values and net weight as well as between gross weight and net weight, while also estimating univariate outliers based on unit values (Customs value divided by weight). 
 
 Macro-edit attempts to come up with the best possible corrections to all of the data points, which fall outside the confidence intervals of both the value/weight regression analysis and the gross weight/net weight regression analysis. It will evaluate the original data and for each point outside of the currently calculated confidence intervals, make an estimation of a data point that would be inside the confidence interval. This imputation is made without referring to the original data, and therefore clearly involves both decision making and guesswork. Although imputations attempt to achieve a higher degree of consistency in the value/weight and the gross/net weight relationships, they may yield implausible results in the univariate statistics. The narrower one makes the confidence interval, the more points will be outside it, the more imputations will be made. This is because all points outside the confidence intervals will be imputed. 
 
 The series-editing Program is a companion program to the macro-editing program, providing quality control on the outcome of the macro-editing and catching errors not easily seen when editing data belonging to one isolated month. Series analysis takes the data of the monthly analysis and prepares a time series. series-editing checks the consistency of the result over time. Its main purpose is the analysis of macro-editing quality and raw data. Macro-edit statistics and unit values are the basis for series-editing analysis. The program observes the total value and weight per transaction group including the number of transactions. 
 
 After the different editing and controlling procedures, the preliminary publication is analyzed by comparing different quarters, same quarter versus previous quarter, quarter versus same quarter previous year. Large differences are investigated as for what the causes may be. The data is adjusted after checking with the Custom department and or the importer. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures No procedure is followed 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results No procedure is followed 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data No procedure is followed 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs No procedure is followed
