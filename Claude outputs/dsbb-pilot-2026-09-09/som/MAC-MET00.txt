I. Analytical Framework, Concepts, Definitions, and Classifications
 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Concepts and definitions adopted in the compilation of Macao’s merchandise trade statistics are in line with the International Merchandise Trade Statistics (IMTS) – Concepts and Definitions, 2010 of the United Nations. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization - For compilation of external merchandise trade statistics, the classification of merchandise is based on the Harmonized System (HS 2017), which contains 6641 commodity subdivisions at 8-digit level. The first 6-digit is an adaptation of the structure defined by the World Customs Organization and the last 2-digit is designated to cater for the characteristics of Macao. 
 
 - The Standard International Trade Classification – Revision 4 (SITC – Rev. 4) is used mainly for trade analysis. 
 
 - In view of the distribution of the commodities in the external trade of Macao, two other commodity classifications are also used: Classification by Principal Products of Export (PPE) for exports of goods and Classification by Broad Economic Categories (BEC) for imports of goods. 

II. Scope of the data
 2.2.1 Scope - For compilation of external merchandise trade statistics, the classification of merchandise is based on the Harmonized System (HS 2017), which contains 6641 commodity subdivisions at 8-digit level. The first 6-digit is an adaptation of the structure defined by the World Customs Organization and the last 2-digit is designated to cater for the characteristics of Macao. 
 
 - The Standard International Trade Classification – Revision 4 (SITC – Rev. 4) is used mainly for trade analysis. 
 
 - In view of the distribution of the commodities in the external trade of Macao, two other commodity classifications are also used: Classification by Principal Products of Export (PPE) for exports of goods and Classification by Broad Economic Categories (BEC) for imports of goods. Scope of the data Macao’s external merchandise trade statistics refer to the movements of merchandise between Macao and its trading partners by mode of transport. The External Merchandise Trade Statistics cover all goods passing through the customs control of Macao, excluding the followings: personal belongings of travellers; postal parcels for personal use or non-commercial use; temporary imports or exports; transshipment goods; goods under operational lease; monetary gold; bank notes and coins in circulation, etc. 

III. Accounting Conventions
 2.4.1 Valuation - Any transaction value in foreign currency is converted to MOP-equivalent in accordance with the exchange rates of the Monetary Authority of Macao. 
 
 - Value of merchandise imports and exports is expressed in MOP. 
 
 - Imports are valued c.i.f. and exports (domestic exports and re-exports) are valued f.o.b. 
 
 - Quantities of imports or exports are expressed in kilogram (kg). In addition, supplementary measurement unit (e.g. m 2 , l, kw) is recorded if available. The weight recorded represents the net weight of goods. 2.4.2 Recording basis The Macao Customs Service is responsible for ensuring that the information stated on the licenses/declarations corresponds to the actual goods transported. 

IV. Nature of the Basic Data Sources
 3.1.1 Source data collection programs - Macao’s merchandise trade statistics is compiled according to the information on import/export licenses/declarations, as well as those of the Electronic Data Interchange (EDI). 
 
 - Information taken includes commodity coding, description, quantity and value of goods, partner country (origin, final destination, consignment), type of customs clearance, name of importer or exporter, port of loading or unloading, and mode of transport, etc. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The source data ensure classification of trade data is consistent with the Harmonized System (HS 2017), the SITC – Rev. 4, the Classification by Principal Products of Export (PPE) and the Classification by Broad Economic Categories (BEC). 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Copies of the licenses and declarations are sent to the DSEC on the following working day after completion of customs procedures. 

V. Compilation Practices
 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Upon receipt of the licenses and declarations, the documents are subject to further processing, namely codification of commodities, data-capturing, consistency checks and data-processing. 

VI. Other Aspects
 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Merchandise trade statistics not frequently disseminated are available upon request subject to assessment on data quality and statistical confidentiality.
