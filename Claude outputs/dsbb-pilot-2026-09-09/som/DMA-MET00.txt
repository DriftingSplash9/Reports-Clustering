I. Analytical Framework, Concepts, Definitions, and Classifications
 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Merchandise trade statistics are compiled in accordance with recommendations on International Trade Statistics Concepts and Definitions of the United Nations, Statistical Papers series M No. 52 of 1970. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Data are disseminated by commodity classification, volume, value supplementary quantity where applicable and by country of origin/destination. Commodities are classified using both the Harmonized System (HS) and the Standard International Trade Classification (SITC) Rev.3. 

II. Scope of the data
 Scope of the data The merchandise trade covers the movement of goods to and from The Commonwealth of Dominica and the rest of the world via sea, air, and parcel post (goods passing through the post office). Items traded include the value of improvements and repairs to goods, non-monetary gold, and goods leased for a period exceeding one year. 

III. Accounting Conventions
 2.4.1 Valuation The valuation of imports is C.I.F. and the sum of domestic exports and re-exports are recorded at F.O.B. 

IV. Nature of the Basic Data Sources
 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Trade statistics are compiled using the special system of trade. Hence goods are only considered imports after they have been cleared by the Customs and Excise Department. Imports therefore exclude all goods brought into the country but are held in customs bonded warehouses; likewise goods re-exported from customs warehouses are excluded from exports. 
 Imports data are compiled primarily from the customs declaration forms (trade warrants) processed at the Customs Department, using ASYCUDA (Automated Systems for Customs Data). The raw data is forwarded to the CSO on diskettes and then downloaded into EUROTRACE, Computerized Trade Processing System for processing. Total-Exports are compiled from Customs declaration forms which are edited and coded at the CSO, and processed in the office using EUROTRACE. In addition, information on banana exports is obtained from the main banana companies. Soaps and related exports are obtained from Dominica Coconut Products and Refresh Companies.
