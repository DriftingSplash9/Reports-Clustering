I. Analytical Framework, Concepts, Definitions, and Classifications
 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The framework for National Accounts is based on the "System of National Account 1993" ("1993 SNA"). The Statistical Office disseminates the national accounts in the "St. Kitts and Nevis Annual Digest of Statistics". This data is also published by the ECCB in its "National Accounts Digest". GDP is compiled using both the production and expenditure approaches. The main data sources are the national accounts establishment survey, government accounting records, administrative records and external trade statistics. Expenditure on the GDP is comprised of total consumption, gross capital formation, and net exports of goods and non-factor services. Adding net factor incomes from abroad to the GDP derives the GNI. Adding net current transfers from abroad to the GNI derives the Gross National Disposable Income (GNDI). Savings are derived as the difference between GNDI, and total consumption. For further details on the BOP, see the GDDS comprehensive framework and data categories and indicators tables for the External Sector. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Economic activities are classified using the International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC) Of all Economic Activities, Revision 3. The 13 activities covered are differing from the ISIC presentation by combining some sectors and showing others separately. They comprise (with ISIC categories shown in parentheses): Agriculture (A - B), Mining & Quarrying, Manufacturing (D), Electricity & Water, Construction (F), Wholesale & Retail Trade (G), Hotels & Restaurants (H), Transportation (I: 60 -6), Communication (I: 64), Banks & Insurance (J), Real Estate & Housing (K), Government Services (L), and Other Services (K. 71-74+M+P+O+P+Q). II. Scope of the data
 Scope of the data The national accounts cover the entire economic territory of St. Kitts and Nevis. In principle, all resident institutional units operating within the economy, plus their transactions with the rest of the world, are included in the national accounts. 
 The Statistical Office and the Eastern Caribbean Central Bank (ECCB) in their annual "Digest of Statistics" and "National Accounts Digest", respectively, disseminate national accounts tables. These statistics include GDP in the current and (1990) constant prices, expenditure on the GDP at current prices, Gross National Income (GNI), and Gross National Disposable Income (GNDI). The reference period is the calendar year, and data are available from 1977 to 1999. All economic activities performed in St. Kitts and Nevis are covered. The data include estimates for underreporting in Construction. Valuation is at factor cost for GDP, but Expenditure on the GDP, GNI AND GNDI are valued at market prices. National accounts data are disseminated in EC$ millions. Unrecorded activity No adjustments are made for illegal activities. Own-account production of goods is not covered by the available sources of data, and is indirectly estimated.

III. Accounting Conventions
 2.4.1 Valuation Output by industrial activity is recorded for St. Kitts and Nevis at producers' prices, which are defined as purchasers' prices less invoiced trade and transport margins. The data is published by industry for output at factor cost, which is derived from output at producers' prices by subtracting net taxes on products and production. Based on the BOP estimates, exports and imports are valued at Free on Board (FOB). GDP by final use is valued at market prices, which is equivalent to the "SNA 1993" purchasers' price valuation concept. 2.4.2 Recording basis Transactions are recorded on an accrual basis. An exception is the government revenue and expenditure data, which is recorded on a cash basis. Recording period: Data are compiled for the calendar year. However, a number of enterprises compile accounts on a fiscal accounting year. Their annual data are included in the national accounts for a given calendar year if their fiscal year begins before June 1st of reference period.

IV. Nature of the Basic Data Sources
 3.1.1 Source data collection programs 
 Production approach: 
 Agriculture: Output of crops is obtained from the Ministry of Agriculture. Intermediate inputs for other crops are comprised of imports obtained from External Trade Statistics and the local inputs are extrapolated based on a benchmark survey. Data on the output and intermediate input of forestry activities are based on a benchmark survey. Fishing output and intermediate consumption are derived as direct estimates from Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries Division. 
 Mining and quarrying: There is no mining activity. Estimates of output and intermediate consumption for quarrying are obtained from the government accounting records. 
 Manufacturing: Output and intermediate consumption are obtained primarily from the Annual National Accounts Establishment Survey (NAES) and Inland Revenue Department. 
 Electricity and water: Direct estimates of output, intermediate input, and value added for Electricity, and Water are obtained from the government accounting records. 
 Construction: Output is based on customs data on imports of building materials from the trade database. 
 Wholesale and retail trade: The value added is estimated on the basis of the annual accounts obtained via the NAES and is supplemented by information obtained from the Inland Revenue Department. 
 Hotels and restaurants: Output of accommodation services in current and constant prices are estimated from data on number of tourists in paid accommodation, average length of stay, and average daily expenditure obtained from a benchmark visitor expenditure surveys and the NAES and Inland Revenue Department. 
 Transport: Output and value added are directly estimated using data from the NAES, Port Authority, Traffic Department and Inland Revenue Department. 
 Communications: Output and value added for communications sector are derived using data from the NAES and the Government Accounts from the Treasury Department, Ministry of Finance. 
 Banks and insurance: Data for banking is obtained from the NAES and from the Eastern Caribbean Central Bank commercial banking survey. Data for insurance comes from the NAES, and is supplemented by the insurance rates collected from the insurance companies. Data on both sub-activities are also obtained from the Inland Revenue Department. 
 Real Estate and Housing: Value added is computed by extrapolation based on a benchmark survey. 
 Government services: Value added is computed using the income approach, and comprises labor costs, which are obtained from the financial statements of Social Security, and Government accounting records. 
 Other services: Estimates for professional and business services, private health, education, and entertainment activities are based on data received from a variety of sources including the Social Security, Inland Revenue Department of the Ministry of Finance and the NAES. Expenditure approach: 
 Private final consumption: This component of expenditure along with increase in stocks are determined as the residual of GDP at market prices less Government final consumption, gross capital formation, and net exports of goods and non- factor services. 
 Government final consumption: Consumption of general government services is defined as output less the value of commodity and non-commodity sales and of own-account capital formation. The source data are government accounts from the Treasury Department. 
 Gross fixed capital formation: Capital formation for transport and other equipment is compiled from customs sources using a commodity flow approach, as there is little domestic production of these items. Construction is compiled via a commodity flow approach, as in the treatment of the construction industry in the description of the production approach to compiling GDP above. Land development is compiled based on data obtained from the administrative records of the Ministry of Finance or from construction companies doing the work. 
 Increase in stocks: this is not measured, but considered as part of the residual balance. 
 Exports and imports of goods and non-factor services: These data are taken directly from the Balance of Payments compiled by the ECCB and the Central Statistical Office. See the GDDS comprehensive framework and data categories and indicator tables for the External Sector for details on the compilation of net exports of goods and services. Discrepancies: There is no discrepancy between the production and expenditure approaches as the expenditure component of the private final consumption and increase in stocks is derived as a residual. 

V. Compilation Practices
 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Production approach: 
 Coverage adjustments: Various methods are used for the treatment of missing values of output and intermediate consumption for individual establishments in the NAES and other sources. Among these are estimating current intermediate consumption on the basis of its past share in an establishment's output, estimating output on the basis of the past ratio of output to selected cost data, extrapolating the most recent past observed level of output, intermediate input, and value added on the basis of growth rates observed either in the past or currently in reported data for similar establishments. 
 Estimation of current price data: Value Added (GDP) by industry is the difference between output and intermediate consumption. For crops, quarrying, electricity and water, air and sea transport, post and communications, and banking and insurance, output and intermediate consumption are compiled from accounting records sampled in the NAES or obtained from administrative sources. Banking services are measured as net interest and other income. There are insufficient data at present to allocate intermediate and final consumption. Consequently, these services are treated as intermediate consumption of a notional industry. For general government, value added is compiled from the income approach, rather than as the difference between output and intermediate consumption. For manufacturing, the level of output and intermediate consumption are estimated from the NAES. For construction, output is estimated using a commodity flow method, as the sum of production costs for materials and labor unique to the industry, plus trade and transport margins for those items. For non-sugar crops, fishing, hotels and restaurants, and road and sea transport, output is produced from estimates of quantity produced multiplied by average price. For crops, intermediate input is estimated via a commodity flow method. For construction, wholesale and retail trade, hotels and restaurants, and road transport (in non-benchmark years), value added is estimated as the product of an estimated ratio of value added to output from a sample survey, either the NAES or an ad hoc or special collection, multiplied by estimated total output for the industry. For Real Estate and Housing output and intermediate consumption is estimated by extrapolation using benchmark surveys. 
 Estimation of constant price data: One of two methods is used for compiling constant price data, depending on the industry. In the first method, constant price value added is computed by deflating current price value added by a price index for telecommunications, construction, wholesale and retail trade, and insurance. In the second method, constant price value added is computed by extrapolating a reference year current price estimate of value added by an output volume index of physical index for manufacturing, mining and quarrying, and post. Expenditure approach: 
 Coverage adjustments: There are no adjustments in the sources and estimates of expenditure on GDP not already mentioned under the production approach. 
 Estimation of current price data: Private consumption is determined as the residual of GDP using the production method less government consumption, gross capital formation, and exports less imports of goods and services. Gross fixed capital formation comprises investment in equipment and structures. Equipment investment is estimated via a commodity flow method principally using data on imports, and structures investment is the gross output of the construction industry. There are no adjustments to equipment investment to include set-up costs. As noted in section IV above, exports and imports of goods and services derive from the balance of payments prepared by the Statistical Office in collaboration with the ECCB. Private consumption and increase in stocks are determined as a residual, so GDP using the expenditure approach is not independently determined and is, by definition, reconciled with GDP by production. Exports and imports of goods and services used in the calculation of GDP and net factor incomes from abroad used in the calculation of GNP (GNI) are taken from the Balance of Payments, and therefore reconcile with those data by definition. 
 Base year and update cycle: The year of reference for constant price series is 1990, and is updated on a 10-year cycle.
