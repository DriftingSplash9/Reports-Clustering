 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: Consolidation of monthly returns on assets and liabilities submitted by commercial banks on their operations in Singapore. Classification: Domestic and external accounts are distinguished on the basis of residency of account holder. The definition of residency is broadly consistent with the BPM6 . Domestic sectors include: (i) Government of Singapore, (ii) Statutory Authorities of Singapore, (iii) Banks, and (iv) Nonbank Customers. For classification of deposits, Nonbank Customers are further divided into Financial Institutions Other than Banks and Other Customers. Public corporations are not separately identified. Detailed definitions and explanations pertaining to residency and sectoral classification of accounts are provided in MAS Notices to Banks under "Notice 610 - Submission of Statistics and Returns". MAS Notices are available from the MAS website. Definitions of monetary aggregates: M1 consists of currency in active circulation and demand deposits with banks. Excluded are commemorative, numismatic and bullion coins issued by the MAS, and cash held by commercial banks and the MAS. The Board of Commissioners of Currency, Singapore, has merged with the MAS since 1 October 2002. M2 comprises M1 plus quasi-money. The latter includes fixed, savings and other deposits with banks as well as negotiable certificates of deposit in Singapore dollar issued by Singapore banks. M3 consists of M2 plus net deposits with finance companies. Prior to the merger of the Post Office Savings Bank with the Development Bank of Singapore in November 1998, M3 also included net deposits with the Post Office Savings Bank. The overall structure of concepts and definitions for the analytical accounts of the banking sector is consistent with the principles outlined by the IMF in the Monetary and Financial Statistics Manual and Compilation Guide. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: Domestic and external accounts are distinguished on the basis of residency of account holder. The definition of residency is broadly consistent with the BPM6 . Domestic sectors include: (i) Government of Singapore, (ii) Statutory Authorities of Singapore, (iii) Banks, and (iv) Nonbank Customers. For classification of deposits, Nonbank Customers are further divided into Financial Institutions Other than Banks and Other Customers. Public corporations are not separately identified. Detailed definitions and explanations pertaining to residency and sectoral classification of accounts are provided in MAS Notices to Banks under "Notice 610 - Submission of Statistics and Returns". MAS Notices are available from the MAS website. Non-performing loans (NPLs) are those graded Substandard, Doubtful and Loss. The definitions are as follows: Substandard: loans are classified substandard when their normal repayments are overdue or may be in jeopardy. Also included in this category are performing loans which are graded substandard solely because of the borrowers' weak financial situation. The banks make 10% provision for the unsecured portion of the loans classified as substandard. Doubtful: loans are classified doubtful when full liquidation of outstanding debts appears questionable and the accounts suggest that there will be a loss, the exact amount of which cannot be determined as yet. Banks make 50% provision for doubtful loans. Loss: loans are classified as loss when outstanding debts are regarded as uncollectible. Banks make 100% provision for loss loans. Domestic banks automatically classify loans as NPLs once the principal or interest payments are 3 months or more in arrears. In addition, they are continuing to treat all loans to borrowers with weak financial situation as NPLs, regardless of whether they are in arrears. 2.2.1 Scope Classification: Domestic and external accounts are distinguished on the basis of residency of account holder. Scope of the data Covers MAS, finance companies and commercial banks comprising full, wholesale and offshore licensed banks. The permitted scope of activities of the various types of commercial banks is defined in the "Guidelines for Operation of Wholesale Banks” and “Guidelines for Operation of Offshore Banks" issued by the MAS. Exceptions to coverage Institutional coverage-other banks: Merchant banks are excluded. 2.4.1 Valuation Accounting Practices: Securities (debt and equity) are valued at the lower of cost or market value. With effect from financial year 1998, banks will implement the recommendations of the Committee on Banking Disclosure on financial valuation basis and disclose the accounting policies adopted. A copy of the Committee's report is available from the MAS website. Valuation of foreign-currency-denominated instruments: Commercial banks submit data in national currency equivalent. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources for central bank (if included in the analytical framework): Data from the MAS are used to compute monetary aggregates since the merger of the Board of Commissioners of Currency, Singapore, with the MAS in 1 October 2002. Data sources for other banks: The sources of data are statistical report forms from commercial banks, and finance companies. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Data verification: The statistical returns submitted by banks to MAS are subject to audit by independent external auditors of the banks. MAS also conducts periodic on-site examination of banks' operations and their record keeping procedures. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Estimation/sampling: Not applicable as data are not reported on selective basis. Statistical adjustments: No statistical adjustments are made. Significant subsequent data revisions made by banks are indicated in the Monthly Statistical Bulletin published by MAS. None
