 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Data are disseminated in millions of U.S. dollars on total exports (f.o.b.) and total imports (c.i.f.). Data are compiled according to the sixth edition of the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual ( BPM6 ). Definitions: Merchandise exports include all the goods produced or assembled in Costa Rica that cross the customs frontier for sale to nonresidents. Merchandise imports include all goods from abroad acquired by the economy's residents. Relationship with international guidelines: Recording methods for imports and exports are based on the IMF recommendations (as suggested in the BPM6, those imports and exports of Free Zones on which there is no change in ownership are excluded). The tariff system used to record goods coincides, at the level of four digits, with the Harmonized System that has been adopted worldwide. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: International merchandise trade in Costa Rica is divided into two major groups: general merchandise and goods for processing. The former includes all goods imported or exported by individuals or legal entities outside of special trade regimes. The latter includes goods imported or exported by enterprises that are supported by one of the special regimes and are subdivided into: free trade area enterprises and inward processing enterprises. Scope of the data Coverage: The General Trade System is used, whose principal criterion is the economic territory, as recommended by the IMF. All merchandise that crosses the frontier for commercial purposes in included. Geographical coverage: The databases of the General Directorate of Customs include the country of origin/destination for every registration number. Activities recorded under country code 950 are excluded from exports, as they represent the sales of regular enterprises in free trade areas. Unrecorded activity Activities not recorded: There are no estimates of unreported or under-reported activities. 2.4.1 Valuation Unit of account: External trade figures are recorded and published in current U.S. dollars. No adjustments are made to calculate the figures in constant terms. Valuation method: Exports are recorded f.o.b. (excluding insurance and freight), while imports are recorded c.i.f. (including insurance and freight). 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: The criterion used is the shipping date. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Sources: Information is obtained from the databases of the The National Customs Service (NCS) and includes all statements submitted by the various customs agencies on the operations of regular enterprises and individuals, as well as transactions under special regimens. Type of data compiled: Date of issue of the form, customhouse permit number, tariff item, gross f.o.b./c.i.f. value in U.S. dollars, gross weight in kilograms, country of origin/destination code, shipping date, exporter/importer code. Base for country code: The country of origin is used for imports and the country of destination for exports. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The timeliness is mainly determined by the requirements of the SDDS. Data collection and processing timetables adequately meet the standards of timeliness and periodicity recommended for disseminating merchandise trade data. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Adjustments: The Statistics of Goods and Trade Area of the Central Bank of Costa Rica compares the General Directorate of Custom's database with that of Institute of Statistics and Census (INEC) and the agency for promoting exports (PROCOMER), to detect any inconsistencies between them, investigate their causes, and produce a more accurate database. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Processing of information: Customs software (TICA) of the General Directorate of Customs Processing site: A computer processing system by BCCR.
