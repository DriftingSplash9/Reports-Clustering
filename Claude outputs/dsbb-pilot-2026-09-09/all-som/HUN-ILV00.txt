 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Concept: Gross international reserves are external assets that are readily available to and controlled by the Magyar Nemzeti Bank (MNB) for direct financing of payments imbalances, for indirectly regulating the magnitude of payments imbalances through intervention in foreign exchange markets to affect the Hungarian forint exchange rate, and/or for other purposes. No other foreign asset of the MNB is classified as reserve asset than the liquid external assets, which meets the criteria set in the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual. Definition: Gross international reserves are a statistical statement of end-month outstanding liquid external assets, which are owned and not just controlled by the MNB. Relationship to international guidelines: The official reserves data are compiled according to the methodology specified in the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual . The reserves template data are compiled in accordance with the IMF's International Reserves and Foreign Currency Liquidity: Guidelines for a Data Template, 2013 . 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: Instruments included within the definition of international reserve assets are classified into monetary gold, Special Drawing Rights, the Reserve Position in the IMF, and other reserve assets. 2.2.1 Scope Classification: Instruments included within the definition of international reserve assets are classified into monetary gold, Special Drawing Rights, the Reserve Position in the IMF, and other reserve assets. Scope of the data Institutional coverage: The MNB controls the official reserve assets of Hungary. Instrument coverage: Repayable margin accounts - as part of deposit assets - linked to financial derivatives are included in the gross international reserves. Exceptions to coverage Financial derivatives coverage: Financial derivative instruments are not included in gross international reserves. The central bank's foreign exchange claims that are denominated in non-convertible currencies are also excluded. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: Starting from January 2004, official reserve assets are valued at market price including accrued interest. Since 2008 securities data are derived from securities statistics. Gold is revalued at the end of each month at the spot price announced on the Reuters page EFX=prevailing at 11 a.m. C.E.T. on the reference date. Conversion rates: Reserves data are converted at prevailing end-month exchange rates into the national currency equivalent. The Reserve Position in the IMF is converted into national currency on the basis of official exchange rates of the forint against the SDR. 2.4.2 Recording basis Frequency of calculation and revaluation: International reserves are compiled monthly. Data are publicly disseminated on a monthly basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs International reserves are based on the balance sheet position data of MNB and the securities statistics. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Statistical adjustments: None.
