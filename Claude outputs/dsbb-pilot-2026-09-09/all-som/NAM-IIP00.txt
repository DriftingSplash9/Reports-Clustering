 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The International Investment Position (IIP) for Namibia is a balance sheet summarizing the stock of external financial assets (claims by resident on non-residents) and liabilities of residents (claims by non-residents on residents). The Bank of Namibia (BON) is responsible for the compilation of the IIP statistics. The IIP is compiled in accordance of BPM6 of the IMF. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The IIP statistical framework mostly follows the BPM 6 . The collected data are aggregated into five main accounts: direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives, other investment as well as reserves. 2.2.1 Scope The IIP statistical framework mostly follows the BPM 6 . The collected data are aggregated into five main accounts: direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives, other investment as well as reserves. Scope of the data The geographic territory of Namibia, including Export Processing Zones, is the territory of reference for the IIP. Transaction Coverage: In principle all major IIP stock data are covered. The stock data covered in the IIP statistics are those of Namibian residents with the rest of the world and vice versa. Unit Coverage: In principle, all the major resident units of the reporting economy that have foreign financial assets and liabilities. Exceptions to coverage Reconciliation continues between MFS and IIP pertaining to OFC still remain a data gap for the IIP. 2.4.1 Valuation The stock data on international investment position are mainly collected and expressed in Namibia dollar. However, in cases where data are reported in US dollars, such data are converted to Namibia dollars by using the end-of-period exchange rates of the relevant period. Methods of valuation are employed for the valuation of stocks in the IIP, i.e. market value or book value. 2.4.2 Recording basis The time of recording follows the guidelines of BPM 6 . Arrears and financial transactions are recorded on accrual basis. Reporting of foreign currency denominated debt is valued in Namibia Dollars according to the end of period exchange rate. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Broadly, data for compiling the IIP are captured on a gross basis and follows the IMF BPM6 guideline. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Direct investment in and abroad, the main source of information is surveys on foreign direct investment. The survey collects levels of foreign assets and liabilities at the beginning and end of each quarter, with separate information for equity, retained earnings, and debt. Portfolio investment into and by the private sector is collected through surveys. Data on the major pension and insurance funds are received through surveying the companies which manage these funds on behalf of these institutions. But more needs to be done to ensure the correctness of the received data. Other investment information is collected through surveys of government departments, depository corporations, and administrators of the pension funds and life assurance funds. Survey data by enterprises that are involved in trade credits, other loans payable/receivable and loans from companies other than direct is also collected for this category. Reserve assets are obtained from the Bank of Namibia's administrative records. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Definitions, classifications, valuation and time of recording are explained through the notes accompanying the survey forms sent to the data suppliers. Further, annual visits are made to various entities, which supply the data and more explanations are done to clear misunderstandings. The broadening of the scope of data is done in various ways, such as through Media, company's registration office or through the word of the mouth. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The IIP data is collected and compiled on a quarterly basis with one quarter lag. The quarterly survey are sent out two weeks after the end of each of quarter and expected to be received about four weeks after the end of the quarter. The data is normally received in a timely manner from the reporting agents with a reasonable response rate achieved which makes it easier to compile the BOP and IIP. Namibia currently meets the GDDS requirements and is in the process of migrating to SDDS. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Sample surveys are being assessed over time. In addition, new companies entering the market are captured through the Media and an exploratory survey is rolled out to every 10 years to establish their viability. Data for non-respondents are estimated based on dummy survey for such companies' data and compiled with the rest of the received data, while follow up for actual data continues. This method was found as a more reliable way of estimation as opposed to the carrying forward previously submitted data. Survey data is crossed checked yearly with AFS and revisions are done accordingly. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Statistical techniques that are in line with data requirements are explained in the survey form and are required from data sources. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Data on fiscal year are converted to calendar year. Although there was no clear and consistent method of adjustment for missing data at all times. The compilers are currently supplementing missing data with data obtained from the exploratory survey conducted and quarterly adjustments are done based on new data respondents and partly flows observed in the ITRS related to companies not responding. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results There are processes in place to validate data discrepancies in most cases, but extensive monitoring is required to maintain consistency with other data producing agencies. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data IIP data is verified with AFS on an annual basis and validated quarterly by observing changes in the balances. Outliers are crossed checked with the ITRS system and follow ups are done with data respondents. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revision policy is in place for BOP data but not for the IIP. The revision and explanatory notes are made for the previously quarterly released data in the Quarterly Bulletin. Footnotes are also included in each data publication to indicate provisional data that might be revised in the future publications. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses There is currently none, although there are plans to have the revision policy published on the website of the Bank. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request None.
