 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: Compilation and dissemination of External Debt is done according to the internationally recognized standards, set in the IMF’s 2013 External Debt Statistics Guide for Compilers and Users and the IMF's Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual, sixth edition (BPM6). External debt by domestic sectors is shown in the same manner as in the IIP. Data on the principal and interest payment projection are not available to the CBBH. Data is broken down by following sectors: General government, Central Bank, Deposit-Taking Corporations and Other sectors while direct investments are a separate category. Each sector (except direct investment) is further divided by original maturity (short term and long term) and by debt instruments. Debt instruments cover all BPM6 defined debt instruments. Definition: Gross external debt is the outstanding amount of external debt liabilities of residents to nonresidents, those actual current, and not contingent, liabilities that require payment of principal and/or interest. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification in BH Gross External Debt is based on the IMF’s 2013 External Debt Statistics Guide for Compilers and Users. Standard components of Gross External Debt are classified by sectors and by instruments, with separate presentation of direct investment. Gross External Debt is compiled by sectors as recommended in IMF’s 2013 External Debt Statistics Guide for Compilers and Users: General Government, Central Bank, Deposit Taking Corporation and Other Sectors while direct investments are a separate category. Each sector (except direct investment) is further divided by original maturity (short term and long term) and by debt instruments. 2.2.1 Scope The classification in BH Gross External Debt is based on the IMF’s 2013 External Debt Statistics Guide for Compilers and Users. Standard components of Gross External Debt are classified by sectors and by instruments, with separate presentation of direct investment. Gross External Debt is compiled by sectors as recommended in IMF’s 2013 External Debt Statistics Guide for Compilers and Users: General Government, Central Bank, Deposit Taking Corporation and Other Sectors while direct investments are a separate category. Each sector (except direct investment) is further divided by original maturity (short term and long term) and by debt instruments. Scope of the data The geographic territory of reference is the territory of Bosnia and Herzegovina (BH). Residency principle, as defined in the BPM6 manual, represents the basic principle followed for recording of gross external debt for BH. Data are disseminated in millions KM, showing components of gross external debt by sectors and by instruments, with separate presentation of direct investment. Sectorial coverage: Quarterly data on external debt liabilities are collected from the banking sector, the government and the other sectors (other financial institutions, enterprises and households). The sector classification involves the sector classification of residents according to ESA 2010. Geographical coverage: Data on external debt cover the entire BH economy. The Gross External Debt is presented in relation to the rest of the world, without geographical breakdown. Exceptions to coverage Data on the principal and interest payment projection are not available to the CBBH. Data on debt securities of Other nonfinancial sector in external debt statistics is incomplete regarding coverage. The direct investment survey is developed with the ability to compile data on portfolio investment, as well. Thus, covered are only portfolio investment of the companies with direct investment. Data on debt securities of General government sector are obtained from Government Finance and Financial Accounts Statistics. Data on debt service payment schedule are missing. Unrecorded activity N/A 2.4.1 Valuation Debt securities are valued at market prices. The debt instruments are valued at nominal value using accrual methodology. For conversion to unit of account data reported in foreign currencies are converted to BAM using Central Bank od Bosnia and Herzegovina 's end of period midpoint exchange rate. 2.4.2 Recording basis Principle used for recording debt position data is on accrual basis. Data providers are encouraged to report on accrual basis where the debt balance includes principal and interest that have accrued but not yet payable, interest arrears and future principal payments. Requirements for accrual recording are met to a high degree but not fully, the Government loan liabilities are exception as these do not include accrued interest. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Grossing and netting procedures, followed in CBBH’s presentation of data, correspond to IMF’s 2013 External Debt Statistics Guide for Compilers and Users and BPM6 guiding principle. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The gross external debt statement is compiled in conformity with the methodology set forth in the IMF’s 2013 External Debt Statistics Guide for Compilers and Users and IMF's BPM6. Primary data sources for compiling external debt statistics are: Direct Surveys (survey on direct investment, survey on trade credits and advance payments), Statistical and Administrative sources (BH Ministry of Finance and Treasury, Federal Ministry of Finance, Ministry of Finance Republika Srpska, Bank for International Settlements), and Statistics compiled within the CBBH (Monetary and Financial Statistics (MFS), Government Finance and Financial Accounts Statistics (GFFAS), and BH Balance of Payments Statistics). These data collection programs proved to be comprehensive providing reliable and accurate data. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The framework for compilation of the Gross External Debt fully follows the concepts and definitions prescribed in the IMF’s 2013 External Debt Statistics Guide for Compilers and Users and IMF's BPM6. Direct surveys (survey on direct investment, survey on trade credits and advance payments) are prepared and conducted in line with recommendations stated in BPM6 and OECD benchmark definition of DI fourth edition. These surveys have detailed explanations and definition of terms used. Accrual recording, appropriate valuation method, classifications and time of recording are required by the surveys and met to a high degree by reporting units. Monetary statistics data as a source for compilation of the External Debt follows standards and rules set up in the Monetary and Financial Statistics Manual edition 2000. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Source data required for compilation of quarterly Gross External Debt are available with frequency and timeliness as follows: Survey on direct investments – quarterly frequency with 45 days timeliness, Survey on direct investments – annual frequency with 6 months timeliness, Survey on trade credits and advances – annual frequency with timeliness by the end of March, Monetary and Financial Statistics (MFS) – monthly frequency with 4 to 5 weeks timeliness, except for Other Financial Corporation (quarterly frequency with 4-month timeliness), Government Finance and Financial Accounts Statistics (GFFAS) – quarterly frequency with 45 days timeliness 3.2.1 Source data assessment Data on direct, portfolio and other investment collected through survey on direct investment are assessed against Monetary and financial statistics for sector of Deposit Taking Corporation and Other Financial Corporation. Data on direct investment, obtained through the survey, are assessed against the data from financial statements of enterprises, following positive law regulations collected by FIA (Financial Information Agency) and APIF (Intermediary Agency for IT and financial services), two official agencies who are in charge of collecting financial statements of enterprises. Assessment refers to the data on profit and loss, reinvested earnings, loans and dividends. This assessment improves the coverage of the companies with small amount of registered equity, but significant amount of reinvested earnings and other capital, including loans. Data on external debt of government sector obtained from Government Finance Statistics are also assessed against data from BH Ministry of Finance and Treasury. Stocks related to SDR and IMF accounts are assessed against the IMF accounts available on the IMF web site. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Direct investment: CBBH conduct a regular quarterly (on a sample) and annual (full coverage of companies with DI exceeding a KM 100,000 threshold) direct survey on DI. The survey includes financial and non–financial corporations which have, or are assumed to have, foreign investment from abroad, which invest abroad or are borrowing funds from foreign countries. For the companies with direct investment below the threshold, CBBH makes estimates based on data obtained by courts on registered invested foreign capital. The survey is conducted in accordance with BPM6 and OECD benchmark definition of DI fourth edition. For the purpose of compilation of quarterly Gross External Debt data on deposit-taking corporations (DTC) and other financial corporations are collected through DI regular quarterly surveys. For non-financial corporations data obtained from annual survey are used for quarterly estimates. The estimate takes into consideration the consequences of financial transactions and other economic changes which took place over the observed period. Portfolio investment: The direct investment survey is developed with the ability to compile data on portfolio investment, as well. However, compilation of portfolio investment data in this respect is incomplete regarding coverage. The survey covers only portfolio investment of the companies with direct investment. As an additional data source for DTC and other financial corporations, MFS data are used while for the non-financial corporations ITRS is additional data source. For the General government sector data are obtained from (GFFAS) Government Finance and Financial Accounts Statistics. Other investments: The data mostly obtained from the Monetary and Financial Statistics, GFFAS, CBBH Banking Department. Data from Bank for International Settlements (BIS) quarterly reports are used as a source of currency and deposits of non-financial corporation. Data on other investment for other sectors are obtained from the CBBH survey on direct investment (which covers data on other investment of companies), and data on loans of nonfinancial corporations provided by Federal Ministry of Finance and Ministry of Finance Republika Srpska. Data on trade credits and advances for DTC and Other financial corporations are obtained from Monetary and Financial Statistics. The data for non-financial corporations are compiled through the CBBH direct quarterly survey on trade credits and advance payments. Trade credits and advance payments survey covers the biggest importers and exporters. Data on trade credits collected through DI survey are crosschecked with data collected through survey on trade credits and advance payments to avoid overlaps, and these are treated as direct investments. Data from the survey, are also used to estimate quarterly positions on trade credit and advances. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Trade credits and advance payments survey is conducted on a sample and statistical techniques are used to estimate data for entire population. Data on trade credits collected through DI survey are crosschecked with data collected through survey on trade credits and advance payments to avoid overlaps, and these are treated as direct investments. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Source data used for the compilation of External Debt are reliable and accurate to the extent possible. The population used as a base for a sample, the creation of sample, response rates, coverage, and accuracy of reported data are regularly monitored and updated. Thus, data sources as well as data itself are considered comprehensive and reliable. Results of the assessments carried out are monitored and made available to guide future statistical processes. Validation of intermediate results are applied as follows: Logical and mathematical data validation in surveys. Opening balance should be in line with the closing balance that are reported in the past period. Closing balance must be equal to sum of opening balance, net financial transactions and other changes. Cross-checking of different source data, and if there are substantial discrepancies investigation is conducted to check if certain transactions are unrecorded, or coverage and scope issues exist, or there are some valuation and time of recording issues. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Assessment of discrepancies is conducted as follows: Data on direct investment, obtained through the survey, are assessed against the data from financial statements of enterprises, collected by FIA (Financial Information Agency) and APIF (Intermediary Agency for IT and financial services), two official agencies who are in charge of collecting financial statements of enterprises. Assessment refers to the data on profit and loss, reinvested earnings, loans and dividends. Data on direct, portfolio and other investment collected through survey on direct investment are assessed against Monetary and financial statistics for sector of Deposit Taking Corporation and Other Financial Corporation. Data on external debt of government sector obtained from Government Finance Statistics are also assessed against data from Ministry of Finance and Treasury BH. Discrepancies are analysed jointly with GFS and MFS Sections, and where possible corrections are made. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses No formal revision studies are carried out, but data is regularly revised and updated.
