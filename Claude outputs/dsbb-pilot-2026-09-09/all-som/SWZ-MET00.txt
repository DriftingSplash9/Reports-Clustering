 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Customs uses the special trade system instead of the internationally recommended general trade system. There are several issues with use of special trade system, the most important of which is that data is not entered into the country when it is assumed that there is a change of ownership, that is when merchandise crosses the border. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Customs uses the harmonized system (HS) to classify merchandise trade data. HS6 is used by the in-house data capture application and HS8 is used by ASYCUDA. Scope of the data The Central Statistical Office (CSO) has a unit based at Customs that works co-jointly with the data collection/capturing unit in ensuring that the compilation and dissemination meets the required standards. There is a large number of businesses that use bonded warehouses to delay paying taxes until after merchandise is sold to consumers. Some vehicle dealers and many of the liquor distributors, among others, designate their places of business as bonded warehouses. 2.4.1 Valuation The import data are valued on a cif basis. The Statistics section also conducts a quarterly survey on major exporters to compare with the border declarations. Exports are valued on a fob basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data is collected from all the borders, including dry ports, by customs using customs forms. The data is captured in real time using ASYCUDA or at the head office using the in-house application. Both systems make an extraction of the captured datasets into a custom application for trade that is Eurotrace © . This application is able to merge the data from the two platforms for dissemination. There is a publicity office within the department that ensures that traders and the general public is informed of customs procedures. None None
