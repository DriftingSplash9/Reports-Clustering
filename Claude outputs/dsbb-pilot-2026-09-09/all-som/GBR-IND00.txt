 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Concept: Indicator of the monthly change in the output of industrial production. Definition: The index measures the real monthly change in gross value added or net output in the mining and quarrying, manufacturing, electricity and gas and water and waste sectors using a fixed set of representative products of constant price and quality. Gross value added is monitored by means of proxies: In the manufacturing sector total turnover is used under the assumption that the ratio of value added to gross output remains constant over the short term. The exceptions are volume of production for mineral oil refining and iron and steel production. In the mining and quarrying sector volume of production is primary data source although total turnover is also used. In the electricity and gas sector volume of production is used. In the water and waste sector volume of production and total turnover are used. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: The UK Standard Industrial Classification 2007 (SIC - equivalent to ISIC REV and NACE REV ) is used to classify businesses by kinds of activities. Scope of the data Scope of the data The primary purpose of the IoP is to produce a short-term measure of the output of the production industries within the UK economy and show the monthly movements in the gross value added (GVA) of the production industries ( Standard Industrial Classification 2007 (SIC 2007) (sections B to E). GVA is defined as ‘total outputs minus total inputs'. The four main components of the production industries are: mining and quarrying (B), manufacturing (C), electricity, gas, steam & air conditioning (D) and water, sewerage, waste management & remediation activities (E). The IoP contributes 13.6% to the output estimate of GDP . The index is estimated using the same data sources and National Accounts methodology as the quarterly estimate of production industries' gross value added within the output approach to measuring GDP. Transaction coverage: The production boundary is in accordance with the ESA2010. Geographic coverage: Great Britain and Northern Ireland, excluding the offshore islands (the Channel Islands and the Isle of Man). For Index of Production (IOP) sectors B, D and E, most source data is based on production volume administrative data. For the manufacturing sector (C) the monthly business survey is the primary data source measuring turnover values. Data is available as a monthly index for all industries. Source of weights: please see the following for details: Derivation of weights 2.4.2 Recording basis Timing of production observations: Report forms are despatched approximately a week before the end of the reference month. The data are transferred into the computer system for the Index of Production (IoP) on the 18th working day after the end of the reference month. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources for IoP are listed as part of the GDP(O) source catalogue The Monthly Business Survey (MBS) is the main source of information for calculating the indices of output for the manufacturing industry. The survey covers approximately 96 percent of total manufacturing or 74 percent of total production. The survey sample is drawn from the ONS Inter-Departmental Business Register (IDBR) . Response rates to the survey are published as part of the bulletin. Unit selection: Businesses are selected according to their main activity and employment size, which determines whether a business is randomly sampled or fully enumerated. Sample sizes: Around 6000 businesses every month. Data is also collected by the by the Department for Energy, Security and Net Zero (DESNZ) covering a variety of different sectors: Extraction of crude petroleum and natural gas (UK SIC 2007 Divisions 05 and 06) Manufacture of mineral oil refining (UK SIC 2007 Division 19) Electricity and Gas (UK SIC 2007 Division 35) Other data sources include data on steel production from the International Steel Statistics Bureau for SIC Division 24. This is a monthly survey of the major manufacturers. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Nature of indicators used: For production volume data consistent volume indicators are collected, for example tonnes of oil for mining and quarrying and as described for petroleum refining and electricity and gas as detailed in these links: Extraction of crude petroleum and natural gas Electricity and Gas Manufacture of mineral oil refining For total turnover, monthly total and export turnover data for the reference month and delimiters of the period if the data for the reference month are not available. Appropriate adjustments to standardize the contributor data to the reference month are made where necessary. The turnover data are deflated to the reference period by the relevant components of the producer price index and export price index at 2, 3 and 4-digit levels of SIC. The data is then seasonally adjusted to arrive at a gross output proxy at the 2, 3 and 4-digit industry levels. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The data are transferred into the computer system for the Index of Production (IOP) on the 18th working day after the end of the reference month. Oil and gas source data timeliness: A deadline is given for data suppliers to respond if they are unable to meet the deadline for monthly data. If the data is delivered late then a forecast provided by DESNZ is used for the latest month. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Procedures for non-response: Non-response is generally low - around 25 percent of sampled units at first calculation stage, falling to around 18 percent within two months. Response rates to the MBS are published each month and can be found here: Monthly Business Survey (production) response rates Non-responding units are followed up on telephone. There is rigorous follow-up of non-responding units, but if the problem persists, the data from the previous period are carried forward, or they are imputed using the movement in the aggregate index. As the data are collected under UK legislation (1947 Statistics of Trade Act) units can be legally enforced to respond. Treatment of missing data: When data are temporarily missing they are imputed by following the same procedures as in the case of non-response of sampled units (see above - procedures for non-response). 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Nature of weights: Details on how the IOP weights are derived can be found in the following link: Guide to derivation of weights Aggregation: The lowest level indices are progressively aggregated to the higher levels indices and overall index using base year weights. Separate indices are compiled at the aggregate level for mining and quarrying, manufacturing and for electricity, gas and water supply. Deflation: The data are deflated by means of a combined home and export price deflator. This is to remove the effect of price changes (or inflation) from the set of values to derive the volume.
