 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Malta has compiled annual national accounts since 1954 without, however, fully adopting the System of National Accounts 1968 (SNA 1968). In 2004, Malta adopted the European System of Accounts (ESA 95), which is consistent with the System of National Accounts 1993 (SNA 1993), for reference years 1995 onwards. In September 2014, Malta implemented the European System of Accounts 2010 (ESA 2010), which is consistent with the System of National Accounts 2008 (SNA 2008). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization One of the stepping stones for the implementation of the 1995 ESA in Malta was the adoption of the classifications consistent with internationally accepted guidelines, standards, and good practices. Notably, the adoption of the NACE and the Classification of Products by Activity (CPA) were considered landmarks for the Maltese statistical system. These were followed by the adoption of the Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP) to classify household consumption, the Classification of Functions of Government (COFOG) to classify government transactions, and the Classification of the Purposes of Non-profit Institutions (COPNI) serving households to classify the expenditures of non-profit institutions serving households. Current classification versions are NACE rev.2 for industry, CPA 2.1 for products, COICOP 2018 for household consumption. Classification of institutional units, transactions, and flows broadly follow the ESA 2010 . The ESA 2010 guidelines on the classification of market and non-market transactions are applied. Institutional sectors are published and made available to users for both the production account and the generation of income accounts. None 2.4.1 Valuation The valuation rules used for recording flows and stocks are in accordance with the ESA 2010 . Market output is valued at basic prices before any taxes on products (VAT, import duties, excises, and other taxes on products and imports) are added and before any subsidies on products are deducted, as recommended by the SNA 2008/ESA 2010 . Output for own final use by the institutional units is valued at basic prices, as recommended by the SNA 2008/ESA 2010 . Government's non-market output is valued at cost by summing up the components of intermediate consumption, compensation of employees, and consumption of fixed capital, consistent with the SNA 2008/ESA 2010 . Intermediate consumption is valued at purchasers' prices with adjustments for holding gains on changes in inventories. VAT, excluding the deductible part, is included in the valuation of intermediate consumption. Final consumption expenditure of households, general government, and NPISHs are valued at purchasers' prices. Those goods and services produced by households for own use and retained in the final consumption of households, according to the ESA 2010 recommendations, are valued at equivalent market prices. Gross fixed capital formation is valued at purchasers' prices (including all costs associated with ownership transfer), if it is bought. However, if it is produced for own consumption, the figure is recorded at market prices Services of owner-occupied dwellings are valued using the user cost method, which includes also consumption of fixed capital and a fixed rate (by Eurostat) for operating surplus. This method was recommended by Eurostat, due to the specific features of Maltese's rental market. Both imports and exports are valued free on board (f.o.b). Information on insurance and freight of merchandise is available; the Balance of Payments Unit within the NSO compiles the f.o.b. measures for the imports. 2.4.2 Recording basis With some exceptions, transactions and flows are recorded on an accrual basis. Work-in-progress in the form of capital formation is recorded in the period the production takes place. With regards to the government sector, transactions for the budgetary central government are reported on a cash basis. The accruals adjustments made by the compiling unit in the NSO are based on aggregated information provided by all departments through the treasury department. In this regard, it should be noted that accounts of the extra-budgetary units and the local councils are already on an accrual basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Most transactions between establishments (local types of activity units) within the same enterprise are recorded on a net basis since data are reported at the enterprise level. However, for some large companies, which provide detailed information by establishment and activity, transactions are recorded gross as recommended in the international guidelines. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The most important data sources for the production approach are listed below 1. Administrative sources: Fiscal data, ,Jobsplus employer's data, Intrastat/Extrastat, Monetary and banking statistics, audited annual accounts and financial statements, Farm Accountancy Data Network (FADN) 2. Surveys and censuses: Structural Business Statistics (SBS), Accomstat and Tourstat, Census of Aquaculture, Census of Agriculture, Census of Fisheries, and financial statements English Language Schools (ELS), Short-Term Statistics (STS), Labour Force Survey (LFS) and Labour Cost Survey (LCS). Regular annual enterprise surveys are available for activities in NACE Sections B to S (except Sections O, K and NACE 94). These are all covered by the SBS survey. Other enterprise surveys include those for English Language Schools (ELS) and Aquaculture, whose coverage is complete. The Accomstat and Tourstat expenditure surveys are also very important for the production approach. These are monthly surveys and are used for Accommodation and food service activities (Section I). Periodic enterprise surveys and censuses are mainly related to the agriculture and fisheries. These surveys are done every ten years and thus administrative data (e.g. Farm Accountancy Data Network (FADN)) is used to derive estimates in between censuses. The Household Budgetary Survey (HBS) is compiled approximately every eight years and is also a data source for the production approach. The HBS, together with the Census of Population and Housing (CPH) which is conducted every ten years, is used in the compilation of owner-occupied dwelling services. Administrative Records such as annual accounts and financial statements are available with a time lag of two years. Annual accounts and financial statements are used across all industries and are mostly important together with fiscal data for NACE sections which are not covered by the SBS in the non-financial sector. Fiscal information includes data on all the active population in the Business Register; however it cannot be used for the banking and insurance sub-sectors. Annual accounts and financial statements may sometimes be presented in an abridged format and thus would not be usable for national accounts purposes. However, this issue is no longer relevant now that fiscal information is also available. In the financial sector these two sources are used together with monetary and banking statistics which are available on a quarterly basis. These two data sources are also particularly important with respect to NACE Sections P to T. 3. The Jobsplus employers' database is another important source for the production approach. It provides the number of jobs by NACE class. It is used as a quarterly indicator in absence of annual data for most NACE divisions. Employed persons are fully covered by enterprise surveys. However, in some NACE sections such as Education, Human health and social work activities, Arts, entertainment and recreation, and Other personal service activities, the Jobsplus employers' database is used to supplement estimates for enterprises not covered by fiscal data or annual accounts and financial statements. It is an important data source as it ensures exhaustiveness in national accounts. 4. Where feasible, the compilers undertake the necessary adjustments to approximate the national accounts concepts. Main areas where adjustments are made concern the following: Supplementary source data are sought, and adjustments are made to capture all types of transactions in the economy, such as payments in kind and other nonmonetary transactions, which are important for national accounts estimation. In estimating the quarterly accounts, the national accounts compilers use alternative sources where accounting data are not available on a quarterly basis, or their level of detail is insufficient. Adjustments are made to customs data to capture transactions recorded by two different databases (Intrastat and Extrastat). This is a follow-up to Malta’s accession to the EU. The coverage of total economic activities in terms of value added by the data sources is good. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Source data are incorporated into the national accounts as they become available. The annual estimates released are the sum of quarters until annual source data become available, in particular survey results. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Administrative and survey data are regularly assessed to identify errors and inconsistencies. For surveys, checks conducted include, among others, the review of annual changes, the review of possible errors in classification, and the comparison of certain ratios like productivity with similar units. Editing procedures include detecting and analysing outliers and confirming improbable values with the source data providers. Administrative data are considered more reliable, but they are also corrected for discrepancies as necessary, just like information from other sources. Consistency checks are made against previous years' information. For some indicators, comparisons are made with the results of other statistics, such as employment data. In some other cases, redressing of data inconsistencies detected is left to the compilers of the basic statistics. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Before incorporating source data into national accounts, the sectoral specialists treat the data, using sound compilation practices. Using additional external information, they impute for missing units (particularly for small enterprises or self-employed persons) to achieve a full coverage of the sector where quarterly survey results are found weak, other administrative data are employed (see also 3.1.2). The sectoral specialists also compare the basic data with external information for their assigned industries. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Specialists studied the non-observed economy for the first time in 2000 and used the results in the following years as benchmark data. Some estimates for the informal sector in areas such as agriculture, personal services (doctor, dentist, lawyers, etc.) were captured in the national accounts previously. The main types of phenomena covered by the 2000 study related to the (1) underreporting by enterprises and misclassification of activities in survey/censuses, (2) unregistered entrepreneurs (for tax evasion), and (3) estimates for some of the illegal activities (such as narcotics and prostitution). The main activities covered included agriculture and fishing, quarrying, manufacturing, construction, trade, hotels and restaurants, transport, other business, and personal services (notably, services by paid domestic staff). Production approach The estimates of output and intermediate consumption are compiled at the two-digit or three-digit level of the NACE, where feasible and relevant. Both output and intermediate consumption are compiled for 88 industries. Source data used to derive the output and intermediate consumption are different according to the industry particularities. In general, estimates are based on enterprise financial statements derived from either surveys or administrative sources. Adjustments are made to the indicators as recorded in the bookkeeping to follow national accounts concepts, as well as to ensure a comprehensive coverage by activity. For example, where survey data do not cover small enterprises or the response rates are low for some activities, benchmark data from previous periods are used to estimate the value added. On the specific issues regarding GDP compilation, the following are noted: Owner-occupied dwellings: Housing output is valued using the user cost method, owing to specific characteristics of the Maltese Islands' rental market. Output is obtained by summing up the expenditure components (intermediate consumption, consumption of fixed capital, net taxes on dwellings, and net operating surplus on dwellings and underlying land). Housing output is valued using the user cost method, owing to specific characteristics of the Maltese Islands' rental market. Output is obtained by summing up the expenditure components (intermediate consumption, consumption of fixed capital, net taxes on dwellings, and net operating surplus on dwellings and underlying land). For some of the cost components, the benchmark data used were derived from the HBS, complemented by other sources. Adjustments were made to avoid double counting and to correct the imputed value on empty dwellings. The calculation Consumption of Fixed Capital (CFC) is based on two approaches. The direct method was applied in the case of Dwellings, whilst the Perpetual Inventory Model (PIM) was applied for all the remaining assets. In case of Dwellings, the stock is first valued at replacement cost and CFC is subsequently compiled by assuming an equal depreciation of dwellings over and average period of the service life of 70 years. The PIM has been applied across sectors i.e. for S.11, S.12, S.13, S.14 and S.15, and at NACE Section level. Specific service lives were applied for forty different asset types. Taxes and subsidies on products, government revenue, and government expenditure are converted from cash to accrual basis or time adjusted cash as requested by Eurostat. Volume measures of GDP are derived at previous year's prices (PYP) and in Chain-Linked Volumes (CLV) using single indicator methods (deflation or extrapolation) directly on GVA or double indicator methods (deflation, extrapolation, or a combination of both) on both output and intermediate consumption. GVA by industry at previous year's prices is compiled at A88 (NACE division level) then aggregated at A64, A21 (NACE section level) and A10. Computations are carried out for the three sectors listed below: Private sector (S.11 + S.12 + S.14) General government sector (S.13) Non-profit institutions serving households sector (S.15) Chain-linked volumes indices are derived using the annual overlap technique, which offers the advantage of maintaining temporal consistency between quarterly and annual values. Chained-indices are obtained by multiplying the ratio ( previous year's price quarterly figure at year T / average quarterly current price figure at year T-1) with average quarterly chain-linked volume figure at year T-1.Several price and volume indices are used to derive previous year's prices estimates. Two separate estimates are compiled; one for Value Added Tax (VAT) and another one for Other taxes and subsidies on products. In order to obtain VAT at PYP, an implicit volume index is derived. With regards to Other taxes and subsidies on products, each tax is extrapolated by a specific volume indicator, either explicit or implicit (derived from a price index) in order to obtain the value of the tax at PYP. Then, all taxes at PYP are aggregated and CLV estimates are computed following the methodology mentioned earlier. The same approach is used for subsidies. GDP in CLV is obtained after adding GVA at PYP with net Taxes and subsidies on products at PYP. Expenditure approach procedures All components of GDP by expenditure are derived independently. Household final consumption expenditure is generally compiled at a four-digit level of the COICOP. The HICPs used to deflate most of the components of household final consumption expenditure. Expenditures by residents abroad are deflated using the inflation rate of the country visited. Government final consumption expenditure is compiled at the two-digit level of the COFOG. The volume estimates are derived using an average price index of the two main components of the expenditure compensation of employees (deflated by an index covering the 20 salary scales weighted by the number of full-time equivalent employees in each scale) and expenditures on goods and services are deflated with a specific deflator which takes into consideration the product breakdown of intermediate consumption of S.13. Gross fixed capital formation: With the introduction of ESA 2010, the compilation of GFCF was broken down in three categories, namely, by type of fixed asset, by kind of activity, and by product. The activity and product breakdown used for GFCF are generally based on those used in the production approach i.e. the A60 and the P.90. GFCF is also subdivided by type of producer, i.e. market (S.11 and S.14) or non-market producers, the latter being further subdivided into central government (S.13111), local government (S.1313), and non-profit institutions serving households (S.15). All this information is presented in an investment matrix which allows various cross-classifications such as; Activities by products, Activities by type of asset, Type of producer by type of asset, Type of producer by type of product, and Activities by type of producer. Changes in inventories are split in the four suggested categories in ESA 2010, i.e. materials and supplies, work-in-progress, finished goods and goods for resale. However, this breakdown is not explicitly shown in the published national accounts, given that only the total figure is published. Proper techniques are used to address specifics of expenditure on GDP compilation: Government final expenditure excludes incidental sales. Expenditure of residents abroad is included in estimates of imports of services. Expenditure of non-residents in the domestic economy is included in exports of services. Volume estimates by category of expenditure are derived using annual chain linking with the year 2020 as the reference year. Specific quarterly compilation techniques. Compilation of quarterly GDP estimates started with 1972, but ESA 2010 figures are available from 1995 onwards. They are compiled using some of the same data sources as the annual estimates plus the infra-annual surveys and administrative data where feasible. Quarterly estimates are seasonally and calendar adjusted (when relevant) using the TRAMO/SEATS method, an ARIMA model-based method. Series at current prices and in chain-linked volumes are available. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Sectoral experts check the data compiled from the main sources for coverage and consistency against other independent sources. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Potential discrepancies in major intermediate data are assessed, and adjustments, using data from alternative sources, are undertaken to address discrepancies. Attention is given to the changes from the previous estimate for the same period and to the consistency between different components of an aggregate. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The Supply and Use Tables (SUT) are not yet available on a yearly basis due to resource constraints. Consequently, the intermediary versions of the national accounts for the subsequent years incorporate the discrepancy into the expenditure estimates. It is, nevertheless disclosed separately internally. The most recent SUT available pertains to the reference year 2019 and was published in December 2024. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions occasioned by major changes in methodology or source data are fully documented and explanatory notes are published. For regular revisions, their magnitude is observed from one period to another, but no explicit studies are undertaken to identify trends or systematic biases. Useful explanations may be drawn by studying the differences between the initial estimates and the revised or final estimates are provided with such explanations. For quarterly accounts, the estimates are revised continuously as more source data, either from annual or previous quarterly surveys, become available. No study of the differences and the impact of these revisions is made to inform the public unless the revisions are major. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses The publications do not document revisions, but users are informed about the methodological aspects underlying production of the new data. Analyses of the differences between the revised and preliminary annual data are not published except in exceptional cases (see also 4.2.2). 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Data that are not routinely disseminated on the website or in the News Release are made available to users on request. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The NSO's website and hardcopy publications list the name of the compiling agency, as well as the name, e-mail addresses, and contact numbers for National Accounts Unit's manager. The publication also identifies the NSO's website, through which users can obtain additional information about the agency or the dataset in question. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs NSO publications can be accessed fully for free on the NSO website.
