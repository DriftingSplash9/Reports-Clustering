 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The balance of payments covers in principle all transactions of government, households, individuals, and enterprises resident in Zimbabwe with nonresidents. The Zimbabwean balance of payments is compiled by the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe (RBZ) together with the Zimbabwe Statistical Agency (ZIMSTAT) and, officially published by ZIMSTAT under the Census and Statistics Act. Data are presented in U.S. dollars, since 2009 when the country adopted the multicurrency regime. The RBZ obtains statistics from a number of institutions and agencies, including the Zimbabwe Revenue Authority (formerly Customs Department), commercial banks, non-bank financial institutions, bureau de change, various ministries and private companies. The data collection system is a combination of administrative records, bank records and surveys. The major players in the compilation of BOP statistics are the Zimbabwe Revenue Authority, the Zimbabwe Statistical Agency (ZIMSTAT) and the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe. Zimbabwe's BOP data are compiled on the basis of the 4th edition of the "Balance of Payments Manual" (BPM4). The Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe is, however, in the process of adopting the guidelines of the 5 th manual (BPM5) and 6 th manual (BPM6). The information is disseminated in the ZIMSTAT Monthly Stats Flash, ZIMSTAT "Quarterly Digest of Statistics" and Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe "Annual Report", "Weekly Economic Highlights" and "Monthly Economic Review". International Investment Position (IIP) is not being compiled. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The current main components (BPM4) include: Current account (goods, services, income, current transfers) Capital account (direct investment, portfolio, other capital, capital transfers) Net errors and omissions Overall balance Reserve assets Scope of the data Geographic Coverage: The geographic territory of reference is the territory of the Republic of Zimbabwe. Unit Coverage: BOP data produced by RBZ covers all resident units, Export Processing Zone entities included, doing transactions with non-residents. Transaction Coverage: The balance of payments covers in principle all transactions of government, households, individuals, and enterprises resident in Zimbabwe with non-residents. Retained earnings and foreign direct investment and portfolio investment are difficult to capture, estimates are used instead. The balance of payments is broadly divided into the current and capital accounts. There is no clear separation between the capital and financial account. Current Account Merchandise trade statistics are compiled by the Zimbabwe Statistical Agency (ZIMSTAT) on the basis of export and import declarations submitted by the Zimbabwe Revenue Authority who, through ASYCUDA, have computerized over 80% of the entry/exit ports in Zimbabwe. For balance of payments purposes, the data are supplemented with and verified from various balance of payments surveys and direct contacts with the major exporters and marketing organizations. Services are divided into transportation, travel, and other services. Data on transportation services are mainly obtained through monthly, quarterly and bi-annual returns, submitted to the RBZ by the major transportation service providers. Shipment services data including freight charges, however, are estimated as a historical average percentage of imports (C.I.F.). Travel services data are mainly obtained from authorized dealers (commercial and merchant banks) and bureaux de change returns. Other services data are mainly obtained from the authorized dealers and include expenditure incurred abroad by government, transactions with residents made by diplomatic missions accredited to Zimbabwe and, payments to non-residents for services provided in Zimbabwe. Income data , covering interest earnings, dividends and profit remittances, insurance and bank charges are obtained from the authorized dealers (commercial and merchant banks) through returns. Data on current transfers such as annual contributions to international organizations, cash transfers, pensions, non-capital grants and workers' remittances are obtained from exchange control records, banks and from the Ministry of Finance. Capital Accounts Capital transfers data are obtained from the Ministry of Finance. Direct investment data are derived - using an average implementation rate - from project approvals by the Zimbabwe Investment Authority (ZIA). Portfolio investment data are sourced from the Zimbabwe Stock Exchange (ZSE). Government foreign loans data are obtained from the Ministry of Finance. Parastatal loan information is sourced from individual parastatals and public entities. Private sector data are sourced from local correspondent banks reported by them to the External Loans Coordination Committee (ELCC). Other financial transactions by banks are obtained from the survey. The balance of payments data are compiled on a calendar year basis by the RBZ and ZIMSTAT. The information is disseminated in the ZIMSTAT Monthly Stats Flash, ZIMSTAT Quarterly Digest of Statistics and Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe Annual Report, in U.S. and Zimbabwe dollars (up to 2008). Currently these bulletins are in U.S. dollars. Exceptions to coverage International Investment Position (IIP) is not yet covered. Unrecorded activity There are no estimates for illegal activities like smuggling and informal transfers by Zimbabweans in the Diaspora. 2.4.1 Valuation Since the adoption of the multicurrency regime in 2009, the balance of payments presented in US dollars. Data reported in other foreign currencies are converted to US dollars using the mid exchange rate on the value date of transaction. Transactions are valued at market prices. Imports data from Zimbabwe Revenue Authority are on a c.i.f. basis and, an adjustment factor of 12 percent is applied to obtain f.o.b. valuation. 2.4.2 Recording basis The time of recording of goods (exports and imports) is at the time of crossing the customs border. Data drawn from the Exchange Control records - mainly for services, compensation of employees, and some private transfers - are recorded at the time of payment. Investment income transactions are on an accrual basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The major sources of statistics for BOP are as follows: Ministry of Finance, Zimbabwe Statistical Agency (ZIMSTAT), Tobacco Industry and Marketing Board (TIMB), Zimbabwe Sugar Sales (ZSS), Zimbabwe Sugar Refineries (ZSR), Cold Storage Company (CSC), Fidelity Printers and Refiners, ZMCC, Chamber of Mines, Zimbabwe Mining Development Company (ZMDC), Zimbabwe Electricity Supply Authority (ZESA), National Oil Company of Zimbabwe (NOCZIM), Zimbabwe Tourism Authority (ZTA), Grain Marketing Board (GMB), Cotton Growers and Ginners Association, Horticultural Promotion Council (HPC) and Ministry of mines. Commercial banks, non-bank financial institutions, bureaux de change, various ministries and private companies are also an important source of balance of payments statistics. The data collection system is a combination of administrative records, bank records and surveys. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Exports and imports of goods Data for exports and imports are recorded on the basis of administrative records i.e. Customs records supplemented by surveys. Merchandise goods include general merchandise, raw materials and equipment, duty free purchases and non-monetary gold. Services Transportation services data are mainly obtained through monthly and quarterly returns submitted to the RBZ by the National Railways of Zimbabwe and airlines operating in Zimbabwe. The returns were supplemented by semi- annual surveys, undertaken by the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe (RBZ) up to 2005. The RBZ is in the process of restarting semi-annual surveys. Travel services data are mainly sourced from records of the Zimbabwe Tourism Authority (ZTA), returns from tourism operators as well as returns from authorized dealers. Other services transactions, including expenditure incurred by government abroad and diplomatic missions accredited to Zimbabwe, are mainly sourced from Exchange Control records. Income Data on compensation of employees are obtained from Exchange Control records. Data on interest earnings, dividends, profit remittances, insurance and bank charges are obtained from daily returns submitted by authorized dealers (e.g. banks) to the RBZ. Data on interest payments are obtained from loan agreements. Information on re-invested equity earnings is not yet available. Current Transfers The treasury department in the Ministry of Finance provides data on official current transfers. Data on other transfers such as; annual contributions to international organizations, cash transfers, pensions, non-capital grants and worker's remittances are obtained from the Exchange Control records and the Ministry of Finance. Capital transfers The main source of information is the domestic and international finance sector of Ministry of Finance. Direct investment Data on foreign direct investment (FDI) are derived - using an average implementation rate - from project approvals by the Zimbabwe Investment Center (ZIA). No information is yet available on the reinvestment of earnings. Portfolio investment Data on portfolio investment are sourced from the Zimbabwe Stock Exchange (ZSE). Other investment Actual information on government long-term capital flows is obtained from the Ministry of Finance. Parastatal loan information is sourced from individual parastatals and public entities. Private sector loans are sourced from correspondent banks and, reported through the local financial institutions to the External Loans Co-ordination Committee (ELCC). Data on other financial transactions by banks are obtained from the monetary survey. Reserve assets Data on reserve assets comprising monetary gold, Special Drawing Rights, reserve position in the Fund and foreign exchange are obtained from the RBZ balance sheets and data on the reserve position in the fund is obtained from the IMF website. Exceptional financing Data on exceptional financing including arrears, debt rescheduling, debt forgiveness and grants/borrowing for BOP purposes are obtained from the Ministry of Finance’s Debt Management Office (DMO), while borrowing for BOP purposes is obtained from RBZ. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Current Account Goods Historical data on merchandise trade are compiled mainly from customs records. For merchandise trade statistics, forms are filled in at the border or designated customs entry points. Exporters and importers fill in forms before goods cross the border and before goods are released, respectively. The data from the various borders or designated customs entry points are aggregated at the Head Office. However, for ex-ante evaluation of the BOP situation, much of the information is obtained from exporting companies and various representative bodies of major export sectors (as estimates before real data become available), which include: The Zimbabwe Tobacco Association (ZTA); Tobacco Industry and Marketing Board (TIMB); The Zimbabwe Chamber of Mines; Minerals Marketing Corporation of Zimbabwe (MMCZ); The Horticultural Promotion Council (HPC); Commercial Farmers' Union (CFU); Ministry of Agriculture; Ministry of Industry and International Trade Other valuable insights are derived from the Crop Forecasting Committee's reports. This committee - comprising of a broad array of representatives from government ministries - is charged with forecasting agricultural production. The Reserve Bank also sends out surveys to key exporters and major importers, such as those for fuel, electricity and grain. These surveys, which are sent semi-annually, usually have a three-year forward-looking range. Services Tourism data are compiled using records from Zimbabwe Tourism Authority (ZTA), returns from tourism players, banks and bureaux de change, immigration data on visitors and arrivals. Consistency checks are carried out between these various sources. On shipment services, assumptions are made, using the CIF or FOB values to derive the freight and insurance components from import and export data. The following is the current convention used: Freight payments = 12% of CIF value of imports. Freight receipts = 2% of the value, exports (FOB) less internal freight Income Data on dividends and profit remittances are compiled using daily records from authorized dealers (commercial and merchant banks). Interest payments on both public and private loans are generated from the scheduled loan agreements, using a data management system (DMFAS). Capital Account · Direct investment (FDI): Figures on foreign direct investment are compiled by the Zimbabwe Investment Authority (ZIA). Where actual figures are not available, ZIA uses figures on project approvals. · Portfolio investment: The Zimbabwe Stock Exchange compiles data on portfolio investment. · Other Capital (loans): With the aid of a computer based debt management system (DMFAS), the Ministry of Finance compiles government debt figures, while the Reserve Bank computes debt statistics of the parastatals and private sector. · Reserve Assets : Data are compiled by the Reserve Bank.
