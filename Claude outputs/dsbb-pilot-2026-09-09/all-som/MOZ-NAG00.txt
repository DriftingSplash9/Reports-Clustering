 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The National Statistics Institute (INE) produces and publishes the National Accounts of Mozambique. The methodology and the conceptual framework for compiling the National Accounts follow the recommendations of the 1993 United Nations System of National Accounts ( SNA 93 ). Compilation is quarterly and annual and covers the following aggregates: Annual: Gross Domestic Product (GDP) using the production approach, 1991-2008 (P), at current prices and constant 2003 prices, detailed for 15 economic activities: farming, livestock and forestry; fishing, aquaculture and related services; mining; manufacturing; electricity and water; construction; trade and repair services; hotels and restaurants; transport and communications; financial services; real estate and services to business; public administration, defense and social security; education; health; other services. Gross Domestic Product (GDP) using the expenditure approach, 1991-2008 (P), at current prices and 2003 constant prices: Private Final Consumption; Government Consumption; Gross Fixed Capital Formation; Changes in inventories; Exports and Imports of Goods and Services. Gross Domestic Product (GDP) using the income approach, 1996-2008, at current prices, detailed for the following categories: Compensation of Employees; Net Mixed Income/Net Operating Surplus; Fixed Capital Consumption; Production Taxes Net of Subsidies; Import Duties. Quarterly: Gross Domestic Product (GDP) using the production approach, 2000-2009 (P), at constant 2003 prices seasonally adjusted and non seasonally adjusted, detailed for 15 economic activities: farming, livestock and forestry; fishing, aquaculture and related services; mining; manufacturing; electricity and water; construction; trade and repair services; hotels and restaurants; transport and communications; financial services; real estate and services to business; public administration, defense and social security; education; health; other services. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Generally speaking, the classifications used are those recommended internationally in the SNA 93 : Classification of Institutional Sectors; International Standard Industrial Classification of All Economic Activities (ISIC Rev.3); Classification of Transactions and Other Flows and Balances; Classification of Individual Consumption According to Purpose (COICOP); Classification of the Functions of Government (COFOG); Harmonized System of International Trade. Product Classification: based on the Central Product Classification (CPC), a National Classification of Goods and Services (CNBS) has been prepared, with 151 products. That classification was the starting point for the entire work of preparing the supply and demand balances by product, which were subsequently aggregated into 27 product groups and 15 industries in the published tables. Scope of the data The National Accounts cover the entire geographic territory of the Republic of Mozambique, including the free zones. All transactions within the production boundary of the SNA, including the informal sector, are covered in principle. Transactions of the informal sector are estimated on the basis of coefficients obtained from the results of the Household Budget Survey ( IAF 2002/3 ), the latest of which relates to 2002-2003 and the informal survey realized in 2004. The National Accounts cover all resident units in Mozambique, including those of the informal sector, that conduct economic transactions. Unrecorded activity Illegal activities (for example drugs) are not measured and are not estimated. 2.4.1 Valuation Production is valued at producers’ prices, because of the difficulty in separating the impact of subsidies, by product and by activity, for calculating production at basic prices. Final consumption and intermediate consumption are calculated at purchasers’ prices. Gross Domestic Product is measured at market prices. Total exports and imports are valued on a f.o.b basis. 2.4.2 Recording basis With the exception of General Government transactions, which are estimated on a cash basis, all transactions are recorded on an accrual basis (flows are recorded at the time economic value is created, transformed, exchanged, transferred or extinguished). The fiscal year in the national accounts coincides with the calendar year. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Production Approach GDP from the production approach is based on various sources of information including surveys, administrative records and indirect estimates for calculating output. Agriculture, livestock and forestry: the major statistical information sources are the livestock registry provided annually by the Livestock Department, reports of Advance Notice on production and yields of seven major crops, information from the Cotton Institute; the Cashew Development Institute, and the Household Survey for 2002/2003 and the results of the agricultural census (CAP 1999/2000). Fishing : the report of the Fisheries Ministry on fish landings provide statistical information for both commercial and traditional fishing activity. Mining : mining statistics are derived primarily from reports of the Ministry of Mineral Resources on production and sales in both the internal and external markets, and prices for major mining products as well as monthly and annual surveys on the gas extraction enterprise. Manufacturing : the main sources are the monthly and annual industry survey conducted by the INE, In the case of Mozambique, industrial production units of the household sector cover all unincorporated units with fewer than 10 workers, producing goods and services for the market, including output for own final use, primarily through subsistence production. Electricity and water : data are taken, respectively, from reports of the Public Electricity Company of Mozambique and the Provincial Water Companies, which provide information on water billings. These are the only utilities operating in Mozambique in their respective areas. Electricity production estimates include both legal and illegal consumption, since the source data include total production, sales, losses, exports and imports. Construction : the data on occupation by activity from the 2002/2003 Household Survey and the General Population and Housing Census of 1997 provide information for estimating the base year. Other data sources on the sector include the report on roads and bridges from the Ministry of Public Works and Housing, the Reports of the State Properties Corporation (APIE) on building repairs, and the annual construction survey of the INE. Information on traditional construction is estimated from the results of the 2002/2003 Household Survey and the 1997 General Population and Housing Census, projected for following years using population growth rates and other appropriate indicators. Trade and repair services : data are provided by the reports of the Ministry of Trade. The final total value of output from trade is derived from the balancing tables of supply and demand, by product. Hotels and restaurants : the INE conducts monthly and annual surveys of hotels and restaurants. Administrative data from annual reports of the Ministry of Tourism are also used. Transport and communications : data are taken from the annual financial statements of public and private enterprises, and reports of the Ministry of Transport, covering freight handling, passenger transport (land, air, and see) and oil pipelines. Financial services . The sources are: financial statements from all banks operating in the financial system, obtained through the Central Bank; balance sheets, audits and financial statements obtained directly from insurance companies; and balance of payments data prepared by the Central Bank. Property rental . Reports of the State Properties Corporation (APIE) constitute the main source of data. The volume index is derived from the 2002/2003 Household Budget Survey, extrapolated for following years on the basis of population growth and the consumer price index. Government, defense and social security . Government Budget Execution Reports and the Government General Account ( Conta Geral do Estado ) , covering central and provincial governments, as well as the report of the National Social Security Institute. Off-budget revenues and expenditures are not included. Health and education . The General State Budget, the Government General Account and reports of the respective ministries are the main source of data on health and education. The ministry reports include information on public and private activities, while the 2002/2003 Household Budget Survey provides information for the base year. Other services . This category covers other community, social and personal services, and families with domestic employees. The source is the 2002/2003 Household Budget Survey; Non profit Institutions Serving Households Survey. Import duties . Information on customs duties and import taxes comes from the National Directorate of Taxation and Audit, compared and adjusted against information calculated from customs import data. Expenditure Approach Final consumption expenditure . For the base year, the 2002-2003 IAF provides information on household income and expenditure. For years not covered by household budget surveys, this category of expenditure is estimated as a residual on the basis of forecasts of final resources and uses. Government consumption expenditure . Data from the Government General Account and Budget Execution reports. Gross Capital Formation . Information is compiled by industry, by type of asset. For certain farm products, such as grains, information is available on stocks. For the base year, the 2002/2003 IAF also provides data on construction and housing rehabilitation, purchases of durable goods and valuables. Imports and Exports of Goods and Services . Customs data processed by the INE: balance of payments data prepared by the Central Bank; and sector reports from firms and government. Income Approach For each category, information is obtained from the following sources: Compensation of employees . General Budget Execution and Government General Account, for compensation of workers in the general government. For other components, workers’ compensation is estimated, primarily on the basis of annual and monthly business surveys, the report of the National Social Security Institute, and reports by banks and insurance companies. The business register makes it possible to identify and calculate the personnel employed by companies. Employment in the household institutional sector results from the difference between total employment and employment in other sectors. Compensation paid by households was obtained by multiplying average compensation from the 02/03 IAF by the number of persons employed in household units, from the General Population Census of 1997. Mixed Income/Operating Surplus. For formal businesses, profit data are derived from financial statements; for households, the 02/03 IAF provides data on income and receipts from productive household activities. Production and export taxes net of subsidies . Data from the Government General Budget Execution Reports, and information on taxes and subsidies from the Directorate of Audit and Taxation. Quarterly Accounts Quarterly GDP from the production approach is mainly based on surveys and administrative data (in most cases reports from the Ministries). 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording 3.1.3 Source data timeliness 3.2.1 Source data assessment 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The process of compiling the national accounts for Mozambique begins with identifying sources of information, obtained from surveys, censuses, administrative records and special studies. The intermediate data are then aggregated and converted into national accounts transactions, through a process of reconciliation based on several levels of adjustment, to obtain final values consistent with national accounting concepts. In calculating estimates of GDP, the production approach is favored in annual accounts. In Quarterly accounts the GDP is compiled only by the production approach. Production Approach Calculations of GDP from the production approach begin with the preparation of resources and use tables for 151 products, based on information obtained from the sources listed in Section IV. Farming, Livestock and Forestry . For the seven most important agricultural products, values at constant prices are estimated directly from quantity data obtained from the above-mentioned sources. Those sources provide not only physical data on the quantities of livestock products, but also data on production, processing, marketing and export prices for forestry projects. The quantities of meat marketed are converted into live-animal terms, the basis of reference for measuring livestock production, using technical coefficients obtained from the Ministry of Agriculture. In the case of products for which no information is available, base-year values are extrapolated for the following years, using approximate indicators, an index for similar products, or the population growth rate, depending on the nature of the product. For commercial production, intermediate consumption was estimated with coefficients taken from studies by the Ministry of Agriculture on production costs by type of farm product and livestock species, including the principal inputs. For households, the coefficients were adjusted in light of producer practices in this institutional sector. Fishing. Source data are used to extrapolate values for the base year derived from the 02/03 household survey. Processed fishery products that constitute intermediate consumption for the traditional fishery are converted into fresh products using technical coefficients taken from studies by the Fishery Directorate. Intermediate consumption was calculated with accounting data from a sample of firms by economic activity, provided by the Taxation and Audit Directorate of the Ministry of Planning and Finance. Mining. The values for the base year are extrapolated using appropriate indices derived from quantities and prices contained in annual and quarterly reports from the sector. This category includes production of sand, estimated as a constant proportion of the supply of cement, assuming that use of this product is directly related with the use of cement for construction. In the absence of direct data on intermediate consumption, a technical coefficient derived from international statistics was used. Manufacturing. The base-year values were derived from annual and monthly business surveys and extrapolated on the basis of price and quantity indices derived from current surveys. The base-year values for units with fewer than 10 workers were derived from the 2002/2003 Household Budget Survey and projected for subsequent years on the basis of the growth rate of the population that is considered the major consumer of output from this sector. When there is no specific information, it is assumed that the informal sector behaves in a manner similar to the formal sector. Intermediate consumption was calculated using the sample of accounting information provided by the Taxation and Audit Directorate. Electricity and Water. Since physical quantities, value and price data are available; the value of electricity production is estimated as the invoiced value. Intermediate consumption is derived from information taken from balance sheets and financial statements. The values of piped water production are calculated with accounting information from the Maputo Water Enterprise and production quantity indicators and information from provincial distributors. Intermediate consumption of these services was calculated using direct data from the Maputo Water Enterprise, and provincial utility data. For non-piped water services, only value added is considered, and not intermediate consumption costs. Construction. Calculation of construction output is based on information on the supply of construction materials (cement) and traditional construction (government investment expenditures). The value of output is based on technical input-output coefficients taken from construction surveys. The most important item of reference is the supply of cement, from which are calculated values for other basic materials used in cement construction; these are then compared to the respective values from the supply side. The final estimate also includes the value of services, compensation and operating surplus, using coefficients from the sample of businesses provided by the Taxation and Audit Directorate. The gross value of new household construction for own use is estimated from the number of people working for their own account, and information extrapolated from the 2002/2003 IAF. Trade and Repair Services. Trade and transportation margins, which are taken as the production of this industry and the transportation industry, are estimated from the difference between the value at purchasers' prices and the value at producers' prices for all goods and services that were transacted in the economy during the reference period. The value of transportation costs, which must be deducted from this differential and assigned to output of the transportation industry, is calculated using fixed coefficients for each product category, estimated in previous studies of commercial enterprises. The final total value of trade results from the preparation of supply and demand balances by product. In order to distinguish business production from household production, an employment indicator was used, based on data from the business sample of the Taxation and Audit Directorate in the first case, and information from the 2002/03 IAF for the household sector. Hotels and Restaurants. Values for the base year 2003 are extrapolated on the basis of quarterly and annual hotel surveys, which provide information on the number of guests, bed-nights, revenues from accommodation and expenditures, showing compensation among other items. This information is supplemented with data on the most important restaurants, and adjusted on the basis of the travel and tourism item in the balance of payments. Transport and Communications. For the base year, commercial output was estimated from the number of licensed units registered in the Ministry of Transport, combined with average output per unit extrapolated using data from current surveys. For unrecorded activity, base-year values derived from the household surveys were extrapolated using studies compiled from small-scale surveys targeted at particular drivers to determine their frequency of work, average daily or monthly income, and production costs. Total production for the transport industry, estimated in this way, is reconciled with the value of transport margins resulting from the above-mentioned practice for “trade and repair services.” Financial Services and Insurance . In practice, production of financial services and insurance is estimated in the same way as the respective institutional sector. Current values both for production and for intermediate consumption were deflated with indices drawn from the consumer price index. This industry includes an estimate of informal trading by the household sector, calculated from data on people working for their own account in auxiliary financial services, which are assumed to correspond to the individual traders of foreign exchange. Property Rentals . This category distinguishes actual rental by property owners (lessors) to other agents (tenants) from imputed rent on owner-occupied properties in urban and rural areas. Values at current prices are deflated using the consumer price index. Government, Defense and Social Security . The production of non-market services by government is estimated by costs, compensation, including wages in-kind, and other public expenditures. Education and Health. The base-year values for private education and health services are estimated on the basis of data from the 2002/2003 IAF, which provide information on educational fees and on expenditure for medical consultations, in both formal and traditional health services. For estimating intermediate consumption of the private component, coefficients derived from government activity were used, extrapolated with the population growth rate. Other Services (Community, Social and Personal). In addition to the information from the accounting sample of businesses from the Taxation and Audit Directorate, production values for the base year are extracted from the 1996/97 IAF, including information on employment and compensation in these activities. For following years, estimates are extrapolated using the population growth rate or other appropriate indicators. Other Aspects: Accounts of economic activity have as their main source the balances by product. The aggregation of typical products constitutes the industry in question. On the basis of this information, three production matrices are constructed: for production by corporations (financial and nonfinancial) and government, for marketed household production, and for household own-use production. The latter two matrices represent “pure” categories, assuming that there is no secondary production in the activities of the household sector. The production records for each product calculated in the balances are in principle considered as the total of output produced in the country. This product output may be allocated to production of a single industry (typical output) or it may be attributed to the industry of which it is the typical product and to other industries as secondary output. Consequently, the production value of the product balance is considered as the total produced by all industries. Each balance feeds into a column vector of the production matrix (industry-product). The Expenditure Approach The production approach is central to the process of estimating GDP, the value of which is fixed for the expenditure approach. Estimates of different expenditure components are based on the data sources referred to in Section IV. Final Household Consumption Expenditure. Household consumption expenditure is estimated for the base year from the 2002/2003 IAF, annualized for 2003 using the CPI disaggregated by products. In the following years, final consumption expenditure is in principle calculated using “purchasing power indices”, a reference indicator the influence of which on the balance is analyzed from the perspective of the general balance for the product; this means that this component of expenditure is actually estimated as a residual. Government Consumption Expenditure, Gross Capital Formation, and Imports and Exports of Goods and Services. Starting with the sources listed for each component, annual estimates are made, based on analysis of product flows or the balancing items from supply and use tables. The values are deflated using the appropriate component of the CPI and other indicators. In cases where the CPI component cannot be identified, the total CPI is used. The Income Approach Under the income approach, GDP is equal to Compensation of Employees plus Gross Operating Surplus/Mixed Income plus Taxes Net of Subsidies on production and export. The income-approach GDP for Mozambique, at current prices, is published with a breakdown into these components: Gross Value Added for the whole economy, Compensation, Production Taxes Net of Subsidies and Net Operating Surplus/Mixed Income. Compensation for the base year was estimated using employment by type of economic activity from the 1997 General Population and Housing Census, together with information on average compensation for worker from the 2002/03 IAF. For subsequent years, employment values are extrapolated from indices of changes in employment derived from the monthly labor force survey, while wages and salaries are determined from statistical information gathered through annual business surveys, disaggregated at the single-digit level of the ISIC Rev. 3. For specific activities where there is direct information, such as electricity, communications, port services, financial services and general government, the respective accounting data were incorporated into the corresponding activities, either for the base year or for current years. The proportion of compensation to social contributions is compiled using data from the National Social Security Institute. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Reconciliations The reconciliation process consists of comparing the results of the adjusted basic data from business accounts with the results of the national accounts. This process is also known as verifying the vertical balance, and includes three phases: (i) assessing saving in the national accounts against the saving obtained from business accounts data, using financial statements for the year; (ii) analyzing financial flows in the national accounts and the business balance sheets; (iii) explaining discrepancies in the National Accounts with data from business accounts. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data There are two types of discrepancies: those that relate to common variables of the integrated economic accounts and the table of resources and uses; and those that relate to common variables between the tables for production, intermediate consumption and the components of value added. All of these discrepancies are eliminated through a process of adjusting the worksheets: the final decision results from an analysis from the viewpoint of industry or product dimension, as well as the type of transaction and the sectors involved in it. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Discrepancies are corrected by identifying the specific business accounting records, adjusting national accounts transactions in the adjustment columns, so as to reduce discrepancies, and recording transactions that were not previously identified in the process of compiling each transaction in the National Accounts. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses 5.1.5 Dissemination on request
