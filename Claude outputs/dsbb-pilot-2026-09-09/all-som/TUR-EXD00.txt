 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The data are generally compiled in accordance with the concepts, definitions, and methodology specified in External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users Manual 2013 as well as the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6). Definition: Türkiye's external debt shows the debt liabilities of all institutional sectors to the rest of the world. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization External debt is classified in accordance with the standard components of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) and the External Debt Guide. Data on intercompany loans under direct investment relationships are separately identified. 2.2.1 Scope External debt is classified in accordance with the standard components of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) and the External Debt Guide. Data on intercompany loans under direct investment relationships are separately identified. Scope of the data The scope of the data is broadly consistent with guidelines outlined in the External Debt Guide and BMP6. External Debt statistics attempt to cover all of Türkiye's foreign liabilities, together with all related transactions. All institutional sectors are covered. Private sector long-term external debt shows the liabilities of private banks, institutions and households to the rest of the world. Geographical coverage: The geographic coverage corresponds to the national economic territory excluding the free trade zones. Exceptions to coverage Debt of residents issued in domestic market is not included in external debt if owned by non-residents and debt of residents issued in foreign market is not excluded from external debt if owned by residents. 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to unit of account: External Debt statistics are disseminated in U.S. dollars. The transactions denominated in other currencies are converted into U.S. dollars using the reporting date’s exchange rates. Valuation: All data are calculated on the basis of the face value obtained from the records. 2.4.2 Recording basis Gross external debt stock of Türkiye is measured at face value. This means, in particular, that gross external debt stock is not affected by changes in market yields, and excludes accrued interest. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures All external debt is recorded on a gross basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources: The data base External Financing Information System (EFIS) which is designed and developed by the Ministry of Treasury and Finance is broadly sufficient to compile the public sector long-term external debt statistics. Short-term loans and private sector long-term debt data for debt securities, trade credits, loans, currency and deposits, intercompany loans are collected by the CBRT through detailed monthly reports of the banks reported on behalf of themselves and their clients. Short-term trade credits are based on trade statistics and estimations. Intercompany lending: Liabilities to affiliated enterprises and liabilities to direct investors recorded in are also collected through detailed reports of banks and the data are included in the external debt statistics. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data are broadly consistent with the definitions, scope, classifications, and time of recording of external debt data and future external debt-service payment schedules relate only to outstanding debt, excluding any payments on undisbursed amounts. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness In order to get the information on Treasury guaranteed or non-guaranteed external loans on time, there are regulations about registration, accounting and budgeting principles and procedures of an external loan of public institution. Those regulations are available at the website of the Ministry of Treasury and Finance for the public information. The data providers to the Central Bank are made aware of the deadlines set for data reporting and they are contacted to ensure the timely receipt of the data. 3.2.1 Source data assessment In order not to make an erroneous entry into the debt database, transaction information is received from both creditor and debtor of a loan, which may be Treasury guaranteed or non-guaranteed, and these data are compared to each other to achieve the data accuracy. Article 43 of the CBRT Law authorizes the Central Bank to investigate and supervise the accuracy of the information provided from the aforementioned institutions and to request additional information and documents. Within this framework the CBRT, when necessary, may contact directly the debtors to get detailed information about their external liabilities to maintain the accuracy of the external debt data. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Generally there are no statistical adjustments in coverage, classification, timing, and valuation of data except for reporting errors or stock corrections. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses In case of a privatization of a public sector institution, the institutional sector is revised beginning from the date of the privatization in the external debt data. The information of the names and the privatization dates of the public institutions are available in the web site of the Privatization Administration. In addition, there is an explanatory note available at the web explains the data monitoring and dissemination process of privatized institutions. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished data are not disseminated on request to individuals except that the questions and demands about external debt received within the context of The Law of Acquiring Knowledge are formally answered by the Ministry of Treasury and Finance and/or CBRT. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The data file also contains contact information for the knowledgeable support service from both the CBRT and theMinistry of Treasury and Finance, responsible institutions of compiling external debt. E-mail addresses and telephone numbers of the EDP compilers are given within the data for users' needs and questions in detail. In addition, questions and demands about external debt received within the context of The Law of Acquiring Knowledge are formally answered by the Ministry of Treasury and Finance and/or the CBRT.
