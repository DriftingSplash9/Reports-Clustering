 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions - The method for estimating total output at constant prices follows the recommendations from the UNSD and other international organizations, also approved by the a NSOM Chairman Order of 1998. - The method for calculating the industrial production index is based on the United Nations manual “Index Numbers of Industrial Production 2008”, recommendations by a Regional Advisor, Statistics Division, UNESCAP, and methods of other countries, was approved by a NSOM Chairman Order No 01/132 of 09 October, 2008. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification used for compiling the data is the Mongolian Industrial Classification (4-digit level), which is an adaptation of the International Standard Industrial Classification, rev. 3.1 (ISIC, rev.3.1). The estimates are published at the ISIC 2-digit and at selected 3-digit levels. 2.2.1 Scope The classification used for compiling the data is the Mongolian Industrial Classification (4-digit level), which is an adaptation of the International Standard Industrial Classification, rev. 3.1 (ISIC, rev.3.1). The estimates are published at the ISIC 2-digit and at selected 3-digit levels. Scope of the data All large units (with 10 and more employees) are covered. Exceptions to coverage Coverage of the smaller units, which account for about 2% of total production, is incomplete. 2.4.1 Valuation Production and sales are valued at producer prices. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The estimates for the 1995-2002 period are compiled from responses to the monthly Production of Industry Survey of industrial enterprises, which covers all large units (with 10 and more employees). Coverage of the smaller units, which account for about 2% of total production, is incomplete. The survey lists are derived from the Business Register of legal entities supplemented by local intelligence. Survey coverage was gradually improved during the period, particularly as a result of the 1998 Establishment Census. Adjustments are made for changes in coverage between survey periods. The monthly Production of Industry survey collects information on total production, total revenue from all sources, sales from main activity (manufacturing, mining, utilities), of which domestic and foreign sales and numbers employed (total and main activity), as well as quantities and values produced for about 210-230 major products, for the current month and, on a cumulative basis, for the year-to-date period. 3.2.1 Source data assessment NSOM officers are review the data coverage, cumulative amount, average prices, and product codes. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The constant price estimates of gross industrial output for each industry class are derived as the sum of the goods and services produced by that industry, expressed at base year prices (by multiplying the current period quantities of the specified products by their respective 2000 unit prices and deflating the other (unspecified) group with the consumer price index). 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The estimates are not seasonally adjusted.
