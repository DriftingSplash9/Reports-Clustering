 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The national accounts of Mauritania are compiled according to the recommendations of the 2008 edition of the United Nations System of National Accounts (SNA 2008). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The sectoral classification used is an adaptation for the Mauritanian economy of the International Standard Industrial Classification of All Economic Activities (ISIC, Rev. 2). The classification consists of: (1) the rural sector (agriculture, forestry and lumbering/livestock/artisanal fishing), (2) mining, (3) manufacturing (processed fishing/other manufacturing industries/arts and crafts), (4) construction, (5) transportation and telecommunications, (6) commerce, restaurants, and hotels, (7) other services, and (9) [sic] the nonmarket government sector. Scope of the data Geographic coverage: the geographic coverage of the accounts is the national territory of Mauritania, to which is added the Mauritanian territorial enclaves abroad (embassies), and from which the foreign territorial enclaves in Mauritania (embassies and representation missions of international organizations) are subtracted. Transaction coverage: all transactions that are for goods and services, including the output of agricultural households for their own personal consumption, are covered. Unit coverage: the national accounts cover the economic activity of all units resident in Mauritania. Each year the Economic and National Accounts Statistics Directorate publishes an edition of the provisional accounts for the year n-1 and final accounts for the year n-2: There is a table that indicates GDP Uses at current prices. National savings are not calculated. Since April 2019, the national accounts have been prepared using the 2008 System of National Accounts (2008 SNA). A series of final accounts from 2005 to 2016 is published according to the 2008 SNA. Provisional accounts are also published in June and revised each year in December. They are prepared according to two approaches (use and production) according to branch of economic activity and at constant prices, base year 2014. The main sources of data used to prepare the national accounts are the corporate tax returns (DSF) of businesses, the annual survey of households and farmers (EMEA), data on the size of herds, data on artisanal and industrial fishing, data on the automobile stock and on the consumption and price of diesel oil, the ongoing survey on household living conditions (EPCV), the employment and informal sector survey (ENESI), the financial statements of companies in the modern sector, the table of government revenue and expenditure, the report on the execution of the government Consolidated Capital Budget (BCI), and the balance of payments. Exceptions to coverage Fishing activities from ships that are not resident in the exclusive economic zone (EEZ) of Mauritania are not included in the coverage of output (and related catches are not shown in exports). Unrecorded activity Some informal sector activities are not covered, such as the slaughter of cattle, the mining of construction materials (gravel, sand), the mining of salt, the processing of grains, etc. Some informal sector activities, such as those of exchange bureaus are also poorly monitored. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation methods use base prices for resources and purchase prices for uses. Imports are valued c.i.f., and exports are valued f.o.b. The valuation period for the national accounts is the calendar year (January 1-December 31). 2.4.2 Recording basis The time of recording is the same as in accrual accounting, except as regards government tax revenue, which is recorded on a cash basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The principal sources of data used for compiling the national accounts are the annual agricultural survey of households and farmers (EMEA), the data on the size of herds, the data on artisanal and industrial fishing, the data on the automobile stock and on the consumption and price of diesel oil, the 2000 survey on household living conditions (EPCV), the financial documents of enterprises in the modern sector, the table of government revenue and expenditure, the report on the execution of the government Consolidated Capital Budget (BCI), and the balance of payments. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Output approach: Agriculture: The data are obtained from the Agricultural Statistics Division of the Ministry of Rural Development and the Environment (MDRE) and are based on the results of the annual agricultural survey of households and farmers (EMEA). Livestock : Livestock data are estimated and supplied by the Department of Livestock and Agriculture of the MDRE. These data are compiled on the basis of herd offtake rates and estimates of herd sizes. Fishing: The Department of Halieutic Studies, Development, and Research (DEARH) provides data on catches from artisanal and industrial fishing. Forestry and lumbering: The data are estimated, based on the results of the survey on household living conditions (EPCV), which provides details on the consumption of wood and charcoal. Mining : The financial documents of the public iron mining company (SNIM) are available and provide the necessary data. Manufacturing : The sources used are the financial documents of enterprises that are available at the General Department of Taxes or at the Department of Industry. Arts and Crafts: There is no reliable source of information available for this sector. Energy: Electricity and water data are obtained from the companies responsible for the production and distribution of water (SNDE) and electricity (SOMELEC). Construction : The data used for this sector are the Consolidated Capital Budget (BCI) and the report on BCI execution, for work carried out in the public sector; and the tax returns of construction companies, for work done in the modern private sector. The amount of credit granted to construction sector enterprises is also taken into account. Transportation and telecommunications : The National Federation of Transportation provides data on the automobile stock. The Department of Energy provides data on the consumption and price of diesel oil. The declarations of wages paid by the local agencies of foreign airlines are also used. For telecommunications, the regulatory authority provides data on the mobile telephony companies (Mattel and Mauritel-Mobile). The financial accounts of these two companies, which are available at the General Department of Taxes (DGI), are also analyzed. The public mail company (Mauripost) provides data on mail activities. The cable-controlled telephony company (Mauritel) also provides the number of telephone booths managed by the private sector. Commerce, hotels – bars – restaurants: The only data obtained are the financial documents available at the DGI. Other market services: The sources used are the financial documents of enterprises, collected directly from the enterprises in question (in the case of financial corporations) or consulted at the DGI (in the case of nonfinancial corporations). Nonmarket services : The data used are obtained from the table of government revenue and expenditure compiled by the Department of Budget (Ministry of Finance). Taxes and fees: The data on taxes and fees come from the same source. Expenditure approach: Foreign trade: The source used is the balance of payments compiled by the Central Bank of Mauritania (BCM), which provides mainly import and export figures for goods and services. GFCF: The source used for estimating GFCF is the report on BCI execution prepared by the Ministry of Economic and Development Affairs (MAED). Changes in stocks: The only source used is the data on changes in stocks reported by the iron mining company (SNIM). Final consumption: The final consumption of government departments is calculated from the data supplied by the Ministry of Finance on government expenditure. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques For estimates of the value-added of each sector, it is assumed that the annual change in value-added is the same as for output (the rates of intermediate consumption having been set). Informal sector data are not really included in the accounts. An indirect estimation method is used to capture the rest of the modern sector that is not recorded, based on a list that allows for extrapolation of the data collected for samples of enterprises. Output approach: Agriculture: Net grain outputs, to which are added the outputs of dates and vegetables, are estimated at producer’s prices. The rate of intermediate consumption is assumed to be constant. Livestock : Output of livestock, milk, and skins is established on the basis of numbers provided by the Ministry responsible for livestock, zootechnical parameters that are considered fixed (growth rate, offtake rate, number of females, average lactation per female), and wholesale prices for live animals. Fishing: The outputs of artisanal fishing and industrial fishing are calculated on the basis of quantities and prices reported by the DEARH. The values-added are estimated on the basis of intermediate consumption rates that are assumed to be fixed (different for the two types of fishing). Forestry and lumbering: Output is estimated on the basis of the wood and charcoal consumption of rural population groups. The rate of intermediate consumption is assumed to be constant. Mining : Output and value-added are estimated directly on the basis of the financial documents of the SNIM. Manufacturing: Changes in the value-added of this sector are estimated on the basis of a sample of enterprises whose financial accounts are available at the DGI and whose share of the sector is assumed to be constant. Arts and crafts: The value-added of this sector at current prices is assumed to evolve at the same rate as that of the manufacturing industries. The deflator used is the consumer price index of the “Equipment” group. Energy: Output and value-added are estimated directly on the basis of the financial documents of the electricity and water distribution companies. Construction : This is broken down into two sectors: organized and nonorganized. The value-added of the organized sector is estimated on the basis of the construction expenditure executed in the BCI, the financial accounts of construction companies available at the DGI, and the amount of credit granted to construction companies by the banking sector. The value-added of the nonorganized sector is assumed to represent a fixed portion (40 percent) of the value-added of the organized sector. Transportation and telecommunications : The value-added of ground transport changes in terms of volume in the same way as the consumption of diesel oil and in terms of price, in the same way as the price of diesel oil. The financial accounts of air transport and sea transport companies (including port activities), which are available at the DGI, facilitate the estimation of the value-added of these companies. The value-added of the local agencies of foreign airlines is assumed to change in the same way as the wage bill they distribute. The value-added of telecommunications enterprises is estimated on the basis of the data shown in their financial accounts. The value-added of telephone booths managed by the private sector is estimated on the basis of an assumption (constant) of average value-added. Commerce, hotels – bars – restaurants: For the formal sector, the value-added is estimated on the basis of the financial accounts of enterprises available at the DGI, extrapolated to the entire sector, based on the number of known enterprises and the average value-added per enterprise. For the informal sector, the estimates are based on an estimation of the number of enterprises and on an average turnover (or an average margin) per enterprise. Nonmarket services : The value-added of financial corporations is calculated directly, based on the financial accounts of the BCM, the commercial banks, and insurance companies. For other enterprises, the value-added is estimated on the basis of the financial accounts of enterprises available at the DGI, extrapolated to the whole sector on the basis of the number of enterprises known and the average value-added per enterprise. Taxes and fees: The amounts used for the calculation of GDP correspond to fees on goods and services and on international trade, such as shown in the government tax revenue data. Expenditure approach: Foreign trade: The figures used for imports and exports are taken directly from the balance of payments, which is compiled by the BCM in the manner recommended in the fourth edition of the Balance of Payments Manual . GFCF: The GFCF amount for the public sector is provided by the MAED. The GFCF for the private sector is assumed to be a fixed portion of government GFCF. Changes in stocks: The changes in stocks taken into account are only those related to live animals (growth not taken off), the value of which is calculated by the ONS, and those reported by the iron mining company (SNIM). Final consumption: The final consumption of government departments is calculated on the basis of data provided by the Ministry of Finance on its expenditure. Private final consumption data are obtained by balance. None
