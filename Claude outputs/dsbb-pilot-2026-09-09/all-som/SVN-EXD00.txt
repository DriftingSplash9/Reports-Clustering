 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical Framework: The gross external debt of Slovenia is compiled by the Bank of Slovenia. Data are available from 1994 onwards. Methodology of gross external debt is based on the IMF's External debt statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users and on the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual. Definitions: Gross external debt of Slovenia is the outstanding amount of those actual, current, and not contingent, liabilities that require payment(s) of principal and/or interest by the debtor at some point(s) in the future and that are owed to nonresidents by residents of Slovenia. Gross external debt consists of debt liabilities from international investment position (IIP). Short term debt is debt repayable at notice or with an original maturity of one year or less. Long-term debt is defined as debt with an original maturity of over one year or with no maturity stated. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: Gross external debt position is disaggregated by institutional sector (general government, monetary authorities, banks and other sectors), by original maturity (short-term and long-term) and by type of debt instrument (debt securities, loans, trade credits, currency and deposits, other debt liabilities). Intercompany lending between entities in a direct investment relationship is separately presented. Scope of the data Coverage of the external debt: All data in accordance with the IMF classification are included. Data on the gross external debt cover the entire economy of Slovenia. 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to unit of account: The official central bank's (Bank of Slovenia) exchange rate at the end of reference period is used to covert debt liabilities to EUR. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Primary Sources: Data sources for gross external debt of Slovenia are the same as those for debt liabilities in the international investment position. Debt securities: Liabilities of debt securities of government, banking and other sectors are available from Central Securities Clearing Corporation and from the reports VRP (operations in securities reported by brokerage houses, banks and investment companies) as well as central bank' s accounting department's data. Assets of debt securities are covered in VRP report, central bank's accounting department's data and SHSS statistics (data on securities holdings in the Eurosystem - used since 2015 to cover data gaps). Sources for trade credits, advances, loans and other debt liabilities as well as currency and deposits of non-banking sectors are monthly reporting forms (KRD report, previously divided into two reports – KRD and SKV). Bilateral data from ECB database on loans of households borrowed from the banks abroad are included from 2012 onwards. Currency & deposits and loans of banking sector are derived directly from PORFI – a system of statistical reports for commercial banks. Central bank's data are derived directly from accounting department's data. The allocation of SDRs to IMF members is shown as a liability incurred by the recipient under SDRs. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Generally there are no statistical adjustments in coverage, classification, timing, and valuation of data.
