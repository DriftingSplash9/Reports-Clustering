 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions In principle, the concepts, definitions and procedures used in the compilation of the line items in the balance of payments are based on the standards specified in the Sixth Edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification of the balance of payments for Mauritius is consistent with the main aggregates of the BPM6. 2.2.1 Scope The classification of the balance of payments for Mauritius is consistent with the main aggregates of the BPM6. Scope of the data In principle, only transactions between residents and nonresidents are covered in the balance of payments statistics. Effective 2010Q1, a major data coverage shortcoming was addressed. The BoPs include the cross-border transactions of Global Business License Holders (GBLH) and is further supplemented with the results of the Foreign Assets and Liabilities Survey (FALS) and External Sector Statistics-National Account (ESSNAC) survey. The BoPs data cover the entire economy to a large extent. Transaction coverage: Statistics Mauritius covers all merchandise entering and leaving the country (all exporters and importers are included). Cross-border services data include all those that go through banks and are complemented by transport surveys (domestic and foreign shipping and airline companies). Effective October 2025, GBC services exports and imports, estimated from ESSNAC survey results have been incorporated in the balance of payments statistics for the quarters 2018Q1 onwards. Income data, effective 2010Q1 include reinvested earnings, which are culled from surveys. Current transfers, private, relate to cross-border transactions effected through banks while government current transfers are obtained from the MoFEPD. Transactions of banks, general government, and public corporations are fully covered while those of the other financial and non-financial corporations are covered to a large extent. With regard to the household sector, only transactions effected through banks are recorded. Geographical coverage: The BOP includes transactions in relation to all countries of the world. Exceptions to coverage The only exception to the present coverage is the cross-border transactions of Authorised Companies. Unrecorded activity The BOP does not contain any estimate made for illegal activity and for other transactions for which data are not available. 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to unit of account: The BOP is compiled in millions of Mauritian rupees; transactions are converted from other currencies to Mauritian rupee using the exchange rate prevailing at the time of the transaction. When the actual date of the transaction is not available, the average exchange rate for the reporting period is used. Some transactions are derived from the statements on the external position; adjustments are made to remove valuation and 'other volume' changes. Valuation: Transactions are generally recorded at market prices. If the market price (value) is not known, book or historical value is used. Imports are adjusted from a c.i.f. basis to a f.o.b. basis. 2.4.2 Recording basis Transactions culled from the ITRS are on a cash basis. Imports and exports are recorded at the time they go through customs. Recording of income: Income on the reserve assets of central bank and interest income on banks' and the corporate sector foreign assets and liabilities are recorded on an accrual basis. Income on government credits and dividend income on direct and portfolio investment are recorded on a settlement basis. Loan drawdowns and repayments are recorded at the time of settlement. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The current and capital accounts, for the most part, record transactions on a gross basis while the financial account transactions are recorded net of repatriation. However, the following current account transactions are recorded on a net, rather than gross basis: (i) those services transactions or income transactions that are subject to withholding taxes, as the data source is the banking records, so that the payments are recorded net of withholding taxes, instead of gross, as recommended in BPM6; and (ii) those intercompany transactions that are settled on a net basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The main data sources are Statistics Mauritius (merchandise trade statistics f.o.b./c.i.f., bunkers and fuel), Ministry of Finance (official transfers and external debt of the general government and public financial and non-financial institutions), enterprise surveys relating to transport (freight, insurance, passenger fares), banking records (services, income on direct and portfolio investment as well as direct, portfolio and other investment) and the financial statements of banks and Bank of Mauritius. Effective 2010Q1, the balance of payments data have been complemented by the ESSNAC Survey and FALS for income and financial account transactions mostly. Merchandise trade Goods: The main data source is Statistics Mauritius, which compiles international trade statistics on the basis of trade returns received from the Customs Department of Mauritius Revenue Authority. Goods exported are valued on a f.o.b. basis and goods imported on a c.i.f. anf f.o.b. basis. Data on goods reflect those in the national accounts statistics. Goods procured in ports: These are included as part of merchandise trade in accordance with BPM6. Data on goods procured in Mauritian ports by foreign airline and shipping companies are obtained from Statistics Mauritius, and data on goods procured in ports abroad by Mauritian airline and shipping companies are obtained from airline and shipping companies through quarterly surveys conducted by the Bank. Services Transport : The data sources are the quarterly survey of airline and shipping companies, banking records and Statistics Mauritius. Transport credits measure receipts by the domestic carriers from passenger fares and freight on export, other port disbursement and income earned from postal and courier services. Transport debits measure payments made to non-resident carriers for passenger fares, freight on imports, other port disbursements and income paid for postal and courier services. Travel : Travel credits are derived from the purchases of foreign exchange by banks and money-changers/foreign exchange dealers from tourists, business travelers, hotels and traders. It also includes transfers received from non-residents by travel agents and hotels as well as credit cards settlements effected by non-residents tourists. Travel debits obtained from sales of foreign currencies to residents. Similarly, it also includes transfers effected by residents to travel agents and hotels as well as redit cards settlements effected by residents abroad. Other Services : Other services are categorised according to the standard components of the BPM6. Data on other services are derived from banking ITRS records. Receipts for service exports or payment for services imports through banks by banks' clients are reported by banks to the Bank of Mauritius on specifically designed returns. Simultaneously, services imported and exported by banks are captured separately on other tailor-made returns for banks. Additional inputs for "other services" are also captured through the transportation survey. Effective October 2025, GBC services exports and imports, estimated from ESSNAC survey results have been incorporated in the balance of payments statistics for the quarters 2018Q1 onwards. In addition, FISIM is estimated, and included in the services account and corresponding adjustments are made in the primary income account in line with IMF BPM6. Primary Income Compensation of employees : Data on compensation of employees, for both credits and debits, are obtained through a survey of transport (shipping and airline) companies. Investment Income: Income on direct Investment: Data on dividends paid to nonresident direct investors and dividends received by resident direct investors are obtained from the banking records submitted to the Bank by banks for their clients' transactions, from other reports provided by banks suppl for their own transactions, supplemented by results from the FALS and ESSNAC Survey. Reinvested earnings drawn from survey results have been incorporated in the BOP statistics since 2010 Q1. Other Investment: Debit covers interest payments and other charges on foreign loans of the government, public corporations, the private sector, and banks with the data sourced from banking returns submitted to the Bank of Mauritius as well as administrative records and survey results. Credit measures the interest receipts on foreign investments of residents with similar data sources as in the case of dedit entries. Effective October 2025, adjustments are made in the primary account for FISIM, in line with IMF BPM6, for the quarters 2018Q1 onwards Secondary Income Data on private current transfers are derived from the banking records, remittances survey and ESSNAC survey. For government current transfers, data are derived from the records obtained from the Accountant General's Office of the MOF. Financial Account Direct Investment : Data are derived from the banking records, from the Bank Supervision Department and surveys namely, FALS and ESSNAC Survey conducted by the Bank. Data are supplemented by administrative data obtained from the EDB. Portfolio Investment : Data are derived from the banking records and supplemented by information obtained from the Stock Exchange of Mauritius and ESSNAC survey. Effective 2010Q1, survey results are used to complement the existing data sources. Financial Derivatives: Financial derivatives vis-a-vis non-residents are obtained primarily from the balance sheets of commercial banks and the annual ESSNAC survey. Other Investment : Data on drawdowns and repayments of loans on account of the government and public financial and non-financial institutions are obtained from the MoF. Data on investment by banks for their own account are obtained from their balance sheet reported to the central bank monthly. Transactions are estimated as the difference between two outstanding stocks and adjustments are made to exclude valuation and ‘other volume' changes. Data for all other enterprises' other investment transactions are derived from banking records supplemented by information provided by non-bank institutions. Effective July 2004, trade credit is estimated and included in "Other Investment-Trade credit and Advances." As from 2010Q1, FALS and ESSNAC Survey results have been supplementing existing data sources. Reserve Assets : Data are obtained from the Bank financial statements and comprise monetary gold, SDRs, Reserve Position in the Fund, foreign exchange balances of the Bank of Mauritius, and other claims. Adjustments are made to exclude valuation changes. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The source data drawn from surveys and the ITRS are consistent with the definitions, scope and classifications of BPM6. Given that ITRS data are reported on a cash basis, the time of recording may not be consistent with the settlement of the transaction. Survey data tend to be closer to the conceptual definitions, classification, valuation and time of recording of BPM6. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The data received from banks are timely. The data are reported on a monthly basis through the eXtended Business Reporting Language (XBRL) platform (for banks) or by e-mail (for other respondents). Data for the monthly returns are required to be reported within ten working days after the end of the reference month while for the quarterly returns, the deadline is twenty working days after the end of the reference quarter. The Bank of Mauritius Act 2004 provides the legal basis to ensure that survey data be reported to the Bank for statistical purposes. The Bank relies also on surveys, namely, the FALS and the ESSNAC Survey and much effort is exercised by the ESS staff in order to obtain and process the data from these surveys on time. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Survey results and other data collected are included in BOP statistics in line with concepts and established procedures as rtecommended under the BPM6. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques •Goods: The trade statistics allow the splitting of the item ‘goods’ into general merchandise, and nonmonetary gold. Fuel purchases abroad are also recorded under general merchandise. •Services: Services transactions are recorded at the moment payments and receipts are effected. Data received from the c.i.f./f.o.b. adjustment of the goods item are recorded under freight services. GBC services exports and imports are estimated by Statistics Mauritius from ESSNAC survey results and provided to the Bank for inclusion in the balance of payments statistics. FISIM is estimated by the Bank, and included in the services account and corresponding adjustments are made in the primary income account in line with IMF BPM6. •Primary Income: Direct investment income includes dividends, other distributed profits, and effective 2010Q1 reinvested earnings. Dividends are recorded when they are paid, except as from 2010Q1, with the inclusion of the FALS and ESSNAC Survey results, part of the data are on an accrual basis. Income is based partly on an accrual basis. Income on reserve assets and interest on government debt data are provided to the Bank from administrative records. •Secondary Income: Transactions reflect mainly transfers to and from the rest of world, workers’ remittances and other private (inclusive of GBLH transactions on the debit side as from 2010Q1) and government transfers. Data for GBLH transactions are obtained from ESSNAC survey while for the rest, they are mostly culled from banking ITRS returns. •Capital account: Sales and purchases of non-produced nonfinancial assets as well as capital transfers (representing primarily capital grants received by the country) are included in the capital account. •Direct investment: Transactions in equity capital (incl. reinvested earnings) and in other capital (loans between direct investors and direct investment enterprises) are recorded. •Portfolio investment: All portfolio investment data are reported. . Financial derivatives: Flow data for financial derivatives vis-a-vis non-residents are obtained primarily form the balance sheets of banks and the annual ESSNAC Survey. •Other investment: Government sector transactions (loans disbursements and repayments, capital subscriptions to international nonmonetary organizations) and corporate sector transactions (trade credits, loans disbursements and repayments) are reported. Transactions in assets and liabilities of banks are derived from changes in their external positions, excluding valuation. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Seasonal adjustment: None. Geographic detail: Geographical areas (and industrial branches) are monitored with foreign direct investment (flows and stocks). However, with regard to FDI culled from ESSNAC Surveys, geographical detail is available only for stock data. Other: Statistically acquired data (banking records, enterprise surveys and administrative records) are supplemented with estimates based on regular selective and one-off inquiries and with other information acquired on ad hoc need (requirement) basis. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Banlance of Payments statistics are cross verified with other data sources and data releases to ensure data consistency. Information reported in the media is often tracked to ensure that high-value direct investment and other transactions are reflected in available data sources and incorporated in the balance of payments statistics.. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Depending on the data type, inconsistencies are investigated to confirm the validity of data sources as well as the data culled. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The size of ‘Net errors and omissions’ is closely monitored and efforts are directed towards investigating large numbers in absolute terms. The growth in both magnitude and volatility in net errors and omissions in the past was a determining factor for the Bank’s decision to explore alternative approaches for its data collection process for the compilation of balance of payments statistics. The conduct of FALS and ESSNAC survey and inclusion of their results in the balance of payments statistics have considerably contributed in reducing the size of 'Net errors and omissions' over time. Recourse to counterparty information is sought when available ands accesible. For example, the IMF data on the use of Fund credit and SDRs are used to cross-check the data provided by the Accounting, Budgeting and Payment Division of the Bank. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Users are usually informed of the reasons that have contributed to the revision in the balance of payments statistics. With the inclusion of the FALS and ESSNAC Survey results for the first time in 2010 in balance of payments statistics, a methodological note was published by the Bank to inform users of the break in the data series. An Information Note on the Implementation of IMF's Balance of Payments and International Investment Position (IIP) Manual, Sixth Edition Compilation Framework accompanied the release for the first time of the balance of payments statistics in BPM6 format. Preliminary balance of payments statistics (based on staff own estimates and projections) are released on a quarterly basis to meet periodicity requirements; Upon finalization of the two above-mentioned survey results, these are incorporated in the balance of payments statistics and a revision note generally accompanies the revised data to inform users accordingly. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Important revisions of Balance of Payments Statistics are usually accompanied with an explanatory note that generally contains some analytical observations. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Non-published, non-confidential sub-aggregate data are made available to users on request. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The SDDS DSBB provides information on contact persons, and arrangements have been established within the Bank to ensure that any query received by that contact are directed to the Director-Economic Analysis & Research and Statistics Department. Any requests for unpublished data must be made in writing or by email and the provision of the non-confidential data must have the prior approval of the First Deputy Governor before dissemination. Effective October 2025, all ESS publications provide the section's e-mail address ess@bom.mu for users to direct their queries,if any. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs Not applicable.
