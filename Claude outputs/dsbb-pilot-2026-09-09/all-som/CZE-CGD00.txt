 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Definition: Central government debt is the stock of recognized, direct liabilities of the budgetary central government to the rest of the economy and the world, as follows from the IMF's Government Finance Statistics Manual, 2014 . 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Breakdown by maturity: Central government debt is broken down by original maturity (over or under 1 year). Breakdown by currency: Central government debt is broken down by domestic (Czech koruna - CZK) and foreign currency. According to the SDDS Plus methodology, breakdown by currency substitutes breakdown by holder that cannot be used due to missing data for government bonds issued on the foreign markets. Breakdown by type of instrument: According to the SDDS Plus requirement, the central government debt is broken down into following categories: Securities other than shares (Treasury bonds issued on the domestic market, Treasury bonds issued on the foreign markets, Savings government bonds and State treasury bills) Loans (Loans from international financial institutions and European Commission and other money market instruments) Other accounts payable (promissory notes) 2.2.1 Scope Breakdown by maturity: Central government debt is broken down by original maturity (over or under 1 year). Breakdown by currency: Central government debt is broken down by domestic (Czech koruna - CZK) and foreign currency. According to the SDDS Plus methodology, breakdown by currency substitutes breakdown by holder that cannot be used due to missing data for government bonds issued on the foreign markets. Breakdown by type of instrument: According to the SDDS Plus requirement, the central government debt is broken down into following categories: Securities other than shares (Treasury bonds issued on the domestic market, Treasury bonds issued on the foreign markets, Savings government bonds and State treasury bills) Loans (Loans from international financial institutions and European Commission and other money market instruments) Other accounts payable (promissory notes) Scope of the data Institutional coverage: Central government units covered by the data on central government debt disseminated under the SDDS Plus include all debt obligations of budgetary central government, but not debt obligations issued by extra-budgetary units. This institutional coverage of debt statistics corresponds to the coverage of data disseminated for central government operations (CGO). Instrument coverage: According to §36 Act No. 218/2000 Coll. on Budgetary Rules and amending related laws (Budgetary Rules), the central government debt data include all liabilities in the form of: state-issued government securities (at nominal value), including debt obligations relating to the original amount of the principal of government securities after the maturity date, until the rights related to the securities shall become statute-barred, loans and lending facilities, state-issued promissory notes. Central government debt is adjusted for the nominal values of the government securities, which were firstly booked on the Ministry's asset account in the respective register or were acquired by the state prior to the maturity date and are not simultaneously reported as part of the state debt. Government guaranteed debt: Debt guaranteed by the central government includes: debts guaranteed under the Act No. 218/2000 Coll., on Budgetary Rules; and debts guaranteed for export promotion under the Act No. 58/1995 Coll. on insuring and financing export with state support, as amended. Debts guaranteed under the Act on the Budgetary Rules include all such debts approved by the government cabinet (even in the case that a respective guarantee agreement has not been signed yet). Exceptions to coverage The data exclude the debt of all extra-budgetary units within central government subsector (S.1311) according to the ESA 2010 methodology. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation practices: Marketable securities are valued at nominal value. Government securities sold at a discount are valued at nominal value. Foreign-currency-denominated debt is revalued in local currency terms at each month-end by the Czech National Bank exchange rate. Liabilities denominated in a foreign currency and exchanged through contractual agreements to the national currency are converted into the national currency at the rate agreed on in those contracts. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Gross vs. net: Debt data disseminated under the SDDS Plus are on a gross basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources: The main sources and records used in compiling data on central government debt disseminated under the SDDS Plus are records of the Debt and Financial Assets Management Department (a part of the Ministry of Finance). For holders of State treasury bills denominated in Czech koruna, records of the central bank register are used. For Treasury bonds issued on the domestic market, State treasury bills denominated in the euros and Savings government bonds in book-entry form, records of the Central Securities Depository Prague are used. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Consolidation: Central government debt is reported on consolidated basis. Consolidation of central government extra-budgetary units is not logically possible considering more narrow institutional coverage of central government debt. None
