 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: The United Kingdom (UK) International Investment Position (IIP) data are based on the definition defined in the sixth edition of the IMF's ‘ Balance of Payments Manual ' (BPM6) and the System of National Accounts 2008 (SNA08). Definition: The UK IIP is a statistical statement of the level and breakdown of UK's external financial assets and liabilities. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification system: The UK IIP statistics are based on BPM6 classifications. Assets and liabilities are broken down into direct investment, portfolio investment, other investment, financial derivatives, and reserve assets. These are further classified by instruments of investment and sector. End period stocks of external assets and liabilities are classified according to type of investment (direct investment, portfolio investment, other investment and reserve assets), together with a more detailed instrument breakdown. A full reconciliation analysis is not available. Scope of the data Coverage of external financial assets and liabilities: The UK international investment position covers all sectors' financial assets and liabilities. Geographic Coverage: The UK's economic territory is defined to include the territories lying within its political frontiers and territorial seas and in the international waters over which it has exclusive authority. Exceptions to coverage Coverage of external financial assets and liabilities: The international investment activity of households is partly estimated. Geographic Coverage: The UK offshore islands - Jersey, Guernsey and the Isle of Man - are classified as non-residents to the UK as they are subject to their own fiscal authorities and have their own tax systems. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: Foreign financial assets and liabilities are generally collected at market prices. For direct investment in equity capital, much of which are traded infrequently (if at all), market value is approximated by either a recent transaction price, director's value, or net asset value. Conversion to unit of account: Stock positions originally denominated in foreign currencies should be converted to pounds sterling at the mid-point of the buying and selling exchange rates applying at the beginning or end of the period. Survey respondents are asked to provide data on this basis, but in practice the actual rate used varies according to the source of the data. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Direct Investment: Stocks of direct investment abroad and in the UK are collected through quarterly and annual surveys run by the ONS and the Bank of England. The surveys collect gross assets and liabilities of the direct investor and direct investment enterprise. Up to 2005 Q4, quarterly stocks data were based on accumulating flows to the last annual balance sheet, revalued to take account of exchange rate movements. From 2006 Q1 onwards, balance sheet information is collected on a quarterly basis. Portfolio investment: Portfolio investment abroad is based on a range of surveys of UK banks, securities dealers, insurance companies, pension funds, private non-financial corporations and households. Portfolio investment in the UK is based on a range of sources. Foreign holdings of UK issued equity is based on a biannual Share Ownership Survey of share registers. Levels of British government stocks held by non-residents are measured from banking statistics and other Bank of England sources including the General Gilt Office. Inward investment in bonds and notes issued by UK monetary financial institutions and other sectors are estimated from information derived from the Bank of England and London Stock Exchange records of UK company bond issues. Estimates of UK equity and debt security liabilities are compared with the results of the annual IMF Coordinated Portfolio Investment Survey. Other investment: Estimates of currency and deposits abroad are based on banking statistics data, ONS surveys of securities dealers and Bank for International Settlements (BIS) data on UK private sector assets with non-resident banks. Trade credit, loans and other assets are based on a range of Bank of England and ONS surveys together with some government administrative data. Similarly, other investment in the UK are based on banking data, BIS data and information on borrowing from the European Investment Bank (EIB) and administrative data on the deposit liabilities of UK central government. Reserve assets: Reserve assets information are recorded in the Exchange Equalisation Account by the Bank of England. 3.2.1 Source data assessment A wide variety of verification procedures are used during data collection and compilation. Company balance sheets are checked against previous estimates for consistency; flows and levels are checked for reconciliation problems at aggregate level; balance of payments stock data are fully reconciled with UK national accounts. None 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Revisions are published quarterly in the ‘Quarterly Balance of Payments Statistical Bulletin' and ‘UK Economic Accounts' giving information on the scale and reason for revision.
