 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The structure of Australia's national accounts is in accordance with the System of National Accounts ( SNA 2008). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification systems: The accounts cover all institutional units in the economy and distinguish four domestic institutional sectors: nonfinancial corporations; financial corporations; general government and households (including unincorporated businesses and Non-Profit Institutions Serving Households (NPISHs)). The following breakdowns are disseminated: By expenditure category, broken down into household consumption, government consumption, gross fixed capital formation by government and by corporations, changes in inventories, and exports and imports of goods and services. COICOP is used for household final consumption expenditure, although quarterly estimates are published at a fairly aggregated level (17 categories are published). COFOG is used for government final consumption expenditure, but this dissection is only available on an annual basis. International transactions are classified according to the UN SITC Rev 4 (from September quarter 2008). By productive sectors, using the Australian and New Zealand Standard Industrial Classification (ANZSIC 2006), broken down by division for all industries, with additional breakdowns for the following divisions: Agriculture, Forestry and Fishing; Mining; Manufacturing; Electricity, Gas, Water and Waste Services; Construction; Transport, Postal and Warehousing; Information Media and Telecommunications; Financial and Insurance Services; Rental, Hiring and Real Estate Services; and Professional, Scientific and Technical Services. The revised industry classification, ANZSIC 2006, was implemented in September quarter 2009. By income components, compensation of employees, gross operating surplus, gross mixed income, and taxes less subsidies on production and imports. 2.2.1 Scope Classification systems: The accounts cover all institutional units in the economy and distinguish four domestic institutional sectors: nonfinancial corporations; financial corporations; general government and households (including unincorporated businesses and Non-Profit Institutions Serving Households (NPISHs)). Scope of the data The ultimate scope of the Australian System of National Accounts (ASNA) encompasses the full range of statistics that the 2008 SNA recommends for a complete national accounting system. Exceptions to coverage Most illegal transactions are not covered because of lack of adequate data. The accounts exclude Australia's external territories such as Norfolk Island and Christmas Island. Unrecorded activity The accounts do not cover illegal activities such as prostitution and illicit drugs. Adjustments are made for under-reporting of income and the cash economy on both the income and expenditure sides of the accounts in the annual supply-use tables. Refer to Information Paper The Non-Observed Economy and Australia's GDP, 2012 (ABS catalogue number 5204.0.55.008). 2.4.1 Valuation Aggregates are compiled at market prices as far as possible. Imputed rent on owner occupied dwellings is calculated using comparable rental values for similar dwellings. Non-market services are valued at cost. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures A degree of netting is employed in the national accounts in as much as transactions with opposite sign are often combined (e.g. acquisitions and disposals of financial assets are recorded as 'net acquisitions'). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Production approach: Australia does not produce a quarterly current price measure of GDP using the production approach (although two components of current price GDP could be described as being estimated using a production approach--GOS dwellings and farm income). The current price estimates of GDP are compiled using both the income and the expenditure approaches, whereas the chain volume estimates of GDP are compiled using both the production and the expenditure approaches. The annual current price and volume estimates are compiled in a supply-use framework. The major data source for the non-general government sector is the annual economic activity survey integrated with data from the Australian Taxation Office. Quarterly volume estimates are mostly derived using output indicators obtained by deflation. If direct source data are not available, the most suitable indicator series is used to allocate annual estimates to quarters and to extrapolate from the latest annual benchmark. Where no suitable quarterly indicator can be obtained, annual estimates are allocated to quarters and extrapolated using a mathematical trend formula. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Production approach: The accounts do not cover illegal activities such as prostitution and illicit drugs. Adjustments are made for under-reporting of income for taxation purposes when using taxation data for compiling the estimates of GOS and gross mixed income. Not compiled at current prices on a quarterly basis. Quarterly chain volume estimates of gross valued added at basic prices are derived in the prices of the previous financial year ending June 30, using a number of different estimation methods. "Double deflation" is used for agriculture in which some components of output and intermediate input are derived using deflation and others by quantity revaluation. For all the other industries growth in value added is estimated using output indicators - some using quantity indicators such as for mining, the utilities, health and education; and some using deflation such as for retail trade, wholesale trade and manufacturing. Most quarterly indicators within an industry are weighted together using current price value added weights (the previous year's in most cases) and in the remaining cases by revenue. From 1994-95 all quarterly estimates are benchmarked to annual estimates (in the prices of the previous year) derived in supply-use tables. Quarterly estimates are published at the industry division level for all industries and the sub-division level for some industries. Expenditure approach: Household final consumption expenditure: An adjustment is made to include the value of backyard production. Adjustments are made to retail sales data to allow for direct sales to households by manufacturers, wholesalers and direct sellers. Adjustments are made for purchases by households within the cash economy. However, no adjustments are made for expenditure on illegal goods and services, such as illegal drugs and illegal prostitution. Government final consumption expenditure: Adjustments are made to defense expenditure to allow for the timing of deliveries of major items of defense equipment. Estimates for consumption of fixed capital are included. Estimation of expenditure approach at current prices: Household final consumption expenditure: Expenditure on services is compiled from a wide range of data sources including sales to households by various public and private sector enterprises for transport services, health services etc. Bank transaction data is used to extrapolate from periodic annual benchmark for sales of goods to households. Dwelling rent is extrapolated from benchmarks obtained from five yearly population Censuses using average rent data from the CPI and numbers of dwellings added to the stock of dwellings from the Building Activity Survey. Household expenditure on motor vehicles is obtained from data provided by a private sector company providing statistical services to the motor vehicle industry. Private gross fixed capital formation: GFCF on dwellings is estimated using the quarterly Building Activity Survey for new construction and major extensions, and an additional allowance for alterations and additions not covered by the Building Activity Survey using data from a private sector company. GFCF on other buildings and structures is estimated using the quarterly Building Activity Survey and the quarterly Engineering Construction Survey. GFCF on machinery and equipment is estimated by extrapolating annual benchmarks using the quarterly Survey of Private New Capital Expenditure. GFCF on intangible fixed assets are extrapolated using available indicators such as imports of computer software and the quarterly survey of Mineral and Petroleum Exploration Expenditure. Public corporations gross fixed capital formation: Annual benchmarks from Government Finance Statistics (GFS) are extrapolated using quarterly data. General government gross fixed capital formation: Annual benchmarks from GFS are extrapolated using quarterly data. Changes in inventories: Estimates are compiled from the quarterly business indicators survey and GFS. Exports of goods and services: Provided directly from Australia's balance of payments statistics. Imports of goods and services: Provided directly from Australia's balance of payments statistics. Estimation of expenditure approach in volume terms: All volume estimates are compiled in the prices of the previous financial year ending June 30, and from 1994-95 are benchmarked to annual estimates (in the prices of the previous financial year) from supply-use tables. Most components of household final consumption expenditure are deflated using CPIs. For government final consumption expenditure, the health and education components are directly estimated using quantity indicators and the remainder is deflated using wage rate and price indexes related to intermediate inputs. Gross fixed capital formation is deflated using a combination of import and domestic production price indexes. A complex supply-use model is used for equipment to derive Paasche deflators from the Laspeyres elemental price indexes. All components of domestic final demand are derived at the state level and then aggregated to obtain national estimates. Changes in inventories are derived by deflating levels and then differentiating for all nonfarm commodities. Quantity data are used for farm commodities. Imports are derived by deflation at the 3-digit BEC by 5-digit SITC level. Primary exports (about 80 percent of goods) are derived by quantity revaluation at the 8-digit level of the HS. The remainder is deflated. Base year and update cycle: The chain volume measures are derived as annually reweighted chain Laspeyres indexes referenced to the current price values in the latest base year, which is the financial year that preceded the last complete financial year ending June 30. Every year the reference year is advanced one year with the release of the September quarter accounts. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The current price estimates of GDP are compiled using both the income and the expenditure approaches, whereas the chain volume estimates of GDP are compiled using both the production and the expenditure approaches. A chain volume measure for the income-based measure of GDP is also obtained by deflation, using the expenditure implicit price deflator. The so-called "headline" chain volume measure is referred to simply as GDP and is the average of the three independent measures. Explicit quarterly statistical discrepancies are included to equate the sum of the expenditure and industry chain volume measure components with total GDP. GDP at current prices is obtained by reflating the chain volume measure of GDP using the expenditure implicit price deflator. The differences between the sums of the income and expenditure components and GDP are shown as explicit statistical discrepancies. Australia has adopted (with minor changes) the X-11 package from the US Census Bureau as its standard method of seasonal analysis of quarterly series. Calendar effects, where measurable, are estimated using regression techniques. The estimated seasonal and calendar influences together provide the combined adjustment factors by which the original series are seasonally adjusted. Concurrent seasonal adjustment is applied each quarter causing revisions to previous seasonal factors. State final demand and its components and compensation of employees are available for the eight Australian States and Territories. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results A quarterly supply-use model is used to confront the production and expenditure estimates. This model is not used to formally balance quarterly supply-use data but is an editing aid. Refer to the research paper 'A Supply and Use Model for Editing the Quarterly National Accounts' (ABS catalogue number 5258.0) for more details 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The three measures of GDP are balanced in annual terms using the Supply and Use approach, up to the year prior to the most recent complete year (and latest two years in the June quarter release). This process eliminates the statistical discrepancies in annual terms, except for the latest year. The quarterly estimates of GDP are benchmarked to the annual estimates, but within-year inconsistencies remain. Because the measures are balanced annually in a Supply and Use table, the magnitude of the quarterly statistical discrepancies is small relative to the level of GDP. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revision analysis is carried out as part of the regular quality assessment of published data. Detailed revisions analysis on GDP and key aggregates is published at irregular intervals on the ABS website, most recently in the information paper Analysis of R evisions to Gross Domestic Product. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Detailed revisions on GDP and key aggregates is published at irregular internals on the ABS website, most recently in the information paper Analysis of Revisions to Gross Domestic Product. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Some unpublished data are available to users upon request, for a fee. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The ABS provides contact details both for general and specialist enquiries with the release of data on the ABS website.
