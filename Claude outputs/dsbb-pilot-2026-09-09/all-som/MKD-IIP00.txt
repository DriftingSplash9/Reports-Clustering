 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The overall structure of concepts and definitions follows internationally accepted standards. The framework for International Investment Position follows the Sixth edition of the Balance of Payment and International Investment Position Manual ( BPM6 ). The report on the International Investment Position (IIP) contains the standard components prescribed in the Balance of Payments and International investment Position Manual 6th edition (IMF 2009) and the BPM6 Compilation Guide (IMF 2014). According to the Manual and Guide, there are three levels of classification of the components of IIP. The first level of classification divides IIP in two categories: assets and liabilities. The second level is completely consistent with the financial account of BOP, and divides IIP items by type of investment. And the third level of classification is classification by instruments of investment. IIP data are disseminated on the quarterly and annually basis. Direct investments reflect the purpose of achieving long-term interest relationship between the direct investor (resident in one economy) and the direct investment enterprise (resident in another economy). Direct investments consist of investments by which the investor achieves long-term economic relationship and/or the right to manage the direct investment enterprise. Thereby the direct investments are defined in accordance with international recommendations and standards that require minimum 10% capital share (direct or indirect). Despite the initial investment, direct investments include all subsequent investments between the direct investor and the direct investment enterprise. This category also includes reverse investments and investments between fellow enterprises (below10%). Direct investments consist of Equity and investment fund shares (including Reinvestment of Earnings) and Debt instruments arising from intercompany lending between affiliated entities. Debt claims and liabilities between deposit corporations and other financial intermediaries (except insurance corporations and pension funds) are excluded from the debt instruments within the direct investments category and are included in the appropriate financial instrument. Portfolio investments include investments in equity and investment fund shares, where the investor's share is less than 10%, and debt securities. Financial derivatives (other than reserves) and employee stock options are financial instruments which involve specific financial risks linked to another underlying financial instrument or indicator or commodity and which can be traded independently from the underlying instrument on the financial markets. The value of a financial derivative derives from the price of the underlying financial instrument. Other investments contain assets and liabilities based on other equity, currency and deposits, loans, insurance, pension and standardized guarantee schemes, trade credit and advances, other accounts receivable/payable and the allocation of special drawing rights. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization IIP contains three levels of classification of the components. First level Assets and Liabilities Second level - Direct Investment, Portfolio Investment, Financial derivatives (other than reserves) and employee stock options and Other Investment. Third level - by institutional sector: Central bank, Deposit-taking corporations, except central bank, General government and Other Sectors. The sectorization and classifications are in accordance with BPM6. 2.2.1 Scope IIP contains three levels of classification of the components. First level Assets and Liabilities Second level - Direct Investment, Portfolio Investment, Financial derivatives (other than reserves) and employee stock options and Other Investment. Third level - by institutional sector: Central bank, Deposit-taking corporations, except central bank, General government and Other Sectors. The sectorization and classifications are in accordance with BPM6. Scope of the data The scope is broadly consistent with internationally accepted standards, covering all types of financial assets and liabilities between residents and nonresidents. Unrecorded activity Investors in foreign securities (PI-assets) different from the government, financial institutions and the National bank, currently are not covered with the main data source used (Security-by Security database). However these investments are monitored through other sources and currently the amounts are not significant. Ongoing activities are in place to establish high-quality and more comprehensive sources for reporting of these investments as well. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation of the IIP components: Direct investment: (book value) Portfolio investment: market price Loans and credits: nominal value Trade credits: nominal value Monetary gold: market price The quarterly and annual data are currently published in Macedonian Denars, U.S. dollars and Euros. A major part of the instruments are denominated in euros, so the euro is a more relevant currency for assessing international performance. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures IIP data are presented on gross basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Source data are obtained from comprehensive data collection programs that are based upon Macedonia specific conditions. The source data for compiling IIP are: Direct investment -annual direct investment survey - inward (VS 22) and outward (VS 11), which resident legal entities submit to the NBRNM on an annual basis, and are related to the investments to and from other countries. The annual survey for direct investments abroad (VS-11) is on a census basis, while the survey for direct investments in the reporting country, starting from reporting period 2017, was transformed from census to sample survey. Data sources for the compilation: For the subcategory "Equity and Investment Fund Shares"- for the purposes of the annual IIP, reports on stocks and flows between affiliated entities (VS 22 and VS 11) are used as a data source. For the purposes of the Quarterly IIP, the stock is calculated by adding the BOP flows for the current quarter to the stock of equity and investment fund shares at the end of the previous quarter. For the subcategory " Debt Instruments"- for loans between affiliated entities, data are used from the reporting system on external debt (ED) and the reporting system on external claims (EC), while for intercompany lending in the form of trade credits and advances, data are used from the quarterly reports on the claims and liabilities on the basis of international commercial operations of residents (KIPO Form). The survey on debt/claims takeovers among residents and nonresident (POZ) is used as an additional data source. Data sources for portfolio investments are: For the subcategory "Equity securities" on the assets side: for the annual and quarterly IIP, for foreign equity and investment fund shares issued abroad and bought by residents, data on the market value of the purchased securities at the end of the reference period is provided by the residents authorized for participation in foreign capital markets (Reports for stocks and flows data on investments inequity and investment fund shares VHV-1); on the liabilities side: up to 2016 data, for the annual IIP, for deposit-taking corporations and other sectors, reports on stocks and flows between affiliated entities (VS 22) were used as a data source. Starting from reporting period 2017, the source of data on investments in equity is Central Securities Depository (CSD). for the quarterly IIP, for the deposit-taking corporations and the other sectors, stocks are calculated by adding up the BOP flows for the current quarter to the stock of equity securities at the end of the previous quarter. For the subcategory "Debt securities"- for the annual and quarterly IIP the same data sources are used. on the assets side, for the annual and quarterly IIP, for foreign debt securities issued abroad and bought by residents, data on the market value of the purchased securities at the end of the reference period is provided by the residents authorized for participation in foreign capital markets (Reports for stocks and flows data on investments in debt securities VHV-2); on the liabilities side, data on debt securities issued on the domestic market and purchased by non-residents are taken from Central Securities Depositary, while data on the prevailing market prices are used from the Macedonian Stock Exchange. For debt securities issued abroad and bought by non-residents, data from the reporting system on external debt (ED), as well as quarterly report on trade securities on foreign market, provided by the residents authorized for participation in foreign capital markets, are used. Other investment: Other equity For the purposes of the annual IIP, reports on stocks and flows between affiliated entities (VS 22 and VS 11) are used as a data source. For the purposes of the quarterly IIP, the stock is calculated by adding the BOP flows for the current quarter to the stock of other equity at the end of the previous quarter. Currency and deposits for the category currency and deposits, for the annual and quarterly IIP, the data from reports of the banks on assets and liabilities balance (KNBIFO) - the monthly bank's reports on the banks' book - keeping stock are used, whereas for other sectors data from the monthly reports of resident accounts held abroad (MR) are used; for the central bank, for the currency and deposits other than reserves, data from the NBRNM's reports on the foreign assets are used. Loans for the category loans, for the annual and quarterly IIP, data from the reporting system on external debt (ED) and the reporting system on external claims (EC), as well as data from NBRNM`s reports on the official foreign reserves of the Republic of North Macedonia are used. The survey on debt/claims takeovers among residents and nonresidents (POZ) is used as an additional data source. Since 2010 data on loans are presented on accrual basis. Trade credit and advances for the subcategory long-term trade credits - for annual and quarterly IIP the data from the reporting system on external debt (ED) and the reporting system on external claims (EC) are used for the subcategory short-term trade credits - for annual and quarterly IIP, data from the quarterly reports on the claims and liabilities on the basis of international commercial operations of residents (KIPO Form) are used. Special drawing rights for the annual and quarterly IIP, data from the reporting system on external debt (ED) are used. Reserve assets for the annual and quarterly IIP, data from reports of the NBRNM on the official foreign reserves of the Republic of North Macedonia are used. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The source data used for the Macedonian IIP generally adopt definitions; scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording that are close to BPM6 standards. The NBRNM provides reporters with guidance in the form of reporting instructions and ad-hoc consultation for fulfilling of the report forms. The NBRNM's IIP compilers maintain regular contact with reporters to resolve data issues that have important implications for the accuracy of the classification of accounting data for use in IIP. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Survey's from affiliated companies for items: direct investment and portfolio investment equity of the IIP-reporters are obliged to report to the NBRNM within three months after reference year. In accordance with the agreement between NBRNM and Central Depositary, data for traded equity securities on the domestic exchange market with non-residents and debt securities (bonds) issued by the Ministry of Finance, are received 30 days after the reference month. Companies are obligated to submit the survey on assets/liabilities from short-term commercial operation with non-residents (KIPO survey) 1 month after the end of the reference quarter. Source data are generally timely. Data on debt transactions/positions and interest are based on a survey, and the results are provided in sufficient time for IIP purposes. Experience with direct reporting surveys indicates that slow response of enterprises could be a potential problem to be monitored. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data—including censuses, sample surveys and administrative records—are routinely assessed, e.g., for coverage, sample error, response error, and non- sampling error; the results of the assessments are monitored to guide statistical processes. ITRS, Central Depositary and NBRNM's surveys (VS 11, VS 22, KIPO, VHV,etc.) all use enterprise identification numbers that allow related data to be cross-checked. There is extensive cross-checked data among various data sources, which provides for better data quality. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Data compilation employs sound statistical techniques to deal with data sources. The forms for reporting source data are designed to encourage use of computer programs, which enable prompt data processing. The potential for processing errors is minimized by the use of electronic reporting and processing procedures. The ITRS check procedures and cross-checked data among various data sources include checks to avoid misreporting and data are checked for temporal consistency. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The accuracy and reliability of the reported source data for the NBRNM and reporting companies are checked against other sources or information; for instance: Direct investment: 1. FDI data for invested/withdrawn capital, dividends paid, from the Survey for affiliated companies are checked against records of the ITRS. 2.Data for intercompany lending are cross checked with the data from the quarterly Survey on assets/liabilities from short-term commercial operations with non-residents (KIPO Survey) with affiliated companies and with the reporting system on external debt and external claims (with affiliated companies),as well. Portfolio investments-assets: for verification purposes inflows and outflows data from the Reports for stocks and flows data on investments abroad (VHV1 and VHV2) are compared with the data from the ITRS. Newspapers and information agencies are monitored for information about large international transactions, and it is confirmed that the corresponding flows are covered in the balance of payments and IIP respectively. Survey data are checked against ITRS records and fully reconciled. Output data are examined for plausibility. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data IIP compilers identify statistical discrepancies between data from surveys and data from International Transaction Reporting System and in collaboration with the reporters, investigate their causes. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Statistical discrepancies and other potential indicators of problems in statistical outputs are investigated. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses The NBRNM studies and analyzes the revisions as part of its data quality control process. Studies and analyses of revisions are used internally. The NBRNM carefully monitors revisions of individual records. They are documented as to the cause, identified by the staff member making the amendment and approved. It is checked that the revisions flow through to aggregate results. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request The NBRNM meets user's requests for additional information without a fee. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Contact points are provided in all publication formats. Prompt and knowledgeable assistance is provided as well. Requests are kept in a file, and timeliness is monitored (usually within two days for existing data). 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The NBRNM publishes a list of publications on its internet website ( https://www.nbrm.mk/publikacii-en.nspx ) The NBRNM maintains and updates a list of regular recipients of its publications and publications are sent to them regularly. NBRNM publications are also provided to users upon request free of charge.
