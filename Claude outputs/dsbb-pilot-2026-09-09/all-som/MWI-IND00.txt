 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Use of ISICS (currently using ISIC Rev. 3.1) and recommended formula (Laspyres). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The output produced has been categorized into six major groups as follows: Food, Beverages and Tobacco (13%); Footwear, Clothing and Textiles (9%); Other goods (mainly for final consumption) (23%); Products for Building and Construction (18%); Export Industries (23%); and Electricity and Water (14%). Scope of the data The Index of Industrial Production is based on production data of 50 large-scale firms. These are a subset of the Annual Economic Survey, which is a survey of the large-scale business sector. The survey covers Manufacturing and Utilities sectors. Those in the manufacturing sector were chosen to represent 75% of the production of that sector and the utilities sector represents 100% of the sector. The base year for this index is 1984. The Indices are not seasonally adjusted. Exceptions to coverage Only existing enterprises. Unrecorded activity Unadjusted or replacement of dormant enterprises which were in the original basket. 2.4.1 Valuation This is a volume index and has no valuation. 2.4.2 Recording basis 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Most firms supply information on quantity of goods produced, but where output of items varies considerably from month to month, other types of indicators are used (e.g. Consumption of materials, person hours). The weighting of the Index is done in three stages: Items within Industries (e.g. Bread in the Food Industry). Industries within Divisions (e.g. Food in the Food, Beverages and Tobacco Division). Division within the "All Items" Index. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Timeliness has been a problem because respondents have changed overtime. The other reason is that of respondent fatigue due to current legal framework. 3.2.1 Source data assessment 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The weighting pattern is based on the 1984 proportion of value added of the respective firms. There are some firms that have more than one commodity in the IIP basket and these are weighted by their sales value.
