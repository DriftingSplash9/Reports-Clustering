 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The concepts, definitions and terminologies of the revised national accounts are now in accordance with the 1993 SNA. It has, however, not been possible to attain all the revised concepts and definitions of the 1993 SNA that would be required within the scope of Belize’s national accounts. This is basically due to lack of data. In the agriculture, forestry and fishing sector, the process of production extends over a number of quarters, or even years. The growth of crops, fruits, vegetables, and livestock, trees in plantations and fish cultivated in fish farms, which is managed and controlled, constitutes a process of production. Thus, the value of the output produced in each period should be measured as work-in-progress (changes in inventories). For this purpose, the value of the harvested crops, the felled trees, the slaughtered animals, etc., should be distributed over the time of production as an increase of work-in-progress. SNA93 prescribes that this be done by distributing the value of harvested crops, felled trees, slaughtered animals, etc., in proportion to the costs (intermediate consumption and labour) incurred in each period. This definition of output should be implemented only for forestry where the production process extends over years. However, this has not been possible due to lack of data. For agriculture output is instead allocated to the year when the crops are harvested, the animals slaugh­tered, etc., the SNA-definition would have consequences mainly for quarterly estimates. In the case of financial intermediation, the national accounts measure the value of FISIM as total property income receivable by financial intermediaries minus their total interest payable. Any property income receivable on the investment of own funds must be excluded, as such income does not emanate from financial intermediation. An approximate measure, suitable for Belize, is to consider property income exclusive of interest as emanating from own funds. The value of FISIM would then be equal to interest receivable minus interest payable. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization International Standard Industrial Classification of Economic Activities Revision 3.1 Scope of the data The estimates compiled are national in scope, covering the six administrative districts of the country inclusive of islands. The revised annual national accounts cover the following areas: a) GDP by activity is derived by the production approach as the sum of the values added of all economic activities (industries) plus taxes on products. Production accounts have now been compiled for all industries with output and value added at basic prices as recommended by the 1993 SNA. Values added were previously calcu­lated at factor cost, but the difference between basic prices and factor cost is insignificant in the case of Belize and does not have any practical importance. Other taxes on production less other subsidies, which makes up the difference, amounts to only half a per cent of GDP. b) Expenditure on GDP. A systematic commodity flow technique has been implemented, which entails a con­frontation of supply and use data at as detailed a level as possible. This is the main vehicle for estimates of the expenditure side and has resulted in an inde­pendent estimate of private consumption, which was previously estimated as a residual. A weakness in the expenditure estimates is that there are no comprehensive estimates of changes in inventories. c) Rest of the world. The balance of payments compiled by the Central Bank of Belize has been converted into the format of the sequence of accounts for the rest of the world in the 1993 SNA. The current and capital accounts are covered. This allows the derivation of other major national accounting aggregates besides GDP such as national income. Real gross national income (GNI) has also been calculated in accordance with the outline in the 1993. d) General government. A framework for the institutional accounts of general government is available in SNAPC, and accounts for general government have been compiled. However, it remains to evaluate and check the results in particular with regard to central government’s capital budget. It also remains to capture and classify data for local government and the Belize Social Security Board. Exceptions to coverage There are no explicit estimates for certain activities but the impact of this is most probably small. Informal activities may be significant in manufacturing and retail trade. Looking at employment according to the annual Labour Force Survey compared to values added in these two industries, the informal sector seems to be fairly well covered. Unrecorded activity There are no attempts to capture illegal activities through the production approach, which is possibly a reason why this approach consistently yields a lower estimate than the expenditure approach. 2.4.2 Recording basis Information is generally recorded on a cash basis. Appropriate adjustments are made when an establishment’s financial year does not coincide with the calendar year. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The CSO relies on a variety of sources for the compilation of national accounts. These include administrative records, financial statements, establishment surveys and direct reporting. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques In the course of compiling the national accounts, a number of ratios are used. Due to the paucity of data, some of these ratios are held constant until new information becomes available. In a number of cases, it is possible to crosscheck data emanating from different sources. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The base year for the national accounts has been changed from 1984 to 2000 and the estimates have been re-based back to 1990 for GDP by activity and back to 1994 for expenditure on GDP. The reason why the expenditure estimates have not been re-based back to 1990 is that data on exports and imports are not readily available back to 1990. Henceforth, the base year should be updated regularly every five years. This is encouraged by the 1993 SNA as an acceptable procedure.
