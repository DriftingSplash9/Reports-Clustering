 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The international investment position of the Republic of Belarus (IIP) is compiled in accordance with international standards set out the sixth edition of The Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6) (IMF, 2009). The international investment position of the Republic of Belarus is a statistics statement that shows at a point in time the value of: financial assets of residents of the Republic of Belarus that are claims on nonresidents or are gold bullions held as reserve assets; and the liabilities of residents of the Republic of Belarus to nonresidents. The integrated IIP statement reconciles the opening and closing values of the IIP through the financial account (flows arising from transactions) and the changes in financial assets and liabilities account (other volume changes and revaluation). Other changes in the volume of financial assets and liabilities are any changes in the value of these assets that are due neither to transactions nor to revaluation. These changes include those due to cancellation and write-offs, economic appearance and disappearance of assets, reclassification, and the changes in financial assets arising from entities changing their economy residence. Changes resulting from revaluation disclose change in the value of financial resources due to causes not associated with actual transactions. Such causes can include change in the value of stocks due to change in the prices of resources being reported, and a change in the value of financial assets due to change in exchange rate. Difference between external assets and liabilities makes the country's net investment position . Excess of liabilities over assets indicates that the country is a net debtor to the outside world. Excess of external assets over liabilities to the outside world indicates that the country is a net creditor . Principles and concepts used for compilation of the international investment balance sheet comply with the country's balance of payments compilation principles (principles of economic territory of the country, residence, principles governing the valuation and the time of recording of transactions, etc.). The structure of IIP indicators corresponds to the structure of the financial account in the balance of payments. Changes recorded in the balance of payments for a certain period are included in the country's IIP for the same period. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification used are in accordance with the BPM6 recommendations. The following levels of classification for financial assets and liabilities are used: functional category – direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives (other than reserves), other investment, and reserve assets; financial instrument – equity and investment fund shares, debt instruments, and other financial assets and liabilities; institutional sector of resident party – central bank, deposit-taking corporations, except the central bank, general government, and other sectors; maturity – short-term or long-term maturity. The breakdown into long- and short-term maturity component is made according to the initial maturity of the financial instrument. The following institutional sectors are distinguished: central bank includes the authorities that exercises control over key aspects of the financial system, in particular, issuing currency, managing international reserves and regulation of activity of deposit-taking corporations. The National Bank of the Republic of Belarus belongs to such authorities; general government sector includes all general government bodies of the Republic of Belarus, local executive and administrative bodies of the Republic of Belarus, foundations, institutions, nonmarket nonprofit organizations controlled by government, and other publicly controlled organizations engaging in nonmarket activities. Diplomatic and other official representative offices of the Government of the Republic of Belarus located outside the Republic of Belarus also fall into the general government sector of their home country; deposit-taking corporations , except the central bank include organizations engaged in financial intermediation relating to raising of funds for deposits as their principal activity. Banks and non-banking credit and financial institutions (NCFI) belong to this sector in the Republic of Belarus; other sectors include (1) other financial corporations, as well as (2) nonfinancial corporations, households, and nonprofit institutions serving households, unrecorded in the above sectors. Institutional sectors in the IIP refer to the resident sector, not the counterpart sector (the sector of the domestic holder or lender for assets, and the sector of the domestic issuer or borrower for liabilities). 2.2.1 Scope Classification used are in accordance with the BPM6 recommendations. The following levels of classification for financial assets and liabilities are used: functional category – direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives (other than reserves), other investment, and reserve assets; financial instrument – equity and investment fund shares, debt instruments, and other financial assets and liabilities; institutional sector of resident party – central bank, deposit-taking corporations, except the central bank, general government, and other sectors; maturity – short-term or long-term maturity. Scope of the data The IIP includes foreign financial assets of residents and external liabilities to nonresidents. Assets show the cost of investments of residents of the Republic of Belarus abroad as direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives, reserve assets and other investments of the Republic of Belarus at a certain point in time. Liabilities show the cost of investments of nonresidents in the Belarusian economy as direct investment, portfolio investment, other investments, and financial derivatives at a certain point in time. Geographic coverage : The economic territory of the Republic of Belarus covers the country's geographic area under the jurisdiction of the Government of the Republic of Belarus, within which labour, goods and capital can move freely. The country's economic territory includes land and air space, territorial enclaves in other countries (distinct areas situated in other countries, which belong to or are leased by the Government of the Republic of Belarus for diplomatic, military, scientific or any other purposes, free zones or customs warehouses and enterprises used under control of customs authorities). The economic territory of the Republic of Belarus does not include enclaves of foreign governments and international organizations located within the geographic area of the Republic of Belarus. Unrecorded activity The National Bank measures changes in the volume of foreign cash in the sector of nonfinancial corporations, households, and nonprofit institutions servicing households of the Republic of Belarus. 2.4.1 Valuation External assets and liabilities generally are measured at their nominal value. Securities are carried at fair value. The value of monetary gold is measured at the market price. The IIP data are compiled in US dollars. Positions of foreign assets and liabilities as of the end of the reporting period denominated in other currencies, are converted into US dollars using the cross-rates of US dollar to other foreign currencies based on the official BYN to respective foreign currencies rate, fixed by the National Bank as of reporting date. Transactions executed in other currencies are converted into US dollars using cross-rates of US dollar to other foreign currencies based on the official BYN to respective foreign currencies rate, fixed by the National Bank as of the transaction date. Should information on transaction dates be unavailable, the monthly or quarterly average weighted exchange rate of BYN to foreign currencies in the foreign exchange market of the Republic of Belarus shall be applied. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of transaction recording is determined on an accrual basis. According to the accrual basis of accounting, transactions are recognized by the parties simultaneously, and the interest charged on deposits, loans and securities is included in the available amount of financial assets and not treated as a component of other accounts payable. When arrears occur, no transactions should be imputed, but the arrears should continue to be shown in the same instrument until the liability is extinguished. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Accounting of assets and liabilities of a financial account is carried out on net basis separately for each financial asset or liability, without indication of credit and debit entries. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The following main sources of information are used for the IIP compilation: reporting on foreign economic activity submitted to the National Bank by deposit-taking corporations of the Republic of Belarus (relating to foreign economic transactions of their customers that are residents of the Republic of Belarus, and their own foreign economic transactions) and by organizations that are residents of the Republic of Belarus; reporting on foreign economic transactions of organizations that are residents of the Republic of Belarus submitted to the National Statistics Committee of the Republic of Belarus (the Belstat): information on attracting foreign investment into the national economy and providing Belarusian investments abroad by other financial and nonfinancial corporations of the Republic of Belarus; other official sources of information of the Republic of Belarus: the Belstat, the Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Belarus ("the Ministry of Finance"), the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the Republic of Belarus, other government bodies; quarterly information from the Central Bank of the Russian Federation submitted in accordance with the agreed rules of information exchange between the Central Bank of the Russian Federation and the National Bank; the National Bank's estimates; data from international organizations; mass-media, internet resources. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Classification of the IIP items has several levels: assets and liabilities, functional category, type of a financial instrument, institutional sector, and original maturity of debt instrument. Direct investment Direct investment is a category of cross-border investment associated with a resident of one economy having control or a significant degree of influence on the management of an enterprise that is resident in another economy (owns equity that entitles it to 10 or more percent of the voting power in the direct investment enterprise). Direct investment transactions and positions by type of financial instrument are divided into equity and investment fund shares (including reinvestment earnings) and debt investments. Data on direct investment of deposit taking corporations are made available to the National Bank from reporting of such corporations. For direct investment accounting in other sectors the Belstat survey on investment in the Republic of Belarus from abroad and investment of the Republic of Belarus abroad, data from the international transaction reporting system (ITRS), reporting of other financial corporations, administrative data, data from the partner countries, data from international organizations are used. Portfolio investment Portfolio investment is defined as cross-border transactions and positions involving debt or equity securities, other than those included in direct investment or reserve assets. The main sources of information for portfolio investment are data from the international transaction reporting system (ITRS), reporting of deposit-taking corporations, reporting of other financial corporations, data of the Ministry of Finance, the Belstat survey on investment in the Republic of Belarus from abroad and investment of the Republic of Belarus abroad, data from the partner countries, data from international organizations. Financial derivatives Financial derivatives are financial instruments that are linked to another specific (underlying) instrument through which specific financial risks (such as interest rate risk, foreign exchange risk, equity and commodity price risk, credit risk, etc.) can be traded in their own right in financial market. Financial derivatives are divided into forward contracts and options. Data are compiled on the basis of surveys of the National Bank and other deposit-taking corporations. Other investment This item shows a residual investment category that includes positions and transactions other than those included in direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives and reserve assets. This item is classified under the following types of financial instruments: other equity, currency and deposits; loans; nonlife insurance technical reserves, life insurance and annuities entitlements, pension entitlements, and provisions for calls under standardized guarantees; trade credit and advances; other accounts receivable/payable; special drawing rights allocations. Other equity is included in other investment, when it is not direct investment or reserve assets. Other equity is not in the form of securities, so it is not included in portfolio investment. Participation in some international organizations is not in the form of securities and so it is classified as other equity. Currency consists of notes and coins that are of a fixed nominal values and are issued or authorized by central banks or governments. Deposits include all claims that are (a) on the central bank, deposit-taking corporations other than the central bank, and, in some cases, other institutional units, and represented by evidence of deposit. Data on currency and deposits of the National Bank, banks and non-banking credit and financial institutions of the Republic of Belarus are compiled on the basis of balance sheets, reporting of other financial corporations. Moreover, the National Bank measures changes in the volume of foreign cash in the sector of nonfinancial corporations, households, and nonprofit institutions servicing households of the Republic of Belarus. Loans are financial assets that (a) are created when a creditor lends funds directly to a debtor, and are evidenced by documents that are not negotiable. This category covers all loans, including those under overdraft facilities, except accounts receivable/payable, which are treated as a separate category of financial assets. The sources of information on loans and borrowings are: for the central bank – the National Bank's reporting on foreign assets and liabilities; for the general government sector – reporting of the Ministry of Finance on external public debt; for the sector of deposit-taking corporations – the consolidated balance of banks and nonbank credit and financial corporations; for other sectors – the Belstat survey on investment in the Republic of Belarus from abroad and investment of the Republic of Belarus abroad, reporting of other financial corporations, data from the partner countries. Insurance, pension, and standardized guarantees schemes consist of nonlife insurance technical reserves, life insurance and annuity entitlements, pension entitlement, claims of pension funds on pension managers, and entitlement to nonpension funds, and provisions for calls under standardized guarantees. Nonlife insurance technical reserves include a reserve for unearned premiums, which are prepayment of premiums; reserves against outstanding insurance claims, which are amounts identified by insurance corporations to cover what they expect to pay out arising from events that have occurred but for which the claims are not yet settled. Data on insurance, pension, and standardized guarantees schemes are compiled from reporting of insurance companies. Trade credit and advances include credit extended directly by the suppliers of goods and services to their customers, advances for work that is in progress (or is yet to be undertaken) and prepayment by customers for goods and services not yet provided. The Belstat survey on nonfinancial corporations, reporting of other financial corporations, data from the partner countries are used to compile data on trade loans and advances. Other accounts receivable/payable include accounts receivable or accounts payable not classified other than those included in trade credit and advances or other instruments. They cover liabilities for taxes, purchase and sale of securities, securities lending fees, gold loan fees, wages and salaries, dividends, and social contributions that have accrued but not yet paid. This category includes prepayment on those items. Reporting of the National Bank, banks and nonbank credit and financial institutions, other financial corporations, the Belstat information based on survey on nonfinancial corporations, data of the Ministry of Finance are used for compiling data on other accounts receivable/payable. Special drawing rights represent international reserve assets created by the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and allocated to members to supplement to existing official reserves. The a llocation of special drawing rights to members is recorded as the incurrence of a liability in other investment. Assets and allocation of special drawing rights are recognized on the balance sheet of the National Bank. Reserve assets are external assets that are readily available to and controlled by monetary authorities for meeting balance of payments financing needs, for intervention in exchange markets to affect the currency exchange rate, and for other related purposes (such as maintaining confidence in the currency and the economy, and serving as a basis for foreign borrowing). Reserve assets are compiled on the basis of the National Bank's reporting on foreign assets and liabilities, information on precious metals, market value of gold, credit rating of nonresident counterparties, data of the Ministry of Finance, and data of the International Monetary Fund. Distinction between long-term and short-term financial assets and liabilities for debt instruments is based on the original maturity criterion: maturity of short-term instruments is a year or less, and maturity of long-term instruments is more than a year. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The timetable of source data input ensures timely compilation of IIP data and comply with the requirements of the Special Data Dissemination Standard of the International Monetary Fund (SDDS) pertaining to dissemination of data of the external sector. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Compilers of the IIP assess source data on a consistent basis and initiate the source data modification process when necessary, to improve statistical reporting. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The IIP statistics are compiled on the basis of summarized statistical information and is supplemented with the National Bank's estimates when necessary. Direct investment : estimated figure of reinvested earnings calculated on the basis of profit and loss statements of banks and nonbank credit and financial institutions, and the National Bank's information on equity interest of a foreign investor in a bank and nonbank credit and financial institution is included in the volume of direct investment of banks. Data derived from administrative sources on sale and purchase of shares, stocks, and equities, real estate owned by the state are included in the volume of direct investment of the general government sector. The direct investment volume of other sectors includes the volume of households' real estate investment abroad, as well as the volume of investment of nonresident households that have acquired real estate in the Republic of Belarus. When a nonresident country builds large projects in the territory of a resident country (and vice versa) (construction of bridges, dams, electric power plants, main pipelines, etc.), information derived from mass media can be used. The National Bank measures changes in foreign cash volume in the sector of nonfinancial corporations, households, and nonprofit institutions servicing households of the Republic of Belarus. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The data is not seasonally adjusted. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Intermediate results are regularly checked against other reports at the disposal of the National Bank. The IIP statistics are compared with monetary statistics, government finance statistics, and real sector statistics. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Intermediate data are analyzed in terms of internal and temporal consistency. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs When unaccountable wide variation of data occurs, its causes are studies through direct contacts with information providers. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Data for the period under review are intermediate when first released. The data revision policy for the IIP statistics is determined by data revision rules and described in the National Bank's quarterly data analytics bulletin "The Balance of Payments, International Investment Position, and Gross External Debt of the Republic of Belarus", though notes are made that the IIP statistics may be modified in future publications. A separate annex contains the list of modifications introduced in the IIP statistics, indicating the reason and period for which they have been introduced (quarterly), per each item. Modifications may be introduced due to update of reported data, changes in methodology or methods of calculation of certain indicators, development of new sources of information, reclassification of instruments (indicators), update of reported data upon agreement with partner countries, for other reasons. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Historical data series of the IIP from 1996 to 2011 were made accessible in accordance with the fifth edition of The Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM5) and starting from 2000 to the present day in accordance with BPM6. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished non-confidential statistics may be provided on request. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The metadata placed on the National Bank's official Internet site and the quarterly data analytics bulletin "The Balance of Payments, International Investment Position, and Gross External Debt of the Republic of Belarus" specify the name, telephone numbers, e-mail address of the contact person. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The list of publications of the National Bank is available on the National Bank's official Internet site.
