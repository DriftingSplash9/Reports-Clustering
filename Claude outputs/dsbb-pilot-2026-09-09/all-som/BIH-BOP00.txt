 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Compilation and dissemination of Balance of Payments is done according to the internationally recognized standards, set by the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and most recently published in 2009 in the sixth edition of Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6). Definition: The balance of payments is a statistical statement that systematically summarizes, for a specific time period, the economic transactions of residents of Bosnia and Herzegovina with the rest of the world. The standard components of BoP, as presented in the BPM6, are goods and services account, primary and secondary income accounts, capital account and financial account. The different accounts within the balance of payments are distinguished according to the nature of the economic resources provided and received. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization BOP statistics are highly consistent with internationally accepted standards and guidelines. In principle, transactions are classified and recorded in accordance with the BPM6. Standard components are classified in four main categories: current account, capital account, financial account, and net errors and omissions, where the last one is calculated as a residual category. BOP transactions are compiled by sectors as recommended in BPM6. Transactions identified by sector are assigned to the General Government, Financial corporations, nonfinancial corporations, households, and NPISHs for the current account classification, while in the financial account, transactions are distributed between the following sectors: Central Bank; Deposit-taking corporations, except central bank; General government; and Other sectors including Other financial corporations and Nonfinancial corporations, households, and NPISHs. In compilation of services item classifications and recording of transactions is done using MSITS 2010. 2.2.1 Scope BOP statistics are highly consistent with internationally accepted standards and guidelines. In principle, transactions are classified and recorded in accordance with the BPM6. Standard components are classified in four main categories: current account, capital account, financial account, and net errors and omissions, where the last one is calculated as a residual category. BOP transactions are compiled by sectors as recommended in BPM6. Transactions identified by sector are assigned to the General Government, Financial corporations, nonfinancial corporations, households, and NPISHs for the current account classification, while in the financial account, transactions are distributed between the following sectors: Central Bank; Deposit-taking corporations, except central bank; General government; and Other sectors including Other financial corporations and Nonfinancial corporations, households, and NPISHs. In compilation of services item classifications and recording of transactions is done using MSITS 2010. Scope of the data The geographic territory of reference is the territory of Bosnia and Herzegovina (BH). Residency principle, as defined in the BPM6 manual, represents the basic principle followed for recording of balance of payments transactions for BH. Data are disseminated in millions KM, showing exports and imports of goods, exports and imports of services and the balance of goods and services, net primary income, net secondary income, and the capital and financial account, i.e. change in official reserves assets and financial transactions (direct investments, portfolio investments, financial derivatives and other investments). The data cover the entire BH economy. Transaction coverage: All transactions, as recommended in BPM6 are included in the balance of payments. Sectorial coverage: The sector classification of the portfolio and other investment involves the sector classification of residents according to ESA 2010. It is fully harmonized with the sector classification of the gross external debt by domestic sectors and the IIP. Geographical coverage: A geographical attribution of the data is not available for partner countries. Exceptions to coverage The compilation of portfolio investment data in BoP is incomplete concerning coverage. The direct investment survey is developed with the ability to compile data on portfolio investment as well. Thus, covered are only portfolio investment of the companies with direct investment. As an additional data source MFS data are used for DTC and other financial corporations. Assets of monetary authorities in the form of debt securities are included in reserve assets. Regarding assets and liabilities of General government sector, data are obtained from Government Finance and Financial Accounts Statistics. Unrecorded activity Unrecorded activities are estimated using different statistical models (based on correlation with similar data series compiled through surveys, from administrative data sources, ITRS, etc.). 2.4.1 Valuation Most of transactions recorded in the BOP are valued at market prices. The exception is the recording of currency and deposits, item which is compiled using monetary and financial statistics of stock values. As high degree of deposits in foreign currency are in euros, and due to the fixed exchange rate (currency board with fixed exchange rate of KM against Euro; 1KM=0,51EUR), the discrepancy between the value of actual transactions and the value resulting from the difference between stock at the end, and at the beginning of period is null or very low. Transactions recorded in other currencies are converted to the KM using exchange rates at the time of the transaction, where applicable. Export and import of goods are provided by the BHAS. Export of goods is valued at f.o.b. parity, while the import of goods compiled by BHAS is valued at c.i.f. parity and in the CBBH it is estimated at f.o.b. parity. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording is on an accrual basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Grossing and netting procedures, followed in CBBH's presentation of data, correspond to BPM6 guiding principle. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The BOP statement is compiled in conformity with the methodology set forth in BPM6. Primary data sources for compiling BOP statistics are: - Direct Surveys: survey on direct investments, survey on trade credits and advances, survey on transportation services, survey on insurance and reinsurance services, survey on postal and telecommunication services, survey on manufacturing services on physical inputs owned by others, airline services survey. - Statistical and Administrative sources - (publications of the ECB, BIS bank, OECD, EUFOR, money transfer organizations, embassies and international organizations in BH, and statistical bureaus of the countries from the region, and various BH state ministries, BH statistical institutions, pension and disability insurance funds, NOS - Independent System Operator, Service for Foreigner's Affairs, Directorate of Civil Aviation etc.) - Statistics compiled within the CBBH (Monetary and Financial Statistics, Government Finance and Financial Accounts Statistics, and data received from Banking Department, Cash Management Department, Monitoring and Analyses Department and Accounting and Finance Department) - ITRS, (monthly aggregated reporting based on coding of transactions provided by the CBBH) used only if there is no other reliable data source. These data collection programs proved to be comprehensive providing reliable and accurate data. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The framework for compilation of the BOP fully follows the concepts and definitions prescribed in BPM6 and for services item additionally IMTS 2010. Direct surveys (survey on direct investment, survey on trade credits and advance payments) are prepared and conducted in line with recommendations stated in BPM6 and OECD benchmark definition of DI fourth edition. These surveys have detailed explanations and definition of terms used. Accrual recording, appropriate valuation method, classifications and time of recording are required by the surveys and met to a high degree by reporting units. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The frequency and timeliness of primary data sources for compiling BOP statistics are: - Survey on direct investments – quarterly frequency with 45 days timeliness, - Survey on direct investments – annual frequency with 6 months timeliness, - Survey on trade credits and advances – annual frequency with timeliness by the end of March, - Survey on transportation services – annual frequency with timeliness by the end of April, - Survey on insurance and reinsurance services - quarterly frequency and timeliness two months after the end of quarter, - Survey on postal and telecommunication services - quarterly frequency and timeliness two months after the end of quarter, - Survey on manufacturing services on physical inputs owned by others – annual frequency with timeliness by the end of March, - Survey on airline services – quarterly frequency with the timeliness two months after the end of quarter, - ITRS - monthly frequency with timeliness of 20 days. 3.2.1 Source data assessment ITRS data are assessed against transactions collected through surveys. Transactions of SDR are assessed against IMF SDR accounts. Majority of surveys listed under 3.1.3 are full coverage (census) surveys. Data on direct, portfolio and other investment collected through survey on direct investment are assessed against Monetary and financial statistics for Deposit Taking Corporation and Other Financial Corporation sectors. Data on direct investment, obtained through the survey, are assessed against the data from financial statements of enterprises, following positive law regulations collected by FIA (Financial Information Agency) and APIF (Intermediary Agency for IT and financial services), two official agencies which are in charge of collecting financial statements of enterprises. Assessment refers to the data on profit and loss, reinvested earnings, loans and dividends. This assessment improves the coverage of the companies with small amount of registered equity, but significant amount of reinvested earnings and other capital, including loans. Data on external debt of government sector obtained from Government Finance Statistics are also assessed against data from BH Ministry of Finance and Treasury. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Goods: The CBBH makes all necessary adjustments to the data on exports and imports compiled by the Agency for BH statistics based on the Special Trade System. The adjustments made to the statistical data are as follows: classification adjustment of imports (insurance and freight components are deducted from goods imports provided at c.i.f. value to arrive to f.o.b. value); exclusion of goods for processing without change of ownership; exclusion of migrants' personal effects; inclusion of estimates for illegal or smuggled goods; exclusion of exports/imports of international organizations located in the economy. Services: CBBH estimates of manufacturing services on physical inputs owned by others are based on annual survey while quarterly estimates are based on historical data and trade data provided by the Agency for BH statistics. Transportation services for the BOP are compiled through a direct survey (road, railway and air transportation), ITRS and from the Agency for BH statistics. All BH companies registered to provide international transport are surveyed. ITRS, as well as statistical agency data are used as additional sources of data, particularly for debit side. In the travel account, data on travel credits and debits are estimated based on data collected from different sources (ITRS, BH statistical Agency, partner country statistical agencies, and websites of some tourist-oriented locations). Data on construction services are estimated using different data sources (BH statistical agency, ITRS, and data from international military forces residing in BH). Data on Insurance and pension services are mostly compiled through direct survey. Additional source of data for estimates of this kind of services is also the ITRS. Financial services data are collected through the ITRS and from the CBBH accounting office. FISIM is estimated based on DI statistics and BIS bank data. Data on use of intellectual property are derived from the ITRS while data on telecommunications, computer and information services are beside the ITRS based on direct survey. All telecommunication companies providing these services are covered with the survey. Estimates of other business services, as well as personal, cultural and recreational services are based on data collected through the ITRS, while estimates of government goods and services items are based on administrative-based records collected from the responsible state level ministry. Primary Income: Estimates of compensation of employees (following the one-year residence principle) cover compensations received from BH residents employed in international organizations, foreign embassies and military bases located in the BH economy. Also, compensation of seasonal/border workers is included in the overall estimates. Direct investment income data (interests) are obtained as results of the DI survey conducted on quarterly basis, while dividends and reinvestments of earnings are collected through the annual survey (for quarterly BOP data are estimated). Other investment income items are based on data collected from the CBBH accounting office, BH Ministry of Finance and Treasury, commercial banks, microcredit institutions, and BIS bank reports. Secondary Income: Data on personal transfers are estimated on the basis of transfers from/to abroad and collected through the ITRS and the Western Union System. The findings of panel poll on households "Living in BH" conducted by the BH Agency for statistics represent the base for additional, estimates of personal transfers through informal channels. For this estimate additional data set is number of refugees and displaced persons as well as BH emigrants, collected from the Ministry for Human Rights and Refugees. Capital Account: Data for these items presented by donors are collected from the OECD. Financial account: Direct investment: CBBH conduct a regular quarterly (on a sample) and annual (full coverage of companies with DI exceeding a KM 100,000 threshold) direct survey on DI. The survey includes financial and non–financial corporations which have, or are assumed to have, foreign investment from abroad, which invest abroad or are borrowing funds from foreign countries. For the companies with direct investment below the threshold, CBBH makes estimates based on data obtained by courts on registered invested foreign capital. The survey is conducted in accordance with BPM6 and OECD benchmark definition of DI fourth edition. According to the applied IMF methodology retained earnings are presented on a quarterly basis for the period when earned. Data on profits and reinvested earnings are collected on annual basis and published within the deadline T + 8 months. For quarterly BOP reinvested earnings of other financial and non-financial corporations are estimated on the basis of an aggregated estimate and available information on distributed dividends. The annual aggregated value is projected, based on previous periods and index of industrial production (published by BH Agency for Statistics). For DTC sector data on reinvested earnings for each particular bank are estimated based on available cumulative data on profit and distributed dividends for past period of current year provided by the MFS. Estimated data are subject to revisions once annual survey results are obtained. Portfolio investment: The direct investment survey is developed with the ability to compile data on portfolio investment, as well. However, compilation of portfolio investment data in this respect is incomplete regarding coverage. The survey covers only portfolio investment of the companies with direct investment. As an additional data source for DTC and other financial corporations, MFS data are used while for the non-financial corporations ITRS is additional data source. For the General government sector data are obtained from (GFFAS) Government Finance and Financial Accounts Statistics. Assets of monetary authorities in the form of debt securities are included in reserve assets. Other investments: The data mostly obtained from the Monetary and Financial Statistics, GFFAS, CBBH Banking Department. Data from Bank for International Settlements (BIS) quarterly reports are used as a source of currency and deposits of non-financial corporation. Data on other investment for other sectors are obtained from the CBBH survey on direct investment (which covers data on other investment of companies), and data on loans of nonfinancial corporations provided by Entities Ministries of Finance. Data on trade credits and advances for DTC and Other financial corporations are obtained from Monetary and Financial Statistics. The data for non-financial corporations are compiled through the CBBH direct quarterly survey on trade credits and advance payments. Trade credits and advance payments survey covers the biggest importers and exporters. Data on trade credits collected through DI survey are crosschecked with data collected through survey on trade credits and advance payments to avoid overlaps, and these are treated as direct investments. Reserve assets: Source data obtained from the CBBH Banking Department, Monitoring and Analyses Department and Accounting Department. Coverage is fully complete and data are reliable and accurate with full respect of accrual recording. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Balance of payments data are not seasonally adjusted. Export and import of goods are provided by the BHAS. Export of goods is valued at f.o.b. parity, while the import of goods compiled by BHAS is valued at c.i.f. parity and in the CBBH it is estimated at f.o.b. parity. Needed source data for the c.i.f. -f.o.b. adjustment is collected through the survey on trade credits and advance payments. Results of annual surveys are used as bases for estimates of quarterly data. Trade credits and advance payments survey as well as survey on transportation services are conducted on a sample and statistical techniques are used to estimate data for entire population. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Source data used for the compilation of BOP are reliable and accurate to the extent possible. The population used as a base for a sample, the creation of sample, response rates, coverage, and accuracy of reported data are regularly monitored and updated. Thus, data sources as well as data itself are considered comprehensive and reliable. Results of the assessments carried out are monitored and made available to guide future statistical processes. Validation of intermediate results are applied as follows: - Logical and mathematical data validation in surveys. Opening balance should be in line with the closing balance that are reported in the past period. Closing balance must be equal to sum of opening balance, net financial transactions and other changes. - Cross-checking of different source data, and if there are substantial discrepancies investigation is conducted to check if certain transactions are unrecorded, or coverage and scope issues exist, or there are some valuation and time of recording issues. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Assessment of discrepancies is conducted as follows: - Data on direct investment, obtained through the survey, are assessed against the data from financial statements of enterprises, collected by FIA (Financial Information Agency) and APIF (Intermediary Agency for IT and financial services), two official agencies who are in charge of collecting financial statements of enterprises. Assessment refers to the data on profit and loss, reinvested earnings, loans and dividends. - Data on direct, portfolio and other investment collected through survey on direct investment are assessed against Monetary and financial statistics for sector of Deposit Taking Corporation and Other Financial Corporation. - Data on external debt of government sector obtained from Government Finance Statistics are also assessed against data from Ministry of Finance and Treasury BH. Discrepancies are analysed jointly with GFS and MFS Sections, and where possible corrections are made. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses No formal revision studies are carried out, but data is regularly revised and updated.
