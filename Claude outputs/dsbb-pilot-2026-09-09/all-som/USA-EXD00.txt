 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The data are compiled in accordance with the IMF's External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (External Debt Guide), 2013, a copy of which is available in the DSBB. The data are compiled in accordance with the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6). Debts are the gross external banking and portfolio debt liabilities outstanding as of the reporting date. Contingent liabilities and the negative fair value of derivative contracts are excluded. Repurchase agreements are treated as collateralized loans. Banking and portfolio debt includes debt between unaffiliated institutions, debt between U.S.-resident banks and affiliated institutions, and debt between other U.S.-resident financial institutions and affiliated financial institutions. Other liabilities between affiliated institutions are classified as direct investment. In the computation of debt service: Debt without a specific maturity date is considered to continually roll over, with interest in the "over 2 years" column of Table C calculated through year 7 only. See also metadata: ( https://dsbb.imf.org/sddsplus/dqaf-base/country/USA/category/EXD00 ). Scope of the data The data covers all debt liabilities of U.S. residents to the rest of the world. For purposes of these gross external debt data, the United States consists of the 50 States, the District of Columbia, U.S. territories and possessions, and U.S. Government military, diplomatic, and consular facilities abroad. The U.S territories and possessions are the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico, the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands, and the following: American Samoa, Baker Island, Guam, Howland Island, Jarvis Island, Johnston Atoll, Kingman Reef, Midway Islands, Navassa Island, Palmyra Atoll, the U.S. Virgin Islands, and Wake Island. The basic criterion for distinguishing "foreign" or "foreigner" is consistent with the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual, Sixth Edition (BPM6). Specifically, "foreign" or foreigner" includes: (1) Individuals, including U.S. citizens, residing outside the U.S. and its overseas territories and possessions; (2) Entities that file an IRS Form W-8 indicating that the individual or entity is a nonresident alien or foreign entity not subject to certain U.S. information return reporting or to backup withholding rules; (3) Branches, subsidiaries and other affiliates of U.S.-incorporated entities located outside the U.S.; (4) Foreign governments and any subdivision, agency or instrumentality thereof, including all foreign official nonbanking institutions, even if located in the U.S. (e.g., an embassy, consulate, or other diplomatic establishment of a foreign country), and (5) Official international or regional organizations, and affiliated agencies thereof, created by treaty or convention between sovereign states, even if located in the U.S., such as the World Bank, the IMF, and the United Nations. 2.4.1 Valuation Foreign currency denominated liabilities are converted to U.S. dollars using the spot exchange rate quoted on the last day of the reporting period. 2.4.2 Recording basis Debt liabilities are valued at face value as of the reporting date. Liabilities are reported using settlement date accounting. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Debt securities: Data are compiled using information from annual surveys of security-by-security positions and from monthly and quarterly aggregate positions data. The monthly aggregate positions data are collected at market value for long-term securities and at face value for short-term securities. Data are collected from reports filed by banks, bank holding companies, and securities brokers and dealers, investment managers, and other large non-financial sector borrowers. In the detailed surveys, foreign holdings of U.S. securities are collected exclusive of accrued interest. General government liabilities, other than debt securities held by foreigners: Data on other general government liabilities are collected by the Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA). Debt data cover only the federal government, and are believed to be complete. It is believed that local governments have no external debt other than debt securities. The debt data is primarily from deposits of foreigners arising from military sales operations. No information is collected on the currency composition of debt or on the debt service schedule. Monetary authorities: The only data for this sector is believed to be for Currency and Deposits, with almost this entire category accounted for by foreign holdings of U.S. currency. A substantial amount of U.S. currency is held outside the United States by foreign individuals, firms, and governments. These holdings are estimated by marking and tracking a sample of issued currency. Banking and “Other” sectors, excluding long-term debt securities: These data are calculated from Treasury International Capital (TIC) monthly and quarterly data. This data includes information on the currency composition and debt service. The coverage for the banking sector is comprehensive. The coverage for the “other” sector is derived partly from custody data and therefore may not be as complete. For that reason, an estimate is added for debt missed by the TIC system that is owed by the other sector to foreign banks. That estimate is based on foreign source data that does not include information on the currency composition or debt service schedule. For the external position of the banking system, claims and liabilities positions are reported on a gross basis at the face amount of the instrument. These external positions exclude balances arising from banks' issuance of long-term debt securities (securities with an original maturity of more than 1 year). Accrued interest is reported if past due or credited to another account on the reporters' books. Claims are reported net of any charge-offs or specific reserves. Transactions or positions payable in foreign currencies are converted into U.S. dollar equivalents using the spot exchange rates at the close of business on the as-of-date of the particular report. No data are reported in foreign currency units. The TIC monthly and quarterly “banking” data on foreign positions include own claims and liabilities vis-a-vis foreign residents and specified items held in custody for domestic and foreign customers reported by U.S. offices of banks and other depository institutions (including commercial banks; banking Edge Act and Agreement Corporations; branches, agencies and banking subsidiaries of foreign banks; building or savings and loan institutions; mutual or stock savings banks; cooperative banks; credit unions; homestead associations; and consumer banks), plus International Banking Facilities (IBFs), bank holding companies, and securities brokers and dealers located in the U.S. These reporting entities also provide data on positions in short-term debt securities. In addition, the TIC system collects quarterly data on financial and commercial claims and liabilities vis-à-vis foreigners reported by non-banking entities. Direct investment: Data on direct investment are collected by the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) by means of mandatory surveys of U.S. direct investment abroad and of foreign direct investment in the United States. The most comprehensive data on loans and other liabilities are collected in benchmark surveys conducted at five-year intervals of U.S. direct investment abroad and of foreign direct investment in the United States. BEA also conducts annual and quarterly surveys. Estimates are updated mainly using the results of quarterly surveys. Results from annual direct investment surveys are also used to update or supplement the benchmark liability position estimates. Intercompany liabilities of direct investment enterprises are carried forward at their benchmark year levels, unless BEA receives a quarterly report or other information indicating that benchmark levels have changed. The data are reported and compiled at face value. No information is collected on the currency composition of debt or on the debt service schedule. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Debt Securities: Detailed surveys are conducted annually with data collected on a security-by-security basis, which allows for extensive data checking and editing. The detailed security-level data provides information on sector of issuer, currency, coupon rate, and maturity schedules for debt securities held by foreign residents. Every fifth year extensive "benchmark" surveys are conducted, attempting to collect data from all major institutional units. In the intervening four years, data are collected from the largest reporting units on the preceding benchmark survey; those largest reporters account for about 97 percent of the data reported on the prior benchmark survey. Data are collected primarily from U.S.-based custodians (primarily banks and broker-dealers), but data are also collected from investment managers and from U.S. entities that issue securities where these holdings are not routed through a U.S.-based custodian. Benchmark survey data are believed to be quite complete, missing only information on investments held directly with smaller U.S. securities issuers where such holdings are believed to be very small. The security level surveys are conducted as of end-June of each year. Results are available with a 9-12 month lag. Foreign holdings of long-term U.S. securities for other quarter-end months and preliminary estimates for end-June before a new survey is available are derived from aggregate long-term debt securities positions data reported monthly on the TIC Form SLT. The SLT data are available with roughly a 75-day lag. Data on foreign holdings of U.S. securities on the SLT are collected primarily from the same custodians, investment managers, and issuers that report on the annual surveys; the exemption level for reporting on TIC SLT is $1 billion for combined cross-border holdings of U.S. and foreign securities. Foreign holdings of short-term U.S. securities positions are reported on the same TIC forms that provide the monthly "banking" and quarterly "other sectors" data. Estimates of currency compositions and debt service schedules for both long-term and short-term securities are obtained from the most recent annual surveys and applied to the quarter-end positions. Banking and "Other" sectors: "Other" sector debt - monthly and quarterly data are supplemented by the use of international banking statistics to estimate foreign bank loans to the U.S. other sector that are presumably missed by domestic data collections. (See the first paragraph in the section on "Banking and Other Sectors" above.) Most of the reports that underlie the data for the banking and other sectors are collected by the Federal Reserve Banks from reporters in their area. This includes the Treasury International Capital (TIC) data, where the Federal Reserve Banks collect data on behalf of the Treasury. The data are extensively edited for validity and quality, and there is a process for checking edit failures. The TIC data are stored at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York in electronic data archives. Micro archival data are then aggregated in various ways to produce the macro debt data. In general, the TIC source data for the banking and "other" sectors aggregates are taken as reported and added up. There are no sample blowups involved. Much more detail on the data editing operations are described in the Compilation Practices section of the United States' Summary Methodology webpage on the Analytical accounts of the banking sector (Depository corporations survey) at: ( https://dsbb.imf.org/sddsplus/dqaf-base/country/USA/category/AAB00 ). The data collected by the TIC system are based on certain thresholds. All reporting entities are required to submit their respective reports (monthly, quarterly, semi-annual) of all covered amounts once the reportable amounts meet or exceed established thresholds. With regard to the "banking" data on claims and liabilities vis-a-vis foreigners, as of January 2001, these thresholds are either $50 million or more in the aggregate or $25 million or more with respect to any individual country. With regard to the "non-banking" data on claims and liabilities vis-a-vis foreigners, as of January 2003, these thresholds are $50 million or more in the aggregate for either financial claims or liabilities, and $25 million or more in the aggregate for either commercial claims or liabilities. Regarding long-term securities, the TIC system requires monthly data from all U.S. entities on transactions in long-term debt securities when the total of purchases or sales amount to $50 million or more during the reporting month. Once a reporting entity meets or exceeds the reporting threshold in any specified reporting period, the entity must continue to submit forms for each subsequent reporting period of that year, regardless of the amounts. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures There are no seasonal adjustments.
