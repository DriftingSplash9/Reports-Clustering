I. Analytical Framework, Concepts, Definitions, and Classifications
 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Merchandise trade statistics are compiled in accordance with recommendations on International Trade Statistics Concepts and Definitions of the United Nations. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Data are disseminated by commodity classification, volume, value, supplementary quantity where applicable and by country of origin/destination. Commodities are classified using both the Harmonised System (HS) and Standard International Trade Classification (SITC) Rev. 3 & Rev. 4. 

II. Scope of the data
 Scope of the data Tabulated statistics on merchandise imports, domestic exports and re-exports are published quarterly and annually however, monthly data are available upon request from the contact person.. Merchandise Trade covers the movement of goods to and from St Vincent and the Grenadines and the rest of the world via sea, air and parcel post (i.e., goods passing through the post office). Items traded include the value of improvements and repairs to goods, non-monetary gold, and goods leased for a period exceeding one year. Trade statistics is compiled using the special system of trade. Hence goods are only considered imports after they have been cleared by the Customs and Excise Department. Exceptions to coverage Imports data excludes all goods brought into the country but are held in customs bonded warehouses, likewise goods re-exported from customs warehouses are excluded from exports. 

III. Accounting Conventions
 2.4.1 Valuation The valuation of imports is c.i.f and total exports – the sum of domestic exports and re-exports – are recorded at f.o.b. 

IV. Nature of the Basic Data Sources
 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The primary source of data is the customs declarations, which are entered into the Automated System for Customs Data (ASYCUDA). The declaration files are downloaded monthly directly from the Customs Department via the internet.  The data are processed by the Statistical Office using the EUROTRACE computerised trade processing system. In addition, information on banana exports are obtained from Winfarm (formerly the Banana Growers Association (BGA)) and export of fish is obtained from the Fisheries Department. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The Customs declaration files are usually available one week from the end of the previous month. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Batch checks and unit checks are made within the declaration files.  Files are then distributed electronically to Statistical Assistants for corrections to be made using the customs warrants. 

V. Compilation Practices
 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Corrections are made within the declaration files using the customs warrants. Discrepancies are dealt with before data are disseminated.
