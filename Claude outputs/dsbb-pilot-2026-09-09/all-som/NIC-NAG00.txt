 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Nicaragua’s national accounts are compiled in accordance with the United Nations System of National Accounts 1993 ( SNA93 ). In 2003 the base year for national accounts was changed from 1980 to 1994. Data are compiled on an annual and quarterly basis. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization A national system for the classification of activities and products, known as the CNIC, was developed for the compilation of Nicaragua’s national accounts. The CNIC is fully compatible with the International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC) and the Central Products Classification (CPC), which ensure international comparability among countries that have adopted these classification systems. The national classification system was developed for purposes of disaggregating data on the country’s main economic activities, taking into account their contribution to GDP and job creation. The four-digit level of ISIC Rev. 3, for example, divides agriculture into: growing of cereals and other crops n.e.c.; growing of vegetables, horticultural specialties and nursery products; growing of fruit, nuts, beverage and spice crops; whereas the CNIC divides agricultural activities into the growing of: coffee, sugar cane, corn, rice, beans, sorghum, bananas, soybeans, peanuts, sesame seeds, etc. In addition, the Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP) is used to classify household consumption by purpose, the Classification of the Functions of Government (COFOG) to classify government functions, and the Central American Tariff System (SAC) and the Classification by Economic Use or Purpose (CUODE) to classify international transactions. Scope of the data The entire country is covered. Includes all market and nonmarket production, as well as production for own use. Gross value added includes the adjustment deducted for financial intermediation services, which are measure indirectly (SIFMI). Includes all production activities within the economic territory of the country, including activities considered informal. Unrecorded activity Illegal activities such as smuggling are not recorded. 2.4.1 Valuation In Nicaragua’s new national accounts, as recommended by the SNA 93, production is valued at basic prices, mainly because they record the amounts actually received by producers for the sale of their output. Also, the purchaser price is used to value both intermediate and final uses. In the goods and services account, exports are valued on an f.o.b. basis, and imports on a c.i.f. basis, although the SNA 93 recommends also showing total imports valued at f.o.b., which is why the c.i.f./f.o.b. adjustment is made to total imports. In 2003 Nicaragua officially changed the base year of the national accounts from 1980 to 1994 and adopted the United Nations System of National Accounts ( SNA93 ) for the calculation of GDP. This base year includes detailed information representative of all economic activities and transactions that take place within the country’s borders among the various sectors of the economy. The data are obtained through a series of surveys of institutions and enterprises, censuses, financial statements, and tax and foreign trade databases. The data are used to prepare production accounts for 84 economic activities as well as supply and demand analyses (market balances) for 150 products and/or groups of products. With this information, the value of national production is calculated separately for each year and included in the supply and use tables. GDP is calculated at the national level using the production, expenditure (current and constant values), and income (current values) approaches. 2.4.2 Recording basis Transactions between economic agents are recorded on an accrual basis, with the exception of certain government purchases that are recorded on a cash basis. The recording period refers to the calendar year. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The data sources for preparing the production accounts and the supply and use balances are the following: Production approach Agriculture, livestock, fishing, and forestry: 1.- Survey of crop year production (first, second, and third harvests) conducted annually by the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry (MAGFOR) in cooperation with the Central Bank of Nicaragua (CBN) since 1994; the 1998 survey of the production of nontraditional crops. 2.- Survey of agricultural costs targeting exportable agro crops; coffee crop year 1996/97; sugar cane and banana exports for 1997. The 1997 survey of livestock costs; the 2000 survey of oilseed costs and the production costs of wood and firewood harvesting. 3.- MAGFOR bulletins on prices and markets and technological charts of various agricultural crops of the former Banco Nacional de Desarrollo (BANADES). 4.- Third National Agricultural Survey of 2001, prepared by the National Institute of Statistics and Censuses (INEC). 5.- Final consumption vector of the household account, compiled on the basis of the INEC’s 1993 and 1998 national household standard of living survey (EMNV) and the BCN/INEC 1999 household income and expenditure survey (EIGH). 6.- BCN model for estimating the growth of the cattle population and milk production. 7.- MAGFOR municipal and industrial slaughter statistics 8.- Annual bulletin and first National Fishing and Aquaculture Census of 1995, both provided by the Fisheries Administration (AdPesca). 9.- Annual survey of fishing production costs conducted in 1997. 10.- Database of the Directorate General of Customs Services and the Directorate General of Revenue, which are processed to obtain exports, imports, and taxes on products according to Nicaragua’s product classification (CINC). 11.- Information from other institutions, enterprises, and producers associations. For primary sector items, volumes and prices are available for each year, which are used to determine current values. For each of the latter, the volume and price index is calculated, and then the values of each product at constant prices are obtained. Manufacturing: 1-. Annual manufacturing survey carried out since 1992, which provides information on legal status, production, prices, employment, inputs, changes in inventory, fixed capital flow, etc. 2.- Quarterly survey of economic sectors, used to supplement the information from enterprises not included in the annual survey. 3.- Monthly manufacturing survey, which was used to supplement information from enterprises not included in the annual and quarterly surveys; and to prepare the producer price index and physical production (or volume) indices. 4.- Corporación de Zonas Francas (free trade areas corporation), data on exports, imports, wages and employment from enterprises in the free trade area system. 5.- Database of the Directorate General of Customs Services and the Directorate General of Revenue, which are processed to obtain exports, imports, and taxes on products according to Nicaragua’s product classification (CINC). 6.- Final consumption vector of the household account, compiled on the basis of the INEC’s 1993 and 1998 national household standard of living survey (EMNV) and the BCN’s 1999 household income and expenditure survey (EIGH). 7.- Urban economic census of 1996, on which the sample framework and the preparation of expansion factors are based. 8.- MAGFOR municipal and industrial slaughter statistics. 9.- Monthly economic indicators of the CBN. 10.- Specific research and field visits to industrial enterprises that are leaders in their line of business. When data on production volumes and/or physical units are available, the gross value of production at constant prices is obtained by extrapolating from volume and/or quantity indices; otherwise, the corresponding price indices are used for deflating. Mining: The data sources for preparing the activities production accounts and the supply and use balances of these products are the annual mining sector report of the National Geological Resources Administration (AdGeo). Construction: The data for calculating the construction of residential and nonresidential private buildings are obtained from the quarterly private construction survey. The survey records square meters of new construction, which are valued using prices per square meter, compiled according to economic purpose. The annual data are obtained by aggregating the values for the four quarters. Public construction is calculated on the basis of data on public sector projects. Most of this information is provided by the Secretariat of Coordination and Strategy of the Office of the President, supplemented with information from other institutions such as the Alcaldía de Managua and the Nicaragua Social Security Institute, as well as the Ministry of Finance and Public Credit. A composite index is used to calculate public construction at constant prices. The index is calculated by weighting a construction materials price index that is representative of public construction, as well as a wage index for the same activity. Electricity: The gross value of production and the intermediate consumption of this activity are obtained by analyzing and classifying the items in the financial statements. The value of sales, production for own consumption, and the profit margin on sales of imported energy represent the gross value of production. Prices and quantities (KWH) of consumption per economic block (residential, commercial, industrial, government, irrigation, and pumping) are available for estimating values at constant prices. Collection, purification, and distribution of drinking water: The Nicaraguan Water and Sewerage Enterprise (ENACAL, formerly INAA) is responsible for drinking water and sanitation activities. The value of sales represents the gross value of production. The gross value of production and the intermediate consumption of this activity are obtained by analyzing and classifying the items in the financial statements provided by the enterprise. Prices and quantities (cubic meters) of consumption by economic block (domestic, industrial, commercial, and government) are available for calculating values at constant prices. Wholesale and retail trade, repairs of vehicles and personal and domestic articles: The annual trade survey is used to estimate the value of trade production. The results of the survey of marketing channels and gross margins attached to the 1997 annual trade survey are used to distribute the trade margin among the various groups of products. This survey yielded the distribution margins for each product group, which were subsequently divided into transport and trade margins. This research provided a structure of trade margins that was applied to the basic price of each component of demand in each year. The annual survey of services is used to calculate the gross values of production and intermediate consumption of repairs of vehicles and personal and domestic articles. Values at constant prices are obtained by estimating product volumes (by deflating current values or extrapolating base year values), to which the corresponding markups are applied. The sum of these values represents the gross value of production of total trade. In addition, the vehicle inventory provided by the National Police is used to value automobile repairs at constant prices. Hotels and restaurants: Gross values of production and intermediate consumption of these activities are calculated using the annual survey of services and reconciled with tourism statistics from the Nicaraguan Tourism Institute (INTUR), which contains a breakdown of tourism expenditure by foreigners in the country. The number of overnight stays by nationals and foreigners, provided by INTUR (a volume index) and an index of employment in service activities are used to calculate values at constant prices. Communications : The current values for the production and supply of inputs for this activity are obtained by analyzing and classifying the items in the financial statements. The gross value of production is the value of sales. For imports and exports, information on the rest of the world is available in the balance of payments. Cable television service included in this activity is obtained from the 1999 household survey. The structure of costs in the communications sector is used to prepare the production account. Starting in 2001, financial information is available from the two main cable television companies. Values at constant prices are obtained by deflating the transport and communications item with the transport and communications component of the consumer price index. Depending on its availability or robustness, a conventional telephony volume index is also used to validate the estimate. Transport: Information from the Ministry of Transport and Infrastructure (MTI) on the number of passengers carried annually and the fare per route are used to calculate the gross value of production. In the taxi subsector, figures are also available concerning the number of passengers, average fare, and cost structure, as well as the number of licensed taxis, which information are provided by a taxi cooperative and the MTI, respectively. The 1997 annual trade survey provided the distribution margins for each group of products, which were then divided into transport and trade margins. This research yielded a structure of transport margins, which was applied each year to the basic price of each demand component to obtain the level of freight transport production. The transport CPI is used to deflate current values. Financial intermediation and auxiliaries: The financial statements of the component institutions are used to prepare the production accounts for this sector. Total production consists of the explicit commissions collected by financial intermediaries and the implicit charges known as Financial Intermediation Services Indirectly Measured (FISIM), which are calculated as total property income receivable by financial intermediaries minus their total interest payable. This production is assigned to a fictitious sector. Values at constant prices are estimated for depository corporations, based on the flow of assets and liabilities, deflated by the CPI if the assets and liabilities in question are denominated in domestic currency. Assets and liabilities originally denominated in a foreign currency (but “cordobized”) are divided by the current year’s exchange rate, deflated by the PPI (USA) and expressed in base-year córdobas using the exchange rate for that year. Each year’s total assets and liabilities, valued at base-year prices, are divided by their respective base-year values. The asset and liability volume indices estimated in this way are used to extrapolate base-year income and expenditure. Real estate and rental activities: The estimation of actual and imputed residential rents draws on the following information sources: 1993 and 1998 national standard of living survey, the seventh population census and third housing census of 1995, the 1999 household income and expenditure survey, and the quarterly private construction survey from 1998 forward. Information on nonresidential rents was obtained indirectly, using the data recorded under this item in the production accounts of other sectors of the economy. Values at constant prices are obtained from construction price indices. Entrepreneurial activities: The annual survey of services is used to calculate the gross value of production and intermediate consumption. Market education services: The annual survey of services was used to calculate gross value of production and intermediate consumption. Intermediate consumption is obtained from the information provided by a number of institutions, and final consumption from the 1993 national household standard of living survey. The annual number of students supplied by the Ministry of Education, Culture, and Sports provides additional information for the reconciliation of the output of this activity. Market social and health services: The annual survey of services was used to calculate the gross value of production and intermediate consumption. The national health accounts of the Ministry of Health (MINSA) provided additional information for the reconciliation of the value of production of this activity. Other community, social, and personal services: Estimation of the gross value of production and intermediate consumption was based on the annual survey of services, the financial statements of the Lottery and of the Ministry of Government for NPISHs (for these institutions, gross value of production is equal to final consumption, as recommended by the system), the 1993 and 1998 EMNVs, and the 1999 EIGH. Constant values were estimated using the change in the CPI for the respective year and/or the population growth rate. Government services: 1.- General budget of the republic. 2.- Execution of the expenditure budget of each institution, broken down by chapter, item, and sub-item. 3.- Revenue execution by type of tax and by collecting agency. 4.- Breakdown of the execution of transfers. 5.- Report on execution of the general budget of the republic, presented to the national assembly. 6.- Deficit financing breakdowns. 7.- Execution of the public investment program. 8.- General balance sheet and income statement with a breakdown of accounts and subaccounts and notes to the financial statements. 9.- Revenue budget execution by type of tax levied by the Alcaldía de Managua and the rest of the local governments. All of the detailed information mentioned above is provided by the Ministry of Finance and Public Credit, the Public Investment Directorate of the Secretariat of Coordination and Strategy of the Office of the President, the General Revenue Directorate (DGI), the Alcaldía de Managua (ALMA), the Municipal Development Institute (INIFOM), decentralized agencies, state universities, the Nicaraguan Social Security Institute (INSS), and the Institute of Social Security and Human Development (ISDHU) of the Ministry of Government. General government gross production is broken down into public administration and defense, nonmarket education services, nonmarket health services and services provided by associations, recreation, and other services. Government output at constant prices is calculated using the method recommended by the United Nations in the manual of national accounts at constant prices. This method consists of deflating intermediate consumption with Intermediate Consumption Index by products or group of products and extrapolating value added using the employment volume index. Value added (compensation at constant wages) is calculated by extrapolation of the figures recorded in the base year, using an employment index in which changes in the number of employees in each category (or "profile group") are weighted with the total share of compensation for that category in the base year. Expenditure approach: Household consumption: A final consumption vector was calculated for 1999 using the 1999 Household Income and Expenditure Survey (BCN/INEC) covering the 17 urban departmental capitals and the 1998 National Standard of Living Survey (INEC) for the remaining urban and rural areas. This vector was used to extrapolate backwards to 1994, to allow for comparison and reconciliation with the base year vector prepared using the 1993 EMNV. The 1999 vector was also extrapolated for the following years. Government consumption: The information sources for calculating government consumption are the same as those used for government. Government consumption includes services consumed on behalf of the government that benefit society as a whole. These services are those that the public administration provides to society free of charge or at nonmarket prices, such as general administration and defense, and those that are consumed individually, which, like collectives, are provided to society at nonmarket prices but which benefit individual households, such as education, public health, and recreation services. The constant price calculation is described under government. Nonprofit institutions serving households: 1.- List of nonprofit institutions registered with the Ministry of Government, with a breakdown of revenue and expenditure. 2.- Financial statements of all nonprofit institutions registered with the Ministry of Government. Fixed capital formation : Public fixed capital formation is obtained from information on public sector investments, including both the central government and autonomous agencies. This information is broken down into construction, machinery and equipment, and other investment components. The machinery and equipment component for private fixed capital formation is calculated as the difference between total imports of capital goods, obtained from customs records, and the public machinery and equipment component. Private construction is obtained from the quarterly survey of private construction, in which the square meters calculated are valued at prices based on economic purpose. Other private investment component is an aggregate of the investment estimates made for the individual economic activities. The calculation of this component is based on information provided by the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry, which includes the value of new plantings such as coffee, sugar cane, bananas, and pastureland, as well as the value of yearlings, sires, and laying hens. This information comes from customs records and includes new forest plantings such as pine trees, provided by the Ministry of Natural Resources and the Environment. Changes in stocks: Stocks of some industrial products are obtained from surveys; all others are based on the adjustments made to the product supply and use table (SUT). Exports and imports of goods and services: The Directorate General of Customs Services (DGSA) database is used to calculate external trade at current and constant prices. The database contains values in dollars of f.o.b. exports and c.i.f. imports, gross kilogram weight, the Central American Tariff System (SAC) used to classify foreign trade goods, date of policy acceptance, merchandise description, and names of the exporter and importer, inter alia. The daily exchange rate on the date of policy acceptance is applied to the dollar values of f.o.b. exports and c.i.f. imports to express their value in domestic currency. Similarly, the database (DGSA) includes the Provisional Central Classification of Products (CPC) and the Nicaraguan classification of products and activities (CNIC), to allow for harmonization of products in the economy with foreign trade and the Classification by Economic Use or Purpose (CUODE) which defines the purposes of goods imported into the economy. The CNIC-CPC-SAC database was adjusted to correct gross kilograms data and erroneous classifications. Adjustments were then made to the main export and import products. This adjustment process is carried out owing to the time of recording of products and the greater or lesser coverage of the enterprises’ data. Calculation of the constant price values of exports and imports is based on the volume, value, and unit value of a basket of goods, in which unit value indices are calculated according to the CNIC: fixed base (1994) and moving base. The CNIC-CPC-CUODE table of c.i.f. imports in current values is also prepared, which includes CPC reclassifications of reconciled products, as well as reclassifications of capital goods. For nonfactor services, the balance of payments provides information on nonfactor income and expenditure in dollars. To calculate these in domestic currency, the annual average exchange rate by type of export and import transaction is applied for income and expenditure, respectively. The CNIC is used for classification, and reinsurance is included in imported nonfactor services. Income Approach: Compensation of employees: Two methods are used to obtain employees' compensation: Direct Method For electric power, drinking water, financial intermediation, general government, insurance companies, mail, and telecommunications activities, this method consists of using data on compensation taken directly from financial statements. Information from annual surveys is used to calculate compensation in the industry, services, and mining sectors. Indirect Method This consists of calculating agricultural and private construction wages based on labor requirements per manzana (1.7 acres) planted and harvested, and per square meter constructed, respectively. To calculate wages in the coffee, sugar cane, basic grains, banana, and oilseed sectors the basic information used is the number of manzanas of each crop harvested, the unit cost, and the technical demand for labor (permanent and contractual), which are obtained from the 1994 technological charts of Banco Nacional de Desarrollo (BANADES) and those of the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry (MAGFOR). For all other crops, the information in the production accounts of each crop is used to calculate wages, as there is no information on the area harvested in all years. For cattle, wages are calculated using the size of the herd and the demand for and costs of the labor needed to care for the herd. Forestry wages are obtained from the 2000 survey of production costs of wood and firewood harvesting and extrapolated for all years in the series. Wages and the number of persons employed in the fisheries sector are obtained from the 1997 annual survey of fishing production costs and the 2000 survey of farmed shrimp costs. Wages in the agricultural, forestry, livestock, and fisheries sectors are updated each year by extrapolating from the index of minimum wages paid in the market for each activity, which was prepared with data provided by the Ministry of Labor. Operating surplus and mixed income: This is the balancing item for the income-generation account, broken down according to each economic activity based on the responses to the annual manufacturing and services survey of enterprises’ legal status and the classification of their accounting records: 1.- Enterprises that are not legally established (and instead qualify as individuals) and that do not keep a record of their transactions with other sectors of the economy or of their assets and liabilities in financial statements are considered household enterprises, and the balance is classified as mixed income. 2.- Enterprises that are not legally established (and instead qualify as individuals) and that do keep a record of their transactions with other sectors of the economy as well as of their assets and liabilities in financial statements are considered quasi-corporations, and the balance is classified as gross operating surplus. 3.- Enterprises that are legally established and that keep formal accounting records are considered business corporations, and the balance is classified as gross operating surplus. For agriculture, livestock, fisheries, and forestry, mixed income is distinguished from the surplus using the structure reported in the sectoral surveys, which include the production of households and enterprises. Taxes less subsidies on production and imports: 1.- Directorate General of Taxes (DGI) database of revenue by type of tax and economic activity; 2.- Directorate General of Customs Services (DGSA) database organized by type of tax and tariff item; 3.- Revenue of the Alcaldía de Managua and the other local governments. 4.- Revenue of the National Technological Institute Taxes on domestic and imported products are obtained from the databases made available by the Directorate General of Taxes (DGI) and the Directorate General of Customs Services (DGSA), which are classified according to the CPC and the CNIC, for inclusion in the supply and use balances. The other taxes on production consist of municipal taxes levied on productive activities by the municipalities as part of their municipal tax plans. They include taxes on assets and real property, sales and services, registration and licenses, public entertainments, automotive vehicles, etc. Other nonmunicipal taxes are also collected by the National Technological Institute, based on a mandatory contribution of 2 percent of total gross or fixed payroll, payable by all employers in the Republic except, in the case of government, the army and police payrolls. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The 1996 economic directory, which is the source of the sample to be used in economic surveys, included only the urban areas of the country; as a result, the industrial surveys do not include rural production. For beef, the annual survey covers all industrial slaughtering, but not municipal slaughtering, which occurs in rural areas. Production is therefore adjusted, based on the municipal slaughtering statistics of the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry (MAGFOR). For pork, production is adjusted because this is essentially a rural household activity. The adjustment is based on MAGFOR’s pork slaughter statistics and final consumption in the 1999 household account. The industrial survey’s coverage of fishing and fish products is incomplete, as there are processing plants in the Atlantic region of the country. Production is adjusted on the basis of fisheries sector exports and production; approximately 90 percent of this sector’s output is exported. Because dairy is basically a rural, artisanal activity, production is adjusted on the basis of the production of raw milk recorded in the livestock sector and final consumption in the 1999 household account. Owing to the lack of coverage of the bakery industry and the nonresponse of some flour producers, the production of these activities was adjusted on the basis of the final household consumption vector of 1999 and imports of wheat and wheat flour, the main inputs for these activities. Wheat is not grown in Nicaragua and the flour companies import wheat directly. The production of golden rice is adjusted on the basis of the volumes of paddy rice produced, which are obtained from the MAGFOR survey of basic grains production). The production of sawn lumber, which occurs outside the country’s urban centers, is adjusted on the basis of the demand of other activities that use this product as an input, such as the furniture and construction industry (where the demand for sawn lumber is greatest), as well as demand on the external market (exports). The furniture and construction industry obtain the required quantity of sawn lumber based on surveys. With regard to foreign trade in goods, the CNIC-CPC-SAC database is being adjusted to correct gross kilograms data and erroneous classifications. Adjustments are then made to the main export and import products. For exports, the DGSA data on bananas, farmed shrimp, caught shrimp, lobster tails, fresh fish, frozen fish, fish filets, and other seafood products are replaced with data from the export firms. For merchandise imports, DGSA data on crude oil and fuels are replaced with data from the Hydrocarbons Directorate of the Nicaraguan Energy Institute.
