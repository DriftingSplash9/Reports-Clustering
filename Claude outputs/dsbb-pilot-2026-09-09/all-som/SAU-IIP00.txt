 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Saudi's International Investment Position represents the stocks of External Financial Liabilities and Assets as at the end of the reference period. It represents the value and composition of the economy's claims on the rest of the world and value of that economy's financial liabilities to the rest of the world as at the end of the reference period. The difference between the two sides (i.e. External financial assets and liabilities) of the economy gives the net international investment position of the economy. The disseminated data confirm broadly to the 6th edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6). The IIP is fully integrated with Saudi Arabia's balance of payments statistics, specifically with the financial account that is composed of foreign direct investment, portfolio investment, other investment and reserve assets. Frequency of external statistics (i.e., BOP and IIP) compilation and dissemination is quarterly and is in millions of Saudi Riyals. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization International investment position statistics are classified according to BPM6. Assets and liabilities are classified into direct investment, portfolio investment, other investment and reserve assets. Direct Investment is classified according to the directional principle, while portfolio and other investments are disaggregated by instrument, institutional sector, and maturity. There are no known transactions (and positions) in financial derivatives. Data by institutional classification (by sector) where relevant are available but not disseminated yet. Financial Derivatives are not shown as separate item as their transactions are not identified and may be included in other financial components if exist. 2.2.1 Scope International investment position statistics are classified according to BPM6. Assets and liabilities are classified into direct investment, portfolio investment, other investment and reserve assets. Direct Investment is classified according to the directional principle, while portfolio and other investments are disaggregated by instrument, institutional sector, and maturity. There are no known transactions (and positions) in financial derivatives. Data by institutional classification (by sector) where relevant are available but not disseminated yet. Financial Derivatives are not shown as separate item as their transactions are not identified and may be included in other financial components if exist. Scope of the data · Coverage of activity: Saudi's IIP broadly cover all the stock positions of all financial assets and liabilities resulted to transactions between different resident institutional sectors (i.e., General Government, Central Bank, Deposit Taking Corporations, and Other Sectors) with the rest of world, as a result of financial transactions, price changes, exchange rate changes and other adjustments. · Geographical Coverage: The IIP data collected by SAMA cover all regions of Saudi Arabia. Exceptions to coverage No exception of coverage exists. 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to Unit of Account Similar to BOP data, IIP data are compiled in millions of SRs. Stock positions originally denominated in foreign currencies are converted to SR using prevailing market rates of exchange at end of reference period. Valuation Transactions and positions data are sought at market prices. In practice, the values recorded in business accounts may be used. These are considered a close approximation to market prices. In cases where significant distortion may occur to the valuation of direct equity, adjustments are made on the basis of total market capitalization. However, the "other changes" in financial assets and liabilities account that covers changes in volume, other than balance of payments transactions (revaluation of other changes in volume, exchange rate changes, and other price changes) are not fully adjusted due to the limitations of the source data. 2.4.2 Recording basis BOP as well IIP data are recorded on accrual basis depending on the availability of information provided by the source of the data. Data series are not seasonally adjusted. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Mostly on gross basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The data sources for compiling the transactions of the Financial Account are the same for the IIP template compilation. The net acquisition of financial assets is estimated by the External Statistics Department based on information from investment regulating authorities, banks' reports, financial statements and administrative data and records. The net incurrence of liabilities, GASTAT and MISA are conducting surveys to collect data on direct investment (FDI). It is worth mentioning, that in result to the efforts to develop the enterprise surveys for collecting data on direct investment (FDI), an inter-agency working group was established, representing members from MISA, GASTAT, and SAMA. The agency launched a survey to collect the FDI data for the purpose of BOP and Coordinated Direct Investment Survey (CDIS). The survey results were reviewed and checked to make sure they follow the BPM6 guideline. Based on this, the verified results were used and applied to the time series data as an appropriate and reliable data source. As for data on portfolio investment and other financial transactions, they are obtained from CMA, the oil companies, private sector companies, commercial banks, and money exchange houses. Sources of data for the transactions by the commercial banks and money exchange houses are the monthly statements of purchases and sales of foreign exchange, provided by commercial banks and licensed money exchange houses. Data on outstanding stocks of foreign assets and liabilities of the commercial banks are obtained from the consolidated balance sheet of the commercial banks, prepared monthly by the Banking Supervision. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Saudi International Investment position records outstanding positions of financial assets and liabilities. It records for foreign investment (FI), which is subdivided into three functional categories to classify financial assets and liabilities: Direct investment (DI), Portfolio investment (PI), and other investment (OI), which makes it easy to compile the corresponding foreign investment position in IIP. In addition, it includes Reserve Assets. Direct investment (DI): Direct investment (DI) is a category of cross border investment associated with a resident in one economy having control or a significant degree of influence on the management of a company that is resident in another economy. In addition to funds, direct investors may supply additional contributions such as expertise, innovation, technology, management, and marketing. As well as the equity that gives rise to control or influence, DI also includes investment associated with that relationship, including investment in indirectly influenced or controlled companies, investment in fellow enterprises, debt, and reverse investment. Therefore, the ‘net' residents investment flows transactions in equity outside the Kingdom (10% or more of total voting power / right of ownership), reinvested earnings, and net other claims on non-resident subsidiaries, are recorded in the Direct Investment (Assets). While Net non-resident investments flows in the Kingdom (10% role), reinvested earnings, and net other claims for non-resident enterprises in the Kingdom's resident institutions are recorded in Direct Investment (Liabilities). Portfolio Investment (PI): Portfolio investment is cross border transactions and positions involving Equity and investment fund shares and Debt securities, other than those included in DI or Reserve Assets. Equity and investment fund shares are instruments that acknowledge a claim on the residual value of a company. Debt securities are negotiable instruments serving as evidence of debt; they include bills, bonds, negotiable certificates of deposits, commercial paper, debentures, asset-backed securities, and similar instruments normally traded in the financial markets. The flows transaction of residents in foreign equity (ownership of less than 10% of total voting power/ right of ownership), foreign debt securities and foreign financial instruments, held by residents and externally issued, other than those included in foreign direct investment and reserve assets, are recorded in the Assets Side of PI. While the liabilities of PI covers non-residents net purchases of domestic equity (ownership less than 10%), debt securities and financial instruments held by non-residents issued by resident institutions, other than those included in foreign direct investment and reserve assets. Financial derivatives: Represent the transactions of resident's claims (Assets) non-residents claims (Liabilities) arising from transactions in securities of futures contracts and option contracts. However, no entries are recorded in BOP, neither in IIP because of unavailability of data. Other Investment (OI): Includes all financial transactions between residents and non-residents; that are not included in direct investment, portfolio investment or reserve assets. Classified according to the tool to: Other equity, trade credit, loans, currency and deposits and other accounts receivable/payable. Other equity is included in other investment, when it is not direct investment or reserve assets, and is not in the form of securities; therefore, it is not included in portfolio investment. It can include equity in quasi-corporations, such as branches, trusts, limited liability and other partnerships, unincorporated funds, and notional units for ownership of real estate and other natural resources. In most cases, equity in quasi-corporations and notional units for ownership of land is included in DI; however, it is included in other investment (under other equity) if the share of voting power is less than 10 percent. Reserve assets: Reserve assets are external assets available and controlled by SAMA, and by definition, they could be used for financing BOP imbalances. This item reflects changes in the overall official reserves, namely, gold, SDRs, reserve position in the Fund, foreign exchange, deposits with banks abroad, and investment in foreign securities. These data are obtained from SAMA's balance sheet. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The data collection system provides for the timely receipt of data, and has improved recently and reached an appropriate patrol that ensures the adherence to the timeliness of dissemination of the quarterly balance of payments data determined by three months from the end of the reporting period. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data are continuously verified and assessed; by executing trend analysis to isolate data outliers and data checks on banks reports forms and surveys results when necessary are consulted with transaction entity or with the authority responsible for the data collection. All source data used are assessed to check the consistency with international guidelines, concepts and methodologies. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques SAMA uses several techniques to collect data, direct reporting, and financial statements. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Adjustments in line with the BOP and IIP compilations guidelines are applied in the compilation of the IIP data when necessary. No valuation of flows data computed from outstanding positions of the financial account components or vice versa is made in order to exclude Other Changes adjustments. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results BOP and IIP data are validated where possible, unless availability of new data from other sources than it is used. The External Statistics Department regularly meets with other relevant authorities on a technical level in order to crosscheck relevant information and to identify practices, such as differences in accounting rules, recording procedures, and sectorization, classification, and valuation issues. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The IIP data are crosschecked with financial account flows of BOP to assure the consistency and accuracy and to avoid any possible discrepancies. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions of released data on IIP data are made within five quarters to provide data quality and authenticity. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses No studies or analyses of revisions are made public 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Information are made available upon request. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points All SAMA publications and the website identify DSGD's contact points for enquiries by mail, telephone, facsimile, and by e-mail. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs Information on dissemination of documents, publications, and other services is available to all users on the official website of SAMA https://www.sama.gov.sa/en-us/pages/default.aspx
