 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The compilation of Balance of Payments (BOP) statistics is broadly in line with the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual ( BPM6 ) concepts. The Balance of Payments is a statistical statement that summarizes transactions between residents and nonresidents during a specific period. BOP data consist of the goods and services account, the primary income account, the secondary income account, the capital account, and the functional categories of the financial account (direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives and employee stock options, other investment and reserve assets). Imbalances in the accounts resulting from imperfections in source data and compilation are featured under net errors and omissions. Resident institutional units are defined as having a centre of economic interest in Malta, in conformity with BPM6 guidelines. These include special purpose entities. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Institutional units are classified and attributed to the relevant sector according to the BPM6. No distortion has been identified. The Central Bank of Malta stands for the monetary authorities; the deposit-taking corporations comprise the deposit money banks, the international banking institutions and money-market funds; the General Government sector comprises the central government, the EBUs, and the local councils; other sectors have been sub-divided into financial and non-financial entities. Scope of the data In principle, all resident-nonresident transactions are covered. Unrecorded activity Apart from importation of dugs (which is recorded in the national accounts and adopted in the balance of payments), illegal activities are not recorded. 2.4.1 Valuation In general, transactions are valued at market prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis Balance of payments transactions are established on an accrual basis, except for certain categories of private sector revenues, which are posted on a cash basis. When administrative data (in particular the Tax Index of Financial Data) is reported in foreign currencies, conversion to euro is made. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The balance of payments are compliant with the grossing/netting principles established by BPM6 . Current transactions are declared and recorded on a gross basis, while financial transactions are recorded on a net basis distinguishing between assets and liabilities. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The BOP compilation relies on survey data collection and administrative data sources. · Goods: Merchandise trade data are collected from the INTRASTAT System, while extra-EU trade data are compiled by the Government's Customs Department, (EXTRASTAT). BOP compilers make relevant adjustments to the basic merchandise data for BOP purposes. · Services: Data is compiled using survey data and administrative sources, in particular the VAT and the VIES databases. When it comes to travel, gross earnings from tourism and gross expenditure by residents travelling abroad are derived from frontier surveys—TOURSTAT and CRUISTAT- carried out by the Population and Tourism Statistics Unit of the NSO. · Primary income transactions: Apart from survey data, two administrative sources are linked at micro-level to derive an exhaustive measure of cross-border flows for interest, dividends and re-invested earnings. The Tax Index of Financial Data (TIFD) provides information about the income statements and balance sheets of all registered non-financial and financial corporations in Malta. The income statement is inclusive of primary income flows and identifies some cross-border flows. The Malta Business Registry (MBR) administers a database with information about the immediate shareholders and the ultimate beneficiary owners of corporations registered in Malta. Micro-linking allows the derivation of dividends and re-invested earnings paid abroad. The calculation of reinvested earnings takes into consideration the chain of ownership structure. Dividends Receivable and Interest Receivable are directly identifiable from the TIFD and as such no micro-data linking is required in the latter cases. When it comes to Maltese households, Bank of International Settlements (BIS) data is used to derive interest received by households in Malta and paid to households abroad. Primary income flows received by households from investment activity abroad through resident and non-resident investment service providers is captured with survey data collection. · Financial Account - Direct Investment: financial transactions are compiled using survey data of both financial and non-financial enterprises as well as the Tax Index of Financial Data (TIFD). · Financial Account - Portfolio Investment: financial transactions are compiled using survey data of both financial and non-financial enterprises as well as the Tax Index of Financial Data (TIFD). Purchases of domestic equity and debt securities by non-residents on the Malta Stock Exchange (MSE) are reported directly by the MSE. · Financial Account - Other Investment: financial transactions are compiled using survey data of both financial and non-financial enterprises as well as the Tax Index of Financial Data (TIFD). · Financial Account - Financial derivatives: financial transactions are compiled using survey data of both financial and non-financial enterprises. · Financial Account - Reserve Assets: Data is provided by the Finance Office at the Central Bank of Malta. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data correctly approximate the desired definitions, classifications, valuations, and time of recording. With respect to trade data, the information available from INTRASTAT and EXTRASTAT documents allows for the appropriate adjustments for freight and insurance. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The timeliness is mainly determined by international requirements of the ECB, Eurostat and IMF. For survey data collection, reporting agents are provided with a deadline that balances the needs of both parties, giving BOP compilers sufficient time for analysis and compilation while ensuring agents have ample time to fulfil their reporting obligations. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The reporting tool used by reporting agents in most financial sectors incorporates various validation checks to ensure the input data is as accurate as possible. The source data for the rest of the financial and non-financial sectors (surveys and administrative sources), are assessed using self-developed tools designed to identify errors or outliers. When discrepancies or anomalies are detected, reporting agents are contacted to revise or confirm the figures as needed. Additionally, where feasible, balance of payments data is cross verified annually against the financial statements. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Survey data is designed to fit directly into the BOP framework, and do not normally require specific statistical techniques, while data from administrative sources are mapped into the framework using a pre-determined mapping and micro linking process. Data are grossed up where necessary and data for non-respondents are imputed. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Statistical techniques are used mainly for calculating adjustments for freight and insurance, and general coverage. It should be noted that no estimates are made for transactions of the underground economy. The statistical and invoice values contained in INTRASTAT and EXTRASTAT forms are used to calculate the shipment content contained in the import value of goods. When no indications are available, an estimate of 10 per cent of the value of imports is made. Of this estimate, 90 per cent is allocated to freight and the remainder to insurance. This procedure has been applied as from 2004 onwards. In the quarterly balance of payments, adjustments for coverage are made to account for non-quarterly reporters and non-respondents to the annual questionnaire. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Intermediate results are validated using several tools. Information provided directly by reporting agents is checked against financial statements when available and where necessary. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Inconsistencies within data submitted by respondents are immediately investigated directly with the respondents. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Errors and omissions are closely monitored. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions in balance of payments data are unavoidable due to timeliness of administrative sources, late submissions by reporting agents, revisions of initial estimates and methodology improvements, amongst other reasons. Revisions are monitored on a quarterly basis as part of the compilation process, but no research studies are done. When significant revisions are made, the NSO provides comments on these revisions through press information notices and meetings with the users. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Unless the revisions are substantial, no explanation is provided on the nature of the revisions. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request When available, balance of payments data that are not disseminated and are not confidential can be made available to users by the NSO and the CBM upon request. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points There are two contact points: first, the NSO's Dissemination and Communications Unit, the reference of which is indicated in the NSO's News Release and second, the NSO's official website, with a specific module where users are requested to fill in a request form, which is then channelled to the relevant unit of the NSO. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The Balance of Payments data are easily accessible through the NSO's official website which provides comprehensive information on how users can contact the NSO for assistance.
