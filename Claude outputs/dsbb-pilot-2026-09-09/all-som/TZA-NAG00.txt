 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical Framework, Concepts and Definitions The National Accounts for Tanzania Mainland are compiled and disseminated by the National Accounts Department of the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS). The framework is largely based on the 1993 System of National Accounts (SNA). The main data published are GDP at current and constant 2001 prices by 17 activities and by expenditure, gross and net national income, national disposable income, national saving, net national lending ](+)/borrowing (–), and the accounts for general government and for the rest of the world, including the capital account. In addition, regional GDP estimates are produced. Input-output tables have been compiled for 1954, 1961, 1969, 1976, and 1992. The 1992 input-output table has 79x79 sectors; the annual accounts are revised using the surveys as well as the results of input-revised input output table. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification System Economic activities of producer units are classified according to the third revision of the International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC). The seventeen major activities distinguished are: Agriculture (crops, Livestock, forestry and hunting), Fishing, Mining and quarrying; Manufacturing; Electricity and Gas, water supply; Construction; Wholesale, retail trade and repairs, hotels and restaurants; Transport and communication; Financial intermediation, real estate and business services, Public administration, defense and compulsory social security funds, Education, Health and other social and personal services. Financial intermediaries Services Indirectly measured are treated as intermediate consumption by a nominal industry. For national accounts purposes, the Harmonized System classification used in the foreign trade statistics in respect of imports and exports of merchandise is converted to the Standard Industrial Trade Classification (SITC). Current expenditures by general government are classified by 14 categories of the Classification of Functions of Government (COFOG) by purpose and by economic activities. Some transaction categories, such as property income, are only recorded for the Tanzania mainland vis-à-vis the rest of the world. Unit Coverage The National Accounts of Tanzania Mainland cover all units residing on the Tanzania mainland. The economic activities of Zanzibar are not included. Estimates for informal sector, monetary; and non-monetary production are included. These data are estimated on the basis of the 2000/01 Household Budget Survey (HBS), the 1991 Informal Sector Survey (ISS), the 1995 Dar-es-Salaam Informal Sector Survey, agricultural surveys, and Input-Output Table of 1992. Estimates of GDP at current prices by region are available for the years 1998 to 2011 . Nature of the Basic Data Sources Production approach: The NBS maintains a Business Register (BR), which has been constructed on the basis of the census, administrative records on licenses from district councils and municipalities, the Tanzania Revenue Authority (TRA), the National Social Security Fund (NSSF), the Register of companies, and vocational education training centers. Agricultural, fishing, and forestry establishments are included in the BR. The BR is updated with information received from the NBS regional offices mainly through administrative sources. The general approach is to extrapolate estimates for a benchmark year by output, volume and price indices. In many activities, the benchmark is derived from the 2000/01HBS. Agriculture, Forestry, Fishing and Hunting: Volumes of food and cash crops are collected from the Statistics Unit of the Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security. Crop prices are collected from the Ministry of Trade, Industry and marketing. Main food crop : Volumes and prices are collected from the Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security and the Ministry of Trade, Industry and marketing respectively. The benchmark estimate for other crops production is obtained from consumption levels according to the 2000/01 HBS. The monetary consumption estimates were converted to production levels by first revaluing the data at producers' prices and subsequently adding estimates for net exports, inputs to manufacturing, restaurants, and other use outside private households. The value of non-monetary consumption was found by multiplying HBS volumes with available price data. These benchmark levels are extrapolated by annual changes resulting from the Ministry of Agriculture and Food security (MA amp; FS) Information, in particular from the Crop Monitoring and Early Warning Unit and Food Security Department. Livestock: Benchmark levels are based on the 2000/01 HBS and certain data from the Livestock Department of the Ministry of Livestock Development and Fishery. The data are extrapolated by estimated cattle population growth rate. Production of milk is estimated from the Ministry of Livestock Development and Fishery and Livestock Censuses. (The last livestock census dates from the early eighties.) Input structures are derived from the 1992 Input/output table. Prices are taken from the CPI. Forestry: Benchmark estimates are predominantly based on 2000/01 HBS i.e. firewood and charcoal data. Extrapolators are population growth for firewood and charcoal. Fishing: Benchmark levels are based on 2000/01 HBS consumption levels of fresh and dried fish. Annual movements are based on data on production and exports obtained from the Fisheries Department in the Ministry of Natural Resources and Tourism (MNRT). Hunting : Benchmark levels were obtained from the HBS and the Wildlife Section of the Department of Natural Resources and Tourism. These data are extrapolated by population growth, tourism statistics, and information from hunting associations. Agricultural services: Sources for agricultural services include annual reports published by parastatal units and special surveys. In all cases, intermediate consumption at current and constant prices is estimated as a fixed ratio of output. Mining and quarrying: The Department of Minerals of the Ministry of Energy and Minerals supplies data on gold, precious stones, sand, salt, etc. These data include volume of production, value of production, and volume of exports and value of exports from which indices are constructed. Manufacturing: A distinction is made between enterprises belonging to the formal and the informal sectors. Benchmark for the formal sector is the 2001 annual survey of industrial production, 2000/01 integrated labour force survey and the analysis of 2001 employment and earnings survey. Volume and price estimates are based on the Quarterly Survey of Industrial Production. Benchmark estimates for the National informal sector are based on the 1991 Informal Sector Survey (ISS) and 1995 informal sector survey in Dar-es-Salaam. Electricity Gas and Water supply: The data Source are the power generation Companies i.e. Tanesco, IPTL and Songas. Other small-scale plants are also covered. The production of electricity from enterprises producing electricity as a secondary activity has been neglected. Gas: The source of data on Gas production is Tanzania Petroleum Development Corporation (TPDC), ARTUMUS and Panafrican energy. Gas Production started on July 2004. Water supply: Questionnaire is filled out by respective Water Authorities/Companies. An addition was made for the volume of water used for irrigation on the basis of information about parastatal activity in the benchmark year; this element is extrapolated by population. Construction: For the benchmark year, Public construction activity is derived from government and parastatal accounts, supplemented by data on aid funded construction that is not included in the government budgets. Deflator is the building material cost index compiled by the National Construction Council (NCC). Rural own-account construction and construction of urban dwellings is estimated based on the stock of dwellings according to the population census, estimated life span, and population growth. Construction in urban dwellings is estimated in a similar way, but adjustments are made to allow for evidence of housing shortages and increased household sizes. The CPI is used as a deflator for rural construction, and the NCC building material cost index for urban dwellings. Estimates for commercial buildings of various types have been made based on evidence of known major private sector activities. Value added/output ratios were estimated from this source. Annual estimates are derived based on the net cement consumption. Wholesale, Trade and Repair: The benchmark figure is based on the Survey on Construction, Trade and Transport (SCTT) and the 1991 ISS. The estimate also includes trade margins from the sale of agricultural products, extracted from the HBS monetary data at purchasers' values. The annual movements are estimated by extrapolating the benchmark trade margins on the production levels of monetary agriculture, mining, manufacturing, and relevant components of imports. For repairs, the volume indicator is a combined weighted index of urban population and deflated road transport output. Hotels and Restaurants: The benchmark estimate for restaurants and hotels is derived from the 2001 SUT. Annual movements are among other things based on hotel statistics. The Ministry of Natural Resources and Tourism (MNRT) provides data on tourism expenditure (including visitors for business purposes); the benchmark data for the informal sector are extrapolated by population growth. Transport : Road transport: Based on the Analysis of Government expenditure on Road, number of Passenger carried and the volume of freight transported. The quantity index for passenger transport is the number of buses operating in urban areas. For freight, the quantity index is a combined weighted index of trade margins derived from agriculture, Fishing, Mining, manufacturing, and imports. The transport price sub index of CPI is used in both cases to obtain value indices. Rail transport: Current price data are obtained by analyzing the accounts of the Tanzania Railways Limited (TRL) and TAZARA (a joint venture with Zambia) and estimates at constant price are compiled based on the volume of goods and number of passengers carried. Water transport: Accounts of the Tanzania Ports Authority, Marine services Company Ltd. And SINOTASHIP , complemented by data on number of ships, passengers and the quantity of cargo handled. A small addition is made for private sector activity. Air transport : Accounts of the Air Tanzania Company Limited, Precision Air, and Swissport where data on number of passenger and volume of cargo carried are collected for constant price estimates. Communication: Accounts of Tanzania Posts Corporation and private post operators, Telecommunications companies including private mobile phone operators TTCL, Tigo, Airtel and Vodacom. Constant price estimates are based on changes in the number of posted letters and parcels handled (Posts), deflated output (tellecom), the deflator is all item CPI. Financial intermediation: Covers (banks, bureau de changes, and insurance companies). Usually the questionnaires are sent to the companies and in addition their annual accounts are collected. Constant price estimates are computed based on levels of employment, deflated levels of deposits and number of insurance policies. Real Estate and Business Services: Renting of dwellings is based on the analysis of parastatal accounts and 2000/01 HBS data extrapolated by population growth and the rent sub index of the CPI. Rental of commercial premises derived mostly from parastatal accounts. The benchmark renting value of owner-occupied dwellings was derived from the HBS. Separate estimates are made for rural and urban dwellings and both are assumed to move with the appropriate population growth. The deflator is the rent sub index of the CPI. Business services covering services such as legal, accounting and other services are estimated on a case by case. Public Administration defense and compulsory social security funds: Compiled from reports prepared by the Accountant General for central government, and reports of Income and Expenditure of Local Authorities, Agencies and social security funds. The volume indicator is based on the minimum wage and the CPI. · Education: Benchmark levels were obtained from the SUT. The benchmark estimates were extrapolated by a combined weighted index derived from the number of students enrolled in different school levels (primary, secondary, teachers colleges and higher learning institutions) and the number of tutors. The deflator is the CPI component for secondary school fees. Gross output at current prices is estimated based on the government expenditure on education and the education fees paid by households. · Health: Benchmark levels were estimated based on the expenditure by Government, Household and NGO’s on health services. Gross output at current prices is estimated by extrapolation of benchmark estimate using the value index derived from the recurrent government expenditure on health. Gross output at constant prices is compiled by extrapolation of the benchmark estimates using the quantity index derived from the number of patients attended. Other social and personal Services: Benchmark data was derived from the HBS. Gross output at constant prices was estimated by extrapolation of benchmark estimates using urban population growth rate (quantity index). Gross output at current prices was estimated by extrapolation of benchmark estimate using the value index derived from the urban population (quantity index) and the CPI (Geometric mean of all items index, personal care component and the recreation and entertainment component). Expenditure approach Household Final Consumption Expenditure: Main sources for the benchmark year were the 2000/01 HBS, adjusted for intermediate consumption items such as purchases of fertilizer and seeds. Also adjustments were made on the underreported local beer and cigarettes. For the years other than benchmark years final consumption expenditure by household is calculated as a residual at both current and constant prices. Government Final Consumption Expenditure: Derived from government accounts and information on aid-funded expenditure outside government budgets, split between current expenditure on public administration, education and health. Constant price estimates are derived using the implied deflators of total output in each of these sectors. In addition, the final consumption expenditure of central Bank is added to that of government at both current and constant prices. Gross Fixed Capital Formation: are derived from the output of the construction industry, downward adjusted for repair and maintenance that should be classified as current expenditure. Investment in machinery and equipment are derived from imports data. Changes in Inventories: Includes changes in livestock made available by the Ministry of Water and Livestock development and fishery. International Merchandise Trade: Compiled by the TRA. No price indices of foreign trade are being compiled. Imports and exports are extrapolated using import/export volume indices Exports of major crops and oil are extrapolated with available export volumes, while other exports are deflated with the CPI. International Trade in Services: Main source is the BOP compiled by the BoT. The data are made public around mid-June in the budget speech. 3.2.1 Source data assessment During major revisions data from censuses, sample surveys are incorporated in the estimates. The administrative records among the major sources of data for the compilation of quarterly as well as annual estimates. Revisions are always done whenever the data from the censuses/survey are available. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques V. Compilation Practices Production approach: The benchmark data are extrapolated at current and constant prices with the indicators discussed under section IV. GDP at basic price is then derived as the sum of all value added estimates less Financial Intermediation Services Indirectly Measured (FISIM). A distinction may be made between monetary and non-monetary value added in the activities of agriculture, construction, and owner-occupied dwellings. GDP at basic price plus net taxes on products gives GDP at market prices. Expenditure approach: GDP cannot be derived from the expenditure approach, due to deficient coverage on Household final consumption expenditure. Therefore, GDP by expenditure is calculated as a residual at both current and constant prices. Reconciliation: No reconciliation at the detailed goods and services level takes place. Revision and dissemination policy: Two estimates are prepared in every year, one is provisional and the other is revised and final. Provisional estimates are ready by end of May of the reference year while the preceding estimate is revised and becomes final. Overall revisions of the national accounts methodology are made when there is evidence that the current sources and methodology do not lead to data that reflect actual activity. Direct cause is often the preparation of an input-output table. No particular schedule exists for these overall revisions. National accounts data are first disseminated by the Minister during the Budget speech. Some National Accounts Tables are published in the Ministry of Finance Publication called the Economic Survey . The NBS publishes the National Accounts of Tanzania Mainland in the month of November. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Validation is carried out in all stages from data collection to the final estimates before dissemination. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revision of National accounts estimates are normally carried out after five years in line with household budget survey. During the revision data from surveys and studies are fully incorporated and the result disseminated to the public. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses The revised estimates are always disseminated to stakeholders through user-producer workshop, media soft and hard copies. In addition the text is supported by the statistical tables and charts. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished and non confidential statistics could be available under the authorization of Director General.
