 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: The IIP is compiled by the Central Reserve Bank of Peru (BCRP) and generally follows the guidelines established in the sixth edition of the IMF Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6). The concepts and definitions are consistent with IMF recommendations. The data is available on a quarterly basis. Definition: The International Investment Position (IIP) is a statistical summary of the stock of Peru's external financial assets and liabilities as at the end of each published period. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The data disseminated in the Weekly Report consists of the following: assets, broken down into reserve assets held by the Central Bank, financial system assets, and other assets; and liabilities, broken down into public- and private-sector external debt (including bonds)–divided into medium and long-term, and short-term; direct investment; and equity securities. Scope of the data Coverage of external financial assets and liabilities: The standards and guidelines recommended by the IMF on residency are followed. No financial asset or liability is intentionally omitted. 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to unit of account: The unit of account is the United States dollar (USD). • In the case of the monetary authority, assets and liabilities denominated in currencies other than the unit of account, except for balances expressed in SDRs, are converted at the exchange rate prevailing on the New York market, as provided by the Reuters money market listing service. • Balances originally valued in SDRs are converted at the exchange rate set by the IMF. BCRP gold holdings are valued at market prices (specifically, at the average of the international prices prevailing on the London, New York, and Zurich markets). • Financial system assets and liabilities are derived from the data on balances reported by the institutions to the BCRP for monetary account purposes. The balances in question are valued through application of the exchange rate prevailing on the pertinent date, as established daily by the Superintendency of Banking and Insurance and pension funds (SBS). · Balances of Direct investment: The stock of foreign direct investment is updated from the transactions of directed investment recorded in the balance of payments. The data on assets and liabilities correspond to book-value records. • The balances of portfolio investment are updating by the data provided by the quartely and annual surveys, by the General Directorate of Public Credit in the MEF (DGCP), Lima Stock Exchange (LSE) through CAVALI SA ICLV (a securities clearing and liquidation institution) and international private agencies. Portfolio investment: The data on nonfinancial private sector assets correspond to book-value records. In the case of the financial sector, assets are valued at market prices, except for holdings of securities when they are not publicly listed, in which case the amounts taken are those obtained using the purchase price or the market price, whichever is lower. Also, the values of securities purchased to be held to maturity are recorded at their purchase price. The data on liabilities is primarily the one published by CAVALI ICLV SA, which provides it at market value. · Other investment: The non-financial sector data is recorded at book and market values. The data on the external debt of the general government, the monetary authorities, banks, and other sectors are compiled in nominal and historical terms. Reserve assets: In the case of the foreign securities that form part of the international reserves, the BCRP adopts the conservative approach of recording the value at purchase price or market price, whichever is lower. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources: The basic data is obtained from the financial institutions, the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF), other government institutions, the Bank for International Settlements (BIS), private sector enterprises, and the records of the BCRP itself. The Balance of Payments Statistics Department carries out quarterly and annual surveys in order to pick information regarding services, external debt, foreign investment, and income. The surveys are carried out quarterly on approximately 850 enterprises. • Specific Headings: o Other investment: general government debt data do not cover public enterprises (financial and non-financial). The debt of public enterprises is included in "Other sectors." These data are based on those compiled by the MEF's General Directorate of Public Credit. None None
