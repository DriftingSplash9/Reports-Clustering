 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Definition: Gross domestic product (GDP), valued at market prices, is the value of goods and services that are produced by the Netherlands. The data are compiled in accordance with the methodology set out in the European System of Accounts 2010 (ESA 2010). Gross domestic product (GDP) at market prices is estimated with reference to quarterly compiled supply and use tables both in current prices and in prices of the previous year. QNA uses a make-use table as an instrument for the integration of short-term statistical data on output, expenditure and income. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classifications used are those of the NACE Rev.2 and CPA. Imports and exports use the more detailed Harmonised Code. A geographical breakdown is only available for the annual national accounts. Scope of the data The following breakdowns are disseminated: by expenditure category, by productive sectors, and by cost components. The breakdown by major expenditure category includes the following components: consumption expenditure by households and by government, consumption expenditure, individual and collective, gross fixed capital formation (GFCF) by government and by corporations, changes in stocks, and exports and imports of goods and services. The breakdowns are disseminated both in current and in chained volume series (price level 2021). Transactions coverage: The production boundary is in accordance with the European System of Accounts 2010 (ESA2010) Geographic coverage: The GDP data cover the entire economy. The delimitation of the Netherlands economic territory and the designation of residents conform to the definitions given in ESA 2010, paragraphs 2.01 to 2.11 inclusive. The Dutch economic territory only consists of the Netherlands that is the territory of the Kingdom in Europe. Unrecorded activity Data should be representative for the whole economy. Since the national accounts (NA) require complete estimates for several branches, data from surveys have to be grossed up for small firms (usually in proportion to the number of employees). Occasionally estimates are made for hidden transactions (like allotment gardens in agriculture). 2.4.1 Valuation Use-table figures are recorded at purchaser's prices. In the supply table, domestic production figures are recorded at basic prices and imports at cif-prices. Therefore three additional columns are included in the supply table in order to fill the valuation gap. These columns relate to trade margins, transport margins and taxes and subsidies on products. Taxes and subsidies on products are not fully allocated to individual industries or sectors. Therefore the estimation of GDP at market prices using the income approach starts with total value added at basic prices as the sum of value added at basic prices on the industry/sector level. GDP at market prices is then calculated by adding separate estimates of taxes (+) and subsidies (-) on products. For imported goods two ways of valuation are of interest, namely the "purchaser's price" and the "c.i.f. price". The difference consists of taxes on products, including import duties, (as plus items) and subsidies on products (as minus items), transport margins on the Dutch territory, trade margins and VAT (if applicable), which are included in the former but not in the latter. 2.4.2 Recording basis GDP components are recorded on accrual basis. In some cases the cash based recordings are converted into an accrual based. For example the figures of taxes on products (mainly VAT) are not on a cash base but on a one month delayed cash base which is thought to allow the best approach for accrual base data. Imports and exports are based on the change of ownership principle. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures In the 2014 Revision of the National Accounts grossing/netting procedures were revised, as far as necessary, in line with the ESA 2010 guidelines. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Production approach Agriculture, forestry and fishing: The two main sources for estimating agriculture are the Business Information Network of the Agricultural Economics Research Institute (LEI), and the Product boards . These bodies collect a great deal of functional information on the output and industrial processing of agricultural products and foodstuffs. Besides these two sources a number of other sources are also used, such as CBS crop estimates, the Agriculture Survey (CBS/Ministry of Agriculture), Dairy Products Statistics (CBS/Product Board) and Slaughter Statistics (Product Board). Statistics Netherlands compiles monthly data about the supply in Dutch fishing ports and about the prices of fish. Data on the supply of fish are provided by the Ministry of Agriculture, Nature Management and Fisheries. Price information of the product board of fishing is used by Statistics Netherlands. Mining and quarrying: Coal and metal ore: These commodities are imported mainly for intermediate use in production processes. A small part is re-exported (Foreign Trade Statistics Department of Statistics Netherlands). Crude petroleum production and natural gas production: The information on intermediate consumption is provided by an annual publication of the Dutch exploration and production company (NAM). The Netherlands gas supplier (Gasunie) buys all the natural gas produced by NAM and also imports some natural gas. Gasunie, supplies the natural gas to regional gas distributors, large (electricity) industries (with a direct connection to the major natural gas pipelines of the Gasunie) and to other countries. Gasunie data are enriched with data from a survey of these distributors and electricity and chemical companies. The volume changes provided are used for quarterly accounts. The producer prices are used as deflators. Crude petroleum is mainly imported by oil refineries. The amount produced by NAM and other oil extracting firms is also bought by the oil refineries or sold to other countries. A monthly survey among oil refineries and information from NAM are used to compile a crude petroleum account. The volume changes given in this account are used for quarterly accounts. A price for deflation is obtained by using the producer price index. Other mining and quarrying: This category is subject to the methodology described in section ‘Manufacturing’. Processing begins with the production statistics (size categories 5-9). The production statistics are then supplemented by Light Industry Survey data (size categories 0-4). Manufacturing: Five types of information can be distinguished: quantities produced, turnover data, use of raw materials, information about hours worked and industrial output price indices. The above-mentioned types of information are all used for the compilation of the production index per industry. The main source of this index is a statistical survey. Depending on the activity, enterprises report gross production, turnover, changes in stocks, physical quantities of products, energy consumption, and use of raw materials or hours worked. Production and distribution of electricity, gas and water: In addition to information about natural gas and crude petroleum, the energy accounts also include information about the energy and water companies. Construction: In the quarterly national accounts six industries are distinguished for construction: site preparation, construction of dwellings and other buildings, construction of civil engineering works, installation activities, completion of construction activities and the rental of building machines. These industries produce eight types of commodities: new dwellings, maintenance of dwellings, new buildings, maintenance of buildings, construction and installation of civil engineering, installations for dwellings, installations for buildings and other building activities. The turnover index provides the development in current prices for each of the six industries. The turnover for the construction of dwellings and other buildings is divided into newly built and maintenance. Maintenance is a rather autonomous process, whereas the building of new dwellings and buildings fluctuates over time. The development of maintenance of dwellings and non-residential buildings in previous years is assumed to continue. The rest is the total of newly built residences and buildings. This total is split into construction of dwellings and other buildings using the progress survey for these two activities. The number of productive hours is used to check the plausibility of the turnover index. Hotels and restaurants: Statistics Netherlands is obliged to supply data to the branche organisation of the hotel and restaurant sector. This means that source statistics on sales or revenues on a short term basis are available. Unfortunately this information is not in time for the flash estimate. Supplementary information on labour inputs and domestic consumption is used. Transport, storage and communications: For railways, air transport and sea transport both transport data and traffic data are available, as well as sales indicators. In addition, subsidies to the railways are obtained from government records. For air transport the quarterly business reports of KLM (Royal Dutch Airlines) are used. For inland shipping companies the main indicator of output, both domestic and abroad, is the trend of the number of ton-kilometres. Price indices are derived from the Rhine freight rates and the average proceeds per ton-kilometre in tramp shipping. The output of transport-related companies (storage companies, travel agencies, port companies, etc.) is measured from indirect data, viz. quantities of transferred goods and numbers of incoming sea-going vessels. Price trends are derived from wage trends. Output trends of road freight transport are estimated from data supplied by the Economic Bureau of Road and Water Transport. These data are obtained from a periodic panel survey and refer both to prices and to revenues. The communication establishments are covered by a large number of quantity indicators, such as numbers of phone calls, units of mail, telex messages, and so on. Data on phone rates are used to compile price indices. In addition quarterly business reports of KPN (dominant telecom enterprise) are used. Banking: data of the Central Bank of the Netherlands (DNB) are used. The DNB supervises the financial markets. Financial institutions that participate in these markets are obliged to provide their data to the DNB. The data of the DNB are published in statistical reports and on the DNB website. Supplementary quarterly business reports of banks are used. Insurance and pension funds: Since 2006, a quarterly source is available from the Central Bank (DNB). This source (the so called direct reports DRA) is developed for the Quarterly Sector Accounts and gives information in current prices. Among others, this source is used to estimate wages, social contributions and intermediate use of insurance and pension funds. On an annual basis the annual reports of enterprises are used. Supplementary quarterly business reports of insurance companies are used. Business services: For a number of branches source statistics on a short term basis are available, but only for regular quarterly estimation. These branches are: computer and related activities, legal and economic activities, architectural and engineering activities, advertising, activities of employment agencies and other business activities. For other branches, such as real estate activities, renting of movables and research and development few or no sources are available. Supplementary labour information is used as a check. The breakdown of value changes into volume and price components can be problematic. This issue is subject to constant study. Government: For the regular quarterly estimates, source data are used for a very substantial part of the government; namely the Dutch State, the Municipalities, Provinces, Waterboards and the Social Security Funds. These sources cover compensation of employees, other taxes and subsidies on production, intermediate consumption expenditures, sales and social benefits in kind. However, the data on compensation of employees are replaced by the Labour Accounts data, which covers all of the government. The labour accounts (LA) gives ex post quarterly information on jobs, labour input and compensation of employees, decomposed into wages and salaries and social contributions. These estimates are usually already used for the quarterly flash estimates. Labour input plays an important role in the constant price calculations. Expenditure approach Household final consumption expenditure: Because of the absence of a short term budget survey, the compilation of the monthly consumption index is based on the following internal and external sources; these are seen as representative for the changes in different consumption categories: Monthly turnover retail trade and commercial and personal services, consumer price index, consumption of energy (in volumes) and turnover of petrol stations, international trade in services: consumption by non-residents in the Netherlands and by resident households in the rest of the world, consumption of other services, for example medical services and welfare, transport and communication services, other internal sources: statistics on housing and population growth, external sources: turnover of supermarkets and sales of vehicles. Government expenditure: The standard procedures are usually followed to estimate the various variables. Government production follows from the expenses, while government production less sales gives government consumption, which is decomposed into an individual and a collective part. The results meet the requirements of the Eurostat manual on quarterly non-financial accounts for general government (ISSN 1725-0048) . The Council of Social Insurance (CVZ) gives quarterly data on the social benefits in kind. These social benefits predominantly relate to compulsory social insurance, essentially under the Medical Health Insurance Fund Act (ZVW) and the Exceptional Medical Health Expenses Act (AWBZ). The two Acts contribute more than 85% to all expenditures on social benefits in kind. The remaining benefits are estimated on the basis of the expenses on the two Acts mentioned. These concern mostly expenses on disablement support, legal aid support, individual subsidies on rent, student transport subsidies and other social assistance. The calculation framework is given below: Calculation framework Plus Wages (source Labour Accounts) Plus Social contributions by employers (source Labour Accounts) = Compensation of employees Plus Consumption of fixed capital (PIM-method) = Gross domestic product (factor costs) Minus Other subsidies on production (Ministry of Finance) Plus Other taxes on production (Ministry of Finance) = Gross domestic product (market prices) Plus Intermediate consumption (direct sources, see text) = Production (basic prices) Minus Sales (direct sources, see text) = Final production own consumption Plus Social benefits in kind (CVZ and direct sources) = Final consumption (of government) Gross fixed capital formation: In contrast with the estimation of gross fixed capital formation in annual accounts, the gross fixed capital formation in quarterly accounts is based on the supply side of the economy (indirect method). Information is based, for example, on construction statistics, imports and turnover data of different industries. The annual accounts are compiled by using information about the input of industries during the process of production (direct method). This input is of course specified in different assets. Exports and imports of goods: The major source for imports and exports of goods in the quarterly national accounts (QNA) are the International trade statistics compiled by the international trade department. Data (current prices) are obtained monthly from surveys. Data must also be supplied in prices of the previous year. Exports and imports of services: Data is based on surveys by Statistics Netherlands. A brief description of the sources and the method can be found in section 5.16 in the GNI inventory 2001 (footnote 17). Compensation of employees: The administration of the Tax-authorities (Walvis) is the source for estimations on employment and wages for quarterly flash accounts. The response is limited at the time of the flash. Normally, Walvis provides data on the number of jobs and total labour input, both by sex, and earnings per SBI category. Another source is the Labour Force Survey (LFS), which gives the number of jobs and employed persons, including and excluding the self-employed. During compilation of the flash, the estimated employment growth is checked against the LFS results. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The statisticians of the National Accounts Department have their influence on the design of the source statistics. In particular, looking to the production process as a chain of producing statistics activities, the National Accounts form the final link in the chain. The head of the National Accounts Department is acting in this process as the chain-director. This view on the statistical production process is of recent date, but it illustrates the position of National Accounts in the Statistical Office. Of course, also non-CBS information is used in the National Accounts. National Accounts will rely more and more upon administrative sources. For this kind of sources more effort is needed to bring the source data in line with the requirements of the SNA and the ESA 2010. In the process tables in the GNI inventory 2001 (link in footnote 17) is seen to what extent the GDP estimate relies on surveys and censuses (59% in 2001). 3.1.3 Source data timeliness In most cases the source data are timely for the National Accounts statistics. Of course, for flash estimates less data are available than for the regular quarterly estimates. E.g. for flash estimates, sometimes only two months of the quarter are available. But, by and large, the source data meet the requirements of National Accounts. For the preliminary estimates the release date is 45 days after the quarter under report, for final quarterly estimates this is 90 days. The first annual figures on National Accounts (just like the quarterly accounts mainly based upon short term sources) are released three months after the end of the reporting year. The improved annual estimate is released one year later and the definitive National Accounts are published 2,5 year after the reference year. 3.2.1 Source data assessment A routinely assessment of the source data in the National Accounts production process is the balancing procedure in the Supply and Use Table. As far as necessary, the results of these assessment procedures are given as feedback to the source statistics. The source statistics are, furthermore, regularly audited by the division PIM (see paragraph 0.4). 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques General estimation method: To extrapolate the economic structure, a production function with fixed coefficients is assumed. The volume change in gross production is used to calculate the volume change in intermediate consumption by commodity of the branches of industry. The next step is to add data on household and government consumption, capital formation, exports and imports and, if available, data or estimates on the change in stocks and work in progress. The result is an estimation of total demand (= total use). This is confronted with the estimation of gross production plus imports (= total supply). Subsequently the various output and expenditure indicators at current and constant prices are used to calculate a price index for intermediate use. The result is a column of statistical discrepancies at current and constant prices. Differences between the estimates of the supply and the use of a product group are eliminated by adjusting elements in either the use table or the supply table. Much depends on the quality of the basic sources. If gross production is changed because demand is found to be higher than production, then the whole column (input) of this branch must be recalculated. This of course leads to new statistical discrepancies. This is called the process of iteration. If a figure in current prices is adjusted, the consequences for the corresponding figures in prices of the previous year, the volume index and the price index are examined. If a figure in constant prices is adjusted, a similar procedure takes place. The results, especially big adjustments, are then examined and discussed with the National accounts industry analysts and the final demand analysts. For a more detailed description of the QNA methodology, see: Janssen, R.J.A. and Algera, S.B., 1988, The methodology of the Dutch system of Quarterly Accounts (National Accounts Occasional Papers, number 25). S.B. Algera and R.J.A. Janssen, 1991, The development of short-term macro-economic statistics, in: CBS Select 7, statistical essays on statistical integration. Data are further subjected to plausibility checks. For example, to check labour productivity quarter-on-quarter changes of total output can be compared with changes in labour input; or: the output/input ratio of an industry can be verified if source data of the output as well as input are used. Additional more specific information about compilation practices on the level of branches or expenditure categories (production and the expenditure approach) is given below. Production approach Agriculture, forestry and fishing: No information is available about agricultural services, gardening services or forestry when the flash or regular quarter is compiled. Hourly wage rates per industry are used as an indication for the price. The volume of the agricultural services is left unchanged from the corresponding quarter in the previous year. Mining and quarrying: Crude petroleum production and natural gas production: The information on intermediate consumption is provided by an annual publication of the Dutch exploration and production company (NAM), from which a production/consumption ratio can be obtained. NAM accounts for approximately two-thirds of Netherlands production. This ratio is considered representative for the entire category. Other mining and quarrying: Output, intermediate consumption and value added are analysed for plausibility and continuity and, if necessary, adjusted. Construction: The volume change of the supply of building materials is compared with the volume change of the construction in each industry. These volume changes should be in line. Wholesale and retail trade: The calculation of retail and wholesale margins is not based on direct surveys. Instead, the retail margins are directly linked to household consumption. Wholesale margins are linked to household consumption, gross capital formation, imports and exports and intermediate consumption. Every aggregate is linked with a certain tariff that is extracted from the most recent supply and use table on an annual basis. Insurance and pension funds: As no sources are available on a quarterly basis, the trend of the previous year is extrapolated to the quarter under review. Care and other services: Due to a lack of short term information, trends in this industry are extrapolated using annual forecasts and professional publications. In health and social work activities in particular, changes are based on data on public financing. Supplementary data on the number of employees and wages and salaries is used. review. Government as an industry: For this specification the government is broken down into the usual categories of public administration and defense as well as subsidised education. Some government units are grouped into other industries like forestry, environmental services and health services. These, however, concern only a very small part of total government. Direct information on expenses and sales are available for large parts of government (Dutch State, the Municipalities, Provinces, Waterboards and the Social Security Funds). Expenditure approach Household final consumption expenditure: Although more details are published, the year-on-year value and volume changes of a few main commodity categories are the essence of the consumption index. These categories are the consumption of food, beverages and tobacco, durable consumer goods, other goods, services and total and domestic consumption by households, including non-profit institutions (NPI) serving households. Government expenditure: The government as a sector is split up into sub-sectors. For the government as a sector only three consumption categories are estimated. These are the social benefits in kind, actual individual consumption and actual collective consumption by government. Taxes and subsidies: data on taxes and subsidies are obtained from the Ministry of Finance. The cash receipts from the various taxes are transformed to the accrual recording basis used in National Accounts by applying a one-month time-lag. In accordance with the current EU-regulations, the 90% criterion is fully respected. Almost all of these data used direct information. Stocks: The changes in stocks are obtained as a residual. The differences between the estimation of the production side and the sum of the main components of the expenditure side are included in the changes in stocks. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Whereas the focus in the compilation of the annual accounts is on the (continuous) level-estimates given by the source data, in the compilation of the quarterly accounts, changes with respect to the corresponding quarter of the previous year play a more important role. The estimated changes are used to extrapolate the quarter in the previous year to the quarter under review. The quarters in the previous year are compiled by adjusting the totals of the quarters to the totals of the preliminary annual estimates. In this way the preliminary annual data are divided into quarters without losing the structure information of the quarters. Production approach Other mining and quarrying: Constant-price analysis of output, consumption and value added is based on the monthly price index and, where available, production unit values. Manufacturing: The source data may measure the volume changes in production both directly (e.g. number of produced cars) and indirectly (deflated turnover data). As far as turnover and value of stocks are used for the calculation of the index, industrial output price indices are used for deflation. Production and distribution of electricity, gas and water: Constant-price analysis of output, consumption and value added is based on the monthly output price index and, where available, unit values of production. Construction: The output price for residences and other buildings, the price index of civil engineering works and the input price index for the construction costs of residences is used to deflate turnover. Government as in industry: For deflation a number of sources is utilized. The expenses of the government, broken down by commodity, are deflated on the basis of the CPI (Consumer Price index). The deflation of the consumption of fixed capital is pursued on the basis of the PIM-method. A volume index of labour costs is derived by deflating the wages and salaries by the average change in the hourly wages agreed in collective negotiations between employers and labour unions. In the Dutch circumstances this index, generally speaking, is a good approximation of a pure price index. However, additional research is pursued to assess whether a part of the ‘incidental’ wage change must be considered a pure price component. As a result, changes in the wages and salaries caused by higher education or working experience and by changes in the composition of the labour force are included in the volume index. So all quality changes are included in the volume index. Employer’s social contributions in constant prices are estimated by multiplying the value in the previous period by the volume index of wages and salaries. The volume measure of other taxes and subsidies is calculated on the basis of the related variables. Value added tax (VAT), for example, is calculated on the basis of consumer expenses, investments and intermediate consumption. Finally, the volume measure of government productions is calculated residually, just like the value measure. Expenditure approach Government expenditure: Volume measures from the Council of Social Insurance (CVZ) are utilized for constant price estimates of social benefits in kind. The volume measure of final government consumption is calculated as a residual. However, for subsidised education a specific volume method is utilised based on the number of pupils (for primary and secondary education). Exports and imports of goods: Even though the department for foreign trade statistics collects volume information during the production of their statistics, producers’ price data are used. These are price indices of exports, imports and domestic prices. These files are classified according to the EU’s Classification per Product Activity (CPA), which are aggregated into NA commodity groups. To deflate the exports of Dutch products, both to the EU and beyond we use the producers’ price index of exports. For imports and re-exports to de EU and beyond we use the price index of imports. If export and import prices are not available, the domestic price is used. Seasonal adjustment: Two methods are used to calculate the quarterly volume changes. The first method is the year-on-year method, in which seasonally unadjusted figures are compared with the same quarter of the previous year. In this way seasonality is avoided. Chain-linked series compiled by the Over the year procedure with the reference year 2021 are used. The second method is called the quarter-on-quarter (or seasonally adjusted) method. Here chain-linked series complied by the Annual Overlap procedure with the reference year 2021 are used. JDemetra+ application of Eurostat is used for seasonal adjustment. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results In the production process of the Supply and Use table, as well as in the other integration frameworks of the Sector Accounts and the Labour Accounts, the intermediate results are nearly continuously checked by the chief-integrator(s) and the branch and sector specialists. In these procedures they pay special attention to things like the coherence between price and volume changes, the coherence and consistency with VAT estimates, and other tax information, the comparability in time of the estimates and so on. In the last stages of the compilation process and more special in the balancing process, specialists are involved in the corrections that has to be made to meet consistency in the whole framework. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data The balancing process employs all kinds of adjustments and plausibility tests to ensure that the original estimates are mutually consistent. For the Quarterly National Accounts the balancing of data takes place in a supply and use framework (118 industries and 118 commodities). The quarterly economic structure of a base quarter is updated simultaneously in current prices and constant prices. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Expenditure is estimated in the context of the supply and use table, where independent estimates of output, intermediate consumption and final use are compared and brought into balance. This integrated procedure ensures the equality of the GDP estimates using the expenditure approach and production approach. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Statistics Netherlands quarterly investigates the updates in National Accounts.
