 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: The external debt is compiled by Banco de Portugal and the data are generally compiled in accordance with the concepts and methodology set out in the External Debt Statistics: Guide for compilers and users (2014 edition) as well as the sixth edition of the Balance of Payments Manual ( BPM6 ) published by the IMF. Definition: The gross external debt position is the outstanding liabilities side of Portugal’s overall financial relations with countries abroad. A liability is considered to be a debt instrument when the holder has the unconditional right to receive in the future some payment(s) related to principal and/or interest. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Portugal’s external liabilities are a subset of the International investment position and are based, on the same methodology. The sector breakdown is fully consistent with the guidelines set forth in the BPM6 and in the External Debt Guide , including: a) The separate identification of the central bank and the general government external debt liabilities; and, b) The inclusion of the external debt liabilities of non-monetary private enterprises and of households under “other sectors”. Households include own-account workers. Deposits of nonresidents with domestic banks are included. "Other debt liabilities" is a category that includes liabilities that respondents could not classify into one of the major categories (bonds and notes or loans and deposits). Trade credits owed to nonresidents are included and are shown in a separate line in the case of "General Government" and "Other sectors". The short-term/long-term classification is on an original maturity basis. The coverage of short-term debt includes: money market instruments, short-term loans, trade credits, and other short-term debt liabilities. Major reclassifications are clearly identified through table footnotes. (For example, reclassifications of external debt liabilities by institutional sector due to changes in the debtor resulting from debt rescheduling/refinancing are identified). External debt liabilities are attributed to the sector of the immediate borrower. External debt liabilities are classified under the sector category of the debtor and not of any guarantor. Scope of the data Positions coverage: In comparison to the liabilities side of the International investment position, the quarterly external debt does not include any financial liabilities arising from participation rights, i.e. shares, investment funds shares, other equity, reinvested earnings and real estate investment. Other: External debt data are compiled on the basis of covering debt positions between residents and nonresidents. External debt liabilities payable in goods and/or services are included in the external debt position. Private sector debt is included in the gross external debt position classified either in “banks” or in “other sectors” (in the case of the private non-monetary financial institutions, private non-financial companies or households), according to the immediate borrower sector. External debt data measure both domestic and foreign currency debt owed to nonresidents. Positions in financial derivatives and equity instruments, are excluded from the external debt position. External debt includes securities issued in the domestic markets by residents and purchased by nonresidents. Financial leases with nonresidents are treated as loans and included in the gross external debt position. Exceptions to coverage Not applicable Unrecorded activity Not applicable 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to unit of account: Positions are converted into euro by making use of end-of-period exchange rates. Valuation: Positions valued at market prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis Outstanding liabilities arising from accrued interest not yet paid are included in the gross external debt position, as part of the value of the underlying debt instrument. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The international Investment Position follows the sixth edition of Balance of Payments Manual ( BPM6 ). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Source data are consistent with the definitions, scope and classifications of External Debt. Source data covers the full range of institutional sectors and is kept under continuous review to ensure that the data collection program is comprehensive. Also these data collection programs are sufficiently open and flexible to provide for new developments in sources. The main sources for compiling the External Debt, accordingly to functional category, are: 1. Direct Investment : transactions are captured through the direct reporting system for external accounts statistics named COPE ( Comunicação de Operações e Posições com o Exterior ) where resident entities (exception made to households) report directly to Banco de Portugal their cross border operations and to some extent the related positions. Up to 2013, on an annual basis, FDI incorporated the results from a direct investment survey, which was based on a sample. From 2014 onwards direct investment is based on the annual accounts reported by all resident corporations through Simplified Corporate Information ( IES - Informação Empresarial Simplificada ), an administrative source. 2. Portfolio Investment : Stocks data are collected monthly from major respondents, on a security-by-security basis. Monetary Financial Institutions report monthly their own assets outstanding amounts, and the securities of their customers, when they act as custodians. Direct reporting to the External Statistics – COPE is also an important source for quality control and complete information provided by the internal securities database. In addition, for the unlisted shares we use information from Simplified Corporate Information (IES - Informa ção Empresarial Simplificada) . 3. Other Investment : Direct reporting system for external accounts statistics - COPE ( Comunicação de Operações e Posições com o Exterior ), Banco de Portugal's balance sheet, Treasury, banks' balance sheets, Deposit taking corporation's balance sheet, international banking statistics from BIS and a Quarterly Survey on Non-Financial Entreprises (ITENF). On an annual basis, exhaustive data on trade credit is obtained from the Simplified Corporate Information (IES) – for further information on statistics compiled from the IES please refer to Supplement 1 | 2008 to the Statistical Bulletin. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data are consistent with the definitions, scope, and classifications adopted for External Debt. Data are not available to allow the decomposition into public sector external debt and publicly guaranteed private sector debt. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data collection and processing timetables are arranged to meet timeliness and periodicity for disseminating the external debt statistics. These timetables are distributed to all respondents. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Direct Investment: Statistics for direct investment are compiled according to estimates mainly based on the accumulation of BOP transactions data. Once the results of IES are made available, the exercise of reconciliation of stocks and transactions data aims at verifying and checking the statistical information concerning direct investment. Portfolio Investment: Information concerning other Monetary and Financial Institutions is checked with money and banking statistics. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Data compilation employs sound statistical techniques to deal with data sources. Some compilation procedures are implemented in order to minimize processing errors and to estimate missing observations. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Estimates based on the accumulation of flows since the last positions surveyed are produced to obtain quarterly statistics and most timely annual data. A security-by-security reporting system, based on the ISIN code, provides the information for portfolio investment. Besides the security-by-security information, the assets side of investment funds is covered by an additional source of information (CMVM – Securities Market Commission); the balance sheet of Banco de Portugal provides data for compiling the central bank sector. Other investment: When owed to non-residents, all the components of other investment – trade credits, loans, currency and deposits and other debt liabilities – are included in the gross external debt position. Data on Central Bank assets and liabilities are derived from Banco de Portugal's balance sheet. Data on banks assets and liabilities are collected from external positions of the banks' balance sheets, as reported for Money and Banking statistics. Currently, for the other sector, data on transactions and stocks is collected through a direct reporting system. For the past, a reconstruction of series was made based on the old data and the data available from the new report system. Also, for deposits (assets side) a mix methodology is used, onceInternational Banking Statisticsinformation is incorporated. For quality control, a quarterly survey to the major exporters / importers is used to produce estimates of stock data on trade credits. On an annual basis, exhaustive data on trade credit is obtained from the Simplified Corporate Information (IES) – for further information on statistics compiled from the IES please refer to Supplement 1/2008 to the Statistical Bulletin. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Direct Investment: Once the results of the data sources are made available, the exercise of reconciliation of stocks and transactions data aims at verifying and checking the statistical information concerning direct investment. A comparison with some EU partner country data is also used as a supplementary tool. Portfolio Investment: Information concerning other Monetary and Financial Institutions is checked with money and banking statistics; as regards listed instruments, information made available by Euronext Lisbon is used for verification; for foreign securities, the ESCB Centralized Securities Database (CSDB) is used. Whom-to-whom tables comparison with the internal database on securities for debt securities – portfolio + direct investment and by instrument. Other Investment: Deposits and loans are compared with international banking statistics of the BIS, balance sheet of monetary and financial statistics for the bank's sector. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Statistical discrepancies in intermediate data are investigated, and all the information is checked. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Studies and analyses of revisions follow the revisions calendar annually made public, and are harmonized with the revision policies among international institutions. The statistical quality unit of the department makes periodic reports on the quality of the information concerning BOP/IIP (which includes information on portfolio investment) pointing out specific points for further analysis and potential correction if applicable. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Revisions are measured, assessed, and explained internally but normally this differences are not communicated on a regular basis. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished but non-confidential statistics can be disseminated upon request . 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Prompt and knowledgeable service and support are available to users of statistics.
