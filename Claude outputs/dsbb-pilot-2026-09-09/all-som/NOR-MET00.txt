 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The external trade statistics (ETS) give information about the commodity flows between Norway and other countries, i.e. exports of goods and imports of goods, the coverage of which is based on the guidelines of the United Nations' Statistical Commission. Relationship of national practices with international or regional guidelines: Some exports and imports of goods (while calculated and included in the National Accounts and in the Balance of Payments) are not covered by the ETS. These are mainly imports directly to the installations on the Norwegian Continental Shelf and expenditures on fuel (shipping and air companies' expenditures abroad). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The ETS commodities are grouped according to two different classifications: the six-digit Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS) of the World Customs Organization, extended by two national Norwegian digits, and the Standard International Trade Classification (SITC) of the United Nations. Classification by country of origin, dispatch and destination follows the alpha-2 codes of the ISO-3166 standard, with a few additional Norwegian codes, marked X. Scope of the data Exports and imports cover goods leaving and entering the statistical territory. The Norwegian statistical territory consists of mainland Norway and its territorial waters, Jan Mayen, Svalbard with Bjørnøya, the Norwegian part of the continental shelf and Norwegian vessels, aircraft and mobile oil platforms. With a few exceptions, the statistics follow the general trade system. There are four different types of customs warehouses. Goods waiting on declearance, can be stored in either a general or a central warehouse. For imports, the time of recording is set to when the goods are taken out of the warehouse and not when arrived the statistical area. This means that the statistical processing of these data follows the special trade principle. However, in practice the time difference between storage and withdrawals from warehouses is usually so short that the statistical processing is considered to be approximately equal to the general trade principle. For the remaining two types of customs warehouse; duty free and storage for further processing, the statistical treatment is according to the general trade system. Exceptions to coverage Exceptions to coverage Certain goods and movement are excluded from the statistics. This concerns monetary gold and means of payment which are legal tender; goods for temporary use; goods moving to and from territorial enclaves (includes embassies and national armed forces) abroad; goods used as carriers of customised information and downloaded information; particular goods which are not the subject of commercial transactions; goods for and after repair; means of transport travelling in the course of their work; goods in transit. Statistical threshold is NOK 1 000. Goods totalling less than NOK 1 000 are excluded from the statistics. Imports of goods less than NOK 350 and exports of goods less than NOK 5.000 (except from goods that are subject to taxes and restrictions) are not subject to declaration by the Customs. Imports and exports that are exempted from ordinary declaration are referred to in the Customs Act §§ 4-10 and 4-11. In addition, the external trade statistics does not cover certain trade in goods which are transported directly to and from the Norwegian shelf, imports to Norwegian vessels, floating oil platforms and aircrafts abroad, exports of bunkers to foreign ships and aircrafts in Norwegian ports and airports. 2.4.2 Recording basis The statistical value is defined as the value of goods when crossing the Norwegian border. Duties, VAT and other taxes are not included. In general, CIF for imports and FOB for exports. Statistical value of trade with vessels, mobile oil platforms and aircraft is the transfer value/market value at the time when the change in ownership took place. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The vast majority of data are compiled from documents collected by the Customs Authorities on imports and exports of goods (the so called SAD documents). However, some additional data is collected separately. Imports and exports of vessels and mobile oil platforms from Norwegian ship registers and importers/exporters; imports and exports of aircrafts from aviation companies; exports of crude oil and natural gas in a gaseous state from enterprises in the petroleum sector, the Norwegian Petroleum Directorate and the Ministry of Petroleum and Energy; data on direct export of fish caught outside Norwegian customs territory from Directorate of Fisheries; exports of coal from Svalbard from exporters. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The data are based on the General Trade system, but has elements of special trade characteristics on two accounts. First, a shipment of foreign merchandise is not included in the statistics if it passes through a customs warehouse with no inbound declaration and is sent abroad again. Second, if the shipment proceeds into free circulation it is recorded as an import at the time of release, not at the time of entering the customs warehouse Type of data collected: Commodity identification (eight-digit commodity number), values, quantities (net weight) and partner country. Other types of data include mode of transportation and nationality of the carrier. Basis of country data: For import consignments, country of origin and country of consignment are recorded. For exports, the last known country of destination is recorded. Data are disseminated in Norwegian kroner (NOK) on exports (valued on a f.o.b. basis) and imports (valued on a c.i.f. basis). 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Time of clearance for customs based data (for the most part coinciding with the period of transaction); time of transaction for areas covered by Statistics Norway's direct data collection. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Processing system: The Norwegian computerized declaration system TVINN (the Customs Authorities' Information System with the Business Society); manual system in Statistics Norway for entering the data on transactions outside the Norwegian customs territory, but inside the statistical territory. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Adjustments to improve coverage/classification/timing/valuation: The data in the customs declarations are firstly subject to various controls in the Customs Authorities electronic declarations system. Secondly, there are controls run in Statistics Norway concerning the delineation of the data material as well as for all variables that are published. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Seasonal adjustment: Monthly figures for value (various aggregates) and quarterly volume indices (aggregate level and selected commodity groups, mainly two-digit SITC level) are seasonally adjusted. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Reconciliation: None. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Studies concerning discrepancies comparing national figures to external trade data from partner countries (mirror statistics) have been carried out as well as analysis of data revisions for the purpose of developing better and more efficient methodology for control of data. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Tables showing revisions on preliminary monthly figures for imports and exports by SITC 2 digits group are published monthly from 2016 onwards. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points External Trade Information Service, e-mail: (externaltrade@ssb.no) , telephone: +47 21 09 47 53 The inquiry service guides the public on questions related to external trade statistics and can provide customized statistics on request, ad hoc or as a subscription. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs All external trade statistics are disseminated on Statistics Norway's website, http://www.ssb.no/english/subject/09/05 .
