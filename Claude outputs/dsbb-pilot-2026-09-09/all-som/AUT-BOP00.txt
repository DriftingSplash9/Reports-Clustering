 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Definitions: The Austrian balance of payments is a statistical statement summarizing the economic transactions of residents of Austria with the rest of the world during a quarter. Austria's balance of payments statistics are compiled in accordance with the guidelines published by the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6) and the European System of Accounts 2010 ( ESA 2010). A detailed description about Austria's BOP can be found in the BOP book published by the ECB: http://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/pdf/EUbopintlinvposstmeth.pdf?7205cc78a323d45227b89a5716c38 e52 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification system used to compile Austria's balance of payments statistics is in accordance with the guidelines published by the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6) and the ESA2010 . Scope of the data Transactions coverage: The data cover all transactions between residents and nonresidents. Geographic coverage: A geographical attribution of the data is available for all partner countries, but due to confidentiality reason not all countries will be published. Exceptions to coverage Transactions coverage: Nonresident-nonresident transactions are systematically excluded. The survey design relies in principle on concentration surveys. Transactions below thresholds or transactions that can not be directly observed are estimated based on administrative data and international best practices. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: Transactions are valued at market prices. Conversion to unit of account: Data reported in foreign currencies are converted to local currency (Euros) at period average exchange rates. 2.4.2 Recording basis Recording of income: Data are recorded on an accrual basis, except income on loans and deposits of other and direct investments where reported income can be on a cash basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Austria has switched to a "direct reporting system" for balance of payments on January 1st 2006. Since then, banks and other depository institutions have to report only on their own assets, liabilities and transactions plus all securities deposits of their customers. Securities (including domestic securities) are reported on a security-by-security basis. Non-banks must report quarterly on their trade in services, and monthly on assets and liabilities vis-à-vis non-resident entities. Financial institutions, non-financial corporations, governments and non-profit organizations have to report on their cross-border transactions (except goods) on their own behalf. Households must report only on financial accounts transactions, while their current account transactions are generally based on estimates, sampling or administrative data. Several thresholds apply in different areas of the balance of payments. The reporting frequency ranges from monthly to annually, depending on the type of transaction as well as on the quantitative relevance of the reporter. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording CURRENT ACCOUNT: Goods: General merchandise data are obtained from Statistik Austria's Foreign Trade Statistics on a monthly and quarterly basis. The method of data collection is different for merchandise between member countries of the European Community (monthly reports of companies, so-called Intrastat) and merchandise with third countries (customs declarations, the so-called Extrastat). While goods imports and exports as recorded in international trade statistics reflect the physical flow of goods across borders, goods imports and exports on a BoP basis reflect the economic transfer of ownership. Therefore, the original international trade data need to be adjusted for BoP purposes. Goods procured in ports by carriers and transactions in nonmonetary gold are also included in balance of payments statistics and separately identifiable. Imports undergo a cif-fob conversion in order to fulfill the economic ownership concept of the balance of payments. Further on, estimates apply for transactions in goods not crossing the Austrian border and transactions by units which are not resident according to the definition laid down in the BPM6 and ESA 2010 are deducted from total transactions. Services: Data on International Trade in Services provided or used by nonfinancial corporations are collected by Statistics Austria by means of a dedicated quarterly survey. The number of enterprises surveyed for Trade in Services is about 5.000. Quarterly "matrix-reports" (by country and by type of service) are submitted. A threshold of 500.000 Euros for annual exports or imports of services applies. The Oesterreichische Nationalbank compiles corresponding data for banks and insurance companies to record cross-border trade in financial services. The reporting threshold for fees and commissions paid or received per calendar year for cross-border services is EUR 10 million for banks. For insurance companies, no threshold applies to insurance transactions, only to other services (EUR 20 million p.a.). Travel: The main source for estimating Travel receipts are Tourism data on nights spent by foreigners in different types of accommodation. Average expenditures per night are approximated by making use of credit card data (ECB Payment Statistics), business cycle information and price development. These data are complemented by Mobile Positioning Data (MPD) to estimate travel which cannot be captured by accommodation statistics (same-day tourism, tourists staying in their own apartments or with their relatives or friends). The main source for Travel expenditures is a quarterly household sample survey, which comprises 3.000 households per quarter. The survey is carried out in close connection with tourism statistics. Additional information is provided by credit card companies and also MPD are used. Financial services: Credits are mostly earned by banks, which report their service charges to the Oesterreichische Nationalbank at least quarterly. Services imported by banks are compiled under the respective headings. Operational leasing: encompasses e.g. the leasing of means of transportation and capital goods, with the business risk borne by the lessor. This item is clearly distinguished from financial leasing, where the capital component is assigned to loans, while the income component is shown in the income category. Government services: This item includes all transactions by Austrian embassies, consulates with the residents of the host country and of foreign embassies and consulates in Austria, if they are not included elsewhere. Primary Income: Investment income is calculated on a monthly basis, mostly on an accruals basis. For direct investment profits in the current reporting year are based on estimates on an individual enterprise basis until these estimates are replaced by annual survey results with a time lag of 21 months. Dividends are reported monthly, which allows for the calculation of monthly reinvested earnings. In line with international standards, reinvested earnings appear in income and in the financial account as identical amounts. Portfolio investment income on an accruals basis is calculated from the monthly stock reports on a security-by-security basis with the help of a securities database, which includes all the relevant information. Income on other investment is based on monthly reports, on a cash basis. The income corresponding to loans and deposits reported by domestic banks is estimated via separately reported interest rates of a subset of these entities. Compensations of employees are based on administrative data. The data on the gross earnings of Austrians working abroad are derived above all from mirror statistics compiled by Austria's neighboring countries. The data on labor income payable to nonresidents are based on payslip statistics and broken down by nationality. Primary income also includes taxes on products, product subsidies and other subsidies. The data are based on Austria's final budget accounts. Secondary Income (formerly Current transfers): Redistribution of income of the public sector relies on regular government records. The most important transfers are contributions to and receipts from the European Union Institutions. Data on private transfers comprise of insurance transactions (following the service charge principle) and of workers' remittances which are based on administrative data and estimates. Non-profit organizations are surveyed quarterly on their cross-border aid provided. CAPITAL ACCOUNT: Public sector capital transfers mainly consist of those EU reimbursements which are not attributable to current transfers. They are available from public sector records. Private sector capital transfers comprise, among other things, debt forgiveness, legacies, the setting up of foundations and the like, which are reported by banks, enterprises, tax consultants and notaries. The category acquisition/disposal of non-produced, nonfinancial assets reflects e.g. the sale/purchase of carbon emission certificates, the sale of customer bases, transfer fees for sportspeople and so on. FINANCIAL ACCOUNT: Direct investment: In line with the definitions set forth in BPM6 , direct investment covers equity, reinvested earnings and "other capital", including transactions between fellow enterprises. The 10% rule is strictly applied. The cross-border acquisition and disposal of real estate also falls in the direct investment category. Reporting is required – on a case by case basis for individual investments - above a threshold of Euro 500.000.- per transaction on a monthly basis. Indirect reinvested earnings have been compiled since reporting year 2023. Portfolio investment: A comprehensive and reliable compilation system is used for portfolio investment flows (and stocks). The system is based on data collection on a security-by security basis using the International Securities Identification Number (ISIN). Based on the reported ISIN code in connection with the securities database, all necessary BoP (and IIP) classifications (by financial instrument, sector and country) are made. Depository institutions (banks) report monthly on their own (stocks) and on their customers deposits (stocks and flows) without any reporting threshold. Information on repos and reverse repos is part of the reporting requirements. The information from banks is supplemented by compulsory reports by non-banks (corporations and households) on securities which are held outside resident depository institutions, which means securities held at non-resident custodians or in own custody. Reporting by non-banks is required quarterly, for all securities held on deposits abroad if the stock exceeds Euro 5 mio. The reported information on securities held outside resident depository institutions is enriched with data on Third Party Holdings in the Euroarea from the ESCB Securities Holdings Statistics (SHSS) as well as estimations for securities held outside the Euroarea based on data on deposits from the BIS. Financial derivatives: Reporting on cross-border Financial derivatives is required monthly, if net payments related to financial derivatives exceed Euro 1 mio. Specific reports on stocks are collected on a quarterly basis from enterprises which make up their balance sheet according to IAS/IFRS, if assets or liabilities exceed Euro 1 mio at the end of the reporting period. Other investment: Data on "other investment" are based on monthly reports by banks and non-banks. Both report on stocks and possible write-offs broken down by country and by currency. Transactions are derived net based on the difference in stocks, taking into account exchange-rate and valuation changes. There are separate reports for Trade credits and for "other instruments" (loans and deposits). For each of the two reports a separate threshold of Euro 10 mio of foreign assets or foreign liabilities applies. If the threshold is exceeded, a full report is required. The reports are broken down into assets and liabilities as well as into counterparts within or outside the enterprise group. In addition, other investment is enriched by estimations regarding cross-border loans and deposits of domestic households as well as financial and non-financial corporations vis-a-vis non-domestic banks including estimates comprising the associated income. Reserve assets: are based on internal reports of the Oesterreichische Nationalbank. It should be noted that Austria is part of the Euro area and therefore data on reserve assets should be interpreted in the context of a currency union only. Data for the Euro area as a whole are published by the European Central Bank. Errors and omissions: If all data reported would be complete, on time and without mistakes, errors and omissions theoretically have a zero value. Errors and omissions are frequently used as a quality indicator of BOP statistics. Errors and omissions should remain small and, most importantly, have a zero arithmetic mean over time. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Seasonal adjustment: No seasonal adjustment takes place. None
