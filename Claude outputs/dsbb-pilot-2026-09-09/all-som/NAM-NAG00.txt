 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The National Accounts Concepts and methodologies underpinning the quarterly and annual compilation of National Accounts at both current and constant prices as well as by all three GDP compilation methods are based on international and integrated guidelines set in the 2008 System of National Accounts and harmonized with the International Standards for Industrial Classification (ISIC) revision 4, COICOP, CPC rev2 and Harmonise System of trade. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The data are classified by industry and the estimates are compiled and published using the International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC, rev.4) slightly modified to Namibian requirements, more specifically for minerals. Other classifications used are the Classification of the Functions of Government (COFOG), Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP), Central Product Classification (CPC), the Standard International Trade Classification (SITC) and Harmonize System (HS). Annual GDP estimates are produced and disseminated by both the production and expenditure approach, at current and at constant (2015) prices whereas the GDP by income method is only compiled at current prices. Namibia Statistics Agency (NSA) also produces and disseminates estimates of Gross National Income (GNI), National Disposable Income (NDI), Gross Fixed Capital Formation and Savings. The savings estimate as a balancing item - the difference between disposable income and final consumption expenditure. GDP constant quarterly estimates are compiled at 2015 price, whereas both current and constant prices estimates are compiled and disseminated. The production approach is deemed more reliable compared to the expenditure approach. This is considering that the production approach employs the more robust data sources and methods compared to expenditure approach where the private final consumption expenditure is compiled as a residual. The data source for quarterly GDP are administrative and surveys conducted by Namibia Statistics Agency in conjunction with the Bank of Namibia. The quarterly estimates are compiled at a more aggregated industry level than the annual estimates. This is due to the fact that less comprehensive and suitable quarterly data source for the compilation of GDP indicators are available only for agriculture; fishing; mining and quarrying; manufacturing; electricity and water; hotels and restaurants; wholesale and retail trade; part of transport; information communication; health, education, public administration and financial service activities. In cases where quarterly data sources are unavailable, estimates have been compiled based on the statistical estimation techniques (benchmarking) on the annual data (Lisman and Sandee), including interpolation and extrapolation of the annual levels. The quarterly estimates are benchmarked to the annual estimates. 2.2.1 Scope The data are classified by industry and the estimates are compiled and published using the International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC, rev.4) slightly modified to Namibian requirements, more specifically for minerals. Scope of the data Transaction Coverage: Transaction used in compilation of National Accounts covers all activities within the economic territory for Namibia as per the definition of economic territory outlines in the SNA 2008 framework. Geographic Coverage: The Annual and Quarterly GDP estimates are a representative of the entire Namibia economic territory. Unrecorded activity The unrecorded activities cover the non-observed economy, ranging from the illegal activities and the underground production. The informal industry is measured on the annual basis based on the baselines established in the Namibia Informal Sector Survey conducted in 2001 and extrapolated forward with movements in activities of the corresponding formal activities. The quarterly informal activities are compiled together with formal activities and classified based on the ISIC rev 4. The informal activities for Namibia mainly covers part of the agriculture, forestry and fishing, and Wholesale and Retail Trade sectors. 2.4.1 Valuation Output is valued at basic prices and domestic final expenditure at purchasers' prices. Changes in inventories are valued at average prices in the accounting period. Total imports are valued at Cost Insurance and Freight (c.i.f) because household consumption incurs expenditure which include cost, freight and insurance while exports are valued at free on board (f.o.b) because the cost, insurance and freight are borne by the external consumers. Valuation of GDP is at market prices after adjustments of net taxes. 2.4.2 Recording basis Transactions are recorded on an accrual basis, except for central government transactions that are recorded on a cash basis. The recording period is based on calendar periodicity. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Gross and net values are obtained according to prescribed methods in the 2008 SNA considering consumption of fixed capital. Estimates of consumption of fixed are calculated based on assumption of economic life span of different assets i.e. buildings, infrastructures, machines, vehicles and equipment. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs National accounts estimate in Namibia are compiled from a broad array of administrative records, surveys, and censuses. Quarterly data sources are mainly composed of administrative records from respective industries, VAT and quarterly surveys for Manufacturing and ‘Wholesale and retail trade' sectors. The quarterly sources are aligned to the annual data sources as much as possible, where data is available at higher frequency. As for the annual data, the sources are detailed by sector below. GDP by production approach (industrial activity) is based on the following: · Agriculture: Livestock farming: Livestock data are obtained from administrative records. · Crop Farming: Crop data are obtained from the administrative records. · Forestry: 2009/10 Namibia Household Income and Expenditure Survey is the benchmark survey and extrapolated to later years with appropriate indicators for respective variables. · Fishing: Data on quantities landed and price data for the main species of saltwater fish are provided by the Ministry of Fisheries and Marine Resources (MFMR), estimated from the fish model produced by the ministry. The informal activity for the sector is estimated using the informal sector survey of 2001 as a benchmark. · Mining and quarrying Included in this category are a) diamond mining, and b) other mining and quarrying, which includes other ores and minerals (e.g., copper, lead, silver, gold, etc.) as well as uranium mining. Basic data (quantities produced and exported) on mining are from administrative records of the Ministry of Mines and Energy, while intermediate consumption data are obtained through annual surveys of mining companies. The informal activity for the sector is estimated using the informal sector survey of 2001 as a benchmark. · Manufacturing: The sector has fifteen subsectors: Meat processing, Grain Mill products, other food products, Beverages, Textile and wearing apparel, Leather and related products, Wood and wood products, Publishing and Printing, Chemical and related products, Rubber and Plastics products, Non-metallic minerals products, Basic non-ferrous metals, Fabricated Metals, Diamond processing and other manufacturing. The number of traditional houses is taken from the 2011 Population and Housing Census, which is the benchmark estimate. The informal activity for the sector is estimated using the informal sector survey of 2001 as a benchmark. · Wholesale and retail trade, repairs: Annual survey, VAT data and Labour force data are the source of data. The informal activity for this sector is estimated using the informal sector survey of 2001 as a benchmark and extrapolated with the Labour force data. · Hotels and Restaurants: Annual survey and VAT data is the data source. · Transport: Annual survey is the source of data for this sector. The sector has 8 sub-sectors namely, Railway, Passenger transport by road, Freight transport by road, Air transport, Port services, Airport services, Travel agencies; tour operators, and Other supporting transport activities. Where deficiencies remain, they are addressed through data imputation and outlined in the technical report of sources and methods. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The Preliminary National Accounts is made available within three months after the reference year and the Revised National Accounts is disseminated within eight months after the reference year. The quarterly National Accounts is available within 90 days after the reference quarter. 3.2.1 Source data assessment All secondary data used in the compilation of national accounts are assessed for validity and quality before enforced. These data are checked for consistency, coverage and validity. The current economic surveys are conducted using a random sampling and expert knowledge to include all large establishments due to the absence of the fully functional statistics business register (SBR). The SBR is currently under construction and will go a long way to improve the quality of the national accounts. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The conversion of data submitted in financial year into calendar year is treated on basis of each company financial year using calendar year conversion ratios. Adjustment and imputations are made on non-responses following leading economic phenomena occurred in the compilation period. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Annual GDP Estimates Production approach: Methods of estimating value added by industry: During the rebasing, detailed demand side of GDP is balanced with the supply side and where data sources are weak are adjusted with informed indicators. GDP is compiled on the annual basis independently and through the benchmarking process it is equal to the sum of four quarters. Single laspeyes deflation mechanism with corresponding price indices for output, and intermediate consumption is applied which is then used to derive value added in some sectors. Some sectors use volume movements to extrapolate real GDP. A summary of sources and methods used in annual GDP estimates is published in the National Accounts report each year. Quarterly GDP Estimates Quarterly GDP by expenditure approach: The value added at current and at constant prices is estimated and published for all the 17 industries of the economy. The Namibian Industries are: − Agriculture and forestry; − Fishing; − Mining and quarrying; − Manufacturing; − Electricity and water; − Construction; − Wholesale and retail trade; − Hotels and restaurants; − Transport and storage; − Information and communication; − Financial service activities; − Real estate and Professional services activities; − Administrative and support services; − Public administration; − Education; − Health; − Other services; − Taxes on products; Quarterly estimates of value added in real terms are less comprehensive and therefore need to be aligned to the annual real estimates. Short-term indicators are used to estimate the quarterly GDP. In terms of coverage, quarterly indicators are not as comprehensive as in instances where the results of annual estimates are used. Therefore, the quarterly estimates must be adapted to the independent annual estimates when such estimates become available. To this extent, the Proportional Denton Method, which is a technique that generates a series of the quarterly estimates as proportional to the indicator as possible subject to the restrictions provided by the annual data is used. Quarterly GDP by expenditure approach: GDP by expenditure equation is denoted as Y = C + G + I + (X - M). Expenditure on Gross domestic product (GDP) = Consumption expenditure by households + Government consumption expenditure + (Gross fixed capital formation + change in inventories) + Value of export - Value of imports. Private Final Consumption Expenditure (PFCE) is derived as residuals by subtracting the available components from the GDP estimate compiled from the production approach. Government Final Consumption Expenditure (GFCE) covers the current outlays for goods and services by central government. Gross Fixed Capital Formation (GFCF) includes acquisition of non-financial assets by both the private and central government. Changes in inventories are measured by the value of the entries into inventories, less the value of withdrawals. The international trade statistics comprises of imports and exports of goods and services, adjusted to conform to National Accounts compilation practices. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Annual and quarterly national accounts estimates are revised up to the latest three years, and the latest eight quarters, using more comprehensive data as it becomes available and revised data from the sources. Data received through surveys is verified with annual reports and audited financial statements as they become available. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data The reconciliation between the production, income and expenditure approaches of GDP is made to ensure consistency in economic reporting between three approaches. Discrepancy is allocated to line items estimated with low level of confidence and where timely data may not be available. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The statistical discrepancy between the production, income, and expenditure approaches, in nominal and real terms, is consistently monitored. The discrepancy is closely evaluated, and amended when required. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revised estimates of GDP are published in the subsequent quarter for quarterly GDP and in the subsequent year for annual GDP. The revision table shows the previous revised estimates and magnitudes of revisions are published consistently. Revisions are performed for the latest three years while major revisions made in rebasing are conducted every five years. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses No other studies and revision analyses are published apart from sectoral revision table outlining the revision made in each sector. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request All queries are attended to upon request for all unpublished non-confidential data.
