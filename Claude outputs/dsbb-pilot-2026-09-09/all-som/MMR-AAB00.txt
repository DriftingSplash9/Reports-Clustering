 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The DCS is broadly compiled in accordance with the Monetary and Financial Statistics Manual and Compilation Guide ( MFSM CG ) methodology. This means the monetary statistics are compiled on an institutional basis, respectively, for the central bank and other depository corporations (ODCs). The state and privately-owned commercial banks and foreign bank branches broadly meet the MFSM CG definition of ODCs. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The principles for classifying financial institutions’ clients by economic sector in monetary data for Myanmar are as follows: Concerning definition of residents/nonresidents, The Foreign Exchange Management Law (of August 10, 2012 ) states: (a) Residents are:  Individuals who have been physically present or have had their main residence in the State for a period of at least 183 days during the preceding 12-month period, with the exception of foreign civil servants on diplomatic or similar assignments.  Legal entities incorporated under the local law and branches of legal entities incorporated under foreign law that are established in the State;  Myanmar civil servants on diplomatic or other foreign assignments. (b) Non-resident are those who are not included in the list of residents. The definition of residents/nonresidents in The Fo reign Exchange Management Law (of August 10, 2012 ) is broadly consistent with the application of the center of predominant economic interest criteria (see below). However, the current practice is still the one in which the banks identify a client as a resident/nonresident based on the “passport:” physical persons with foreign passports, even those qualified as “resident foreigners” based on their visa, are classified as nonresidents. Legal persons/entities, such as enterprises, which are foreign-controlled (as identified in their Articles of Associate/Incorporation and the operating license issued by the Ministry of National Planning and Economic Development) are classified as nonresidents. The resident sector is further subdivided according to the sectorization scheme of the IMF’s Standardized Report Forms (SRFs)—namely, central government; state and local governments; other financial corporations; public nonfinancial corporations; and private sector. The following three comments are in order on the application of this scheme. The fiscal decentralization envisaged in the Constitution of 2008 would provide autonomy to the local governments (state/regional/municipal authorities). However, at present, local authorities are effectively an extension of central government at a local level. In the current monetary statistics compilation framework, their positions with the banks are classified separately as the banks’ positions with the central government and state/local government. The banks recognize a client as a publicly-owned enterprise (a public nonfinancial corporation ) based on the documentation, issued by the Budget Department of MOF, that identifies the client as a “state economic enterprise” (SEE). These are 100 percent state-owned. The banks’ accounting framework provides for a separate identification of the SEE accounts (under the SFA(SEE)) and the summary positions of the main SFA(SEE) are included in the source data made available to the CBM’s compilers. A large number of MFSM CG -defined financial instruments can be discerned in the source data currently used as the basis for Myanmar monetary statistics. But, in addition to loan , securities , shares , current / time / saving accounts, there are a number of accounts, arising from the Myanmar current settlement/payment system, which are identified in various ways as “settlement/payment/receivable/payable” accounts. Some of these accounts have been classified in settlement accounts under other accounts payable/receivable with respect to the economic sector of counterparty, if identified. 2.2.1 Scope The principles for classifying financial institutions’ clients by economic sector in monetary data for Myanmar are as follows: Concerning definition of residents/nonresidents, The Foreign Exchange Management Law (of August 10, 2012 ) states: (a) Residents are:  Individuals who have been physically present or have had their main residence in the State for a period of at least 183 days during the preceding 12-month period, with the exception of foreign civil servants on diplomatic or similar assignments.  Legal entities incorporated under the local law and branches of legal entities incorporated under foreign law that are established in the State;  Myanmar civil servants on diplomatic or other foreign assignments. Valuation conventions summarized in this table are not based on market prices or market price equivalents (fair values), as recommended in MFSM (paragraph 185). 2.4.2 Recording basis Accrual accounting is practiced but not on the monthly recognition basis: accrued interest “due to” is recognized on a quarterly or semi-annual or annual basis depending on the bank. Accrued interest “due from” private clients is typically deducted (as per contract) from the client’s deposit account. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Loans to clients are on a gross basis (with provisions recorded in separate liability accounts), except in MFTB where a 2 percent provision is deducted from the loan amounts. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The following source data are provided to the CBM’s compilers:  The CBM accounts (prepared by the Payment and Settlement System—PSS Department);  The accounts for the commercial banks, five sets: a. MEB accounts; b. MFTB accounts; c. MICB accounts; d. MADB accounts; and e. Consolidated accounts of private banks. f. Consolidated accounts of foreign bank branches. The ODC source data comprise six sets of accounts, each one in a different format: a separate set of accounts for each of the four state-owned banks and one set for the private banks as a group and a separate set for foreign bank branches. The banks report data to CBM both in paper copy and soft copy with excel and pdf files. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness End-month data for CBM and the commercial banks are provided to the CBM monetary statistics compiler with the following time-lag: - For private banks: within 15 days after the end of the referenced month (in according with the CBM requirements), for most private banks; - For foreign bank branches: within 15 days after the end of the referenced month (in according with the CBM requirements), for most private banks; - For MICB: within 21 days after the end of the referenced month; - For MFTB: usually 15-21 days after the end of the referenced month; - For MEB: usually 30 days after the end of the referenced month; -For MADB: usually 45-60 days after the end of the referenced month; - For CBM: usually 60 days after the end of the referenced month. Except end of financial year, it may take time more than 90 days after the end of the referenced month. (Note: preparation of CBM statement of accounts requires data from MEB on the State Fund Accounts, which are maintained by the MEB.
