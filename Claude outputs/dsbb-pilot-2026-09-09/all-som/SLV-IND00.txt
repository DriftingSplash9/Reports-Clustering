B. Mining and quarrying: the related indirect indicator used to measure activity is cement production, as reported by manufacturing companies, in addition to salt extraction data from the Superintendency of Health Regulation (SRS). C. Manufacturing: Data comes from National Statistics and Census Office (ONEC) Monthly Economic Surveys (EEM from its Spanish name: "Encuesta Económica Mensual") and Producers' Price Index (IPP, from its Spanish name: "Índice de Precios al Productor"). They let know enterprises' sales, production and prices. The surveys have country coverage. The data for the maquila assessment comes from BCR Department of External Sector. It comes from the Current Account record "Manufacturing services for inputs belonging to others". The Department of External Sector also provides the export and import records for Salvadoran maquila companies. D. Electricity, gas, steam and air conditioning supply: For the Distribution and trading of Electricity indicator the data comes from ONEC, and for the Generation indicator data comes from Transactions Unit (UT from its Spanish name: "Unidad de Transacciones"). UT is the electricity market supervisor, it offset electricity demand and offer. Water supply, sewerage; waste management and remediation activities: For water supply indicator the data comes from ANDA's (from its spanish name " Administración Nacional de Acueductos y Alcantarillados") output and consumption data. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Compilers make diligent efforts to analyze the data collected from the main source of information needed for the national accounts. They are the process to validate the data. The base year for the IPI is 2005, the reference year is 2014, and the data comes from monthly economic surveys, which collect sales, production, and prices from companies, as well as other reference indicators. The IPI weighting factors come from El Salvador's Supply and Utilization Tables for the reference year 2014 and from annual calculations by quarterly sum. The IPI is adapted to fulfill international manuals to estimate economic indeces. It is harmonized for other more frequent indicators consistency. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The BCR statistical reports are published on due time. The BCR is fully responsible of them. The monetary, foreign trade, balance of payment and financial system figures are published under the IMF Special Data Dissemination Standard (SDDS). Also, price indices and the national accounts figures are published on SDDS. CPI and PPI are published on due time. Non-financial private sector data not always comes on time so the data is revised as the records are completed. The original data is periodically supervised by national accounts compilers to check out veracity of data. There is a forward publishing calendar of statistical reports. This forced the compilers to get data to estimate some figures for the national accounts statistical reports. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The data validation and consistency process includes a detailed analysis of administrative records, for the economic sectors estimated by these; to guarantee data consistency in terms of definition, validation and time of reference. The ONEC surveys data is revised and processed to be used for National Accounts purposes. There are criteria to revise and consistency of data supported by related indicators. The enterprises are asked when some data looks abnormal or it is missing. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The data compilation processes are based on criteria to validate data for guaranteeing data consistency through their codes and abnormal data identification to minimize processing errors. The economic activity output and product classification is made using National Accounts Nomenclature (NCN for is Spanish name: “Nomenclatura de Cuentas Nacionales”). It is derived from ISIC Rev. 4. The imputation procedure when data is missing is based on calculus over the sample parameters. The expansion factors are modified to try to cover the missing data over the sample. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Every economic activity indicator is summed up to estimate IPI. It is used chained volume method, the Denton Benchmarking methodology. The Denton Methodology combines the monthly data and quarterly one. The seasonal adjusted series are estimated using statistical methods to temporary adjust the series and taking off the series irregular components (X-13 ARIMA program). 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The data validation process is periodically. The data is compared with other economic indicators as they are available. The production chain prices are validated by producer and consumer price surveys, they are compile by different agents. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data There is a process to evaluate data statistical differences. It is called consistency verification process, and here are made any needed adjust on data. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs If after the data is validated a discrepancy is detected, the needed adjusted is made. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses The IPI series are revised every three months as new data is available. The revision could modify the series for a three years period. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses The publishing norms says that any further revision of revised figures must publish a technical note to explain why the change occurs. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request When it is requested, the data could be delivered as non- published detailed tables if the data is not classified as confidential. There is not published conditions and availability of requested data. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The SDDS website publishes contact person’s name, email address and phone number for any question. And finally there is a free diffusion program for any potential user of the national accounts statistical reports or series. They are also available on internet.
