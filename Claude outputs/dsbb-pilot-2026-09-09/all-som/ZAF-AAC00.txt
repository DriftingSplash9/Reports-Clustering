 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: The analytical accounts of the central bank and the CPD are derived from trial balance sheets and broadly conform to the guidelines outlined in the IMF's Monetary and Financial Statistics Manual and Compilation Guide. The analytical accounts enable the central bank to identify domestic and external factors contributing to the creation of reserve money. The primary disaggregation of assets and liabilities is as follows: Reserve Money: Note and coin liability of the central bank plus domestic currency denominated deposits of private sector banks and mutual banks with the central bank. Domestic claims on the private sector: Central bank's loans and advances to and holdings of securities issued by domestic non-bank, non-government units. The private sector includes public enterprises and, in principle, local government. Domestic claims on the government: Central bank's loans and advances to and holdings of securities issued by the national and (in principle) provincial government. The provincial government receive the bulk of their revenue from the national government, and are therefore classified as part of the government sector or Inter Governmental Cash Coordination (IGCC). Gross foreign assets: Central bank's holdings of gold and claims on non-residents. Gross foreign liabilities: Central bank's liabilities to non-residents--basically loans received from non-resident banks and other non-resident institutions. The national residency criteria is based on the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: The central bank survey presents as stock data the following: (i)Reserve Money; (ii) Domestic claims on the government sector; (iii) Domestic claims on the private sector; and (iv) External Position - Net foreign assets. Domestic and external accounts are distinguished on the basis of residency in accordance with the definition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual. Scope of the data Institutional coverage: The published statistics is a consolidation of the accounts of the South African Reserve Bank (SARB) and the Corporation for Public Deposits (CPD). The Banknote Company, SA Mint and CODI are subsidiaries of the SARB. The SARB's transactions with its subsidiaries are recorded as separate book-entries, so that its assets and liabilities reflect claims on or amounts payable to its subsidiaries, respectively. Exceptions to coverage N/A Unrecorded activity N/A 2.4.1 Valuation Accounting practices: The monthly financial statements of the central bank is prepared in accordance with International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS), as far as they are applicable to a central bank, and so as to reflect rand amounts. The monetary analysis is based on the consolidated accounts of the South African Reserve Bank (SARB) and the Corporation for Public Deposits (CPD). The operational subsidiaries' (the Banknote Company, SA Mint and CODI) are not consolidated with the Reserve Bank because their activities differ significantly from those of the Bank. The historical cost convention, as modified by the revaluation of certain investments to fair values, is applied. Valuations are done with consideration of the following: Investments: Locally registered stock and related derivative financial instruments which are held as trading portfolios are valued at fair value. Other investments are valued at cost less provision for loss where applicable. Fixed assets and depreciation: Before 31 March 2001, fixed assets acquired were written off against the fixed property and equipment reserve in the year of acquisition. Adequate allocations to cover expected and capital expenditure were periodically transferred to the reserve from the income statement. As from 31 March 2001, fixed assets are written off over their expected useful lives. Gold coin and bullion: Gold coin and bullion are valued at 90 per cent of the average of the last ten gold price fixings determined on the London gold market before the month-end. Foreign currency activities: Assets and liabilities in foreign currencies are converted to rand at the ruling exchange rates at the close of the period. Exchange profits and losses are assigned to the account of the Government and are charged to the Gold and Foreign Exchange Contingency Reserve Account (GFECRA). Deferred taxation: Deferred taxation is provided using the liability method. Full provision is made for all timing differences which are expected to give rise to a tax liability within the foreseeable future. Deferred tax assets are not applied. Repurchase agreements are treated as secured lending. Assets temporarily sold under repurchase agreements are retained on the balance sheet. 2.4.2 Recording basis Accounting practices: All liabilities and assets include accrued interest up to the end of the period to which the statement relates. Only in exceptional circumstances will accrued interest be included under "other assets and liabilities". 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Balances are generally reported on a gross basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data is based on the balance sheets of the SARB and CPD, which is derived from reports generated by an electronic general ledger system based on complete reporting. Source transactions are captured and the reports are generated from a predetermined classification of each account. The SARB and CPD balance sheets are presented at various levels of detail for various purposes and user groups. For instance, a fairly detailed "Statement of Assets and Liabilities" is included in the monthly financial review submitted to the Reserve Bank's management and is available on the internet at http://www.resbank.co.za . under publication, statements. Annual financial statements are also available. In the annual financial statements the Bank has chosen to use International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) as a guide in deciding on the most appropriate accounting policies to adopt and as a model for the presentation and disclosure framework followed in its financial statements. In these statements all subsidiaries are consolidated. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The frequency of the central bank survey is adequate to support the publication of the important indicators of the economy such as reserve money, domestic claims of the government and private sector and net foreign assets. Source data is consistent with the definitions, scope and classification of central banking indicators. Source data is consistent with the time of recording and the valuation of assets. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The Financial Services Department is made aware of the deadlines set for reporting and data is disseminated to the users and general public within the time limits laid down by SDDS requirements. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Quality control is conducted through manually checking the consistency of data against previous values and following up inconsistencies with the Financial Services Department. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Compilation practices are based on full and responsible disclosure and appropriate accounting policies. Complete reporting is applied. Data are recorded on a monthly basis. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures No significant estimations or statistical adjustments are made. Data can be arranged to reflect seasonal adjustment, but are generally reported in unadjusted form. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results A detailed analysis of each account provides for the necessary control in accordance with accounting procedures to ensure balancing and cross-checking. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Quality control is conducted through manually checking the consistency of data against previous values and following up perceived inconsistencies with the Financial Services Department. Records are maintained of explanatory feedback. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs In cases of new or adjustments to accounts, changes are made to ensure accurate representation of data before dissemination of statistics. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Data is usually revised when there are structural changes to the data. Any revisions are confirmed with the Financial Services Department. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses As a rule, major changes would not occur frequently in this data. The public is advised of major changes in methodology, source data, and statistical techniques in a special “Notes to tables” at the time of release of the Quarterly Bulletin. Major changes affecting data is also indicated as footnotes to the tables in the Bulletin. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Data are available via e-mail from the relevant contact person, on request. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Presentations on the latest economic developments are given at the time of the release of the Quarterly Bulletin. The press are invited to the presentation and are allowed to ask questions which are clarified by the presenters. A query facility is also available on the website, where questions are sorted by category and allocated to responsible persons in the various units. In general queries are received on an ad hoc basis, mostly by telephone or e-mail and referred to and handled by the various experts. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs Catalogs of publications, documents, and other services, including information on any changes is available on request.
