 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The CPI is based on the Consumer Price Index Manual: Theory and Practice (2004) and on the Consumer Price Index Manual: Concepts and Methods (2020), produced by ILO, IMF, OECD, Eurostat, United Nations, and the World Bank Definition: The CPI measures the average percentage change recorded by the prices of a set of goods and services between two specific periods.. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: The classification system used for the CPI is the original version of the most recent COICOP (COICOP 2018). The CPI only uses the first 13 divisions, which refer to individual household consumption expenditures. Scope of the data Reference population: All individual households in urban areas of the country, of all size and belonging to all socioeconomic groups or income levels. Geographical coverage: The geographic coverage of the CPI based in December 2020 is National Urban and consists of the urban districts of the six planning regions of the country. The country's urban areas concentrate most of the household expenditure, most of the country's population and the largest number of establishments. Urban districts are used as a reference to choose the index basket and the weights of the goods and services included in the basket. The geographical coverage of ​​the price collection is a subset of urban areas made up of 53 urban and predominantly urban districts, located in the six planning regions of the country, which are characterized by being the ones with the highest concentration of commercial establishments. These districts are also characterized by having a high population representation. Item coverage: The CPI is composed of 13 divisions, 43 groups, 81 classes, 132 sub-classes, 289 articles, and 546 varieties. Exceptions to coverage Geographical coverage: Geographical coverage excludes all rural districts. 2.4.1 Valuation Types of prices: Both basket weights and monthly prices used in the compilation of the CPI are valued at market prices, including sales taxes and applicable discounts. Detailed product characteristic data including terms of transactions are included as part of the specifications for monthly price collection 2.4.2 Recording basis Timing of price observation: Both the expenditure data from the income and expenditure survey and the price data for the monthly price survey are recorded on an accrual basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The weights for durable goods in the CPI market basket are based on net purchases (the value of purchases less the value of sales) of these goods, in accordance with international recommendations. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Sources of weights: The list of goods and services included in the CPI basket and their corresponding weights were derived from the National Survey of Households' Income and Expenses 2018 (ENIGH 2018 for its acronym in Spanish) that was conducted during the period February 2018 through March 2019. This survey covered households from all socio-economic groups in both the urban and rural sectors of the entire country. The sample of 9 828 households was selected using a sampling frame based on the 2011 Population Census. Time period of current weights: February 2018 to March 2019. Frequency of weight updates: The weights applied to the items of the basket are updated every five years, approximately; the most recent updating was in December 2020. Item selection: The basket includes items meeting any of the following criteria: 1. the spending on the item in question must represent 0.05 percent or more of the total expenditure reported by the reference households. 2. the item is consumed in 7 percent or more of households. Outlet selection: The sample design was carried out in two stages: in the first, the unrestricted random sampling was applied to calculate sample sizes per item and, in the second stage, the distribution by quotas was implemented for the establishments in the districts of the geographic coverage. The quotas were determined by two characteristics: geographic location of the establishments and type of establishment. Sample sizes: Each month, approximately 24 200 price quotations are collected for 546 varieties of 289 products from 2 600 outlets and 51 houses for rent in 53 urban districts in Costa Rica. Most of these data are collected through personal visits. All data collected is processed and tabulated at INEC's facility in San José. Price collection methods: Personal visits, phone calls, email and web pages using digital forms designed to gather information at the establishments in the sample, distributed over the course of the month. To determine prices of utilities and other public services, researchers call the offices of the enterprises providing those services, which are verified in the pertinent edition of the official diary La Gaceta. Item/product specification: Specifications of each of the items (goods and services) are found in a Specifications Manual, describing the characteristics of the items in question so that price researchers may readily identify the target items. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The ENIGH 2018 provided detailed expenditure data for 2 400 goods and services for the geographic areas covered by the CPI. This level of detail was more than sufficient for selecting an item sample for the CPI basket. The concepts and definitions also match those of the CPI. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The data collection in the field is organized in seventeen business days, beginning on the first business day of each month, so that the collection is carried out on the same number of days every month of the year. The days after the closing of the collection are used to recover pending or other thematic, operational or administrative tasks. Centralized data collection is carried out from the first to the last business day of the month. The index is published on the fifth business day of the month. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data for the CPI is monitored on a continuous basis for errors of all types. For the monthly price survey, the price and specification data on the survey digital forms are checked for completeness and accuracy in the field. Then the captured price data is subjected to automated checks for atypical and/or extreme values. Any atypical/extreme values that are identified by this process are checked for data entry errors. If problems persist, the respondent is contacted a second time to verify the accuracy of the price data. Consistency checks are also made across the dataset. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Computation of lowest level indices: Elementary aggregates are estimated using Jevons's indices (geometric mean of the short-term price relatives). Aggregation: The index for higher level aggregates is compiled using the short-term formulation of the Laspeyres index. Alignment of expenditure and base period: Basket weights derived from expenditure data from the ENIGH 2018 were updated to December 2020, which is the reference base for the CPI. Reference period: The CPI reference base is December 2020=100. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Treatment of missing prices: Temporarily missing prices are subject to "price imputation" procedures. These methods are characterized by two main aspects: the algorithm to estimate the imputation factor and the aggregation levels used to calculate said factor. According to the algorithm used, three methods are identified: the carry-forward imputation, the global mean imputation and the combined procedure of the global mean and the carry-forward imputation. These procedures are associated with each item in the index, depending on its characteristics. The global mean imputation is based on assuming that the price change of the missing price is like the average variation of the prices actually observed for the item or a higher aggregation. If the quantity of prices observed at the elementary level is considered insufficient or not representative, the imputation is calculated based on the price variations of a higher aggregation. A series of criteria are applied, in sequential and ascending order, with respect to the aggregate to which the missing price belongs (from the lowest level of aggregation to the highest), depending on an established minimum percentage of observed prices with respect to sample size. Adjustments for quality differences: Mostly, implicit quality adjustments are made (overlap method, class-mean imputation, carry-forward, comparable replacement). Adjustments in quantities of the product is the only explicit quality adjustments that are made. Selection of replacement items: When it is known that a particular item is to become permanently unavailable, the item in question must be replaced with a comparable product, with another brand albeit having the same specified characteristics as the previous item. Introducing new products: New products may only be introduced into the basket in the event that the base of the index is changed, something which happens every five years. Owner-occupied housing: The index only includes the prices of actual rental housing. Seasonally adjusted indices: The index undergoes no adjustment to reflect the seasonal behavior of the items included in the index. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results No checks are made by comparing the CPI indices with similar indices from the PPI, agricultural price indices, or implicit deflators from the national accounts. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Verification of prices: Consistency checks are made on a monthly basis to identify atypical price levels and price movements. This is done for the price movements by comparing price changes from the previous period to the current period for all price quotations for a given product. Any atypical values are investigated and corrected when appropriate. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses The information is disseminated in INEC's web page. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request All non-confidential unpublished statistics request are attended for the CPI staff. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The INEC has an information center that provides assistance to users.
