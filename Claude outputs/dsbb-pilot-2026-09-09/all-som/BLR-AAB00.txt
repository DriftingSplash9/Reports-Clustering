 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: The analytical framework used for compiling monetary statistics reflects concepts and definitions that are, in general, consistent with the IMF's Monetary and Financial Statistics Manual and Compilation Guide (IMF's Manual) . The balance sheets of the National Bank of the Republic of Belarus (NBRB) and other depository corporations, which underlie the monetary statistics, broadly follow international accounting standards. Major aggregates of the broad money survey compiled by the NBRB are as follows: 1. broad money, disaggregated into: cash in circulation; transferable deposits; other deposits; foreign currency deposits; securities included in broad money in national and foreign currencies; precious metals deposits. 2. net domestic claims, disaggregated into: net claims on general government, further disaggregated into: - claims on and liabilities to central government in national and foreign currencies; - claims on and liabilities to local governments in national and foreign currencies; claims on public nonfinancial corporations; claims on private sector: - claims on other nonfinancial corporations ; - claims on natural persons; claims on other financial corporations . 3.deposits that are not included in the broad money; 4.securities, other than shares, that are not included in the broad money; 5. credits; 6. shares and other forms of capital participation; 7. financial derivatives; 8. other items (net); 9. net foreign assets of depository corporations. This detailed breakdown is published in the Statistics Bulletin , which is available on the following website: http://www.nbrb.by/engl/statistics/bulletin . Separate data on central bank's accounts and those of other depository corporations are also disseminated, as well as a number of more detailed breakdowns, including loans by sector, currency, and maturity; and nonperforming loans by sector, currency, and maturity. The following monetary aggregates are derived from the broad money survey: cash in circulation (M0); narrow money (M1), comprising M0 plus transferable deposits in Belarusian rubles; money supply according to national methodology (M2), comprising M1 plus other deposits in Belarusian rubles; ruble money supply (M2*), comprising M2 plus legal and natural persons' funds in Belarusian rubles-denominated securities issued by depository corporations of the Republic of Belarus; broad money (M3), comprising M2* plus transferable deposits, other deposits in foreign currency, precious metals deposits, and legal and natural persons' funds in foreign currency-denominated securities issued by depository corporations of the Republic of Belarus. Definition of monetary aggregates: Broad money (per monetary survey methodology): M0 (cash in circulation) – the most liquid part of money supply available for immediate use as a means of payment. It includes banknotes and coins in circulation held by natural persons and at legal persons' cash offices, excluding cash at cash offices of the deposit corporations of the Republic of Belarus; Transferable deposits – monetary funds in Belarusian rubles and foreign currency held by natural and legal persons with depository corporations in line with legislation which may be repaid on first demand without payment of any fine, are subject to no restrictions, and may be used directly to make payments. They include balances of current (settlement) accounts, accounts designed to record demand deposits, and other client's accounts (temporary accounts and charitable accounts of legal and natural persons); Other deposits – time deposits and deposits in escrow (excluding transferable deposits) placed by natural and legal persons in depository corporations according to the legislation, that are confirmed by the banking deposit agreement and bear interest, as well as balances of other clients' accounts (designed to carry out settlements in the form of the letter of credit, bank payment obligation, clients' trust management accounts, and accounts designed to record funds received as collateral); Securities issued by other depository corporations include securities in Belarusian rubles and foreign currency issued by other depository corporations of the Republic of Belarus and placed among natural and legal persons of the Republic of Belarus; Deposits in foreign currency include transferable and other deposits in foreign currency held by natural and legal persons with depository corporations of the Republic of Belarus; Precious metals deposits include demand precious metals deposits and time deposits and precious metals deposits in escrow held by natural and legal persons with depository corporations of the Republic of Belarus organizations in line with legislation, and natural and legal persons' unallocated bullion accounts. Net claims on general government – claims of depository corporations of the Republic of Belarus on general government of the Republic of Belarus less liabilities of depository corporations thereto; Claims on general government - credits extended to the general government; investments of depository corporations in securities issued by general government of the Republic of Belarus; commercial credits and advances, as well as funds involved in settlements under operations with the general government of the Republic of Belarus; Liabilities to general government – balances on the accounts of general government of the Republic of Belarus; deposits and other funds attracted from general government; external public loans (credits) obtained by the Government; and budgetary funds of the Union of Belarus and Russia; Claims on public nonfinancial corporations – credits and loans (including overdue debt), accrued and overdue interest on credits and loans granted to public nonfinancial corporations ; funds provided to public nonfinancial corporations under REPO operations; funds invested by depository corporations of the Republic of Belarus in securities of public nonfinancial corporations and interest accrued thereon; commercial credits and advances, as well as funds involved in settlements under operations with public nonfinancial corporations of the Republic of Belarus; Claims on other nonfinancial corporations – credits and loans (including overdue debt), accrued and overdue interest on credits and loans granted to other nonfinancial corporations ; funds provided to other nonfinancial corporations under REPO operations; funds invested by depository corporations of the Republic of Belarus in securities of other nonfinancial corporations and interest accrued thereon; commercial credits and advances, as well as funds involved in settlements under operations with other nonfinancial corporations; Claims on natural persons – credits and loans (including overdue debt), accrued and overdue interest on credits and loans granted to resident natural persons of the Republic of Belarus; commercial credits and advances, as well as funds involved in settlements under operations with resident natural persons of the Republic of Belarus; Claims on other financial corporations – credits and loans (including overdue debt), accrued and overdue interest on credits and loans granted to other financial corporations ; funds provided to other financial corporations under REPO operations; funds invested by depository corporations of the Republic of Belarus in securities of other financial organization and interest accrued thereon; commercial credits and advances, as well as funds involved in settlements under operations with other financial organization; Deposits excluded from the broad money – monetary funds of other financial corporations placed on deposit accounts, including precious metals deposits, with the National Bank; deposits, inclusive precious metals and precious stones deposits, of other financial corporations, nonfinancial organizations, and resident natural persons of the Republic of Belarus placed with other depository corporations that undergo the liquidation process; deposits the withdrawals of which are restricted during the long period of time; monetary funds placed on escrow accounts; Securities, other than shares, excluded from the broad money – securities issued by the National Bank and held in portfolios of Belarusian other financial corporations; Loans – liabilities of other depository corporations (funds received under REPO operations, credits and loans) to other financial corporations , nonfinancial corporations and resident natural persons of the Republic of Belarus; Shares and other forms of capital participation comprise authorized and reserve funds, other funds, as well as the results of securities and foreign currency funds revaluation, financial performance of the year under review, and profit of previous years less the loss of previous years of depository corporations of the Republic of Belarus; Other items (net) – the balance of mutual claims and liabilities of the National Bank and other depository corporations as well as the balance of other assets and liabilities that are not included in the above-mentioned aggregates and that characterize operations aimed at ensuring the activity of depository corporations; Net foreign assets of depository corporations – the balance of asset and liability transactions of the National Bank and other depository corporations with nonresidents of the Republic of Belarus in foreign currency and Belarusian rubles. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The principles of sectorization applied in the monetary statistics compiled by the NBRB are satisfactorily consistent with the IMF"s Manual, which recommends distinguishing between resident and nonresident units on the basis of the concept of "center of economic interest" and classifying resident institutional units into domestic sectors according to their objectives, functions, and economic behavior. In the monetary statistics compiled by the NBRB, the following sectoral groupings of resident institutional units are distinguished: central bank; other depository corporations; other financial corporations; central government; local governments; public nonfinancial corporations; private sector. Notwithstanding certain differences in naming convention, this sectoral classification can be easily reconciled with the guidelines of the IMF"s Manual. The accounting records supporting the production of the monetary statistics contain sufficient detail to cover the complete classification of financial instruments, as categorized in the IMF"s Manual. Consistently with the IMF"s Manual recommendations, deposits in financial corporations that are closed pending liquidation or reorganization are considered as restricted deposits and are excluded from money aggregates. In accordance with the IMF"s Manual, the distinction of nonresident units is based on the concept of the center of economic interest. There are no branches of foreign banks operating in the Republic of Belarus, and no Belarusian banks have branches abroad. The principles underlying the classification of financial instruments are based on international standards and are generally consistent with the IMF"s Manual. The classification framework is based on the liquidity of the financial instruments and the legal characteristics that describe the form of the underlying creditor/debtor relationship. Beginning from January 2004, the treatment of the securities repurchase agreements (repos) has been changed in line with theIMF"s Manual; securities repurchase agreements are classified as collateralized loans, rather than as outright sales of securities. Monetary statistics include derivative financial instruments data from January 2006. 2.2.1 Scope The principles of sectorization applied in the monetary statistics compiled by the NBRB are satisfactorily consistent with the IMF"s Manual, which recommends distinguishing between resident and nonresident units on the basis of the concept of "center of economic interest" and classifying resident institutional units into domestic sectors according to their objectives, functions, and economic behavior. At present, there are 21 bank fnd 2 nonbank depository corporations in the Republic of Belarus. Thus, the scope of monetary statistics compiled by the NBRB covers all depository corporations (financial corporations whose liabilities are included in the national definition of broad money) resident in the Republic of Belarus and is, therefore, consistent with the guidelines of the IMF"s Manual. 2.4.1 Valuation Accounting practices: Loans are valued at nominal value and include the outstanding principal and interest accruals. Provisions for loan losses are not netted out from the gross value of loans, but are recorded as other liabilities, which is consistent with the treatment recommended in theIMF"s Manual. Debt securities are valued at fair value or at the acquisition prices plus accrued interest depending on their classification. Monetary gold is valued at market prices. Valuation of foreign-currency-denominated instruments: Assets and liabilities denominated in foreign currencies are revalued on a daily basis at the official exchange rates established by the NBRB. 2.4.2 Recording basis Recording is done on an accrual basis: Transactions are recorded simultaneously between parties, and accrued interest on deposits, loans, and securities is incorporated into the outstanding amount of the financial asset or liability, rather than being treated as part of other accounts receivable/payable. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Data are derived automatically by the existing computer program from detailed accounting balance sheets of the NBRB and other depository corporations. All accounts of the NBRB and other depository corporations are first reorganized in appropriate analytical groupings (Stage 1), followed by an aggregation into detailed surveys for the NBRB and other depository corporations (Stage 2), and consolidated into aggregated surveys of the central bank and other depository corporations (Stage 3). Similarly, a detailed and aggregated broad money survey is automatically derived from the data compiled in Stage 2. The data in the detailed analytical balance sheets derived in Stage 1 are not subject to any consolidation or netting adjustments. To permit easy checking of the consistency between the accounting and the analytical balance sheets, the compilation procedure requires that the total assets/liabilities in the corresponding balance sheets are the same. The derivation of the detailed surveys in Stage 2 involves compiling several aggregates on a net basis (net foreign assets, net claims on central government, net claims on other depository corporations, net claims on the NBRB, and other items net); however, gross claims and liabilities that underlie these net aggregates are also provided. Only the aggregated surveys compiled in Stage 3 do not provide the gross components of the items subject to netting and consolidation. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources: The NBRB maintains a comprehensive and complete register of depository corporations. The institutional and geographic coverage of this register is completed. Data included in the monetary survey for the NBRB are based on the detailed daily accounting balance sheet compiled by the NBRB's Accounting Department. This balance sheet covers all accounts of the NBRB. Data for other depository corporations are compiled from a detailed consolidated balance sheet of other depository corporations that is provided by the NBRB's Computer Systems and Technology Department. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data used for compiling monetary statistics provide a reasonably good approximation of the concepts, definitions, scope, classifications, and recording principles for compiling sound monetary statistics that are recommended in the IMF"s Manual. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Daily data on the NBRB’s and other depository corporations’ balance sheets items are provided on the next day. This timeliness of the source data permits the dissemination of monetary statistics well within the SDDS requirements. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Given that the compilation of monetary statistics is based on a direct and mostly automatic transformation of administrative (accounting) source data, compilers of monetary statistics routinely assess source data and initiate their modifications if needed for improvements in statistical outputs. This refers mostly to changes in charts of accounts for the NBRB and other depository corporations, and to valuation principles applied in accounting. The accuracy of source data is tested through a system of formal consistency checks and, when needed, out-of-trend values are verified in direct contacts with reporting other depository corporations. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Estimation/sampling: In cases when the balance sheet used for compiling monetary statistics do not provide all details necessary to classify data by financial instrument and sector, supplementary information is collected. For example, such supplementary information is routinely collected from other depository corporations to provide more accurate sectoral classification of deposits and to permit the breakdown between public and private enterprises. Thus, all data are derived from administrative sources and statistical data estimation techniques are not used. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Statistical adjustments: The compilation of monetary statistics is fully based on administrative sources and statistical adjustments are not carried out. Adjustments for nonresponse are not an issue, because all reporting other depository corporations routinely comply with the reporting requirement. Monetary gold is valued at market prices. No seasonal adjustments to monetary aggregates are at present carried out. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Intermediate results are routinely validated against any other reports available to the Banking Statistics Department (BSD), the Balance of Payments Department, and other departments of the NBRB. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Intermediate data are analyzed for internal and intertemporal consistency. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Any inconsistencies and unexplained large data variations are investigated in direct contacts with the reporting other depository corporations and departments of the NBRB. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Monthly data are monitored to put them in consistency with internationally accepted standards on the methodology of monetary statistics and legislation acts. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Given that both monthly and annual data revisions do not result in statistically significant data changes, no formal revision studies are disseminated to the public. Explanatory notes on the reasons for revisions are provided in footnotes to disseminated tables. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished and no confidential data can be available free of charge upon the request. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The name and phone number for contact person is provided in the Statistics Bulletin . In addition, metadata posted on the NBRB’s website provide name, phone and fax numbers, and email address of the contact person for each data set. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs A list of NBRB’s publications is disseminated in the December issue of the Banking Magazine and is also available on the website. The NBRB’s website provides users with considerable amount of information about electronic and paper-based publications and documents.
