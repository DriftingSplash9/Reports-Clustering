I. Analytical Framework, Concepts, Definitions, and Classifications
 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions 
 The accounts are based on the "System of National Accounts 1968" ("SNA 68"). National Accounts data are disseminated by the Grenada Statistical Office in the Grenada Economic Review and by the ECCB in the "National Accounts Statistics Digest". 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Economic activities are classified using the International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC) of all Economic Activities, Revision 3. Definitions: The concepts used primarily follow the "SNA 68." GDP is compiled from both the production and expenditure approaches.

II. Scope of the data
 Scope of the data 
 The National Accounts cover the entire economic territory of Grenada. In principle, all resident institutional units operating within the economy plus transactions to and from the rest of the world are included in the national accounts Unrecorded activity No adjustments are made for illegal activities. Own-account consumption of goods and services through informal channels are not covered by the available sources of data and are estimated indirectly.

III. Accounting Conventions
 2.4.1 Valuation Output by industrial activity is recorded for Grenada at producers' prices, which are defined as market (purchasers') prices less invoiced trade and transportation margins. Intermediate consumption is recorded at purchaser's prices. Data are published by industry at factor cost, which is derived from output at producers' prices by subtracting taxes on products and production and adding subsidies for products and on production. Value added for government services comprises compensation of employees only. No information is available on consumption of fixed capital since government accounts are on a cash basis. Imports and exports are valued f.o.b. GDP by final use is valued at market prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: Most transactions are recorded on an accrual basis. Exceptions are government revenue and expenditure data, which are recorded on a cash basis. Recording period: Data are compiled for the calendar year. However, a number of enterprises compile accounts on a fiscal accounting year. Their annual data are included in the national accounts for a given calendar year if their fiscal year begins before July 1 of the reference period.

IV. Nature of the Basic Data Sources
 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Production approach: 
 Agriculture: Data on the output of bananas are obtained from the Grenada Banana Co-operative Society. Also, data on nutmeg and mace are obtained from the Grenada Co-operative Nutmeg Association. The Grenada Cocoa Association provides data on the output of cocoa. Data on the production of other crops are obtained from the Ministry of Agriculture. Output is estimated using the production approach, which consists of deducting the value of intermediate inputs from the gross value of output. Data on intermediate inputs are obtained from external trade statistics. The Fisheries division in the Ministry of Agriculture supplies data on the quantity and value of fish produced. 
 Mining and quarrying: There is no mining activity. Estimates of output and intermediate consumption for quarrying are obtained from the Grenada Rock, Asphalt and Concrete Association. Aggregates produced consist of blasted rocks, sand, binding and gravel. 
 Manufacturing: Output and intermediate consumption are obtained primarily from the annual National Accounts Establishment Survey (NAES), and a monthly industrial production survey. 
 Electricity and water: Estimates of output, intermediate input, and value added are obtained from the financial statements of the Grenada Electricity Department and the National Water and Sewage Authority. 
 Construction: Output is measured by the commodity flow approach, which uses information on imports of construction materials. Information is obtained from the imports of construction materials, and the wages of construction labor. The value of trade and transport margins on inputs, which is included in the computation of output prices, is estimated from ad hoc surveys of building contractors and quantity surveyors. Value added is computed as a fraction of measured output. The ratio of value-added to output is estimated from a sample of construction companies. 
 Wholesale and retail trade: The value added is estimated on the basis of the annual accounts obtained via the annual NAES and is supplemented by information obtained from the Inland Revenue. 
 Hotels and restaurants: Output of accommodation services in current and constant prices are estimated from data on number of tourists in paid accommodation, average length of stay, and average expenditure per day on paid accommodation from regular visitor expenditure surveys. The ratio of value added to output is estimated from the hotels and restaurants component of the annual NAES. 
 Transport, storage, and communications: Output and value added of road transport are calculated using the number of vehicles licensed obtained from the Inland Revenue Department. Estimates of value added for taxis, buses, rental cars and vans are obtained from ad hoc surveys conducted by the CSO to obtain information on gross output and intermediate consumption for these types of vehicles. For sea transport data are obtained from the financial statements of the Grenada Port Authority as well as from the annual national income survey of shipping agents. In the case of air transportation data are obtained from the annual national income survey of various regional and local airlines as well as travel agents. For communications output and value added are obtained from the accounts of the Cable and Wireless Company and the General Post Office. 
 Banks and insurance: Data for banking are obtained from the annual NAES of the various financial institutions. Data are also obtained from the Eastern Caribbean Central Bank commercial banking survey. Data for insurance come from insurance returns collected for regulatory purposes by the Registrar of Insurance and by the ECCB regional survey of foreign insurance companies. 
 Real estate and housing: Value added is computed by extrapolation based on a benchmark survey 
 Government services: Value added comprises compensation of employees which is obtained from government accounting records and the financial statements of the National Insurance Scheme. 
 Other services: Value added comprises compensation of employees, which is obtained from government accounting records and the financial statements of the National Insurance Scheme. These accounting records are on a cash basis and exclude consumption of fixed capital. Expenditure approach: 
 Private final consumption: This component of expenditure is determined as the residual of GDP at market prices using the production method less Government final consumption, Gross capital formation, and net exports of goods and services. 
 Government final consumption: Consumption of general government services is defined as output less the value of commodity and non-commodity sales and of own-account capital formation. The source data are government budgetary and administrative records. 
 Gross fixed capital formation: Capital formation for transport and other equipment is compiled from customs sources as there is little domestic production of these items. Construction is compiled via a commodity flow approach, as in the treatment of the construction industry in the description of the production approach to compiling GDP above. Land development is compiled based on data obtained from the administrative records of the Ministry of Finance. 
 Increase in stocks: Increase in stocks (change in inventories) is estimated based on the NAES, Income Tax Returns and extrapolation. 
 Exports and imports of goods and non-factor services: These data are taken directly from the Balance of Payments compiled by the ECCB and the Central Statistical Office. See the comprehensive framework and categories and indicators for the External Sector for details on the compilation of net exports of goods and services. Discrepancies: There is no discrepancy between the production and expenditure approaches as the expenditure component for private final consumption is derived as a residual. 

V. Compilation Practices
 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Production approach: 
 Coverage adjustments: Various methods are used for the treatment of missing values of output and intermediate consumption for individual establishments in the NAES and other sources. Among these are estimating current intermediate consumption on the basis of its past share in an establishment's output, estimating output on the basis of the past ratio of output to selected cost data, extrapolating the most recent past observed level of output, intermediate input, and value added on the basis of growth rates observed either in the past or currently in reported data for similar establishments. The commodity flow approach to output estimation is used for construction to limit potential coverage problems from incomplete survey coverage of informal and/or non-reporting establishments and vendors. 
 Estimation of current price data: Value added (GDP) by industry is the difference between output and intermediate consumption. For banana crops, quarrying, electricity and water, air and sea transport, post and communications, and banking and insurance, output and intermediate consumption are compiled from accounting records sampled in the NAES or obtained from administrative sources. The output of banking is measured in GDP by economic activity as net interest and other income. There are insufficient data at present to allocate this output to intermediate and final consumption. Consequently, aggregate intermediate consumption includes payment of net interest income to a notional industry. For general government, value added is compiled from the compensation of employees. For manufacturing, the level of output and intermediate consumption are estimated from the annual NAES. For construction, output is estimated using a commodity flow method, as the sum of production costs for materials and labor unique to the industry, plus trade and transport margins for those items. For non-banana crops, fishing, hotels and restaurants, and road and sea transport, output is produced from estimates of quantity produced multiplied by average price. For crops, intermediate input is estimated via a commodity flow method. For construction, wholesale and retail trade, hotels and restaurants, and road transport (in non-benchmark years), value added is estimated as the product of an estimated ratio of value added to output from a sample survey, either the annual NAES or an ad hoc or special collection, multiplied by estimated total output for the industry. For Real Estate and Housing output and intermediate consumption are estimated by extrapolation using benchmark surveys. 
 Estimation of constant price data: One of two methods is used for compiling constant price data, depending on the industry. In the first method, constant price value added is computed by deflating current price value added by a price index. This method is used for wholesale and retail trade, telecommunications, construction, insurance and government services. In the second method, constant price value added is computed by extrapolating a reference year current price estimate of value added by an output volume index of physical indicators for manufacturing, mining and quarrying, electricity and water, hotels and restaurants, road, sea, and air transport and banks. Expenditure approach: 
 Coverage adjustments: There are no adjustments in the sources and estimates of expenditure on GDP not already mentioned under the production approach. 
 Estimation of current price data: Private final consumption is determined as the residual of GDP using the production method less government consumption, gross capital formation, and exports less imports of goods and services. Gross fixed capital formation comprises investment in equipment and structures. Equipment investment is estimated via a commodity flow method principally using data on imports, and structures investment is the gross output of the construction industry. There are no adjustments to equipment investment to include set-up costs. As noted in Nature of basic data sources above, exports and imports of goods and services derive from the Balance of Payments prepared by the ECCB and CSO. The production and expenditure methods cannot be reconciled as private consumption is determined as a residual. 
 Base year and update cycle: The year of reference for constant price series is 1990; in principle, it is updated on a 10-year cycle. See Plans for Improvement.
