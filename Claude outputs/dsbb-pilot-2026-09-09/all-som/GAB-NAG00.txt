 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: The analytical framework currently used is that of the United Nations System of National Accounts 1968 (SNA68) . The presentation of the accounts is based on the "Extended System of National Accounts" (SNA76), implemented by France in the 70s. The Directorate of National Accounts of the Directorate General of Statistics and Economic Studies (DGSEE) produces and disseminated detailed accounts for the reference year about 24 months after the end of the year. Since 2001, a system of "rapid accounts" was implemented. It can be used to obtain prior-year GDP estimates in value. It should be noted that the data produced for the accounts are compiled in millions of CFA francs at current and 1989 prices. The Rapid Accounts, however, use a concept of residence based on the SNA93. The national accounts so compiled are used to prepare an input-output table at current prices for 33 branches of economic activity and at 1989 prices for a more limited number of branches. The entire national accounts system will move to the SNA93 in 2003. The DGSEE produces and disseminates: Nominal GDP in billions of CFAF and the growth rate of real GDP, based on the value of GDP at prior-year prices. This estimate is based on the rapid accounts compiled on the basis of a sample of the largest enterprises (90 percent of overall value added). Nominal GDP and real GDP in billions of CFA francs, with the main components of demand; GDP at factor cost; The Input-Output Table and the Comprehensive Economic Table. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classifications used are those recommended in the SNA68 for institutional sectors, the classification of the African, Malagasy and Mauritian Common Organization (OCAMM) in connection with the third revision of the international standard classification, by industry, of all branches of economic activity (ISIC) for branches of economic activity and goods and services. Scope of the data Geographic coverage : The coverage of the national accounts is nationwide, including enclaves within the territory. The rest of the world is taken into account within the framework of the country’s international trade. Transactions covered : All economic activities in Gabon are covered. Unit coverage : Gabon's national accounts data cover all resident units (based on the SNA68), including the informal sector, defined here as all producers who do not keep formal accounts. Data on the informal sector are obtained on the basis of questionnaires, information from administrative sources, and estimates made on the basis of survey results (in particular, the 1994 budget-consumption survey). Data extracted from the statistical and tax declarations (DSFs) are adjusted for non-reporting and underreporting. The DGSEE produces and disseminates: Nominal GDP in billions of CFAF and the growth rate of real GDP, based on the value of GDP at prior-year prices. This estimate is based on the rapid accounts compiled on the basis of a sample of the largest enterprises (90 percent of overall value added). Nominal GDP and real GDP in billions of CFA francs, with the main components of demand; GDP at factor cost; The Input-Output Table and the Comprehensive Economic Table. Unrecorded activity It is currently difficult to ascertain fraudulent operations related to contraband, for example. However, these are determined from the supply-use balances by product in the IOT. 2.4.1 Valuation Production is valued at base prices, imports are valued c.i.f. (cost-insurance-freight), domestic uses are recorded at purchase prices, and exports are measured f.o.b. (free on board). Market prices and exchange rates are used for the conversion of foreign exchange-denominated operations. The rates used are averages of the rates over the year. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: Transactions are generally recorded on an accrual basis, with the exception of some general government operations, which are recorded on a cash/disbursement basis. Recording period: The reference period is the calendar year (January 1 to December 31). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Production Approach: Agriculture-livestock-fisheries: Besides Tax documents for legally established enterprises, the data are provided by the Ministry of Agriculture and the agencies involved in agricultural and rural development (tillable acreage or acreage under crop, yield in volume of goods marketed domestically, etc.). Wood and wood industry: tax bundles, survey of industrial production, data from the labor organization of forestry enterprises, customs data on wood, data from the Gabonese wood marketing agency (Société nationale des bois du Gabon-SNBG) and the logging company Société d'exploitation des parcs a bois du Gabon (SEPBG). Oil: Tax bundles, activities report of major companies, data on the Directorate General of Hydrocarbons. Mines: Tax bundles and questionnaire on industrial production. Other enterprises in the modern sector: Tax bundles and questionnaire on industrial production. Informal sector: Small ad hoc surveys, administrative documents, other estimates based on the 1994 budget-consumption survey, and the 1993 general population census. Insurance and financial institutions: Tax bundles and other regulatory statistical statements. General government: Budgetary documents, Treasury balance sheet, government account statements concerning the debt, social security agency accounts, questionnaires sent to public and private entities (associations, labor organizations). Expenditure Approach: Household final consumption: Estimates on the basis of the 1994 budget-consumption survey and the supply-use balance by product in the context of consumption of certain goods. Government final consumption: Budget documents and Treasury balance sheet. Gross fixed capital formation (GFCF): Tax bundles; administrative and budget documents; Treasury balance sheet for government GFCF. Changes in stocks: Tax bundles and supply/use balances by product. Imports and exports of goods: Customs data, tax bundles, and value added tax (VAT) statements. Imports and exports of services: Tax bundles and balance of payments prepared by BEAC. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The national accounts are compiled on the Excel module, which can determine GDP using a supply-side approach based on data from statistical tax declarations (DSF) and administrative data (sum of value added). This first approach is used to determine GDP by branch of economic activity. The result for each branch of economic activity is then used to determine GDP. The outcome of this procedure can be used to divide value added by branch into gross operating surplus, personnel costs, and taxes. The second approach can be used to determine the components of demand: In the trade balance, customs data reconciled with DSF and balance of payments data are used in the calculation. Treasury balance sheet data are used as the basis of calculation of government consumption. Household consumption is provided by estimating ratios calculated on imports or on local production, depending on the product. Consistency in consumption is assured by comparing the average propensity obtained by the theoretical average propensity. Corporate investment is obtained from the bundles of tax documents, whereas household investment is determined using the composition of expenditure derived from the 1994 budget-consumption survey. The calculation of these various components is used to compile an IOT, which is balanced by the changes in stocks. Data extracted from the statistical and tax declarations (DSFs) are adjusted for non-reporting and underreporting. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Data in volume are calculated using 1989 as the base 100. The data are reviewed in two stages based on the volume of the data entered. The transition is from rapid accounts to semi-final accounts, then final accounts. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request These data are available upon request from the Directorate.
