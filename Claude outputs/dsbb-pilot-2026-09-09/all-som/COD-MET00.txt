 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Preparation of customs statistics on the basis of customs declarations is under the authority of the Statistics and Documentation Division of the Customs and Excise Office (OFIDA). The adopted methodology complies with the common statistics regulations of the Common Market for Eastern and Southern Africa (COMESA), as well as the recommendations of the United Nations and the World Customs Organization in that it follows the internationally accepted codes for the harmonized system (HS) for products (product codes) and standard units (recommended units of measurement), ISO 3166 for countries, and ISO 4217 for currencies, as well as codes on customs procedures and supplementary units. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The adopted methodology follows the internationally accepted codes for the harmonized system (HS) for products (product codes) and standard units (recommended units of measurement), ISO 3166 for countries, and ISO 4217 for currencies, as well as codes on customs procedures and supplementary units. Merchandise is identified using the six-digit tariff nomenclature under the Harmonized System, 1996 version, with a two-digit extension for national purposes. Imports are broken down by country of origin or source country, and exports are broken down by country of first destination. Scope of the data Customs statistics reflect merchandise imports and exports according to the general trade system, in which imports comprise imported merchandise made directly available for domestic consumption and merchandise entering customs warehouses. Postal parcels are included in these statistics, which also take account of smuggling and customs fraud. Customs statistics are expressed in Congolese francs based on customs declarations. Unrecorded activity It should be noted that merchandise in trans-shipment or transiting through the country is not reflected in the statistics under the general system. The same is true for certain merchandise owing to its special nature, such as travelers’ personal effects, items included in household removals, and strategic merchandise such as currency and arms and munitions. By contrast, postal parcels are included in these statistics, which also take account of smuggling and customs fraud. 2.4.1 Valuation Statistical values are based on c.i.f. values for imports and f.o.b. values for exports. Values are converted into U.S. dollars at the official exchange rate determined by the Central Bank of the Congo. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Preparation of customs statistics on the basis of customs declarations is under the authority of the Statistics and Documentation Division of the Customs and Excise Office (OFIDA).
