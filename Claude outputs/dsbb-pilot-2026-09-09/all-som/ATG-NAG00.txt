 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The compilation of national accounts involves a statistical procedure aimed at providing a numerical summary of the many transactions taking place in a country during a given period of time, usually one year. These transactions relate, in particular, to the production and use of goods and services and to the transfers of income or capital between sectors of the economy. The framework and concepts of the national accounts is based on the "System of National Accounts 1968" ("1968 SNA"). GDP is compiled from the production approach as the difference between total output at factor cost and intermediate input at market (purchasers') prices. GDP by expenditure is the sum of final uses of goods and services in the total economy, less the value of imports of goods and services. Producer units are grouped according to principal economic activity. The components of GDP by expenditure follow the standard definitions for final use provided in the "1968 SNA". However exception is made for the treatment relating to the imputation of financial intermediation services, which is allocated entirely to intermediate consumption and excluded from GDP. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Economic Activities are classified using the ISIC REV. 3. The thirteen (13) activities covered differ from the ISIC presentation, hence some sectors are combined and other are shown separately. They comprise (ISIC categories are shown in parenthesis): Agriculture (A-B) Mining and Quarrying (C) Manufacturing (D) Electricity and Water (E) Construction (F) Wholesale and Retail Trade (G) Hotels and Restaurants (H) Transportation (I: 60 - 63) and Communication (I: 64) Banks and Insurance (J) Real Estate and Housing (K:70) Government Services (L) Government consumption expenditure is classified by purpose using COFOG categories Other Services (K:71 - 74+M+N+O+P+Q) Scope of the data The statistical framework follows the "System of National Accounts 1968" ("1968 SNA"). GDP is compiled and disseminated in millions of Eastern Caribbean dollars (EC$) by the Statistics Division at current and constant (1990) prices. The period of reference is the calendar year. Consistent data series begin with 1977. All resident institutional units operating within the Antigua & Barbuda economy and transactions to and from the rest of the world are covered. Commodity flow method is used for activities where response rate to surveys are poor and where significant informal activity predominates. Unrecorded activity Adjustments are made for unrecorded activities in some sectors, mainly Agriculture and Transportation. Own-account production of goods are not covered by the available sources of data and are indirectly estimated. No other adjustments are made for unrecorded activities. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: Output and value added by economic activity are valued at market price, which is equivalent to producers' prices, defined as market prices less invoiced trade and transportation margins. Intermediate consumption is valued at market prices. Data are published by industrial activity at facto cost, which is derived from output at producer's prices by subtracting taxes on products and production and adding subsidies for products and on production. Exports and Imports are valued free on board (f.o.b.) 2.4.2 Recording basis National accounts data are recorded on an accrual basis for the calendar year. Reporting agents are requested to make adjustments where necessary based on their own judgment to cater for conversions during the year. Conversion is usually done based on the transaction rate. One exception is government data in the expenditure account, which are recorded on a cash basis. Recording Period: While data are compiled for a calendar year, a number of enterprises compile their accounts on a fiscal accounting year that does not coincide with a calendar year. Their annual data are included in the national accounts for a given calendar year if their fiscal year begins before July 1 of that year. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs GDP is compiled and disseminated by the production and expenditure methods. The basic data sources for compiling national accounts are the National Accounts Annual Survey and government administrative records. Gross National Income (GNI) is derived by adding net factor incomes from abroad to GDP. Gross National Disposable Income (GNDI) is derived by adding net current transfers from abroad to GNI. Saving is derived as the difference between GNDI and the sum of private and government consumption. For further details on the BOP, see the comprehensive framework and data categories and indicators for the External Sector. The national accounts compilation exercise is a joint effort between the Statistics Division of Antigua and Barbuda and the Eastern Caribbean Central Bank (ECCB). The Statistics Division is responsible for conducting the annual surveys and the compilation of annual estimates. The ECCB provides technical assistance to verify the compilation procedure and assist with projections. Agriculture: Crops: The production of total output of crops is derived from the Agricultural Extension Division (PROMIS) and the Ministry of Agriculture Dunbar's Statistics Section). Output is evaluated at producer's price obtained from the Central Marketing Corporation. To arrive at the value added, the value of intermediate consumption, is deducted from the gross value of output. The double deflation method is used to obtain the value added in constant prices. Total production of agricultural crops is revalued at 1990 prices and inputs are taken to be 20% of the gross value of output at constant prices. Livestock: The Central Board of Health supplies the number of animals slaughtered throughout the island, and an upward adjustment is made for unrecorded slaughter. The quantities of the various animals slaughtered are multiplied by the average weight per animal and then again by the producers' price per pound, thus arriving at the total value of output. The producers' price is taken to be 20 percent less than the market price. In the absence of data on inputs, certain proportions of outputs have been used as material inputs. The change in stock is compiled on the basis of figures supplied by the Agricultural Extension Division. The constant price estimates are calculated by using the double deflation method. The output is revalued at 1990 prices and a fixed percentage is decided as input costs. Forestry and Logging: The output in forestry and logging consists of wattle, which is used by fishermen in building fish traps, and charcoal which is used for cooking, etc. The quantity of wattle used for fishing is estimated on the basis of data obtained from the Fisheries Division. The number of bags of charcoal sold at the public market is estimated to arrive at yearly production figures. Market price is taken to be equal to producers' price. Constant price estimates are computed by revaluating production at 1990 prices and by using fixed percentage as input cost. Fishing: The quantity of fish landed is provided by the Fisheries Division. The total value of production is computed at producers' price, which is fixed by the Government. Input costs are compiled on the basis of a report from the Fisheries Association with minor adjustments. These material inputs consist of fuel, nets, fish trap replacements and repair and maintenance boats. The constant price estimates are obtained by valuing output at 1990 prices deducting input cost revalued at 1990 prices. Mining and Quarrying: There is no significant mining activity. The output covers production of gravel, stone, marble and sand. Data are supplied by two principal producers. The production approach is used to estimate the value added, that is, estimating the value of each component of production. Input costs are calculated on the basis of data supplied by these producers. Constant prices are obtained by revaluing the total production of each component at 1990 prices and likewise for input costs whenever necessary. Manufacturing: The value added in the manufacturing sector is estimated by the income approach. The main source of data is the Inland Revenue Department and surveys conducted by this Division. In cases where companies are not surveyed, or no response is received, the available income tax returns are analyzed and data on wages and salaries, interest, rent, depreciation and profits, which constitute the value added, are compiled. The constant price estimates are worked out by extrapolating the base year estimates on the basis of manufacturing output. Electricity and Water: The value added in this sector is estimated by the income approach. The main sources of data are the accounts of the Antigua Public Utilities Authority. The constant price estimates are extrapolated from base year estimates on the basis of electricity sold and water supplied. Construction: The value added in construction is normally estimated by the commodity flow approach. However, the absence of trade data since 1991 has rendered this method useless for this purpose. An amalgamation is made of building application approved from the Development Control Authority the Government's Public Sector Investment Project, and domestic production of construction materials to derive value added for construction. For the constant price estimates, the deflator used is an index of the unit cost of construction. Wholesale and Retail Trade: The value added in this sector is estimated by the income approach. The main data source is the annual surveys to compile estimates and tax returns at the Inland Revenue Department. Adjustments are made for non-response on the basis of data on employment and remuneration available from the Social Security Department. An allowance for small trades has been made. The constant price estimates are obtained by deflating current prices with the Consumer Price Index (CPI). Hotels and Restaurants: The income approach is used to estimate the value added in this sector. The current price estimates have been prepared by analyzing the accounts of hotels and restaurants from completed National Accounts Survey Forms and available data in the Inland Revenue Department. An allowance for under coverage and an adjustment to the accounts for small hotels and restaurants are made. The estimated number of bed nights is used as a volume index to derive the constant price estimates for hotels, and the number of workers in restaurants is used as a volume index for restaurants. Transport: The transport sector is comprised of road, sea and air transport. Road Transport: The value-added estimates are calculated on the basis of earnings per vehicle and number of vehicles. The vehicles covered are taxis, buses, rental cars and trucks available from the Licensing Department at the Transport Board. Government vehicles (buses and trucks) are excluded. The number of each vehicle is multiplied by its earnings. The constant price estimates are extrapolated by the number of vehicles in each category. Sea Transport: Data from the annual survey of the Port Authority and shipping agents are analyzed to compile the value added. The income approach is used. Constant price estimates are obtained by using total tonnage of cargo landed and loaded as a volume index. Air Transport: The income of travel agents and airport administration is obtained from annual surveys, and 7 percent of LIAT's operating surplus is included representing the share of equity capital held by Antigua and Barbuda. The constant price estimates are based on an index of the volume of passenger movement. Communication: Data obtained in the annual survey of communication companies are analyzed to obtain the value-added estimates. The constant price estimates are derived by deflating the value added from appropriate price indices. For telephone, a volume index based on the number of telephone sets in service is used to extrapolate the base year estimates. Banks and Insurance: Data on the commercial banks and insurance companies are collected in the annual surveys. From these accounts the value added is estimated using the income approach. Where there is no response or incomplete data is provided, the available accounts at the Inland Revenue Department are utilized. Real Estate and Housing: This sector consists of owner-occupied dwellings, rental of dwellings and other building structures and real estate agents. The rental value of taxed dwellings is available from the Property Valuation Department. The valuation is done once every five to ten years. The figures have been interpolated for those years for which data are not available. One month's rent is taken as the cost for repairs and maintenance. Real estate estimates are obtained by analyzing financial accounts for real estate agents. For the constant price estimates, the number of dwellings is multiplied by the 1990 rental value per dwelling to arrive at gross output. In the case of real estate, the number of agents is used as the indicator to obtain the constant price estimates. Producers of Government Services: This sector covers the administrative services of the Government. The public enterprises which render services such as the General Post Office, Harbor, Airport Services, Fisheries and A.P.U.A. are not covered here, but are covered in their respective sectors. The services included in the Public Administration do not have a market price. The value added is reckoned at cost and therefore consists entirely of employee compensation and contributions to unfunded pension schemes. Estimates of employee compensation are compiled from a detailed analysis of Government Accounts. The value-added of the Social Security Fund and the Medical Benefits Scheme, derived from their financial statements are added to this sector. The current price estimates are adjusted in order to show payments of Social Security and Medical Benefit contributions on an accrual basis. A wage index is used to deflate the current price estimates, while constant price estimates for Social Security and Medical Benefits are compiled by an index based on the number of people employed. Other Services: These services refer to professional, community, social and personal, recreational and cultural services. The major sources of information are the Social Security records, tax returns at the Inland Revenue Department and data obtained from the National Accounts Survey Forms. The constant price estimates are derived from a volume index of the number of workers in each division. Imputed Banking Service Charges: The "SNA" recommends that imputed banking service charges be calculated for financial institutions. No attempt is made to allocate these charges to industries, but instead, they are deducted from the GDP in a single line. Imputed banking service charges consist of the excess of property income received on loans and other investments made from deposits held over the interest paid out. The same indicator used in the Banking Sector is utilized to compile the constant price estimates. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Coverage Adjustments: National accounts compilation has been plagued by the continued absence of trade data since 1991. This has seriously impacted on the use of the commodity flow approach to estimate value added in some sectors, especially construction and wholesale and retail trade. For the construction sector, alternative data sources include building applications approved annually (Development Control Authority) and the Government's Public Sector Investment Projects. Wholesale and retail trade estimates are inflated based on movements in CPI and patterns of consumer spending where there is not enough data derived from Annual Surveys. Estimation of Current Price Data: Value added by economic activity is from data collection during annual surveys and/or obtained from administrative sources. These initial estimates are then inflated where necessary using historical data based on benchmark surveys to ensure full coverage within the sector. For most sectors, value added is computed as the difference between output and intermediate consumption (production approach). The exception to this rule is general government where the income approach is used to derive value added. For the expenditure approach, exports and imports of goods and services are derived from the Balance of Payments Accounts. Estimation of Constant Price Data: One of two methods is used to compile constant price data, depending on the industry. In the first method, the constant price valued is computed by deflating current price value added by a price index of commodities (inputs). This is applied to crops, fishing, manufacturing, construction, general government, wholesale and retail, and communications. The second method involves the extrapolation of a reference year current price estimate of value added by an output volume index of inputs. This is applied to mining and quarrying, electricity and water, hotels and restaurants, transportation, financial services, and real estate The deflators for exports and imports are the world export and import value indices published in the IMF's "International Financial Statistics." Reconciliation: As the sum of private consumption and increase in stocks (change in inventories) is determined as a residual in both the current and constant price estimates, the production and expenditure approaches are not independently determined and reconciled by definition. Exports and imports of goods and services used in the calculation of GDP and net factor incomes from abroad used in the calculation of GNI are taken from the Balance of Payments and therefore reconcile by definition.
