 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The National Statistical Office, which is a government department in the Ministry of Development Planning and Cooperation, is responsible for compiling the National Accounts of Malawi. The framework is based on the 1993 System of National Accounts. The Accounts are compiled on an annual basis at a detailed level and comprise about 100 industries and 340 products. The compilation of national accounts is based on many statistical sources: Annual Economic Survey for large scale enterprises, accounting statistics for government, external trade statistics, Integrated Household Survey, agriculture crop estimates survey and livestock census and many others. The process of compilation starts with independent supply and demand estimates of all goods and services. Finally, supply and demand for each of these is balanced by using supplementary information and quality assessments of the various statistical sources. The Malawi National Accounts are compiled using both the Production as well as the Expenditure approach. The following components are compiled at current and constant prices: Gross Domestic Product (GDP) by kind of economic activity GDP by expenditure 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The economic activities are classified based on the UN International Standard Industrial Classification Rev 3.1 (ISIC rev.3.1). For publication purposes, production is disaggregated into fifteen industries, namely: Agriculture, forestry and fishing Mining and quarrying Manufacturing Electricity, gas and water supply Construction Wholesale and retail trade Transport and storage Accommodation and food service activities Information and communication Financial and Insurance Services Real Estate Professional, scientific and technical activities, Administrative and support service activities Public administration and defence Education Human health and social work activities A line for FISIM has been added. The latest report available on national accounts is for the years 2002 to 2005. However, figures for 2006 are available and can be accessed on NSO’s website. Sequence of accounts: The Unit compiles the sequence of accounts for all the Institutional Sectors. Size Classification : The availability and quality of data dictated the split of producers into different size categories. As such, the producers are categorized into: Large-scale Medium-scale Small-scale The criterion for classifying the establishments into these groups is the number of employees. For the large-scale, it depends on the type of industry while for medium-scale it ranges from 5 to 99 employees and those with less than five employees are put in the small-scale sector. Other Classifications: Classification of Functions of Government (COFOG). Classification of the Purpose of Non-profit Institution Serving Households (COPNI) External Trade Statistics are classified based on the Harmonised System (HS). Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP) Institutional sector Classification which lists the main sectors of the economy Activity classification of the national accounts. This is based on the ISIC Rev 3.1 which is currently used in national accounts. The Malawi national accounts contain about 100 industries Product classification of the national accounts. This is based on the UN central product classification (CPC). The Malawi national accounts contain 340 aggregated goods and services. Scope of the data Geographic Coverage: The whole geographic territory of Malawi is covered. Transaction Coverage: The National Accounts cover all transactions and all institutional units that are within the production boundary except for the underground activities and some of the informal sector activities. However, information from Household Surveys covers part of the informal sector activities. Unit Coverage: The national accounts cover in principle all resident institutional units. The framework is based on the 1993 System of National Accounts. All Institutional units that are within the boundaries of Malawi are covered. All transactions, except illegal transactions, are covered and are valued at market prices. The estimates of value added are presented at basic prices. GDP through the Expenditure approach is also compiled with the following expenditure aggregates: exports, imports, fixed capital formation, government consumption and private final consumption The output of financial intermediation services indirectly measured is not allocated to various users. FISIM is recorded as intermediate consumption, and an adjustment to GDP of the total FISIM is carried out. A line of the nominal sector for FISIM is included in the tables to show the value of the FISIM correction on the GDP. There are several Indices that are used in the compilation of Malawi National Accounts. These are: Consumer Price Index Agriculture Farmgate Price Indices Import Unit price Indices Export Price Indices Input Price indices Exceptions to coverage Some of the informal sector activities are estimated indirectly through such sources as household surveys. Intermediate consumption is published in Purchasers’ Prices, i.e. inclusive of non-deductible VAT and other net taxes on products 3. Value added is Output less intermediate consumption, i.e. published in basic prices 4. GDP is measured in Market Prices 5. Imports are recorded c.i.f. while exports are recorded f.o.b.; imports are converted to f.o.b. using a factor of 15 percent for shipping and insurance. 6. Transactions in foreign currency are converted using the average annual exchange rate. 2.4.2 Recording basis The National Accounts of Malawi are compiled on an accrual basis except for government transactions, which are compiled on a cash basis. The National Accounts recording period is the calendar year. Fiscal year data from government are converted into calendar year. Agriculture production is allocated to the harvest year 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The National Statistical Office (NSO) compiles a Business Information Register (BIR), which acts as a sampling frame for almost all the economic surveys in the office such as the Annual Economic Survey (AES) and the periodic Medium Business Economic Survey (MBES). The AES covers all large scale enterprises from BIR and the MBES covers a sample for the medium scale. The BIR is a list of all establishments that are known by the NSO to be operating in Malawi. Establishments are categorized on the basis of employment into large-scale, medium-scale and small-scale as already indicated. There are a few departures from this, like in the agriculture industry where there are only two sectors; large-scale and small-scale. The large-scale is the Estate agriculture and the small-scale is the Smallholder agriculture. Ownership of dwellings is divided into medium and small-scale sectors only and government services are only large-scale. The basic data sources differ, depending on the size group. OUTPUT AND INTERMEDIATE CONSUMPTION Agriculture, Forestry and Fishing: Production statistics for smallholder crops are compiled by the Planning Unit of the Ministry of Agriculture while price data is obtained from the Agro Economic Survey Department in the same Ministry of Agriculture. Information for livestock is obtained from the Department of Animal Health and that for fish is provided by the Department of Fisheries and Maldeco, which is involved in commercial fish catch. Maldeco is a company that is surveyed in the AES. Information for large-scale agriculture is obtained from various enterprises that are covered in the AES. Some of them are the Auction Holdings for tobacco, Tea Association for tea and Illovo Company for sugar. Firms that own forests provide statistics on timber. Mining and Quarrying: Information on current price series for this sector is obtained from the AES for the large-scale sector. Manufacturing: The benchmark data and the current price series for the large-scale sector are compiled from the AES, which is a survey of all large-scale establishments that are in the BIR. The information captured in the AES covers output, intermediate consumption and changes in inventory. For medium-scale sector, information from 1998 Medium Business Economic Survey is used and the years in between are interpolated. This gives current price series. For constant price series, CPI is used to deflate the current price series. From 1999, when data are not available, the medium-scale sector is assumed to be growing at the same rate as the large-scale sector, and therefore, growth rates from the large-scale sector are used to come up with constant price series. For the small-scale sector, the second Integrated Household Survey (HIS-2) was used to estimate the small scale sector for the year 2002. The IHS-2 provided data for 2004 and the Index of Industrial Production was used for deflating the values back to 2002 and 2003. From 2004 and onwards the growth rates for the large scale sectors from the AES have been applied also for the small scale sector. Electricity and Water: These utilities are entirely in the large-scale sector. Information for the current price series is obtained from the AES, which collects this information from the Electricity Supply Commission of Malawi (ESCOM), which is the only electricity provider in Malawi. Data for water statistics are collected from Malawi's Water Boards. Construction: Information for large-scale sector at current prices is obtained from the AES and this is obtained from the companies that are engaged in building and construction. For the medium-scale sector, information is from MBES for current prices and deflation to constant prices is by the CPI. From 1999, when data are not available, the medium-scale sector is assumed to be growing at the same rate as the large-scale sector for the same industry, and therefore, growth rates from the large-scale sector are used to come up with constant price series. For the small-scale sector, the second Integrated Household Survey (IHS-2) was used to estimate the small scale sector for the year 2002. The IHS-2 provided data for 2004 and the Index of Industrial Production was used for deflating the values back to 2002 and 2003. From 2004 and onwards the growth rates for the large scale sectors from the AES have been applied also for the small scale sector Distribution: Large-scale sector information at current prices is from the AES. The AES collects information from companies that are engaged in agriculture distribution, motor vehicle distribution, fuel distribution, wholesaling and retailing. Transport and Storage: In general, the AES provides information for the large-scale sector in current prices for both transport and storage. Accommodation and Food Services: The AES provides data at current prices for the accommodation and food providers. Information and Communication: The data source is the AES that collects information from the Publishers, Mobile phone operators, ground phone operators etc. Real Estate, Professional and Technical Services: Information is obtained from the AES. Financial and Professional Services: The major source of information for data at current prices for the large-scale sector is the AES, which collects information from financial institutions and those institutions that provide professional services. For the medium scale sector, information from the1994 and 1998 medium Business Economic Survey. Public Administration and Defence: The Accountant General’s Department which is under the Ministry of Finance is the source of audited accounts data on public expenditure. For 2002, 2003 and 2004, the data was only at item level and had to be supplemented by the budget data that was available at sub-item level. However, from 2005 the data is available at sub-item level. Education: The main source of data for this sector is the Integrated Household Survey 2 (IHS2) which collected data in respect of mission and Islamic schools. Such data was linked to the school’s income from tuition and boarding fees on one hand and expenditures on books, construction and maintenance works on the other. Most schools are subsidized by the government so that their production is far much higher than their fees. In addition, government audited accounts were also used. Human Health activities : Data on this sector was obtained from the Christian Health Association of Malawi (CHAM) secretariat which compiles data for the health units that it manages. The health units activities are partly funded by Government. Another source is the government audited accounts. Social Work activities: The source of data is those NGOs involved in social work activities GDP BY TYPE OF FINAL EXPENDITURE The GDP is also presented as a sum of final expenditures by government and households, gross fixed capital formation; exports of goods and services net of imports. The figures are derived from the supply and use tables, with the sources described above. The Malawian national accounts is calculated with integrated supply and use tables (SUT). The SUT shows how the supply of goods and services originate from domestic industries and imports, and how those supplies are allocated between various intermediate or final uses, including exports. The sources for final use are: Government final consumption: Data are provided by the government accounts. This comprises; compensation of employees (wages and salaries, allowances and pensions), expenditure on goods and services. Fixed Capital Formation: Expenditure on fixed capital formation has been estimated under four separate categories: dwellings, other buildings and construction, transport equipment and machinery. Exports of goods and services, less imports of goods and services: Basic data are trade statistics compiled by the Trade Unit of the NSO using Bills of Entry provided by the Malawi Revenue Authority. In current prices, both imports and exports of goods and services are estimated fob, while for constant prices, an import unit value and export unit value are used to deflate the current price series. Household Final Consumption Expenditure: This is estimated from the Integrated Household surveys (IHS). Constant Price Estimates Constant-price estimates in the final national accounts are calculated at prices of the previous year, in other words, the base year is changed every year. Subsequently, data on changes of volume are constructed in terms of growth rates and corresponding implicit data on changes of prices. In parallel, time series of volume figures are constructed using prices from a reference year. Since the chaining is carried out separately for all items, the table components do, however, not necessarily add to the totals of the same table. The calculation of constant-price estimates is carried out within the framework of integrated supply and use tables at current prices, by deflating current values at the detailed product level. The deflation approach has in fact two dimensions, (i) differentiated by main categories of supply and use (deflating output, imports, exports and implicitly for domestic use), and (ii) differentiated through valuation (deflating current values at basic prices by price indices and implicitly determining the other value components, including adjustments against values at purchasers' prices). The principle of double deflation is used, i.e. separate deflation of output and intermediate consumption in order to arrive at value added at constant prices as a balancing item, through a detailed input-output framework (supply and use tables). Constant-price estimates for aggregates of supply, uses and value added follow through by adding up and balancing constant-price estimates of products. They include aggregates such as output by industry, categories of exports and imports, categories of final uses, intermediate consumption by industry, value added by industry and GDP. Preliminary Estimates Data is obtained from Agriculture Crop estimates for the Agriculture Sector and from sampled enterprises that are visited during Business Interviews. Business Interviews are meetings that are held between the National Accounts Technical Committee (comprising of National Statistical Office, Ministry of Development Planning and Cooperation, Ministry of Finance and the Reserve Bank of Malawi) and the management of the sampled Business community enterprises to solicit their views on production, investment and employment prospects. The indicators obtained are in volume terms and where monetary values are given, they are deflated by an appropriate index to get the constant price value. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording 3.1.3 Source data timeliness 3.2.1 Source data assessment The main source data to the National Accounts is the Annual Economic Survey (AES) which as the name implies is an annual survey. When results of the survey are out, the National Accounts and the AES teams sit together to discuss the results and get clarification wherever its needed. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The most current year for the current price estimates is 2006. However, the National Accounts unit is currently working on the 2007 figures which should be ready by August 2010. The base year for the preliminary constant price series is 2006, from 2007, growth rates of volumes are used to extrapolate value added in constant prices. Several adjustments are made to ensure complete coverage: To take care of the non-responding companies, the AES and the IIP make some adjustments to gross up to industry totals. For example, growth rates from companies within the same ISIC group and class are used to estimate gross output and intermediate consumption for the non-responding companies to be able to come up with value added figure. In the case of agriculture estimates, the task of estimating crop production is done by the Planning Unit of the Ministry of Agriculture. These estimates are compiled from a crops survey. For national accounts purposes some adjustments are made to cassava to convert it from wet weight to dry weight. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Data is provided on request
