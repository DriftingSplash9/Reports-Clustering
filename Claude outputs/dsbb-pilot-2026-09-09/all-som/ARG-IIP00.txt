 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Definition: the International Investment Position (IIP) of Argentina is a statistical statement that shows at a point in time the value and composition of external financial assets and liabilities. The economy's net IIP is the difference between the economy's external financial assets and liabilities. Quarterly data are compiled. Analytical framework: the conceptual framework and classification structure for Argentina's IIP statistics closely follows the sixth edition of the International Monetary Fund's Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6) standards and guidelines, with extensions to take account of domestic user requirements. The IIP is fully integrated with Argentina's balance of payments and external debt statistics. The IIP also shows the changes in the positions during the reporting period as a result of financial transactions, revaluations and other adjustments. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Argentina's international investment position statistics are classified according to BPM6. Financial assets and liabilities are classified in the following functional categories: direct investment, portfolio investment (showing a breakdown of equity and debt), financial derivatives, other investment and reserve assets. These are further classified by instruments, by institutional sectors and by maturity (long term/short term). The short- and long-term attributions of transactions in the General government, Central bank, Deposit-taking Corporations, except the Central bank and Other sectors are made according to the original maturity of the financial instrument. Scope of the data Transactions coverage: Argentina’s IIP statistics attempt to cover all of Argentina's foreign financial assets and liabilities, together with all related transactions. All institutional sectors are covered, although the international investment activity of households may not be fully captured by existing sources and methods. Geographical coverage: in Argentina's IIP, the entire national economic territory is covered. Exceptions to coverage The international investment activity of households may not be fully captured by existing sources and methods. 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to unit of account: the IIP is compiled in U.S. dollars. The stock positions originally denominated in other currencies are converted into U.S. dollars at the prevailing market exchange rates at the end of the period. Valuation - Assets: Direct investment positions of the Deposit-taking corporations are valued at book value. Real estate investment by the Other sectors is valued at market prices, but other direct investment stocks are valued at book value. Portfolio investment positions of the Deposit-taking corporations are taken at book values. Portfolio investment of the Other sectors is valued at market price. Other investment positions are valued at nominal value. Data on contributions to international organisms and bi-national bodies is valued at book value. International reserves are valued at market prices. - Liabilities: Direct investment positions in Argentina are valued at book value. Portfolio investment positions are mostly valued at market value. In addition, IIP statistics are also shown with debt securities valued at nominal value. Financial derivatives (other than reserves) positions are valued at market value. Other investment positions are valued at nominal value. 2.4.2 Recording basis The accrual basis is used to elaborate the IIP data following BPM6 indications. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The actual recording in the financial instruments is done on a gross basis, separately for financial assets and liabilities. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Direct Investment: Data on direct investment made abroad by Deposit-taking corporations are derived from the financial statements of these institutions. For the Other sectors, estimates of direct investment abroad are based on information from the firms' balance sheets and related statements. Additionally, estimates about real estate investment abroad are based on information provided by the Instituto Nacional de Estadistica of Uruguay, considering investment abroad represents for the others countries 50% of that of Uruguay. Direct investment in Argentina preliminary data are based on (a) quarterly balance sheet information of listed companies, (b) exchange market reporting system information (RIOC, for its Spanish acronym), (c) information of balance sheets of Deposit-taking corporations, except the Central bank and (d) survey on external assets and liabilities of the private sector of the BCRA: Intercompany lending were included since 1Q2020 and Equity and investment fund shares were included since 1Q2023. Portfolio Investment: Equity securities: Equity securities of Argentine enterprises issued abroad (ADRs) are supposed to be held by non-residents. For equity securities issued in Argentina, data on the residence of the holders are available from the records of Caja de Valores (the Securities Central Depository Inc.). Equity securities of abroad from US Department of Treasury, Caja de Valores S.A and Dow Jones. Debt securities: Data for both financial assets and liabilities-related transactions by Deposit-taking corporations, except the Central Bank are estimated from the balance sheets provided by the BCRA. Data for Other sectors assets from US Department of Treasury. Data for Other sectors liabilities are based on the survey on external assets and liabilities of the private sector of the BCRA. The Operational Directorate of International Financing from the Undersecretariat of International Financial Relations for Development and Entidad Binacional Yacyreta (EBY) provides information on financial assets investment abroad by the Central government. Debt securities issued abroad by the Central government are supposed to be held by non-residents, except for holdings of some resident entities, such as banks, Administración Nacional de Seguridad Social -ANSES- (social security entity), Superintendencia de Seguros de la Nación (supervisory agency), and other public sector organizations. For debt securities issued in Argentina by the Central government, data on the residence of the holders are available from the Secretariat of Finance – National Public Credit Office. For both, securities issued abroad and securities issued in Argentina, basic data are provided by the Sistema de Gestión y Análisis de Deuda - SIGADE (Public Debt Administration and Management System) and the residence of the holders is based on the records of Caja de Valores (Securities Central Depository Inc.). A complementary survey of local governments is conducted to collect information on their issues of securities abroad. Financial Derivatives: Data on GDP-linked units issued by the Central government are based on the SIGADE records and on the records of the Caja de Valores (Securities Central Depository Inc.). Data on financial derivatives issued by the Deposit-taking corporations, except the Central Bank are based on information of balance sheets of Deposit-taking corporations, except the Central bank provided by the BCRA. Other Investment: Data on foreign financial assets of Deposit-taking corporations, except the Central Bank, include currency and deposits denominated in foreign currency in Argentina and abroad, loans, trade credits and advances, and other debt liabilities – in pesos and in foreign currency – that the financial sector grants to non-residents. Liabilities include currency and deposits in foreign currency in Argentina, loans, trade credits and advances, and other debt liabilities – in pesos and in foreign currency – of non-residents-. Data are based on information of balance sheets of Deposit-taking corporations, except the Central bank of the BCRA. Data on financial assets of the Central Government are obtained from Operational Directorate of International Financing from the Undersecretariat of International Financial Relations for Development and balance sheets of EBY. Data on debt of the Central Government (excluding securities) are obtained from the SIGADE. Additional data are obtained from a survey about external debt of local governments. In the case of Other sectors, financial assets comprise bank deposits (derived from BIS and Central Bank of Uruguay data), trade credits data (based on own estimates), and other assets, mainly estimates of foreign currency held by residents (derived from currency flows data and from money-demand estimates). Currency flows are based on net remittances of bills, adjusted by income and outlays of cash for travel, and holdings from banks and central bank. On the liability side, it is based on survey on external assets and liabilities of the private sector of the BCRA. Reserve Assets: Report on international reserves/liquidity in foreign currency provided by BCRA.. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data are in general consistent with the definitions, scope, classifications, valuation and time of recording required. Nonetheless there are some deviations such as: Direct investment source data do not have a complete coverage of direct investment companies; The international investment activity of households may not be fully captured by existing sources and methods. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness In general, data collection timetables are adequate to meet timeliness and periodicity requirements. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data are assessed; extreme values and outliers are assessed and confirmed. When possible, IIP data are verified with other sources. Source data are checked against balance of payments financial flows and other changes. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques - Direct investment: Preliminary data about direct investment in Argentina are based on (a) quarterly balance sheet information of enterprises, (b) exchange market reporting system information (RIOC), (c) information of balance sheets of Deposits-taking corporations, except the Central bank and (d) survey on external assets and liabilities of the private sector of the BCRA: Intercompany lending were included since 1Q2020 and Equity and investment fund shares were included were included since 1Q2023. Real estate investment abroad data are adjusted to improve the coverage; stocks and flows are adjusted to estimate the market value. - Portfolio investment: For debt securities issued by the Central Government, the residence of the holders are based on the records of the Caja de Valores (Securities Central Depository Inc.). The estimations of the market value of these instruments are based on available market data at the end of each period. For those bonds with no market price available, the average value of known market values is applied. The market value of equities and bonds issued by the Deposit-taking corporations, except the Central bank are estimated on market prices available for shares and negotiated bonds. For the Other sectors, the estimates related to financial assets transactions are based on a study about the size and composition of their investment portfolio abroad and on information available in other countries on their liability side. In the case of the liabilities, the procedure to estimate the market value of equities and bonds is similar that in the case of the Deposit-taking corporations. - Other investment: In the case of holdings of foreign cash by Other sectors, the estimation is based on a level fixed as of the end of 1997, based on two indicators: a) net remittances of bills from U.S. adjusted by income and outlays of cash for travel, and banks and central bank holdings, and b) the calculation of a hypothetical demand of dollars for transactions, based on a period in which dollarization was not very significant and adjusting it by the spread use of credit cards. Taking into account the limitations of both estimates (leaks in bills remittances and reserve value demand not measured in the latter case), an intermediate stock was assumed (U$ 21.000 millions as of end of 1997). This stock is updated by means of the variation rate of net adjusted bill remittances; net remittances of bills (up to 2001 U.S Report of International Transportation of Currency or Monetary Instruments data, since 2001 data from the central bank, and since 2007 from customs data on imports and exports of foreign currency), adjusted by income and outlays of cash for travel, and banks and central bank holdings. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures No seasonal adjustment to data is made. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Data are also routinely compared with other available information such as company reports and press information, different administrative records, and monetary and fiscal statistics. Consistency between IIP statistics and Balance of Payments statistics are monitored. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Findings of revision work are used to improve preliminary data; these findings are analyzed, discussed and documented. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Major revisions are explained and informed to the public. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Only the disseminated data are available to users.
