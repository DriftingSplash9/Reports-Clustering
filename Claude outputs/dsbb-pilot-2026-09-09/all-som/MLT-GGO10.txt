 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The concepts and definitions used by the NSO to compile the Government Finance Statistics (GFS), which form the basis for fiscal monitoring in Europe, most notably for the statistics related to the Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP), follow the norms of the European Systems of National Accounts 2010 (ESA 2010), supplemented by further interpretation and guidance from Eurostat, in particular the Manual on Government Deficit and Debt. The concepts and definitions of taxes on fossil fuels align with the GFSM 2014 and ESA 2010. The CBM helps vet the annual IMF government finance statistics questionnaire in line with the Government Finance Statistics Manual 2014 (GFSM 2014) by bridging the ESA 2010 standards to the GFSM 2014. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization NSO compiles the general government statistics in the context of national accounts. Consequently, the classification of the revenue, expenditure, nonfinancial assets, and financial assets and liabilities, as well as the institutional sector classification of the general government, are consistent with the European System of Accounts 2010 (ESA 2010). The NSO also compiles the data on the general government expenditure by function, known as COFOG in line with the international standards as described in the Manual on sources and methods for the compilation of COFOG statistics. The NSO compiles the National Tax List for Malta in line with the ESA 2010 methodology. In ESA 2010 methodology the taxes on fossil fuels are classified as taxes on products (D214). Scope of the data The scope of general government finance statistics (GFS) is to be consistent with the international accepted standards and guidelines. In the compilation of GFS, all the general government activities are covered. The coverage of taxes on fossil fuels is the general government sector and it is aligned with the GFSM 2014 and ESA 2010 Sector Coverage In Malta, the general government sector consists of (1) the central government, which includes the budgetary central government and the extra-budgetary units (EBUs), and (2) local government, which comprises 68 local councils, six (6) regional councils and the local council association. The central government 1. The budgetary central government consists of the operations of government ministries and departments. There is an Office of the President, Office of the Prime Minister, the House of Representatives and a number of area-related ministries. Each ministry is further divided into departments and cost centres which are engaged in the public services for social policy, health, education, public administration, finance, environment etc. 2. The EBUs are institutional units, which are non-market producers, owned and supported by the government. These EBUs are established by various Acts of Parliament. They are independent, they have their own set of accounts, have only limited ministerial supervision, and only their operational budgets need to be approved by the Parliament. Their operations are related to various issues such as health, education, housing, transport and recreational issues and some of the agencies manage significant levels of state assets. The local government The local government in Malta consists of 68 local councils, 6 regional councils and the Local Councils Association. The local councils perform specific functions like waste collection, local enforcement system, street cleaning, maintenance of public gardens, etc. They are mostly financed by grants, the latter created by appropriations from the Government. They have, though, a certain degree of autonomy with respect to own expenditures financed by their own local revenue. The group of local councils is deemed as an independent sub-sector of the General Government although their functions in Malta are rather limited and their finances are mainly grants from the Government. 2.4.1 Valuation The flows and stocks are valued at market prices. The aggregate taxes on fossil fuels is valued in US$. 2.4.2 Recording basis Budgetary central government accounts are recorded in the government’s accounting system, the Corporate Financial Management System (CFMS). This system is a comprehensive accruals-based system and its implementation started from 2020. Until date this system has been used to extract cash data only. The Treasury Department continues to gather accrual-based data from ministries and departments through their quarterly accruals returns. Taxes on fossil fuels are recorded on an accruals basis. The EBUs and local council’s accounts are on accrual basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Government finance statistics are compiled on a gross basis in line with the ESA 2010. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The main sources used for the compilation of the annual general government finance statistics are: For budgetary central government: CFMS, a database which includes all the transactions of Central Government and is available in real time. Quarterly accruals returns of government ministries and departments, carried out by the Treasury (to cover accruals adjustments). Financial Balance Sheet, which comprises the stocks, transactions, revaluations and other flows compiled by the Treasury. For the Extra Budgetary units: Financial Statements; where available these are the main source of data. These annual statements are audited. Questionnaires from the Financial Data Reporting System (FDRS). The FDRS was launched by the Ministry of Finance in January 2011 and is geared towards attaining real time financial data pertaining to government entities. EBUs are required to compile: a monthly template (profit and loss account) and quarterly balance sheet with counterpart data within 22 calendar days following the end of the respective period. The Public Finance Unit within the National Statistics Office has online access to this system. For the Local government: The data covering current year ‘n’ are based on estimates which, in turn, are worked out on the actual outturn as reported in the audited financial statements up to year n-1. The year ‘n’ figures are then adjusted based on the total value of subvention transferred (to the Local Councils) from the consolidated fund of central government. The sources for taxes on fossil fuels are: the Government’s accounting system CFMS (for the cash data) and the Customs Department accruals templates which are provided to the Treasury Department on a quarterly basis. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The CFMS data are on cash basis; hence the Treasury has to collect data on accruals from the ministries/department by means of a questionnaire. In this questionnaire data on debtors, creditors, accrued income and expenditure, deferred income and pre-payments are collected. Subsequently, the cash data are adjusted to be accrual. The chart of accounts used by the budgetary central government, classifies the government’s revenues and expenses according to the classification prescribed in the ESA 2010 and GFSM 2014. The chart of accounts is also classified according to the 1-digit COFOG classification. Overall, the source data follow the standard definitions, scope, classification and valuation. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Source data are provided with sufficient timeliness and periodicity from all reporting units of the general government. The main source data, the CFMS, is made available online to authorised persons on the second week after the end of each month, except for the last month of the fiscal year (December), which is available around end-February of the following year. Treasury accruals templates data are available starting from 30 days after the end of the reporting quarter. Same timeliness apply for the taxes on fossil fuel aggregate. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The final source data are based on audited accounts of all general government sub-sectors. However, payments made by one level of government to another level of government are checked against the amount received by the receiving level of government. Preliminary data obtained from the EBUs and local councils are assessed against data obtained from the budgetary central government accounting system, for example transfers made by the budgetary central government to each EBU and local authority. Preliminary EBUs and local council data are also assessed against the audited annual data, and changes from quarter to quarter are monitored. Any queries or the need for clarification are followed-up directly with the reporting agency. The changes in financing data are regularly reconciled with the changes in government debt. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Data for budgetary central government, the EBUs, and local councils are collected from the accounting records and do not require the use of statistical techniques 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The use of other statistical procedures is rarely necessary. However, NSO makes adjustments to convert the cash data for the budgetary central government to accruals, using the Treasury templates. In line with Council Regulation 2516/2000, the main taxes and social contributions are recorded using the time adjusted method (T+1month for VAT and T+2months for the income tax and social contributions) In the case where the NSO does not receive a 100 percent response from the EBUs and local councils, adjustments are made to the data based on the transfer from the central government to the EBUs/local council 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The final GFS data that are compiled using the audited accounts of all levels of government and are based on a full coverage of the general government sector in Malta. Before the submission of these data to Eurostat, European Central Bank (ECB) or IMF all tables are checked for internal consistency.. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Because of the nature of the GFS and the full coverage of the general government sector, no specific studies or analyses of routine revisions are necessary. However, if major revisions are undertaken, their results (analyses and descriptions) are made public in the accompanying News Release. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Detailed analytical presentations non-confidential and not routinely disseminated in the news release are made available to users on request. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs NSO news releases and publications are fully downloadable from the NSO’s website: www.nso.gov.mt . Debt data is also available on the Central Bank of Malta’s website: www.centralbankmalta.org .
