 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Statistics are consistent with the European System of National and Regional Accounts ( ESA 2010 ) regarding definitions, accounting rules and classifications ( Regulation (EU) No. 549/2013 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 21 May 2013. Definition : GDP measures the market value of the goods and services produced in Belgium (output minus intermediate consumption) before deduction of consumption of fixed capital. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification systems: the classifications used are those recommended in ESA2010. For industries, classification NACE Rev.2. is used. Scope of the data The scope is consistent with internationally accepted standards, guidelines, or good practices. The scope is consistent with ESA 2010. Transaction coverage: The production boundary is in accordance with the System of National Accounts 2008 (2008 SNA ). Geographic coverage : nationwide. Exceptions to coverage Nihil. Unrecorded activity The Belgian quarterly accounts cover all transactions included in the estimation of the annual GDP, without any exclusion. Black market activities, illegal activities (drugs, prostitution, and smuggling) are also included in the annual accounts. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: market prices. Output of government and non-profit institutions serving households (NPISHs) are recorded at cost; value of gross fixed capital formation includes installation costs. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: Government income and expenditure are recorded on an accrual basis. Imports are recorded at the time goods are released by customs. Exports are recorded when goods are loaded at ports, except for exports of some large goods (e.g., aircraft), which are recorded at the time of change of ownership (change of ownership in such cases is mostly according to progress of work). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The compilation of the annual national accounts in Belgium makes use of many information sources, both administrative data and surveys. The national accounts are compiled based on an exhaustive business register. All in all, a whole range of consistent and exhaustive statistical information are produced to get an accurate a picture as possible of the state of Belgium's economy. Compilation of the national accounts relies on a methodology defined at European level, in cooperation with the Member States. This guarantees high quality standards, backed up by the fact that the accounts drawn up in each country are monitored by Eurostat. Quarterly national accounts have a more limited coverage than annual accounts. Quarterly national accounts are based on a specific compilation method, using quarterly indicators, and econometric methods. To the extent possible, the choice of the quarterly indicators is closely aligned to the data sources and methodology used for the annual accounts. Production approach : Value added is estimated for some forty industries, but these are aggregated into eleven main branches for publication. The indicators used are as follows: VAT records for agriculture, industry and most market services; Prodcom list for industry; Quarterly balance sheet and survey information for financial services; Monthly budget statements for general government; and Producer and consumer price indices are used to derive volume estimates. Expenditure Approach : Household final consumption expenditure : VAT turnover for retail trade, hotels, restaurant and other personal services, number of vehicle registrations, credit card data and balance of payments data are used; consumer price indices are used for deflation. Final consumption expenditure of NPISHs : There is no quarterly information available about the final consumption expenditure of non-profit institutions serving households (NPISHs). The estimation is obtained by interpolating and extrapolating the yearly figures by a trend. Final consumption expenditure of general government : The estimates for central government are based on the monthly budget statement (new ESA2010 framework statistics). The quarterly final consumption expenditure of local government is interpolated starting from a yearly extrapolation. Gross fixed capital formation : Gross fixed capital formation of companies is based on VAT investments data. A weighted price index is used for deflation. Capital formation on housing is based on statistics on housing starts and building permits. A weighted price index is used for deflation. Gross fixed capital formation for public administration comes from the monthly budget statement. A weighted price index is used for deflation. Changes in inventories : Derived as a residual by subtracting the sum of the other expenditure components from total GDP as measured from the production approach. Exports and imports of goods and services : The balance of payments data are used. A weighted price index is used for deflation. The timeliness for most data sources is shorter than the requirements for the transmission of results to Eurostat and ECB and for the publication deadlines. When this is not the case, reasonable estimates can be made. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques General estimation method: as the main sources for annual data are not available in time to allow the annual methodology to be transposed to the quarterly level, the method used for the Belgian quarterly accounts is indirect, i.e., the absence of quarterly data necessitates recourse to a "proxy" variable. Each statistical series of the annual accounts is coupled with a quarterly series that is available by the time the accounts are compiled and whose behavior is similar to that of the accounting item. This series is called the "quarterly indicator". The quarterly indicator generally differs in annual value from the accounting item for reasons of definition and scope. It is therefore necessary to "calibrate" the quarterly indicator to the annual accounting figure. This operation consists ofsearching for the econometric adjustment which best explains the movement of the annual accounts based on the annual value of the indicator in the past. The statistical link arrived at by this regression analysis, on an annual basis, between the accounting item and its indicator makes it possible to estimate the account in the current quarters based on the quarterly value of the indicator. The econometric model (Denton) used is based on a model that, by its construction, ensures perfect coherence with the annual series and provides optimal extrapolations for the current year. Therefore, the quarterly accounts have the same coverage and valuation quality as the yearly accounts, both in the production and in the expenditure approach. For the yearly accounts, the main sources used are: Production approach : The production side of the annual accounts is based on administrative data (annual business accounts, VAT-files, etc.) and surveys (annual structural business survey). Specific assumptions are made by industry on informal/hidden activities. The exhaustiveness and the coherence of the estimations are ensured by means of the supply and use table. Expenditure approach : Household final consumption expenditure : A continuous household's budget survey. Final consumption expenditure of NPISHs : NPISHs' business accounts for large units and a yearly specific survey on NPISHs. Final consumption expenditure of general government : The budget statements. Gross fixed capital formation : Exhaustive administrative data (VAT investments data and yearly accounts of enterprises). Changes in inventories : Yearly accounts of enterprises. Exports and imports of goods and services : The balance of payments data. The consistency of the estimations is ensured by means of the supply and use table. Current and volume measures: volume measures are calculated using the prices of the previous year. Two means are used for obtaining volume measures in the Belgian quarterly accounts, depending on the nature of the quarterly indicator. First estimation in volume , then in value: When the quarterly indicator is a volume indicator (for instance, industrial production indices for manufacturing industry), the method of estimation described before is applied in volume; the results are converted in value measures using the adequate price indices. First estimation in value , then in volume: When the quarterly indicator is a value indicator (for instance, VAT data), the method of estimation previously described is applied at current prices; the results are converted in volume measures using the adequate price indices. Revision procedures : The quarterly estimates can be revised for three reasons: a change in the annual accounts, a correction of the basic data, and/or the seasonal adjustment. The most recent quarters, belonging to a year for which no estimate according to the annual accounts has yet been published, may be revised at the end of each quarter. The previous quarters are revised only once a year, in the first release following the period when the annual national accounts are published. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Reconciliation of the production and expenditure approach : The changes in inventories are obtained as a residual—the discrepancy between the GDP estimated from the production side and the sum of the main components of the expenditure (final consumption expenditure, gross fixed capital formation and net exports) is included in the changes in inventories. Adjustment for seasonal and calendar effects : The method employed in Belgium is TRAMO-SEATS . Flash estimate : A flash estimate of the real GDP growth is already published after one month. This estimate is obtained following the same structures, methods and concepts as the quarterly national accounts. With respect to the other economic indicators, the GDP flash estimate shows therefore the advantage of relying on a global and coherent picture of the whole economy. Compared to the existing quarterly accounts, the only difference lies in the partial nature of the available basic data (one month against three). 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Contact points are published. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs Catalogs and calendars of publication, requirements for source data, etc. are available on the website of the National Bank of Belgium .
