 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The balance of payments statistics provides information of economic transactions between residents and non-residents in Iceland during each quarter and year. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification system: All classification is in line with the Balance of Payments and International investment position manual, Sixth edition (BPM6). Scope of the data Transactions coverage: The Icelandic balance of payments covers all transactions conducted between residents and non-residents. Geographic coverage: No inclusion/exclusion of territories/regions of the resident/non-residents entities. Exceptions to coverage Transactions coverage: Some transactions may be undercovered due to existing sources and methods. Unrecorded activity 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: The valuation is assumed to be at market prices for all current transactions and special instructions are given to reporters of direct investment and portfolio investment flows. 2.4.2 Recording basis Conversion to unit of account: The BOP is compiled in millions of Icelandic kronur (ISK); transactions are converted from other currencies to ISK using the exchange rate prevailing at the time of the transaction or average exchange rate for the reporting period. Some financial flows are derived from changes in position of assets and liabilities, in which case, the data is adjusted for changes in the exchange rate and other price chages. Recording of income: Interest income and expenses are reported on accrual basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The compilation of the balance of payments data is based on trade in goods and services statistics, reports on foreign transactions and mandatory surveys of official and private entities in all related sectors. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Goods: Merchandise exports and imports are compiled on f.o.b. basis according to foreign trade statistics reported by the Statistics Iceland (SI). The data is derived from the Customs Authority, who record physical movements of goods across Icelandic boundaries. For the purpose of compiling balance of payments, however, the data is adjusted to a balance of payments basis (change of ownership). More detailed metadata can be obtained from SI website www.statice.is Services: External trade in services is reported quarterly by Statistics Iceland (SI). The compilation is based on surveying. All companies that are defined as large enterprises in the sampling frame, about 100, are surveyed quarterly on their trade in services transactions with non-residents in the current year. A sample of about 700 small and medium sized enterprises is taken from the population of about 4500 small and medium sized enterprises. Small and medium sized enterprises are surveyed their trade in services transactions with non-residents during last year and the information is used to estimate their activities in the current year. More detailed metadata can be obtained from SI website www.statice.is Primary Income: Compensation of employees is compiled based on data from the Icelandic Tax Authorities records. Investment income is collected from banks and other direct reporting entities or estimated from international investment position by applying reference market rates. Secondary Income: The Treasury provides data on general government current transfers. Data for other sectors is sourced from reporting entities in the private and public sector. Capital account: Based on the same data as Secondary Income. Direct investment: Majority of FDI companies and foreign affiliates in Iceland report directly to the CBI. The largest companies report quarterly and most other annually. For those not covered by direct reporting the CBI relies on balance sheet data acquired from the Tax Authorities and The Icelandic Company Registry. Portfolio investment: Domestic securities, held by non-residents: CBI receives daily data from The Icelandic Securities Depository on the stock of all securities issued in Iceland and held by non-residents. Foreign securities, held by residents: Data on foreign securities is collected monthly from all financial companies and large investors. Flows for other investors are estimated on the basis of data collected from local custodians. Other investment: Other financial transactions are either derived from data collected from banks and other direct reporting entities or estimated from bank reports on foreign exchange transactions. New borrowing and repayments of long-term debt is calculated at monthly average exchange rate, but other transactions are estimated from changes in the position of foreign assets and liabilities. Reserve assets: Data is sourced from monthly balance sheet of the CBI. It includes stocks, transactions as well as revaluations due to exchange rate and price changes. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Goods: No adjustments are made to prevailing data sources. Services: No adjustments are made to prevailing data sources. Dissemination of first data may be partly estimated due to late response by direct reporting entities. Primary Income: Preliminary quarterly data is superseded by more complete annual sources. Secondary Income: No adjustments are made to prevailing data sources. Capital account: No adjustments are made to prevailing data sources. Direct investment: Preliminary quarterly data is superseded by more complete annual sources. Portfolio investment: No adjustments are made to prevailing data sources. Other investment: Preliminary quarterly data is superseded by more complete annual sources. Reserve assets: No adjustments are made to prevailing data sources. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Seasonal adjustment: No seasonal adjustments are made to BOP data. Geographic detail: Statistics Iceland publishes geograpical breakdown of goods (imports on c.i.f. basis) and services. More details can be obtained from SI website http://www.statice.is/ . 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses The Central bank of Iceland conducts regular revision studies on its externals sector statistics. The studies are mostly for internal use for the purpose of monitoring quality. Some external studies have been published externally, for example in the semi-annual publication Balance of Payments, External Position, and Vulnerabilities (Box 3).
