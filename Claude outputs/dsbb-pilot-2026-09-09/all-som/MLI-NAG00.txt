 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The economic accounts of Mali are prepared by the National Directorate of Statistics and Data Processing ( DNSI ), in accordance with the System of National Accounts 1968 (SNA68) and adapted to certain characteristics specific to Mali. They are compiled on an annual basis and presented as a series of tables, namely: Distribution of and changes in GDP by branch of economic activity at current prices and at constant (prior-year) prices. Table of aggregate supply-use balances: On the supply side, it gives the value of production of goods and services, imports and duties and taxes on imports and, on the uses side, it gives intermediate and final consumption, gross fixed capital formation (GFCF), changes in stocks, and exports. This table is also presented at current and constant prices. National income and income allocation table: Contains balances of income from property, other current transfers and consumption of fixed capital. It also presents private final consumption and public final consumption. Capital accumulation table: Shows GFCF, net purchases of intangible assets, gross accumulation, gross saving, net inward transfers of capital, and borrowing requirements. Foreign operations account: Reflects foreign transactions, the different transfers, and net lending to the rest of the world. Non-market service production account by budget. Production table by branch of economic activity, for both the modern sector and for the traditional sector. Household final consumption and GFCF by product (essential goods). Input-output table using 1987 as the base year. Gross domestic product (GDP) is estimated annually as the sum of the value added of the various branches of economic activity, plus the duties and taxes charged on imports (production approach). It is also estimated as the sum of final expenditure (demand approach): final consumption, gross fixed capital formation, changes in stocks, balance of trade in goods and services. The accounts of the institutional sectors—such as non-financial institutions, households, financial institutions, public administrations, and private administrations—are not compiled, nor reflected in the economic accounts of Mali. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification system used was defined on the basis of the reality of Mali's situation and its economic priorities. This classification covers 28 branches of economic activity, which are different from the UN ISIC classification. It is useful for examining the fundamentals of the national economy and for monitoring structural changes in branches of economic activity. It should be noted that some branches, particularly in the rural economy, comprise a single product. This breakdown is related to the importance of certain subsectors—e.g., rice, cotton, and textiles—, which contribute a relatively large share of GDP in Mali. Scope of the data Transaction Coverage: All transactions by the units listed above fall within the scope of the data. Geographic Coverage: All transactions by the units listed above fall within the scope of the data. Unit Coverage: The economic accounts of Mali cover all resident units. For all branches of economic activity, production is divided into the modern and traditional sectors. The informal sector may be assimilated with the traditional sector, except in some cases for which adjustments are made (e.g., when an agricultural sector is considered "modern"). GDP covers all production activities. Informal production is estimated, but illegal production is not taken into account. GDP is published at current prices and at constant 1987 prices. GDP is estimated annually as the sum of the value added of the various industries, plus the duties and taxes charged on imports (production approach). It is also estimated as the sum of final expenditure: final consumption, gross fixed capital formation, changes in stocks, balance of trade in goods and services (expenditure approach). Unrecorded activity At present, illegal activities are not recorded in the national accounts. Furthermore, imputed rents are not included in the calculation of GDP. Similarly, fixed capital consumption by government is not taken into account in the calculation of government production or of final consumption. 2.4.1 Valuation The production of enterprises is valued at production cost and intermediate consumption is valued at acquisition cost. Domestic transactions are valued at market price. Imports are valued c.i.f. (including cost, insurance, and freight). Exports are declared f.o.b. (free-on-board). 2.4.2 Recording basis In general, transactions are recorded on an accrual basis. However, the data sourced from the budget on government income and expenditure are recorded on a cash basis or on a payments basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Base year data were compiled using the 1989 budget-consumption survey ( EBC 1989), the 1989 informal sector survey ( ESI ), and the 1987 general population and housing census ( RGPH ). Production Approach: Agriculture: For this sector, estimates are based on the results of the economic survey of agriculture, conducted jointly by the DNSI and DNAMR (National Directorate for Support to Rural Areas) since 1986-87, with the introduction of a segment on "grain harvest forecasts. This survey gives the sown areas, yield, output, loss rates, value added, subsistence and commercial crops planted for a number of undertakings (millet, sorghum, paddy rice, acha, cowpea, potato, yam, cassava, other tubers, bambara beans, vegetables). Changes in other agricultural output not cited are assumed to be the same as that for known production. Producer prices for agricultural products are provided by the market information system. Prices for cotton fiber are sourced from the BCEAO balance of payments and tobacco prices are taken from the Upper Niger Valley Operation ( OHVN ). The results of the harvest for year n are considered production of year n +1, based on the principle that, in Mali, production of the major grains, cotton, and groundnuts takes place between December of year n and February of year n +1. It is thought that farmers are only able to realize the value of their harvest output in year n +1. Livestock Rearing, Fishing, and Food-gathering from the Forest: The statistics for these branches are provided by the technical units of the Ministry of Rural Development (National Directorate of Livestock, Malian Livestock and Meat Office, National Directorate of Water and Forests) in the form of tables of production, by products, intermediate consumption, and plant and equipment. Data on livestock refer to numbers, rate of increase, deaths at a young age, adult deaths, and use of cattle, sheep, and goats, as well as other animals and poultry. Producer prices are estimated by the DNSI . The producer prices of fishing products are estimates at market price, net of margins. Production of firewood and charcoal is calculated by extrapolations of the results of the consumption budget survey of 1989 on the value of products, to which home consumption is added, using as a base the rural population and half of the urban population. Use of wood for household purposes and for food gathering is projected on the basis of the total population. Mining Output: The main production items of this sector are gold and phosphates. Gold production is provided by the National Directorate of Geology and Mines ( DNGM ) and gold mining companies. Phosphate production is determined by the National Directorate of Industry ( DNI ); however, this production has been discontinued. Agrofood, Textiles, Other Manufacturing Industries: The source of production data for the modern sector is the industrial census conducted by the National Directorate of Industry in 1989. These data are updated on an annual basis using industrial production indices, obtained on a quarterly basis from a sample survey of certain industrial units by the DNSI . A quarterly industrial production index is calculated for the companies in the sample, which reflects total production and other items of the corporate production account. Production of the informal sector is obtained by extrapolation of the results of the 1989 informal sector survey. Electricity, Water: Data on this sector are obtained from information on intermediate production and consumption by the Mali Energy Company. Construction and Public Works (BTP): Production is estimated on the basis of statistics of locally manufactured construction material culled from the industrial production survey, and of imported materials derived from the foreign trade statistics. Trade: The production of this sector comprises the margins obtained in marketing local and imported products. Transportation and Telecommunications: The data are provided by the National Transportation Directorate and the telecommunications services. Other Non-financial Market Services: Their output is the sum of production of household services, services to enterprises and domestic services to households. The production of services provided to households is valued on the basis of the consumption budget survey extrapolated to all monetized household consumption. The production of services provided to enterprises is the sum of intermediate consumption of services by enterprises, calculated on the basis of the input-output table (IOT). The volume of domestic services production is projected on the basis of population growth, and the price is projected by the general consumer price index. Financial Services: The output of this sector is the sum of the production of banks and insurance companies, obtained from their balance sheets and accounting records and by using the report of the Treasury and Government Accounting unit responsible for monitoring such production. Bank service charges (imputed production) are equal to the difference between interest earned and interest paid. The consolidated value of bank service charges does not include the central bank. Bank service charges are imputed to the production account, but are deducted from national value added. The production of insurance companies is also the difference between premia received and compensation paid out, less premia net of insurance against losses paid to households; these are classified as transfers. Non-market Services : Their production is valued as the sum of the costs comprising non-market services (intermediate consumption, personnel expenditure, indirect taxes net of consumption of fixed capital). Generally, personnel expenditure is included in value added, disregarding government fixed capital consumption. Intermediate consumption covers expenditure on materials, transportation, and miscellaneous expenses. Import Duties and Taxes: These data are provided by the Directorate General of Customs. Demand Approach: Final consumption: Household final consumption is calculated as the balance of the supply-use table (SUT), product-by-product. For certain products, it is calculated directly by extrapolating the volume and value of the results of the consumption budget survey ( EBC ). EBC rates are applied to determine home consumption by product. Home consumption of market services corresponds to real property services imputed to households. Government final consumption is equal to non-market production, by definition. It covers local government consumption and foreign aid received by Mali. Gross fixed capital formation (GFCF): Gross fixed capital formation comprises construction and public works, plant and equipment, and increases in the herds of breeding animals. The construction and public works component is obtained as a balance from production and maintenance. Costs attributed to margins, customs tariffs, and transportation costs are calculated at rates based on the c.i.f. value of imports of capital goods. A small portion of capital goods is produced locally. Changes in stocks: These comprise the supply-use balance for exports (cotton, gold). Only changes in stocks of livestock products are directly calculated. Major changes in harvests of basic subsistence goods are divided between household final consumption and stock building. Based on a normal production growth of 6 percent, the discrepancies observed in actual production are classified as positive or negative stocks, if the relative value is higher than 3 percent. Imports and Exports: Imports and exports of goods are drawn from the foreign trade statistics, which are based on customs statistics validated by a validation committee representative of a number of economic units. Imports and exports of services are derived from the BCEAO balance of payments. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Production Approach: The accounting data of modern sector enterprises and the activity reports of the utilities are supplemented by estimates for the areas where data are lacking, using the results of the EBC and the 1989 informal sector survey. Production at current prices is obtained directly from corporate accounting data or by valuing the quantities produced at producer prices. Production at constant 1987 prices is estimated by projecting prior year data using a volume index. Also used are the industrial production index for manufacturing industries, the composite index of construction materials for construction and public works, the population growth rate for trade. A civil service wage rate index is used for wages and salaries. None
