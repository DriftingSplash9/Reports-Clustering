 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The concepts and definitions of Saudi Arabia's external merchandise trade statistics are in harmony with the United Nation's International Merchandise Trade Statistics Concepts and Definitions, Revision 2 . 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization An 8-digit classification derived from the Harmonized Trade System is used for collecting and compiling the statistics of imports and exports. The classification was adopted since 1410 H (1990). The current statistical nomenclature contains 21 sections of 8-digit commodity subdivisions: the first six digits are from the Customs Cooperation Council and the 8th digit is adjusted to the characteristics of the external trade of Saudi Arabia. Scope of the data Exports include all goods produced or processed in the country and passing through the customs of the country to the rest of the world, and imports are goods coming into the country through customs by land, sea or air. The exports include re-exports. Exceptions to coverage The following are the main items excluded from the coverage of the statistics: hand luggage of travelers, notes, goods in-transit etc. 2.4.1 Valuation The values of imports and exports are converted into Saudi Riyals at the current exchange rate provided by the Saudi Arabian Monetary Authority (SAMA). Imports are valued on a c.i.f. basis, while exports (domestic exports and re-exports) are valued on an f.o.b. basis. Quantities of imports or exports are expressed in kilograms (kg) and units. 2.4.2 Recording basis Zakat, Tax, Customs Authority (ZATCA) is responsible for ensuring the authenticity of the customs declarations from which the GASTAT derives the imports and exports data. The GASTAT, as well, audits and reviews the data in several stages and it applies amendments as required in coordination with the Customs Department. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The imports and exports statistics are compiled from the import/export declarations submitted by importers/exporters to Zakat, Tax, Customs Authority (ZATCA), and from the mining data provided by the Ministry of Energy. The declarations include the following information: commodity code and description, quantity, value, partner country (origin, final destination), port of loading or unloading, mode of transport. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The source data (Customs declarations) have sufficient detail to compile the data with an 12-digit classification (based on the Harmonized System). 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The customs data are sent to GASTAT by Zakat, Tax, Customs Authority (ZATCA) through a sharing link on a monthly basis, within two weeks after the end of the reference month. The data are reviewed and may be sent for clarifications and corrections. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The GASTAT reviews and analyses the data obtained from Zakat, Tax, Customs Authority (ZATCA), comments on them, highlights outliers/errors, and sends them forthwith to the ZATCA for further verification and adjustment, as required by the concerned staff and in line with programs designed for this purpose. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The accuracy of the source data is checked by an Oracle-based computer assisted editing program that validate the classification (against a list of acceptable classifications), value and weight and trading partner. The data are further reviewed by the analysts and specialists of the department in multiple stages. Errors are corrected where they occur. The detailed (8-digit) classification of the trade statistics, by SITC, by commodity, groups, divisions and section, country, etc.& provide a useful framework for consistency checks. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request The GASTAT publish all merchandise trade data. In addition, GASTAT may provide the trade statistics upon specific request depending on the level of detail and type of requested data for all data, on commodity level whether for a year and a series of years.
