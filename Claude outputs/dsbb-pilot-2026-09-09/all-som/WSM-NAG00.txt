 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Nominal and real GDP are compiled and disseminated in millions of tala by the SBS using the industry value added approach (production approach), and the expenditure approach, the latter on an experimental basis. The methodology used is in compliance with the SNA 93 and the base year is 2009. National accounts estimates using the production approach are compiled on the basis of the estimated value added of individual industries classified according to the ISIC rev. 3.1 categories. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification of economic activities: The Samoa classification of production by type of activity is based on the United Nations International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC Rev. 3.1), with some adaptation to reflect the specific aspects of the Samoan economy; for example, the mining sector, which is very small, is included in the manufacturing sector rather than being shown separately. At the most aggregated level, activities are; Agriculture, Fishing, Food, Other Manufacturing, Construction, Electricity and Water, Commerce, Hotels and Restaurants, Transportation, Communication, Public Administration, Financial Services, Business Services, Ownership of dwellings and Personal and Other Services. The Classification of Individual Consumption According to Purpose(COICOP) is used for estimating Final Consumption expenditure in the context of the experimental calculation of GDP by the expenditure approach 2.2.1 Scope Classification of economic activities: The Samoa classification of production by type of activity is based on the United Nations International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC Rev. 3.1), with some adaptation to reflect the specific aspects of the Samoan economy; for example, the mining sector, which is very small, is included in the manufacturing sector rather than being shown separately. At the most aggregated level, activities are; Agriculture, Fishing, Food, Other Manufacturing, Construction, Electricity and Water, Commerce, Hotels and Restaurants, Transportation, Communication, Public Administration, Financial Services, Business Services, Ownership of dwellings and Personal and Other Services. The Classification of Individual Consumption According to Purpose(COICOP) is used for estimating Final Consumption expenditure in the context of the experimental calculation of GDP by the expenditure approach Scope of the data The national accounts data cover the entire resident economic territory of Samoa. This includes all private enterprises, the government sector, subsistence production and owner occupied dwellings. The main institutional unit in the formal private sector is the enterprise, which reports its turnover to the tax authorities for Value-added and Goods and Services Tax (VAGST) purposes. Subsistence or informal activities are estimated on the basis of surveys like the Household Income and Expenditure and Agriculture surveys and Population Census sources to which population trends are applied. Data on government activities are directly extracted from the Government Finance Statistics. The national accounts cover the production and use of income (expenditure) accounts only. Other accounts of the national accounts sequence of accounts are still experimental at an aggregated level; they include generation of income accounts, the distribution of income accounts and the financial accounts. Unrecorded activity Informal activities are estimated. Illegal activities, including contraband, are considered negligible and are therefore not estimated. 2.4.1 Valuation The value added of economic activities, the final expenditures categories and the corresponding GDP are valued at purchaser’s prices including indirect taxes (mostly the VAGST) less subsidies). For subsistence production, valuation is at farm gate prices. Data are produced at current and constant (2009) prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis Most transactions are recorded on a cash basis due to the nature of the data sources (VAGST and government accounts). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Benchmark data are provided from large scale surveys like the Business Activity Survey, the Household Income and Expenditure survey and the Agriculture and Population Census that are being conducted periodically, every 5 years and every 10 years. Moving indicators are used to extrapolate benchmark data to get a good picture of Samoa’s economy on a quarterly basis; these are mostly based on administrative records, complemented by other economic indicators. The VAGST system of recording constitutes the major data source as it provides a very good indicator of movements from which benchmarks from the 1994, 1999 and 2009 Business Activity Survey can be extrapolated. Various changes such as the introduction of threshold exemptions for small business are taken into account. Other major data sources include: the weekly Market survey and manufacturing production indicators, which used to be responsibilities of the Central Bank of Samoa and are now responsibilities of the SBS; government accounts; merchandise trade data like import and export data; National Provident Fund (NPF) data for employment and wages by industry; and the Consumer Price Index compiled by the SBS’ Economic Statistics Division. All of these data sources are supplemented by a range of detailed data compiled for specific purposes. Most of the data used in the Samoan system of national accounts are by-products of existing systems developed and maintained primarily for other purposes. By integrating the various data through the use of common classifications, mainly the International Standard Industry Classification (ISIC), it has been possible to develop timely economic indicators for all sectors of the economy while minimizing the reporting burden on Samoan businesses. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Some adjustments are made to some of the data for under-reporting in the case of fishing and production exported by a large company. - The report on exports on consignment, mainly fish, by the Fisheries Department provides the market values actually achieved by the exporters. This report, which is available only annually, is used to adjust preliminary quarterly data. - The value of export of a large manufacturing firm to Australia is checked against Australian import values. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Data are checked and edited extensively before publication, particularly if there are unusual or unexpected movements. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses No revision studies of national accounts data are currently carried out. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses No revision studies of national accounts data are currently carried out. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Additional statistical information on national accounts statistics can be provided on request.
