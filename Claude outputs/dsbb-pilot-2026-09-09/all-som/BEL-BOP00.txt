 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The balance of payments reflects all transactions - real and financial - during a given period, between Belgian residents and the rest of the world. The methodology is described in various documents available on the website of the NBB . The overall structure of concepts and definitions follows internationally accepted standards, guidelines, or good practices. The methodology is in line with the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6).In the area of FDI, when relevant, they are also in line with the OECD Benchmark definition of Foreign direct investment (4th edition). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification/sectorization systems used are fully consistent with internationally accepted standards and guidelines (the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual ( BPM6 ) and ECB Guideline on the statistical reporting requirements of the European Central Bank in the field of external statistics). Scope of the data Transaction coverage : the collection of source data is based on a combination of different surveys conducted by the National Bank of Belgium covering trade in goods, services, direct investment, other investment and portfolio investment. The data provided by these surveys are supplemented by international trade in goods statistics and data from a variety of external sources. Deposit taking corporations report their foreign payments for their own account. Geographic coverage : Belgium vis-à-vis the rest of the world. Exceptions to coverage Households' holdings which are kept in custody outside the euro area are not covered in the BOP. The same applies for some smaller non-financial and other financial corporations which are not a direct reporter and of which the holdings are kept in custody outside the euro area. Financial derivatives for the households' sector are not included but are considered to be negligible. Unrecorded activity Direct investment income: accrued interest is estimated in the income account but not included in the financial account. Accrued interest is not included in the reported investment positions of debt securities. In order to reach consistency with BPM6 and SDDS Plus Guide, an IT project on the securities database has started to include the accrued interest. The first results are planned to be ready in 2026. Registration of financial lease: presently no registration is made in the BOP financial account for the transactions related to the lump sum of a financial lease contract representative of the underlying capital of the contract; only a proxy is computed based on the registered flows related to financial leasing. Long term constructions and mobile equipment operated in another economy: no registration is made in the FDI components for the value of long-term construction contracts and for mobile equipment operated in another economy for more than 1 year. However, it is assumed that significant contracts lead to the creation of an affiliate and thus are in fact registered as direct investment. Reverse investment and equity links between fellows in the context of direct investment are not recorded appropriately and in a consistent way for stocks and flows. The impact of including reverse investments and equity links between fellow enterprises is only relevant for certain companies. We will therefore only request this specific equity information for these companies. As this requires some changes to our enterprise surveys and IT tools, this data will only be available from 2029/2030 (along with other changes in light of BPM7). 2.4.1 Valuation The system is based on the communication of actual transactions (which mostly corresponds to market prices). 2.4.2 Recording basis The conversion of the original currencies into euros is based on the monthly average exchange rate. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Grossing-up techniques are marginally used in portfolio investment from the reference date of end 2008 onwards, in the scope of security-by-security reporting (see 3.1.1). They allow to extrapolate from the annual frequency to the quarterly frequency if need be. Grossing-up techniques are used since 2009 to compute the trade credits component, as a result of the use of a monthly sample survey on trade credits (assets and liabilities). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The collection system is based on a combination of different surveys (financial and non-financial sectors), and on external sources. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data reasonably approximate the definitions, scope, sectorization, classifications, valuation, and time of recording required. Specific components : Goods : the data collection system is based on the international trade in goods data collection. These data are supplemented with ad hoc surveys on specific transactions based on a cut-off approach or a sampling approach depending on the topic covered. Services : the services account is based on different surveys. The system is a mixture of cut-off and sampling techniques for the non-financial sector. The system is made up of two main subsystems: (i) a full survey of the major enterprises (monthly or quarterly); and (ii) a set of specific surveys for the other enterprises (monthly or quarterly). For the financial sector, different surveys are defined, each covering a specific subsector (e.g., MFIs, insurance companies, collective investment institutions, etc.). The surveys are addressed to the full population of the sub-sectors. Travel : from 2015 onwards, travel services are based on (to a greater extent as before) payment card information. Primary and secondary income : administrative sources & surveys are used; these are supplemented with data from ad hoc external sources and estimates. Investment income and financial account : the collection system is sub-divided according to: the domain covered (functional classification) and the group of respondents concerned (mostly sub-sectors). As a result, the surveys are broken down into three main categories: direct investment other investment (including derivatives) and portfolio investment and securities. The first two categories of surveys use cut-off techniques and cover both flows (gross transactions) and positions in financial assets and liabilities broken down by instrument, currency, and country. For direct investment, the related income is also covered by the respective surveys (dividends declared, dividends paid and received, interest accrued, interest paid and received, etc.), as well as profit and loss accounts. The third category of survey gathers data related to securities. It covers the holdings, issues, and short positions of residents, as well as the custodian activities of residents. The data only concern positions and are reported on a security-by-security basis with ISIN codes (or some other agreed identification codes). The flows are derived from the reported positions, as well as the valuation changes due to fluctuations in prices and exchange rates. Concerning the MFI sector, the data related to other investment and direct investment are provided by the data collection system defined for money and banking statistics. For BOP purposes a yearly detailed stock survey is sent directly to the MFIs for foreign direct investment, while the survey on securities is organized monthly. The frequency of the different surveys can be monthly, quarterly or yearly depending on the activity and relative importance of the enterprise (group). Trade credits (positions): a monthly survey is addressed to a sample of enterprises. Other sources : besides organizing its own surveys, the National Bank of Belgium is authorized to gather all necessary information from any public agency if the information available fulfils its needs. The National Bank is also entitled to agree upon a similar process with any representative association, thus avoiding enterprises must report the same data twice. Administrative sources are also used, e.g., the social security agency provides data on labour income. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Flows: most of the data must be reported at the latest 20 days after the month concerned. Portfolio investment: data must be reported within 11 working days (monthly services) or within 25 calendar days (quarterly and annual surveys). The timeliness of most data sources is sufficient for the compilation and publication deadlines. When this is not the case, reasonable estimates can be made. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Data are checked at the level of the data collection tool for validation purposes, as well as at the technical level, to verify if their content meets the standards required (e.g., adequacy, completeness, etc.) and to verify their consistency. These controls consist in checking, inter alia, the accuracy of the identification number and in performing certain cross-checks (of transaction, country and currency codes). The respondents receive an electronic error report. In the monthly production process, top-down quality control is carried out. In the quarterly and yearly production cycle, bottom-up procedures are applied. Specific tools are developed to identify outliers. Similar procedures are applied to the aggregates. BOP components are cross-checked with other statistics to ensure consistency. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The aggregated data are corrected or adapted for some balance of payments items. Goods The general item "Goods" contains separate entries for general merchandise, nonmonetary gold and merchanting. This is in line with BPM6 . A CIF/FOB adjustment has been introduced from 2015 onwards. Gaseous natural gas: A BOP survey was introduced in 2013 to record the actual change of ownership of gaseous natural gas, replacing the cross-border movements of international trade in goods statistics. An estimation is made to include goods entering/leaving the territory illegally. 5. From 2015 onwards, two additional adjustments were introduced: adding merchandise trade between residents and non-residents of goods that remain in Belgium eliminating transactions in goods that are only temporarily imported or exported. Services Transportation services : Source data distinguish the means of transportation (i.e., sea transport, air transport and other transport), and provide also, within each category, a break-down into passenger transportation, freight and related services. Receipts and expenditures concerning freight transportation contain only that part of freight costs that is distinguished as such by reporting firms. Travel : On the export side, an estimation of prostitution services is added to account for non-residents visiting resident prostitutes in Belgium. The visits of Belgian residents to non-resident prostitutes abroad are assumed to be implicitly included in the credit/payment card information. Insurance services : A distinction is made between four separate items: direct insurance (life insurance, freight insurance, other direct insurance), reinsurance, auxiliary insurance services and pension and standardized guarantee services. Gross premiums received and gross claims paid are collected separately; service charges are estimated and included in insurance services, whereas the rest of the premiums and claims are recorded in secondary income or in the financial account (life insurance and pension schemes), in conformity with BPM6. Receipts and expenditures concerning freight insurance contain only that part of freight insurance that is distinguished as such by reporting firms. Financial services : -Explicitly charged financial services are declared in the services surveys. Asset management costs deducted from income are estimated by calculating the expenses of the undertakings of collective investment not covered by commissions explicitly charged to shareholders. - Financial Intermediation Services Indirectly Measured (FISIM) is calculated as the difference between the interest rates actually received on deposits and paid on loans and a reference rate. The external reference rate used for calculating FISIM's imports and exports is calculated as a weighted average of the rates on interbank claims and debts between resident and non-resident financial intermediaries. Only interbank claims and debts (and relating interest) are taken into account in calculating the reference rate. Other business services : An estimation of prostitution services is added to the import side of personal, cultural and recreational services to account for Belgian residents visiting non-resident prostitutes in Belgium Capital account Debt forgiveness : Information on debt forgiveness is directly collected from the Ministry of Finance, development cooperation institutions and the Belgian Export Credit Agency. Acquisition/disposal of non-produced, non-financial assets : The general data collection system allows to distinguish these transactions from those to be considered as patents and license fees, part of the current account, using appropriate transaction-codes. Direct investment Cut off surveys: The FDI components are provided by ad hoc surveys addressed to a limited number of enterprises selected based on different balance sheets/accountancy items for which a threshold has been defined. The selected enterprises report different surveys (FDI/Foreign Other Investment (FOI) flows by instrument, FDI/FOI stocks, by instrument, income, results, group structure, etc.) by individual counterpart (shareholder, investment enterprise). Deposit taking corporations, Insurance corporations and pension funds are surveyed exhaustively. Portfolio investment For portfolio investment, security-by-security positions are collected monthly or quarterly. A mixed approach, combining direct reporting and custodian reporting, is used. More details are available in the CPIS metadata. Financial derivatives are included as separate instruments in the surveys on other investments. The derivative instruments included in the other investments are: options futures swaps and forward contracts other derivatives. A separate reporting is set up for transaction values (premiums, gains, and losses) to be included in the BOP. The MFI sector reports on options, swaps, and forward contracts in their survey on transactions for own account. The definitions used for the instruments are in line with the BPM6 . Other investment Trade credits : The trade credits, assets as well as liabilities, are estimated by using a specific survey targeting a sample of enterprises. A distinction is made between long- and short-term trade credits. Trade credits between enterprises with a direct investment relation are included in direct investment. Loans : The data about the banks' short-term loans are derived from the assets and liabilities statements established on a monthly basis by the banks. For the monetary authorities, the government and the sector "other" separate figures are collected. Currency and deposits : The figures about the banks' deposits are calculated based on the assets and liabilities statements established monthly by the banks. For the monetary authorities, the government and the sector "others" separate figures are collected. The Bank of International Settlements statistics and balance sheets data (BSI) are used to estimate the assets and liabilities of non-resident banks vis-à-vis Belgian residents. Reserve assets : The transactions figures are derived from the accountancy records of the National Bank of Belgium. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Seasonal adjustment : None. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Automated and manual quality checks are applied to all steps of the production process, such as formal correctness, coherence with accounting data, coherence with other reported data and outlier detection. In case of wrong or implausible data, the reporting agent is contacted. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Statistical discrepancies in intermediate data are assessed and investigated. Statistical discrepancies which show up in the intermediate data are traced back to the primary source data to identify possible reasons. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The discrepancies, anomalies or other issues in statistical output are analysed and solved as much as possible. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Additional non-confidential statistics can be obtained from bop@nbb.be 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Contact information is published. A dedicated mailbox is available (bop@nbb.be). 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs Catalogs and calendars of publication, requirements for source data, etc. are available on the NBB website .
