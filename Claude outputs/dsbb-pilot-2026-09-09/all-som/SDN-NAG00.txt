 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The CBS of the Council of Ministers is responsible for the compilation of national accounts statistics in Sudan. The framework of the compilation of the national accounts is still based on the 1968 SNA. The main data produced and published on an annual basis are GDP at current prices and at constant 1981/82 prices, for ten industry groups and by expenditure categories. Also produced are national income, disposable national income, saving, and capital formation. Accounts for the institutional sectors are not produced. Private final consumption and expenditure of non-profit institutions serving households (NPISH) is calculated as residual due to the fact that the last Household Income and Expenditure Survey was last conducted in 1978/80. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Economic activities of producer units are classified according to the second revision of the International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC rev. 2). The nine major industrial activities, with sub-categories, are: Agriculture, livestock, forestry and fishing (agricultural crops: (i) irrigated, (ii) mechanized rain-fed, (iii) traditional rain-fed, (iv) minor crops, and (v) byproducts). Mining and Quarrying. Manufacturing and handicrafts (the nine ISIC manufacturing divisions, sub-divided for ISIC major groups 31, 32, 33 and 36 into modern and traditional sub-activities). Electricity and Water Supply (separately for electricity and water, with the latter sub-divided into piped water and water porterage). Building and Construction (subdivided into (i) modern construction and public works and (ii) traditional building). Commerce, restaurants and hotels (commerce: (i) wholesale trade, (ii) retail trade, (iii) restaurants and (iv) hotels). Transport and communication (air, road, rail and water transport, communications and pipeline). Finance, insurance, real estate and business services (with real estate further subdivided into modern and traditional). Community, social and personal services. Producers of government services are classified into (i) public administration and defense, (ii) sanitary and similar services, (iii) education services, (iv) health services, and (v) other community and social services. Producers of private non-profit services to households are subdivided into (i) social, recreational and related community services and (ii) domestic services of households. Indirect services of banking institutions are measured under the heading Nominal financial institutions. The expenditure categories are government final consumption expenditure, private final consumption expenditure, gross fixed capital formation, change in stocks, exports of goods and services, and imports of goods and services. Government final consumption expenditure is cross-classified by purpose, using the Classification of Functions of Government (COFOG), and cost composition (compensation of employees, consumption of fixed capital, intermediate consumption, and other outlays less commodity sales. Scope of the data Transaction Coverage: Certain constraints should be noted in compiling GDP. Owner-occupied dwellings and own-account production (especially in agriculture) are not fully accounted for. Exports and imports do not include personal effects. Geographic Coverage: The national accounts, in principle, cover all Sudan. However, due to the prevailing security situation, full coverage of the southern states is not achieved during the past years. Unit Coverage: The national accounts compiled by the CBS cover the economic activity of all residents of the Sudan. Unrecorded activity Poor reflection of the informal sector in the national accounts. Illegal activities are not measured. 2.4.1 Valuation Market prices are used. The calendar year is the accounting period. 2.4.2 Recording basis Accrual accounting is followed by all accounts except those of the government sector, which are on cash basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs GDP by production approach (industrial activity) is based on a variety of sources, including surveys, administrative records and indirect proxies for output. Agriculture, livestock, forestry and fishing. For agriculture, crop statistics are obtained from a number of sources including administrative records of state corporations (particularly for cotton), agricultural inspectors who are responsible for reporting crop area and yield figures, Department of Agriculture enumerators who measure crop cutting, and the Department of Horticulture which measures prices and quantities for fruits and vegetables. Livestock estimates are obtained for cattle, sheep, goats, camels and poultry from the Agency of Animal Resources based primarily on the animal tax, records of watering stations, and records of vaccination centers. Extrapolations are made from an aerial survey conducted in 199? The agency also provides estimates on milk and egg production. Forestry and logging cover mainly production of charcoal, fuel wood, other wood and gum and minor forest products. Charcoal and wood production are estimated by the Department of Forestry, and data on gum production are obtained from the records of the Gum Arabic Company, which has a monopoly on gum trade. Data on fishing cover inland and marine products and are obtained from the Department of Game and Fisheries. For Mining and Quarrying , information is obtained from the Department of Geology and the Sudanese Mining Corporation (salt, chromite, gypsum) and the Ministry of Power (crude oil). Estimates of building materials are derived from the change in construction output. For manufacturing and handicrafts , the results of the industrial survey of 1981/82 served as a basis for commodities for the base year. Excise Tax records, the Bank of Sudan Annual Report and Ministry of Industry data are the main sources for subsequent years. Output of some manufactures is estimated on the basis of available raw materials. Electricity and water. The National Electricity Corporation is the source for electricity output and the National Water Corporation is the sources of the data on piped water. Estimates of water porterage are based on the Household Income and Expenditure survey of 1978-80. Building and construction. For modern construction and public works, estimates are based primarily on the market value of imported building materials, obtained from customs records, and locally produced cement. Data on traditional buildings is indirectly estimated based on the population census of 1983 and an ad hoc inquiry of 1977/78 the inputs for traditional construction. Commerce and restaurants and hotels : Ministry of Commerce supplies estimates of the trade margins for commerce, based on the supply value of traded goods of imports and domestic supplies and on maximum trade margins allowed by law. Direct information on trade margins for sugar and petroleum products are also used. Estimates of hotels are based upon sample survey of small hotels; for bigger hotels the information is collected by direct questionnaire. Estimates of restaurants is made indirectly from the household income and expenditure survey of 1978/80. Transport and communication. Air transport : data about receipt and expenditure of Sudan Airways, foreign airlines and Civil Aviation Dept. as well as air handing and booking and travel agencies are collected directly by means of questionnaire. Road transport : data are provided by the Traffic Office Headquarters covering trucks and lorries, taxis and buses, and animal transport. Rail transport: detailed operation accounts are received annually from Sudan Railways corporation. Water transport : the required data are extracted from the detailed profit and loss account of Sudan Shipping Line. Communication : the required data are furnished by the public-sector enterprises, namely the Post and Telegram Public Corporation, Sudatel (fixed line telephones), Sudanese Mobile Telephone Corporation and Sudanet (internet services). Finance, insurance and real estate : the required information is collected directly from the concerned institutions. For housing, the estimates are prepared on the basis of the population census and the 1978/80 household income and expenditure survey. For non-residential real estate, the rent specified as part of current expenditure for the industry renting premises is used. Government services are available from the final statement of accounts released by the central government and budgets of state governments. Other social, community and personal services : Information obtained from sample surveys in the 1970s, the Ministry of Health, the population censuses and the Ministry of Finance are the main sources to prepare the estimates for this activity. Expenditure approach : The main sources of data for the compilation of government transactions are the actual figures provided by the Ministry of Finance and National economy. The central government has current and development budgets. As for the state governments, no actual figures are available so budget estimates are used. The adopted economic and functional classifications of government expenditure follow the recommendations of the SNA of 1968. Consumption of fixed capital has been estimated separately for building and construction (excluding dams, roads, etc.). Capital goods used for Military purposes were also excluded. The lifetime span of building and construction was taken to be 50 years and for other capital equipment it is 10 years. The inventories are estimated for four components, namely livestock, agricultural crops, inventories held by traders and industrial units. The livestock estimates are based on the projected herd sizes and the agricultural stocks are based on the annual production estimates. For inventories of traders and manufacturers, the main source is the Tax Department with additional information from the Cotton Public Corporation and the annual reports of various enterprises. The values and quantities of exported and imported goods (merchandise) are provided by External trade section of the central bureau of statistics (CBS). No other adjustments are made for external trade. The household consumption estimate (including NPISH) has been derived as a residual, based on GDP measured by the production method less the other directly estimated expenditure components.. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Compilation practices : current prices Agriculture: The production figures were multiplied by the corresponding producer prices to get the value of output. To obtain the value of out put. For livestock valuation, a price of each kind of animal has been adopted, based mainly on prices available in the local auction markets. For the calculation GDP in livestock, it is assumed that intermediate inputs are 7.1% of gross output (1.2% for goods and 5.9% for services), so that value added makes up 92.9% of gross output. Indirect taxes are estimated at 0.5% of output and depreciation 2.5%. Compensation of employees is assumed to be 4.5% of output and operating surplus then results in 85.4% of output. Production accounts at constant prices : Agricultural crops: for gross output, different crops are extrapolated with their respective quantities. Livestock, gum Arabic, charcoal and other forestry products extrapolated with their respective quantity indices. Fishing: GDP extrapolated on basis of quantities of fish catches. Mining: GDP extrapolated on the basis of quantities of major mining outputs. Manufacturing: gap for each division extrapolated on the basis of a weighted quantity index, representing major items of production. Electricity: gross output deflated with a weighted index of different electricity prices. Modern construction: GDP deflated with a weighted average index of 9 building materials, a price index for intermediate services. Traditional construction GDP is deflated with the cost of living index of low income earners. Wholesale trade: GDP extrapolated with different quantities and indices for the following types of traded goods in wholesale trade: exported goods (using quantities of exports). Imported goods (using a quantity index of trade imports). Domestic supplies (different traded supplies extrapolated with their respective quantities). Hotels gross output deflated with an average index of hotel rates and food prices. Restaurant GDP is extrapolated on basis of urban population growth. Road transport GDP is extrapolated with different quantity indices for the following types of road transport: trucks along paved roads (using a quantity index of imports and exports transported by trucks), truck along earth road (using the quantity index for all crops produced), local urban transport (using an average quantity index of relevant supplies), local rural mechanical transport (using the quantity index of irrigated crops excluding cotton and mechanical rain-fed crops), and animal transport (using index of traditional crops), other transport such as buses, taxies, etc. using for each type the number registered. Rail transport, water transport and pipelines: GDP extrapolated on basis of quantities transported. Air transport: GDP deflated with an appropriate of fares. Communication, financial institutions: GDP extrapolated with an index of real labor cost (based on deflating compensation of employees with a weighted index of Government salaries). Real estate: GDP for the residential building extrapolated on the basis of number of household, non-residential building deflated with the implicit price index of modern residential building. Business services GDP deflated with the price index of spares for depreciation and with the cost of living index of the category “other” of high-income earners for other component of GDP. Community, social and personal services (on a profit basi) doctors extrapolated according to number. Other services deflated with the cost of living index of the category “other” of high-income earners. Import duties, GDP extrapolated according to implicit quantity indices import. Private non-profit services to household, GDP deflated with the cost of living index of the category “other” of high-income earners. Domestic services GDP extrapolated according to urban population growth. Producer of government services, deflation of depreciation of construction assets with the deflator used for the GDP modern construction, deflation of depreciation other assets with unit value indices of imported trucks and office equipments, deflation of compensation of employees with a weighted index of government salaries.
