 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Concept: The framework of the BOP of Moldova follows the IMF's Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual, 2009 (BPM6). Data compiled according to BPM6 are available starting from 2009. Definition: The BOP of Moldova is a macroeconomic statistical statement, which reflects all economic transactions between residents of Moldova and non-residents within a reporting period. The ESSD compiles the BOP of the Republic of Moldova on a quarterly and annual basis, expressed in millions of USD. Quarterly BOP data are also converted from USD into EUR, at quarterly average exchange rates. Annual BOP data in EUR are derived by summing the quarterly data converted into EUR. The data in EUR are presented in annexes to the quarterly Analytical Notes and in the IA Yearbook. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The BOP classification corresponds to the standard components specified in the 6th edition of the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6), International Monetary Fund, 2009 . Standard, analytical and detailed (with gross credits and debits) BOP presentations are compiled. The sum of balances of the Current Account and the Capital Account represents the net lending/net borrowing of the Moldova's economy with respect to the rest of the world, and it is conceptually equal to the Financial Account balance. The difference arising between them represents the statistical discrepancy and it is reflected in the BOP under the Net Errors and Omissions item. Classification by sector and financial instrument is fully consistent with the international standards. Standard components of the BOP use the following data classifications: By functional categories: direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives and employee stock options, other investment, reserve assets. By type of financial instrument: equity and investment fund shares (including other equity), debt instruments (SDR, debt securities, currency and deposits, loans, trade credits and advances, insurance, pension, and standardized guarantee schemes, other accounts receivable / payable), other financial assets and liabilities (monetary gold, financial derivatives and employee stock options). By institutional sectors: central bank, deposit-taking corporations except the central bank, general government, other sectors further detailed by two sub-sectors (1) other financial corporations (except banks) and (2) nonfinancial corporations, households and non-profit institutions serving households. By maturity (in case of debt instruments): long-term instruments (with original maturity of more than 12 months) and short-term instruments (with original maturity of 12 months and less). Scope of the data Unit Coverage: The BOP covers all transactions of residents of the Moldovan economy with non-residents. Foreign embassies and consulates, representatives of international organizations accredited in Moldova and representative offices of foreign enterprises located in Moldova are treated as non-residents. Transaction Coverage: The BOP of Moldova covers almost all transactions between non-residents and residents, of all sectors. Geographic Coverage: The economic territory consists of the geographic territory administrated by the Government of Moldova, including the Moldovan embassies, consulates and representative offices abroad. Embassies, consulates, representative offices of foreign institutions and entities and of foreign governments placed on the territory of Moldova are treated as non-residents. Exceptions to coverage Transaction Coverage: The BOP of Moldova covers all sectors; however, the detailed sectorial distribution is not available in all existing sources. Geographic Coverage: The BOP of Moldova does not include data on international transactions residents from the left side of the Nistru River and municipality of Bender. Unrecorded activity The NBM makes estimates for: · shuttle trade unrecorded by customs; · goods under merchanting; · imports of freight services; · value of goods acquired by international carriers; · auxiliary services within road and railway transport; · financial intermediation services indirectly measured (FISIM); · imports of insurance and other services; · grants and technical assistance; · trade credits and advances received or paid in the external trade with goods and services; · transaction related to refugees staying less than 1 year on the territory of the Republic of Moldova or in transit; · unrecorded transactions of individuals, such as: travel expenditures of: - foreign consultants working on technical assistance projects; - individuals involved in shuttle trade; - Moldovan residents employed abroad; - foreigners employed in Moldova; - residents professionally trained abroad; - expenditures of resident/non-resident students trained abroad/in Moldova; - expenditures of refugees during first year of staying on the territory of the Republic of Moldova; compensation of Moldovan employees abroad; compensation of foreigners who work in Moldova; personal transfers; personal remittances; capital transfers between households. When such transactions are settled in cash, they lead to changes in balances of cash foreign exchange and these changes are also estimated. 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to Unit of Account : The BOP data are compiled and published in millions of USD. Transactions denominated in other currencies are converted into USD at the official exchange rate set by the NBM on the transaction date, when available. If the information on the date of transaction is not available, then the average exchange rate for the shortest available period is used. Estimations are made in USD, taking into account the evolution of other currencies, if needed. If flows are calculated as the difference between the positions at the beginning and at the end of the period in the currency of denomination of financial instruments, then the period average exchange rate of the original currency as against the USD is used. Standard presentations of BOP, IIP and ED are also available in EUR. Annual BOP data in EUR are derived by summing the quarterly data converted using quarterly average exchange rates, while IIP and ED positions are converted using period-end exchange rates. Annual EUR-denominated data are available starting with reference year 2013 (presented in the annexes to the Yearbook for 2017) and quarterly data – starting with 2024 (presented in the annexes to the Analytical notes). Valuation: Exports and imports of goods are valued on FOB basis. The NBM estimates the imports of goods at FOB prices based on CIF values. Other transactions are generally valued at market prices. The flows on foreign direct investment in equity, as well as trade credits and advances, are recorded at book value as reported by the reporting entities. 2.4.2 Recording basis Recording convention: For most of BOP items, transactions are recorded on an accrual basis. Interest on loans is recorded on a due-for-payment basis. Dividends of other sectors are recorded on the cash payment basis, while those of the banking sector are recorded on due-for-payment basis. Investment is considered as direct when a non-resident owns 10% or more of the ordinary shares, or voting power (for an incorporated enterprise) or their equivalent (for an unincorporated enterprise). Reinvested earnings are derived from retained earnings and are recorded in the period in which retained earnings accrue. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Grossing/netting procedures: Transactions in the current and capital accounts are recorded on a gross basis, while transactions in financial account are recorded separately for assets and liabilities on a net basis. For analytical purposes financial account transactions in the detailed BOP presentation are recorded on gross basis except for currency and deposits, and trade credits and advances. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The main data sources for BOP compilation are: - the NBS - for data on external trade in goods and services, direct and portfolio investments; - licensed banks, foreign exchange offices and NBM departments; - government institutions (e.g., the MOF, the Ministry of Economy, the Ministry of Education and Research, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and European Integration, the Bureau for Migration and Asylum); - the NCFM, the Central Securities Depository etc.; - some entities, both public and private – for data on transport, travel, energy resources import / export transactions, external loans contracted by residents. These data are supplemented with data from the ITRS. Under this system two categories of respondents are obliged to report to the NBM: - domestic licensed banks that undertake international transactions both for their own accounts and on behalf of their clients; - resident enterprises that have opened foreign accounts outside the domestic banking system. The international transactions of the NBM are also reported to the DSD through the ITRS. A. Goods. The customs declarations database is the main source for compiling data on international trade in goods, covering transaction by both legal entities and individuals. Adjustments for undeclared imports / exports of goods by individuals are made by the ESSD. Additional information on goods acquired in ports by carriers is collected by the NBM from the ITRS and from the NBS report on expenditures of road, air and rail transport companies on fuel and lubricants. The information on purchases and import of energy resources (gas) is collected directly from the main entity in charge and is used for adjustments of customs declarations data on import of energy resources. The net export of goods under merchanting are estimated on net basis using a comparison between receipts and payments recorded in the ITRS for exports / imports of goods and the customs declarations database, by economic entity, in the case of concluding the lack of real (goods) flows. Transactions in goods under merchanting in international trade with goods involving residents / non-residents payments / receipts when goods do not cross the country's border but are purchased by residents and then sold abroad are registered on a net basis, as net exports. B. Services. The NBS's consolidated statistical database on legal entities' reports on international services is the main source for the data on communications, insurance, and recreational services. The customs declarations database is the main source for data on transport and manufacturing services on physical inputs owned by others. The NBM obtains data on all other services mainly from the ITRS. Additional information is collected by the NBM from surveys submitted by legal entities, companies, and other institutions. Manufacturing services on physical inputs owned by others. The customs declarations database is the main source for data on the value added in the process of manufacturing, which represents the fee paid / received for manufacturing services. Maintenance and repair services. The main data source is the ITRS. Transport services. The NBS information is supplemented by the NBM with: - data from the surveys of legal entities collected by NBS; - data on fees charged for transit pipeline transport through Moldova; - information from the main importers of energy resources about transportation costs; - the value of postal and currier services is calculated based on NBS data on the external trade in services; - data derived from the conversion of imports from CIF prices into FOB prices. The CIF-FOB adjustment to the imports of goods is added to the imports of freight services (not including transport of goods for processing). The NBM makes additional estimations on passenger transport based on data of MOF Aid Management Platform and representative offices of international institutions on transport expenditures within technical assistance projects. Travel. The main data source is the ITRS, which is supplemented with the following estimates: - estimations on living expenses of Moldovan employees working abroad in the respective country; - travel services statistics pertaining to residents / non-residents studying or participating in training courses abroad / in Moldova are based on estimates and direct reporting. The estimates are made based on the number of individuals (students, pupils, interns, etc.), country, duration of stay, type and value of tuition, scholarships offered to residents abroad and, respectively, to non-residents in the Republic of Moldova. Statistical data are derived from quarterly reports received from educational institutions in Moldova on the financing of education of foreign citizens in Moldova and of Moldovan citizens attending educational institutions abroad; - some estimations on travel expenses of non-residents / residents are made based on data concerning the sale / purchase of foreign currency by foreign exchange offices; - the value of travel expenditures by non-resident experts within technical assistance offered to the country; - travel expenses of persons involved in shuttle trade are estimated considering the duration of stay and the country of destination, based on the number of declarations submitted to the Customs Service concerning the imports of goods by individuals; - estimations on living expenses of foreign refugees during the first year of stay in the Republic of Moldova are made using an estimation model of refugees related transactions; Other services. - ITRS data are used as basis for calculating the value of construction services, financial, information and computer services, charges for the use of intellectual property (licenses and royalties), other business services, and government services (partly); - FISIM charges are estimated as margins between interbank market reference interest rates and effective interest rates payable on loans and deposits; - the value of telecommunications services, of audiovisual and cultural services is calculated based on NBS data on the external trade in services; - the exports of insurance services are calculated based on NBS data, while the imports of insurance services are estimated by applying a coefficient to the value of insurance premiums paid by residents to foreign insurance companies, obtained from the ITRS. Additionally, estimations are made for vehicle insurance services based on the number of vehicles imported by individuals; - government services rendered to non-residents are calculated based on the data about the flow of currency in the accounts of Moldovan embassies and consulates abroad; - part of the value of government services is estimated from the amount of technical assistance provided by international organizations and foreign governments to the Republic of Moldova. C. Primary income. Compensation of employees. Estimates for the compensation of Moldovan employees working for foreign employers are made using a personal remittances estimation model, which uses ITRS data on money transfers from abroad in favour of individuals, and information from different institutions: Public Services Agency, real estate agencies, licensed banks, some NBM departments and the NBS. The estimates for the compensation of foreign employees who work in Moldova are based on ITRS data and, the estimation of aid received by the Republic of Moldova from other governments and international organizations within international cooperation. Income on equity (on direct and portfolio investments) . The main sources of information are: - for dividends paid to / received from non-residents: banking sector - dividends due to payment to non-residents – data from the Banking Supervision Department of the NBM; all other sectors – the ITRS; estimations for dividends received from non-residents; - for reinvested earnings on direct investment: the NBS' quarterly and annual data on retained earnings for companies with foreign or joint capital, quarterly reports of deposit-taking corporations on changes in equity by components and information on banks' shareholding structure. Interest . Data on the interest payable / receivable within intercompany lending and on governmental debt securities service are obtained from the DMFAS and are supplemented with the ITRS data. Income on other investment . Data on interest payable on loans are obtained from the DMFAS and the MOF reports on public debt service (central and local government and public corporations), income receivable on bank deposits – from the ITRS. Income on reserve assets. Data are obtained from the NBM's Financial Markets Department. Other primary income is compiled using ITRS data. D. Secondary income: Data are derived from reports and records of: - the MOF - data on fees paid to international organizations, and data on voluntary donations disbursed in favour of budgetary institutions; - the MOF IAM (International Assistance Management) database and the ITRS - data on current international cooperation (including technical assistance - TA) provided by foreign governments and international organizations. Estimates of the technical assistance value are based on the budget projected for each TA project; - the Ministry of Education and Research and domestic universities - for the estimates of scholarships granted to Moldovan students by foreign governments and to foreigners by the Moldovan Government; - customs declarations database - data on humanitarian aid in kind; - the ITRS - data on receipts and payments of different types of current transfers, including transfers in favour of individuals (personal remittances estimation model), current taxes on income, wealth, etc. - the NBS data – for the estimates of net nonlife insurance premiums and nonlife insurance claims, and for the estimates of the value of employees' professional training. Estimates on the Republic of Moldova aid to refugees during the first year of staying in the Republic of Moldova are made using an estimation model of refugees related transactions based on information from General Inspectorate of the Border Police of the Ministry of Internal Affairs, NBS, MOF, United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees, Commission for Emergency Situations, Joint Crisis Management Centre, National Agency for Social Assistance, Ministry of Labor and Social Protection, National Employment Agency. Estimates of personal transfers are made using the personal remittances estimation model. E. Capital account. Data are compiled on the basis of: - the ITRS - gross acquisitions / disposals of non-produced nonfinancial assets; - the MOF reports on public debt management - data on public debt forgiveness; - the MOF - data on governmental investment grants; - the MOF Aid Management Platform database and the ITRS – estimates of the value of other investment projects; - the NCFM - data on donations and inheritances between residents and foreign investors provided from secondary securities market; - personal remittances estimation model – capital transfers between households. F. Direct investment. Data on direct investment (DI) - assets are estimated as follows: (i) equity other than reinvestment of earnings – based on the ITRS data; (ii) debt instruments – loans extended to affiliates abroad or to foreign direct investor- based on the ITRS data. The basic data source for statistics on direct investment-liabilities is the database of individual joint ventures' (except for banks), based on quarterly reports on foreign direct investment in equity, retained earnings and transactions in debt instruments with direct investors collected by the NBS and transmitted to the NBM for processing. Individual data received from the NBS are cross-checked and supplemented with data from the ITRS and from the NCFM reports on transactions with non-residents on primary and secondary security markets. Data on DI - equity – liabilities other sectors - are cross-checked with legal entities' financial statements on an annual basis. Data on DI - equity – liabilities - banks and retained earnings, are cross-checked with data of Banking Supervision Department of the NBM data from quarterly reports on changes in banks' equity by components and information on banks' shareholding structure. Data on reinvestment of earnings in other sectors are estimated based on the NBS quarterly and annual statistical reports on foreign investment in the economy of the Republic of Moldova (1-Invest). Annually, for comparison and reconciliation, the value of reinvestment of earnings is derived from the profit/loss statements of legal entities, and the Current Operating Performance Concept is applied. Data on DI – debt instruments include debt positions between affiliated enterprises on loans, trade credits and advances, and other accounts payable. Statistics on loans are compiled using DMFAS data supplemented and cross-checked loan-by-loan with ITRS data. Quarterly data on trade credits and advances liabilities to direct investors are estimated by comparing payments / receipts recorded in the ITRS for imports / exports of goods to the customs declarations database data. Annual debt positions and flows on trade credits and advances to direct investors are derived from annual 1-Invest report collected by NBS. H. Portfolio investment. The NBM compiles data on portfolio investment liabilities based on the information received from the NBS on individual statistical data on foreign investment in joint ventures (report 1-Invest), the NCFM, the Central Securities Depository, DMFAS, information on government securities held by non-residents, submitted by the Financial Markets Department of the NBM, checked and completed with the ITRS data. Data on portfolio investment assets in the form of debt securities of deposit-taking corporations are derived from the report on open foreign exchange positions of licensed banks submitted by the Financial Markets Department of the NBM, supplemented with ITRS data. The ITRS is the main data source for statistics on portfolio investment assets - debt securities - other sectors. I. Financial derivatives (other than reserves) and employee stock options. Data on financial derivatives (other than reserves) and employee stock options are compiled using the ITRS data. J. Other investment. Other equity. Participation in some international organizations is classified as other equity. The main data sources are the MOF reports and ITRS data. Currency and deposits. Data on external assets / liabilities to non-residents of deposit-taking corporations except central bank are derived directly from the reports of licensed banks to the NBM and are cross checked with balance sheets on a quarterly basis. Other sectors' currency and deposits assets are calculated based on the legal entities' reports on their direct accounts abroad. The NBM makes estimates of households' flows in foreign exchange cash based on the comparison of data on demand and supply of foreign exchange and net personal remittances. The evolution of individuals', economic entities', and commercial banks' deposits is analysed to conclude on the nature of transactions, whether they are cross-sectorial or internal movements. Loans. Data on liabilities in the form of loans for all institutional sectors are produced by the NBM based on the DMFAS database, administered by the MOF (public and publicly guaranteed debt of the private sector) and by the NBM (NBM loans from IMF and non-guaranteed external debt of the private sector). DMFAS data are supplemented with ITRS data for deposit-taking corporations except central bank (especially short-term loans), and the data from the reports of the residents from other sectors on their accounts abroad. Trade credits and advances include advance payments by and to non-residents, and credits extended directly by the suppliers of goods and services to their non-resident customers. Quarterly data are estimated by comparing payments / receipts for imports / exports of goods recorded in the ITRS with the customs declarations database data at the level of individual legal entity. Estimation of trade credits and advances in services involves comparing ITRS data to international trade in services statistics, by type of service. Annual data are calculated based on individual legal entities' financial statements, collected by the NBS and processed by the NBM. Other accounts receivable / payable. This category covers information on arrears comprising deferred payments of dividends to non-residents, as well as liabilities to non-residents on transactions with investment etc. The main data sources are the ITRS, the report on transactions in securities in the secondary market involving non-residents, banks' financial statements and reports on changes in equity by components. Special Drawing Right (SDR) allocations – the main source is the DMFAS and the ITRS. K. Reserve assets Data are calculated based on the daily foreign exchange position of the NBM at the official cross-rates of original currencies in reserve assets composition to the USD. In addition, reports on net international reserves submitted by the Financial Markets Department of the NBM are used for cross-checking purposes. The data provided are sufficiently detailed to enable the ESSD to calculate daily flows, exchange rate changes, and price changes. Securities denominated in foreign currency within the reserve assets are reevaluated monthly at their market price as of the last day of the reference month. Accrued interest on deposits included in reserve assets is recorded daily, while accrued interest for securities denominated in foreign currency is recorded monthly. All data are subject to validations procedures using the related sources. Individual records with high values are cross-checked with the ITRS. The necessary follow-up actions are undertaken either to correct misreported data, or to obtain missing data. Intermediate results are checked against various alternative data sources and publications. The use of multiple data sources enables the ESSD to validate statistical outputs. The behaviour of time series is cross-checked with the related series or indicators if available. Financial flows are reconciled with changes in positions. Whenever surveys or administrative data are used, they are cross-checked with the ITRS results. Errors and omissions are monitored quarterly at the end of each compilation period and annually, serving as an indicator of unrecorded or misreported transactions for planning improvements of data collection or compilation techniques. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data generally comply with the methodological standards and guidelines for compiling quarterly BOP statistics. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The source data are timely for compiling and disseminating quarterly BOP statistics according to the SDDS requirements. For surveys, enterprises are expected to report promptly and follow-up procedures to ensure the timely receipt of data. Non-respondents are followed up accordingly. Data from the ITRS are available on a timely basis. Formal arrangements are in place to ensure the timely basis of administrative data. 3.2.1 Source data assessment All data go through validations with related sources. Individual records with high values are cross-checked with the ITRS or other sources. Necessary follow-up is done either to correct misreported data, or to obtain missing data. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The NBM monitors the compatibility of data and ensures the internal integrity of the BOP. Goods: The NBM recalculates CIF values of imported goods into FOB prices values by applying a 10% rate on imports from the CIS countries and a 6% rate on imports from the Rest of the World countries. Official data on imports / exports of goods by individuals, registered by the Customs Service, are supplemented with the NBM estimations that are recorded as adjustments for coverage. Coverage adjustments also include the deduction, from both exports and imports, of the value of returned goods and of temporary exports and imports. The net export of goods under merchanting are estimated on net basis by comparing the receipts / payments recorded in the ITRS for exports / imports of goods with data from the customs declarations database, by economic entity, in the case of concluding the lack of real (goods) flows. Services: The value of imports of transport services is adjusted using the difference between the CIF and FOB values of imported goods, which are further by types of transport. The NBM estimates data on imported / exported travel services by the residents and non-residents trained abroad and in Moldova. These estimations are made by country using data on the number of Moldovan / foreign students and interns trained abroad / in Moldova, their scholarships and expenditures, applying an empirical coefficient. The same method is used for the estimation of travel services rendered to Individuals involved in shuttle trade. Travel expenditures of Moldovan residents working abroad and of foreign advisers' travel expenditures in Moldova are estimated applying an empirical coefficient to the compensation of employees' value and to the technical assistance projects' value, respectively. Additional estimations of travel expenditures are made by applying an empirical coefficient to the data reported by foreign exchange offices. The empirical coefficients mentioned above were developed by the NBM based on analytical studies. Primary income: The estimates for the compensation of Moldovan employees working for foreign employers are compiled using a personal remittances estimation model. The estimations of the compensation of foreign employees working in Moldova are based on the ITRS and on the estimation of aid received by the Republic of Moldova from other governments and international organizations within international cooperation. Personal remittances estimation model: The estimates are based on: (1) data on money transfers from abroad to individuals made through authorized banks, payment service providers and post offices; (2) estimated turnover of individuals' transactions in foreign exchange; (3) labour force survey conducted by the NBS (mainly used to estimate the residency of individuals). The estimation of amounts sent to the country by other channels than licensed banks or post offices implies the analysis of: the evolution of deposits of resident individuals, changes in foreign currency cash held by licensed banks, foreign exchange turnovers by currency exchange offices, the developments in real estate and car markets. The capacity to save of individuals receiving money from abroad and the developments in consumption patterns of households receiving remittances are also considered. The entire amount of money transferred from abroad (including the amount estimated to be transferred by informal channels) in favour of individuals living in the Republic of Moldova is distributed by thresholds based on the ITRS and is registered under the following BOP items: compensation of resident employees; personal transfers (current transfers between households); capital transfers between households, including data on purchases of real estate. Reinvested earnings are compiled using data from the investments report collected by the NBS on the financial results of operating activity and dividends payable to foreign direct investors of companies with foreign or joint capital. The results are compared to the ITRS data on dividends paid to foreign direct investors for each company. According to the current operating performance concept, only net results from the operational activity are considered, excluding the income / losses arising from exchange rate fluctuations, as well as the changes in positions due to revaluation, etc. Secondary income. Estimations on personal transfers from abroad are made using the personal remittances estimation model. Data on other transfers collected via the ITRS are supplemented with estimates of taxes paid by Moldovan residents who work abroad, derived from the estimates on compensations paid and average tax rates in host countries. Current transfers related to net nonlife insurance are calculated as a difference between insurance premium amounts and the estimated payments for insurance services. These data are supplemented with estimates of net nonlife insurance premiums based on imports of vehicles made by individuals (customs declarations). Current transfers related to nonlife insurance claims are calculated based on a specialized survey (NBS) and the ITRS data. The technical assistance within international cooperation programs is estimated based on the budget provided / offered for each project, adjusted with ITRS data. Estimation model of refugees related transactions: - The support of refugees financed by Moldovan residents (public administration, businesses, and individuals) includes humanitarian aid and financial donations for essential goods, health, food, accommodation, local transport services, etc. These transactions are recorded in BOP as exports of personal travel services, in correspondence, with current international cooperation offered by residents to non-residents (secondary income) for the period up to one year of staying in the Republic of Moldova. - Personal travel services on own account of refugees and other expenses covered by international donors during the first year of staying in the Republic of Moldova. Capital account Capital transfers between households are estimated using personal remittances estimation model. The MOF provides monthly data on investment grants offered by foreign governments or international organizations. This information is adjusted with investment grants in kind recorded in the Customs declarations database and is included under other capital transfers item. Currency and deposits: The NBM makes estimates for households' foreign exchange flows outside the national banking system based on data about cash supply and demand by individuals and personal remittances and on the analysis of intersectoral flows (between banks and individuals). A comparison is made between the net supply of foreign currency in cash and net remittances in the same period. If net remittances are smaller than the net supply of foreign cash, it is assumed that the extra cash sold comes from savings made from remittances received in the past (postponed consumption) or from other unknown sources (from the past or current period). In that case, the difference is recorded on the credit side as a decrease in other sectors' currency and deposits assets and as an increase in currency and deposits of deposit-taking corporations on the debit side. In the case when net remittances are larger than the net supply of foreign cash, that reflects a growth in households' assets in the form of foreign cash as a result of saving and represents potential postponed consumption. The difference is recorded as an increase in other sectors' currency and deposit assets on the debit side (with the credit entry in remittances).These adjustments are accompanied by the analysis of the evolution of deposits of individuals and banks in FX and in national currency in order to exclude the intersectoral flows and conversion of FX as a possible drivers of positive or negative differences between the net supply of FX and net remittances for the current period. Trade credits and advances: Trade credits and advance payments received from / made to non-residents in the external trade in goods are calculated by comparing receipts / payments recorded in the ITRS for exports / imports of goods with the customs declarations database records at the level of individual legal entity. Data are supplemented with trade credits and advances related to international trade in services. The calculations exclude transactions of resident nonprofit institutions, transactions that do not involve payments (e.g. humanitarian aid or grants and technical assistance projects), payments that do not involve supply of goods (advance payments offered / received and returned), transactions involving residents when goods do not cross the country's boundary (for instance, intermediation in the transactions with goods purchased and then sold abroad). Quarterly estimations are adjusted annually using the legal entities' financial statements. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Seasonal adjustment: Seasonal adjustments are made for quarterly time series of the main current account components (exports, imports of goods and services, compensation of employees, personal transfers). The seasonal adjustment is made using the X-13 ARIMA method. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Intermediate results are cross-checked against various alternative data sources and estimates. The collection of data from multiple sources enables the ESSD to validate statistical outputs. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data The behaviour of time series is cross-checked with related series or indicators, where available. Financial flows are reconciled with changes in positions. Whenever surveys or administrative data are used, they are cross-checked with ITRS results. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Net Errors and omissions' value and sign (negative or positive) are monitored at the end of each compilation period as an indicator of unrecorded or misreported transactions and as a basis for enhancing data collection and compilation techniques. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Analysis and studies of revisions are carried out on a routine basis and used to improve sources and statistical techniques. The information regarding revisions, by items and periods affected, together with the reasons for revisions is published in the International Accounts of the Republic of Moldova Statistical Yearbook. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Information on revisions in terms of the reasons, items, and periods affected is published in the International Accounts of the Republic of Moldova Statistical Yearbook. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Ad hoc BOP statistics are routinely provided upon request to ministries and to international organizations, embassies, international rating agencies, etc. The ESSD also provides data in response to requests from students and researchers. Customized tabulations are provided free of charge to meet specific requests. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The "International Accounts of the Republic of Moldova" Statistical Yearbook provides clear reference to the division in charge of compiling and disseminating BOP statistics, along with contact details of the person who could assist users. Contact information for public relations and media is also available on the NBM's website, together with an online feedback form for analytical materials and questions. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The NBM publishes all its statistical publications on its website, which also includes a comprehensive list of publications.
