 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The National Accounts of Venezuela are compiled and disseminated by the Macroeconomic Accounts Department of the Economic Statistics Management Office of the Central Bank of Venezuela (BCV). Statistical data on Venezuela's national accounts are organized in accordance with the conceptual and methodological guidelines of the System of National Accounts 1968 ( SNA68 ) published by the United Nations (UN). The base year used in estimating constant prices is 1984, which was implemented in 1989 as a result of a two-stage process to extend coverage, collect basic data, and review methodology: Stage I, 1984-86, and Stage II, 1987-89. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Current publications use the International Standard Industrial Classification, Revision 2 (ISIC, Rev. 2), at the three digit level, and cover the following activities: Agriculture; Crude oil and natural gas; Mining; Manufacturing industry; Oil refining; Electricity; Water supply, Construction; Business, restaurants, and hotels; Transportation, storage, and communications; Financial institutions, Insurance; Real estate; Business services; Community, social, and personal services; Producers of general government services and Producers of private nonprofit services. The Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP) and the ISIC are used for the final consumption expenditure of households. Scope of the data Transaction Coverage: The Venezuelan national accounts cover all money transactions. Imports are adjusted for seized contraband and mining output for the illegal production of gold and diamonds. Geographic Coverage: The geographic coverage of the data spans the entire national territory. No regional estimates are calculated for production and GDP. Samples for estimating the production and output of certain nonfinancial private sector activities include data on economic units located in the major cities of the country: Caracas Metropolitan Area, Maracay, Valencia, Puerto La Cruz, Barcelona, Ciudad Bolívar, Puerto Ordaz, Barquisimeto, Maracaibo, and their surrounding areas. In some cases, approximations are calculated for the rest of the country. Unit Coverage: In principle, the national accounts of Venezuela cover the economic activities of all resident units. This includes legal production activities within territorial boundaries. Also included are indirect estimates of some informal activities such as: crafts, nonorganized passenger transportation, real estate services, and own account services. Formal and informal trade is estimated on the basis of coefficients for trade and transport margins obtained in the 1987 survey of margins and distribution channels. Direct estimates of unincorporated family businesses are not included. The total production of unincorporated productive units in the household sector is calculated indirectly based on data on employment and per capita income by economic activity in firms with less than five employees. The remaining institutional accounts, which correspond to the total nonfinancial private sector, are estimated indirectly (using counterparts) based on the transactions of the rest of the institutional sectors measured. Unrecorded activity There are no direct estimates of the economic activities of small unincorporated enterprises and self-employed workers engaged in informal activities. 2.4.1 Valuation Production is valued at producer prices, intermediate consumption at purchaser’s prices, value added at market prices, and uses at purchaser’s prices. To obtain total supply estimates at market prices, import duties, tax on luxury goods and wholesale sales (VAT-type), and trade and transport margins are added 2.4.2 Recording basis In principle, the time of recording of transactions is the accrual basis, i.e., when the transaction is carried out. The estimation period is the calendar year. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Production approach: By economic activity: 1. Agriculture: Gross value of production (GVP) is estimated at current and constant prices, by agricultural item (products), and by agricultural group and subsector. The main sources of annual data are: the administrative records of the Ministry of Agriculture and Land and the financial statements of the public enterprise National Reforestation Company (CONARE). The sampling frameworks of the surveys of production, cultivated area, yields, fixed assets and costs, conducted for the base year, for annual short-cycle, permanent, and semi-permanent crops, livestock, and agricultural services, were based on the Fifth Agricultural Census carried out in 1985. 2. Crude oil and natural gas and oil refining: Public sector: Given the importance of this sector for the country, oil sector GVP and Gross Value Added (GVA) are estimated. The activities of extracting and producing crude and refined petroleum are calculated on the basis of information in the financial statements and supplementary questionnaires on production and sales, inventories, and investment of the state enterprises Petróleos de Venezuela S.A. (PDVSA) and Bitúmenes del Orinoco (BITOR), which hold the monopoly on this activity. PDVSA is a holding company, with some members of the group specializing in related activities such as trading, transport of fuels, and technical assistance services. However, the basic data can be used to prepare separate annual production accounts for each crude oil, natural gas, refined product, trading, transport, and services establishment. Private sector: With the opening up of the oil sector in 1992, some private enterprises have been participating in the processes of exploration, mining, and marketing of heavy and extra-heavy crude oil since 1998. As a result, private oil companies engaged in operations in the southern region of the country are included. The production of the following enterprises is counted: PETROZUATA, AMERIVEN, SINCOR, and CERRO NEGRO. Questionnaires on the enterprises’ export volume and value indicators are the source, as all of their production is exported. 3. Mining: Private Sector: Estimates are calculated by item: Gold, Diamonds, Coal, Nonmetallic minerals, and Salt, at current and constant prices. The main data sources are the administrative records of the Ministry of Energy and Mines (MEM), direct surveys of producers, and data published by the Gold Field company. The cost structures studied in the producer surveys for 1984 are applied to GVP at current prices to obtain GVA and its components. In the case of production estimates for gold at constant prices for the 1984-1996 series, production levels were obtained using information on the volume of gold purchases from private enterprises by the BCV, as all production was marketed through the BCV. Public sector: Includes the state enterprises belonging to the Corporación Venezolana de Guyana (CVG) and the other enterprises engaged in mining iron, gold, coal, and bauxite (CVG FERROMINERA del Orinoco, CVG Compañía General de Minería de Venezuela-MINERVEN, CVG Bauxita de Venezuela C.A. Operadora de Bauxita-BAUXILUM, and CVG Carbones del Orinoco C.A.-CARBONORCA). The basic data sources are the financial statements and supplementary questionnaires on production indicators, sales, inventories, and investment of these enterprises. 4. Manufacturing industry: Private sector: The data source is the system of regular (quarterly and annual) Industrial Surveys (EI) conducted by the National Statistics Institute (INE) up to 1993 and a monthly survey carried out by the BCV. Separate estimates are calculated for the manufacturing and artisanal sectors, at the ISIC Rev. 2 three-digit level. Volume, price, and value data are used for the manufacturing sector. Information on self-employed workers, employers, employees and other workers in enterprises with fewer than 5 workers and per capita income in the INE’s semiannual Household Survey (EH) is used for the artisanal sector. The cost structures used are those surveyed in 1994 in the INE's household survey. Estimates at constant prices for the manufacturing sector are obtained by deflating the GVP with producer price indices by industrial activity. For the artisanal sector, the volume index is inflated by the corresponding price index. Public sector: The data are taken from the financial statements and supplementary questionnaires on production, sales, inventories, and investment of state enterprises producing alumina, aluminum, and chemical products (CVG Bauxita de Venezuela A. Operadora de Alúmina-INTERALUMINA, CVG Aluminio del Caroní S.A.-ALCASA, CVG Industria Venezolana del Aluminio C.A.-VENALUM, Petroquímica de Venezuela-PEQUIVEN and (until 1998) Nitrógenos Venezolanos-NITROVEN). 5. Electricity: Private sector: Basic data on the volume and value of energy generated and billed (residential and nonresidential by type of use), income and expenditure, as well as electricity rates for residential and nonresidential use, are obtained through the Interconnected System Planning Office (OPSIS) and the Venezuelan Power Industry Chamber (CAVEINEL), and includes data on 7 power companies. Public sector: Includes the state enterprises engaged in power generation and distribution (C.A Energía Eléctrica de Venezuela-ENELVEN, C.A. de Administración y Fomento Eléctrico-CADAFE, CVG Electrificación del Caroní-EDELCA, C.A. Energía Eléctrica de Barquisimeto-ENELBAR). Estimates at current and constant prices are based on financial statements and volume and value indicators. 6. Construction: The GVP of public sector demand for private construction is obtained for general government from the budgets or contracts awarded for projects at the various levels of government. For state enterprises, a questionnaire on expenditure on construction projects broken down by type of project is used. At constant prices, price indices of construction inputs are used to deflate current values. The GVP of private sector demand for construction is determined by licensed and unlicensed construction. Licensed construction consists of buildings and is measured by the physical progress of the projects in terms of approximate square meters (m2), expressed as a percentage, relative to the gross area of the building, with a view to estimating the physical volume of production executed. For unlicensed construction, an indirect estimate is made that includes illegal construction and agricultural construction by force account. The cost structures were obtained from the census of projects carried out in 1984. From 1988 to the present, a sample of selected projects has been surveyed. 7. Trade: The wholesale and retail margin (GVP) is calculated using the flow of goods method, at the ISIC 3-digit level, for which the coefficients of distribution channels (uses) and production and import margins derived from the 1987 survey of producers and merchants are used. The basic information used is the typical production of goods-producing activities, imports, import duties, exports, gross capital formation, changes in stocks of finished goods, goods in process, and raw materials, at current and constant prices. The model yields separate retail and wholesale margins at constant prices. The retail margin at constant prices is inflated by the CPI and the wholesale margin by the wholesale price index, to obtain the respective margins at current prices. The components of GVA are calculated in accordance with the cost structure coefficients studied in the surveys for base year 1984. 8. Restaurants and hotels: Within the framework of the 1989 improvement plan, the estimates were replaced with direct data collected in surveys of establishments for 1984. The production levels at current prices of private sector restaurants were obtained from the 1986 and 1988 EPF (household budget survey) data. The variable "Per diem allowances and Representation Expenses," taken from the surveys and the administrative records of nonfinancial public and private enterprises, financial institutions, insurance and reinsurance companies, and the levels of general government was used, plus restaurant expenditure by tourists, taken from the balance of payments item "Purchases by nonresidents on the domestic market." For subsequent years, the employment data on this activity from the INE's household survey were used. The levels of production of hotels are estimated on the basis of information provided by the tourist board Corporación de Turismo (CORPOTURISMO) on the number of beds for 1986 and the annual occupancy ratio multiplied by the current rates for rated and un-rated hotels in the Caracas Metropolitan Area (AMC) and the interior of the country, by category of hotel. For subsequent years, hotel occupancy data from the household survey were used for measurements at current prices. The constant price estimates are inflated with the price indices by type of service of the CPI. 9. Transportation and storage services: This activity is broken down into: Land, air, and water transport and transportation-related services, with each category further subdivided into cargo and passenger transportation. The basic data come from the income surveys of the main transportation enterprises. Public sector administrative records (Compañía Anónima Metro de Caracas-CAMETRO and Instituto Nacional de Canalizaciones-INC), average compensation indices, and consumer price indices are also used. To calculate the GVA of the private sector and its components, the cost structures obtained for 1984 are applied. In the case of the public sector, income and expenditure data from profit and loss statements are used. For estimates at constant prices, the current figures are deflated with their respective price indices for each mode of transportation. The GVP of storage services at current prices is estimated based on the performance of manufacturers and imports, in current bolívares. To calculate GVA and its components, the fixed cost structures for 1984 are used. For storage estimates at constant prices, volume indices are calculated in function of the trend of the GVP of some sectors of private manufacturing industry and of merchandise imports by tariff segment, obtained from the INE. 10. Communications: The data sources are the questionnaires on income, indicators of traffic in minutes (minutes used) and number of residential and nonresidential subscribers from a representative sample of enterprises that provide basic telephone and cellular service (Compañía Anónima de Teléfonos de Venezuela-CANTV, TELCEL, MOVILNET, and DIGITEL). The cost components are obtained using the fixed cost structure that dates back to 1992. 11. Financial institutions: The data sources are the financial statements of the BCV, financial institutions supervised by the Superintendency of Banks (SUDEBAN) and the Superintendency of Funds and Savings Banks, and other nonbank financial institutions, as well as some supplementary questionnaires. The accounts of banks and financial leasing companies in particular are governed by a single chart of accounts designed by SUDEBAN. The GVP of imputed banking services is estimated in accordance with the recommendations of SNA68 , i.e. using the difference between the amount of property income received and the amount paid by financial institutions. The production and costs of explicit services are calculated on the basis of the data on income from bank service fees and expenditure in the profit and loss statements. Explicit services are estimated at constant prices by deflating current values by the CPI. Imputed banking services at constant prices are calculated by extrapolating base-year banking services using a volume index estimated on the basis of the average monthly balances of the asset and liability items most representative of these institutions’ intermediation operations, deflated by the CPI and adjusted by an own resources (capital) coefficient, as these resources are not generated through financial intermediation. 12. Insurance: The data sources are the financial statements provided by the Superintendency of Insurance, which are governed by the single chart of accounts. The GVP of insurance services is estimated as the difference between premiums collected and compensation paid, by category of insurance: life, personal accident, liability, automobile collision, fire, and other general risks. Constant price estimates are made by extrapolating base-year values using a volume index of insured quantities (number of persons, number of vehicles), production or output of licensed or unlicensed private construction, etc. 13. Real property: This is classified in three groups: Realtors and Property Managers, Services Imputable to Owned and Rented Housing, and Nonresidential Rental Services. Realtors and Property Managers: Includes corporations and quasi-corporations providing property rental services for their own account or on behalf of third parties, for purchases, sales, management, and other related services. Calculations are made for the formal and informal sectors, given that self-employed workers account for a large share of this activity. Formal sector production levels and the cost structure are estimated on the basis of the results of a survey of private services. Informal sector estimates are based on a value index comprising the percentage change in self-employed workers (volume) engaged in this activity, as reported in the INE household survey, and the percentage change in an index of average compensation. Next, the amount of real property registration fees, reported by the auditor general's office, are applied to characteristic gross production and indirect taxes. Services imputable to owned and rented housing: Includes all those housing rental services provided to families (who pay for the service) by a single-person enterprise (another family owning the rental property). GVP is estimated on the basis of the stock of existing and occupied housing registered in the 1981 Population and Housing Census conducted by the INE. Added to the count for subsequent years are the number of finished housing units financed by the public sector, long-term mortgage loans formalized and registered by the Venezuelan Savings and Loan Federation, loans formalized by mortgage banks, and, lastly, the number of illegally constructed housing units, as reported by the construction sector. Next, holding structures by region, obtained through the INE household survey, are applied to these housing units. The average housing rental reported in the CPI for the current year and the base year is used for the valuation at current and constant prices, respectively. Nonresidential rental services: This refers to the rental of offices, premises, facilities, buildings, and other construction for nonresidential use. For this service, the income and expenditure of public and private sector economic activities during 1984-1986 were surveyed to determine the rental of nonresidential property, and a simple average of the three years was calculated to obtain a technical coefficient for use in determining the GVP levels of nonresidential rentals in subsequent years, as a function of the GVP of other economic activities at current and constant prices. The difference between the expenditure (total production) and income related to nonresidential property rentals (uncharacteristic or secondary production) of all other economic activities is assumed to be the GVP of this activity (characteristic or principal production). 14. Business services: Includes the rental and leasing of machinery and equipment, other business services, and legal services. Rental and leasing of machinery and equipment: Covers specialized services related to farm machinery and equipment, mining, the oil industry, manufacturing, construction, sales, accounting, and office services, as well as other similar machinery and equipment. For 1984-1986, the GVP and cost structure of these services were determined on the basis of the results of direct surveys. For 1987 and 1988, questionnaires on the income of enterprises in the sample were used to obtain the GVP. At present, GVP at current and constant prices is estimated indirectly as a function of the percentage change in the GVP of those activities for which the service is provided. Other business services: Includes accounting, auditing, bookkeeping, data production and tabulation, and other services n.i.e. Estimates are calculated for the formal and informal sectors. The gross production and cost structures of the formal sector are estimated by means of a special survey. Gross informal production is obtained through a value index comprising the percentage change in the number of self-employed workers in the INE household survey and the percentage change in the CPI. Legal services: Includes the offices of producers and attorneys engaged in the profession in the private sector, patenting agents, and notaries public. To obtain the GVP and cost structure, the number of attorneys freely practicing their profession is estimated (data provided by the Attorneys' Social Welfare Institute-INPREABOGADO-and other sources), as well as the income and cost structure measured on the basis of data in income tax returns (ISLRs). Next, in keeping with the methodological improvements introduced in 1989, it was assumed that the cost indicator of "Contractual Professional Fees" incurred by the various economic activities determines the income behavior of the legal services. Currently, a value index is estimated from the percentage change in the CPI and the number of attorneys registered in INPREABOGADO. 14. [sic] Community, social, and personal services: Includes training, medical and dental services, entertainment and leisure services, repairs, sanitation and similar services, miscellaneous personal services, associations and chambers. Research based on direct surveys yielded the GVP, costs, and investment by type of service for base year 1984. Medical and dental clinics were surveyed to estimate health services. Training services were expanded to include private academies and institutes. In entertainment and leisure, studies were carried out on radio and television broadcasting; the production, distribution, and showing of films; theatrical productions; composers; baseball games, etc. Repair services included automobiles, motorcycles, etc. The informal sector was surveyed with respect to repair services for footwear, watches, key-making, photography, shoe-shining, ID card lamination, and vehicle cleaning. Among the other community, social, and personal services surveyed were: laundries, barber shops, beauty parlors, photography studios, funeral homes, business associations and chambers of commerce, industry, etc. Methodological improvements were introduced in 1989 in the estimates of gross production of these services at current and constant prices, using as data sources the expenditure of households derived from the 1986-1988 EPF, the employment data in the INE household survey, and data from administrative records. Estimates for health services were broken down into physicians and private clinics, using the employment data in the INE household survey and the number of physicians registered with the Ministry of Health and Social Development (MSAS). Gross production of entertainment and leisure services was obtained by combining data on employment, data from a radio and TV survey, and data on concerts, theaters, games, bullfights, and circuses from the 1986-1988 EPF and the CPI prices for each of these services. 15. Producers of general government services: The data source for estimates at current prices is the actual revenue and expenditure of the Ministry of Finance, OCEPRE, governors' and mayors' offices, as well as the financial statements of administrative units and the agencies that make up the Social Security System. General government services are estimated separately for each level of government. The GVA indicator is employment in the sector, with the exception of the education, for which the number of students enrolled in each level of education is used. Intermediate consumption at constant prices is calculated by deflating the components with government procurement price indices. GVP at constant prices is obtained by extrapolating the base-year values using the aggregate index of GVA and intermediate consumption. 16. Producers of nonprofit private services: Nonprofit institutions (NPIs): A direct survey was conducted, broken down by level and composition of costs of the product and purpose of the expenditure, based on the nine purposes of the service, using a sample of 113 institutions for the base year, from which the integrated national accounts of this sector were derived. Methodological improvements were introduced in 1989, using employment data for some community, social, and personal services related to the purposes of the provision of NPI services, as well as the CPI of those services, to estimate the production accounts and GVA at current and constant prices. Domestic service: Production levels for base year 1984 were obtained through the employment and income data from the INE household survey. From 1988 onwards, data on household consumption expenditure were taken from the EPF for the Caracas Metropolitan Area for 1986, and average income from this service was calculated using the number of persons engaged in this activity, to obtain the GVP. Starting in 1989, a value index was constructed based on employment data and the CPI for domestic service. 17. Import duties, tax on luxury goods and wholesale sales (VAT-type): The data are obtained from the revenue, by type of tax, collected by the National Integrated Tax Administration Service (SENIAT) of the Ministry of Finance (MF). Import duties are estimated at current prices and at 1984 prices, while the tax on luxury goods and wholesale sales (VAT-type) is applied only to current values, as these taxes were introduced after the base year, thus reflecting an implicit price effect between the current and constant GDP values for the economy. Import duties at constant prices are estimated by applying base-year tariffs to imports taxed at constant prices of the year under review. By institutional sector: 1. Nonfinancial public sector: General government: To prepare the institutional accounts of general government, consolidated central government, regional and municipal governments, and the social security system up to the capital financing account, data are drawn from their income and expenditure budgets, the administrative records of the MF on revenue collected and budget expenditure execution, and the financial statements of administrative units of the various levels of government and agencies belonging to the Social Security System. Public enterprises: Measurements related to public enterprises are based on data from the financial statements and supplementary questionnaires of the national oil company (PDVSA) and the universe of nonfinancial public enterprises that produce goods and services. 2. Public and private financial sector: Public: Includes the institutional accounts up to the balance sheet of the Central Bank of Venezuela, other public banking institutions, the Venezuelan Investment Fund, and other nonbank public institutions, including the special financing funds. The data sources are the financial statements of these institutions and a number of supplementary questionnaires. Private: Includes the institutional accounts up to the balance sheet of private banking institutions, insurance and reinsurance companies, and other nonbank private financial institutions. The data sources are the financial statements of these institutions and a number of supplementary questionnaires. 3. Nonfinancial private sector: The data sources are the estimates of the production account and components of GVA by economic activity for the nonfinancial private sector (NFPS), and real and financial transactions that the other institutional sectors report having carried out with the NFPS, including households, nonfinancial private corporations and quasi-corporations, and NPIs. Expenditure approach: 1. General government final consumption expenditure: The data sources are the same as for calculating the production of producers of general government services. The estimate of general government final consumption is based on the production account calculation and is equivalent to gross production less sales of goods and services (merchandise and nonmerchandise). In the accounts, it is identified with the variable “services produced for own use.” 2. Private final consumption expenditure: Household final consumption (CFH) is estimated using the flow of goods and services method. The results for the base year 1984 were checked against those of the 1986-1988 EPF. First, the final consumption expenditure of households on the domestic market is obtained, taking into consideration expenditure by resident and nonresident households. Adjustments are then made for direct purchases abroad by resident households less direct purchases on the domestic market by nonresident households. Household final consumption of goods is calculated using the flow of goods method to define constant values; nominal values are derived by applying the CPI. Coefficients of distribution of supply by economic purpose in 1987 are applied for durable and nondurable goods, and for services the estimates are based on the production of services and the corresponding uses by households. For some specific cases such as automobiles and fuel, direct data on household consumption from PDVSA and the Chamber of Manufacturing and Sales of Automotive Parts (FAVENPA) are used. Trade and transport margins estimated on the basis of coefficients determined in the 1987 study of markups and marketing channels are added to the producer price values. These primary results are cross-checked and adjusted, using as a reference indicators of volume and prices in the agricultural sector and the manufacturing industry, both domestic and imported, and indices of retail sales (nominal and real) by commercial activity. 3. Gross fixed capital formation: The method used is that of the flow of capital goods by origin (domestic or imported) and by institutional sector (public or private). The breakdown includes construction and improvements, machinery and equipment, transportation equipment, major repairs, film production, and livestock. Data on the following are used: production, c.i.f. imports of capital goods, import duties at the ISIC 4-digit level and at current and constant prices, VAT by purpose in bolívares and dollars, f.o.b. exports of capital goods at the ISIC 3-digit level and at current and constant prices, residential and nonresidential construction and improvements contracted out to third parties or performed by force account, as well as expenditure on legal services and of realtors and property managers, the 1987 margin coefficients, investment-related sales of branches of the manufacturing industry, domestic sales of FAVENPA by type of vehicle, average sales prices, production of industrial repair services, production of films, and changes in stocks by type of breeding livestock. To estimate gross public sector fixed investment, data on the fixed assets from the financial statements of financial and nonfinancial public enterprises, administrative units, and Social Security are used, as well as data on government (central, regional, and municipal) budget execution. Private sector investment is estimated as the difference between total investment and public investment, making adjustments based on the related indicators. 4. Changes in stocks: Estimation of this variable is currently classified by sector of ownership (public or private) and by economic activity of origin. The primary figures on the value of stocks of finished products and goods in process, raw materials, and supplies are obtained from various sources, such as direct surveys of producers, income tax returns, balance sheet accounts of enterprises, administrative records, and supplementary questionnaires targeting enterprises and financial and nonfinancial establishments, both public and private. The data on enterprises include capital gains and losses caused by price fluctuations, which must be excluded from the SNA. Thus, once the primary records of beginning and ending stocks by economic activity are obtained, the next step is to calculate the changes in stocks at average prices for the year as an alternative valuation method, as recommended in SNA68 . Changes in stocks in the trade sector (merchandise for resale), are estimated by means of indirect calculations, once the overall balance of merchandise supply and demand has been established, estimating as a residual total changes in stocks in the economy. 5. Exports and imports of goods and services: These statistics are the responsibility of the External Sector Statistics Department (DESE). The statistics on imports and exports of goods and services are prepared using a combination of data sources, including customs declarations (import and export manifests) and data obtained directly from enterprises. External trade statistics are compiled by the INE from customs declarations, and results are obtained in bolívares and dollars. The exchange rate used for the conversion is the prevailing rate at the time of the transaction. In the specific case of exports, the PDVSA data recorded at customs are replaced by the enterprise data provided to the BCV. In addition, the coverage of public sector exports is corrected to include the iron exports of Ferrominera del Orinoco. In the case of imports, the BCV only replaces PDVSA data in the INE statistics since the Statistics Law excludes public sector transactions from INE records. Discrepancies: There are no discrepancies between the production, income, and expenditure approaches. The production approach is used to calculate income and expenditure estimates. Under the income approach, the operating surplus is calculated as a residual. Under the expenditure approach, changes in stocks are calculated as a residual. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques GDP is estimated using the production approach, taking the sum of value added from all productive activities, minus the adjustment for imputed banking services, plus import duties and the tax on luxury consumption and wholesale sales or value-added tax (VAT); the income approach (compensation of employees and workers, indirect taxes, subsidies, fixed capital consumption, and operating surplus); and the final expenditure or demand approach (final consumption of general government, final consumption of households, final consumption of private non-profit institutions, gross fixed capital formation, changes in stocks, exports less imports of goods and services). The estimates based on the production and expenditure approaches are presented in both current and constant prices. The estimates based on the income approach are at current prices only. The determination of marketing markups and freight is based on technical coefficients derived from the 1987 flow of goods model. The base year is 1984. The unit of measurement is millions of bolívares. The supply and use table is developed in accordance with the recommendations of SNA68, at current prices only, using price indicator ratios to validate at constant prices the marketing markups, changes in stocks, and intermediate consumption by goods producing activities. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The production method is the main estimation method. Estimates at constant prices are calculated by deflating nominal values with price indexes, as well as by extrapolation using volume indices or prices per quantity, depending on the economic activity in question. The cost structure coefficients for the private sector in general are set in accordance with the information gathered for 1984-1986, except for the electricity sector which uses cost information from financial statements through 1997, the communications sector where the structure is being updated following the 1992 privatization of the national telephone system, and the manufacturing industry through 1994. Changes in stocks in the commercial sector are derived as a residual, as accounting information is available on stocks valued at average prices for the year, while current and constant prices are used for the remaining sectors of the economy. The income and expenditure methodologies are adjusted on the basis of the production approach estimate. As a result, users are not able to perform reconciliations of the three approaches, given that there are no regular data on private final consumption estimated on the basis of household budget surveys (EPF) and cost structures.
