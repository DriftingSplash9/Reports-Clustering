 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The 1993 SNA is followed as a general framework for compiling the national accounts of Lesotho as far as available source data allow. Notable exceptions are: · government transactions are recorded cash; · work-in-progress is not calculated for agricultural products; and · acquisitions less disposals of valuables are not calculated 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The 1993 SNA is followed to classify institutional units; transactions; and other flows. The sub-sectors of general government is ‘homemade’: S131 Central government; S132 Extra-budgetary accounts; S133 Lesotho Highlands Development Authority; and S134 Local government. ISIC, rev.3.1 is used to classify economic activities. However, the national accounts include a specific category for diamond mining (1423). Except for diamonds ISIC, rev.3.1 is followed mostly at the 2-digit level or the sections (denoted by a letter in ISIC). The level of detail for the calculations is clear from section 3.3.1 below. The publication level is more aggregated. Products are classified by a ‘home-made’ CPA-type classification, i.e. the ISIC-codes are used to classify products in the national accounts. There is no national product classification scheme per se. COICOP is used to classify household consumption in detailed data sources such as a household budget survey. The current estimates of household estimates in the national accounts do net render details for a COICOP breakdown, Government source data can easily be classified by COFOG. Only broad COFOG categories are derived in the national accounts. Scope of the data The following tables and accounts of the 1993 SNA are compiled regularly: · annual value added and GDP at current and constant prices by activity; · annual expenditures of GDP at current and constant prices; · annual value added components at current prices by activity (not published); · annual production and generation of income accounts cross-classified by activity and institutional sector (not published); and · annual rest of the world accounts (until net lending). The delimitation of the economic territory is in principle in accordance with the 1993 SNA: · it includes Lesotho’s diplomatic missions abroad; · it includes migrant workers in South Africa who work in that country for part of the year; and · it excludes extra-territorial entities in Lesotho (foreign embassies, international organizations). With some exceptions, the production and assets boundaries are in principle in accordance with the 1993 SNA. Exceptions: See 2.1.1.2 below Exceptions to coverage The current exceptions are: Production boundary, not included: Research and development on own account; mineral exploration; and production of entertainment, literary, or artistic originals. Also, no attempt is made to estimate production and consumption of dagga (marijuana) Assets boundary, not included: Valuables and historical monuments; agricultural work-in-progress; and intangible assets. Unrecorded activity The informal sector activities are included within the respective sector estimates. Illegal activities are not included. 2.4.1 Valuation The valuation of flows is in accordance with the 1993 SNA, notably: · Market output is valued at basic prices · Output for own final use is valued at equivalent market prices · Detailed import flows are valued CIF, while total imports are valued FOB as per adjustment made in the balance of payments · Only non-deductible VAT is recorded in the commodity flows: an attempt is made to estimate the part of intermediate consumption for which VAT is not deductible; only a minor part of CFCF carries VAT (a guesstimate of GFCF by small scale producers not registered for VAT) 2.4.2 Recording basis In essence, all the basic data are on an accrual basis with the exception of the government data, which are on a cash basis. Therefore, government transactions are recorded on a cash basis with the exception of VAT. Total VAT is derived in commodity flows which in principle render an accrual recording. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Summary list of source data for the national accounts: Enterprise surveys and other (e.g. administrative) enterprise data: · The Bureau of Statistics maintains a business register which, however, is not entirely up-to-date. · Quarterly surveys are conducted of manufacturing and wholesale and retail trade · Annual reports and/or income statements are collected from major enterprises · Data on banks and other financial institutions are obtained from the Central Bank · Sales and purchases from the VAT database (VAT submissions by enterprises registered for VAT Household surveys: · The Bureau of Statistics maintains an up-to-date master sample frame based on the latest population census · An agricultural production survey is conducted annually (replaced by an agricultural “sample census” every ten years) · The Bureau of Statistics launched a continuous multi-purpose household survey (CMS) in 2010 which includes summary data on household expenditure; the CMS will include add-on modules on a regular basis (e.g. detailed expenditure and labour force) · The latest household budget survey (HBS) was carried out in 2002/03. The APS runs over an agricultural year, from August year t to July year t+1. The survey measures area planted and output quantities separately for summer crops and winter crops. All crops are summer crops, i.e. they are planted during summer in the fourth quarter and harvested in the second quarter the following year. Besides, wheat and peas are also cultivated as winter crops, i.e. planted during winter in Jul/Aug and harvested during summer, in Nov/Dec. For example, the APS 2008/09 measures: For summer crops: The area planted in the calendar year 2008 The harvested quantities in the calendar year 2009 For winter crops: The area planted in the calendar year 2008 The harvested quantities in the calendar year 2008 Output is calculated for eight products: wheat, maize, sorghum, peas, beans, other crops, vegetables, and fruits. Five major crops (wheat, maize, sorghum, peas, and beans): Output is based on the APS and is recorded for the year of harvest; thus, no estimates are made of work-in-progress. Quantities are multiplied by average prices, which previously were available from the Ministry of Agriculture. The prices for later years are projected with RSA PPI, grain, for maize, sorghum and wheat; and PPI, vegetables and dried beans, for beans and peas. Output for own final consumption of cereals is raised by 20% to take processing into account. Constant prices are estimated by multiplying the quantities with the base year price. All output of sorghum is recorded as own consumption. For the other four crops own consumption is calculated as follows: 1. The quantities for own consumption in 2003 is estimated on the basis of the HBS, which gives a ratio (per cent) of own consumption over total output. 2. This benchmark quantity for 2003 is moved with the trend for output 1980-1999, projected with a moving average (20 years) for the years after 1999. 3. The series of percentages for own consumption is adjusted to smooth the series and then multiplied with total output to derive an adjusted own consumption; if the trend estimate of final consumption is bigger than total output, then all output is recorded as own consumption. 4. Sales is derived as total output minus own consumption. Other crops include potatoes, barley, oats and some others. A benchmark has been estimated on the basis of the 2003 HBS and deflated by CPI, vegetables. Constant prices are extrapolated by the movement of the five major crops and then reflated by CPI, vegetables. Vegetables: A benchmark has been estimated on the basis of the 2003 HBS and deflated by CPI, vegetables. Constant prices are extrapolated by the average annual increase from 1987 to 2003 (1987 based on the 1986/87 HBS) and then reflated by CPI, vegetables. Commercial production is based on the VAT database. Fruit: A benchmark has been estimated on the basis of the 1999/2000 Agric Census and deflated by CPI, fruit. Constant prices are extrapolated by the movement of vegetables and then reflated by CPI, fruit. Intermediate consumption is calculated for six categories: Seeds, fertilizers, energy, other goods purchased, and services and other costs. Seeds are calculated for the five main crops by multiplying an estimated quantity per hectare with the area planted. The prices used for crops (see above) are increased with 20% as seed prices are a bit more expensive. Fertilizers are estimated at 34 kg per hectare planted. Quantities are multiplied by average prices, which previously were available from the Ministry of Agriculture. The prices for later years are projected with RSA PPI, fertilizers. Energy; A benchmark has been estimated on the basis of the 2003 HBS and deflated by CPI, fuel excl. tax. Constant prices are moved with output at constant prices and then reflated with CPI, fuel. Other goods: A benchmark has been estimated on the basis of the 2003 HBS and deflated by CPI, all items. Constant prices are moved with output at constant prices and then reflated with CPI, all items. Services and other costs: 50% of service charges paid for extension services (government, as estimated for ISIC A 014) are recorded as intermediate consumption for crops (the other 50% for livestock). Other services are mainly charges for milling services. A benchmark has been estimated on the basis of the 2003 HBS and deflated by CPI, all items. Constant prices are moved with the volume of maize, sorghum and wheat and then reflated with CPI, all items. Compensation of employees : A benchmark has been estimated on the basis of the 2003 HBS then moved with intermediate consumption at current prices. Institutional sectors : Commercial production of vegetables (output and intermediate consumption) is based on the VAT database and is recorded as non-financial corporations. Local sales + Exports – Local purchases (animal numbers) are based on what is recorded by the APS. Judgmental adjustments are made if the numbers look unreliable. Current prices are derived by multiplying number by 80-90% of the average price, depending on the species, to take into account that not all animals are fully grown when sold. Changes in inventories: The number of animals is calculated as the change in the herd size (February) from the previous year, e.g. 2009 = herd size in February 2010 minus herd size in February 2009. EXPENDITURE ON GDP + Final consumption expenditure, government + Final consumption expenditure, households + Final consumption expenditure, NPISH + Gross fixed capital formation + Changes in inventories = Gross domestic expenditure + Exports of goods and services – Imports of goods and services = Expenditure on GDP at market prices There is a discrepancy with GDP by activity, which is considered more reliable. ► Final consumption expenditure by government The estimates of output by government non-market producers are described above under GDP by activity. Government final consumption is defined as: Output minus sales and fees receivable by government. Sales and fees can be derived from the GFS and are deflated by an index constructed for administrative inputs. Note that government final consumption of the services of the central bank is calculated in the same way. ► Final consumption expenditure by households Household consumption is derived by the commodity flow technique and is described here under four headings: Acquisitions of goods and services produced in Lesotho; acquisitions of imported goods; output for own consumption; and expenditure by resident households abroad. Acquisitions of goods and services produced in Lesotho The estimates of output are described above under GDP by activity. Output is allocated by product; for many activities there is only one product defined, i.e. it is assumed that the industry produces only principal products (products that are typically produced by the industry). However, for a couple of industries two or more products are defined, notably for agriculture which is based on product data. Judging from the nature of the product, an allocation is done to household consumption and other uses; margins and taxes on products are added as appropriate. The margins are judgmental and based on ad hoc information. However, reconciliation can be made with total margins as calculated for wholesale and retail trade (see above). For many services, a benchmark for 2003 has been derived based on the HBS. This has not been possible for goods as the detailed imports data by product are unreliable. Acquisitions of imported goods As described under imports below the detailed imports data by HS understates total imports. Therefore, “commodity” flows are compiled only for imports by broad economic category (BEC) and household consumption is estimated as follows: · Food and beverages, mainly for final consumption: 95%; 5% is intermediate consumption in restaurants · Fuels and lubricants: 30% · Motor vehicles: 15%. Re-exports of motor vehicles are recorded as negative household consumption · Parts and accessories for transport equipment: 10% · Consumer goods not elsewhere specified: 90% · A significant part of imports is border trade, i.e. Lesotho residents shopping across the border; this not specified by product. 80% is considered household consumption. Margins and taxes on products are added as described above. Output for own consumption This is all household consumption by definition. Output for own consumption is calculated for agricultural and animal products, fish, fuel wood, and owner occupied dwellings. See above under GDP by activity. Expenditure by resident households abroad The major item is expenditure by migrant workers in South Africa, which is deflated by the South African CPI, all items. Expenditure by Basotho students abroad and purchases by travellers are deflated by the average CPI for South Africa, the US, the Euro zone, and the UK. ► Final consumption expenditure by NPISH The estimates of output by NPISH are estimated for education, health and membership organization. See above under GDP by activity. NPISH final consumption is defined as: Output minus sales and fees receivable; the latter are estimated for education and health. ► Gross fixed capital formation GFCF is derived by the commodity flow technique and is described here under two headings: Machinery and equipment (including transport equipment); and buildings and structures, Machinery and equipment Virtually all goods for GFCF are imported except a for a portion of furniture produced in Lesotho. Also, a portion of the output of architectural, engineering and other technical services is recorded as GFCF as well as a portion the output of computer and related services. The commodity flows for imported products are compiled in a similar way as described above under household consumption: · Machinery and other capital equipment: 100% · Parts and accessories (for the above): 10% · Transport equipment: 85%. Re-exports of other transport equipment are recorded as negative GFCF. Imports of architectural, engineering and other technical services are also recorded as GFCF. Buildings and structures 95% of the output of construction is recorded as GFCF except for the construction of the LHWP which is all GFCF. The output of the Department of Works is government final consumption. ► Changes in inventories The estimates of changes in inventories are incomplete and only include livestock and few products that are produced for exports: diamonds, footwear, electric products, and assembled TV-sets; thes are derived as output minus exports. ► Exports of goods and services Current prices: Exports of goods are based on Customs records. These are taken as is with a few exceptions. For example, exports clothing to the US is according to US imports data with a time lag. Exports of services are calculated and recorded in the balance3 of payments. Constant prices: The implicit output deflators are used for deflation of imports. Re-exports are deflated with the appropriate South African PPI. ► Imports of goods and services Current prices: Imports of goods are based on Customs records. As the imports recorded by products are significantly understated, totals are derived by various imputations. The difference between the total and the sum of the detailed imports data are allocated to broad economic categories (and products) proportionally with judgmental adjustments. Imports of services are calculated and recorded in the balance of payments. Constant prices: Imports of goods are deflated by South African PPIs; the PPI for output of South African industries are used imports from South Africa (and other SACU-countries, while the PPI for goods imported to South Africa are used for non-SACU imports.
