Under it, the recorded aggregates include all goods entering or leaving the economic territory of Cyprus with the exception of simple transit trade. In particular, all goods which are received into customs warehouses or free zones are recorded as imports at that stage whether or not they subsequently go into free circulation in Cyprus. Similarly, outgoing goods from customs warehouses or free zones are included in the general trade aggregates at the time they leave Cyprus. Furthermore, all goods that go or leave directly into/from local market are included. External Trade according to the ¨relaxed¨ definition of Special trade system is also recorded and published ( imports and exports ). The concept is the same as for General trade system with the exception of goods entering or leaving customs warehouses and free zones. Intra- EU trade The Intrastat system is based on the concept of movement of goods between Member States. Accordingly, it is applied to the goods which move from one Member State to another. As such, intra-EU trade statistics record the Intra EU imports and Intra EU exports of all movable property recorded by each Member State. Intra EU imports include the following goods entering the Member State of import, which were initially exported from another Member State:: (i) Union goods, except goods which are in transit between Member States; (ii) non-Union goods formerly placed in the Member State of export under the inward processing customs procedure, which are maintained under the inward processing customs procedure or released for free circulation in the Member State of import (iii)) Some goods movements are included in statistics based on specific conditions (i.e. aircraft and ships). Intra EU exports include the following goods leaving the Member State of export for a destination in another Member State: (i) Union goods, except goods which are in transit between Member States; (ii) non-Union goods placed in the Member State of export under the inward processing customs procedure: (iii) Some goods movements are included in statistics based on specific conditions (i.e. aircraft and ships). Statistics do not cover goods in transit that is goods that are merely passing across a Member State, by any means of transport, but are not stored there for any but transport reasons. It is calculated on a f.o.b. basis (free on board), for exports or c.i.f. basis (cost, insurance, freight), for imports . Therefore, it includes only incidental expenses (freight, insurance) incurred in the part of the journey located on the territory of Cyprus from which the goods are exported (in the case of exports) and in the part of the journey located outside the territory of Cyprus (in the case of imports). The statistical value is generally based on the customs value in the case of extra-EU trade, or on taxable value in the case of intra-EU trade. Under the Intrastat system, the statistical value is not provided by all the information providers. For those traders not liable to submit statistical value, the statistical value is calculated by the Statistical Service of Cyprus from the taxable/invoiced amount given in the return. 2.4.2 Recording basis Extra-EU trade The reference period is the calendar month in which the Customs Authorities accepts the declaration. Intra-EU trade Τhe reference period is defined as the calendar month, of import or export of the goods. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Since the accession of Cyprus to EU in May 2004, major changes were brought into trade statistics data. Trade statistics with EU Member States and trade statistics with non-EU countries are compiled mainly from two separate sources. This was the consequence of the abolition of custom formalities between EU Member States, which had served as the traditional source of trade statistics, and the introduction of a new data collection system called Intrastat. Customs declarations, the traditional source of almost all trade statistics, were no longer required for most movements of goods within the EU. A new EU wide system for collecting intra-EU trade statistics, Intrastat, was introduced in May 2004 for the compilation of statistics with Member States. The introduction of Intrastat as from May 2004 involved a methodological break with the past. Intra-EU trade statistics are compiled mainly by using the information extracted from two sources: The first is the Intrastat system in which the providers of statistical information submit detailed monthly returns giving information on intra-EU imports and/or intra-EU exports with EU countries to national responsible Authority. The Legal background of Intra-EU trade statistics is the 2004 National Intrastat Law (L38 (I)/2004) relating to the statistics of the trading of goods between Member States and the 2021 National Statistics Law No. 25(I)/2021, as well as the Community legislation and covers about 96% of Cyprus trade with EU. The second main component of the Intra-EU trade statistics is the VAT return, on which all traders are required to record the total value of goods imported from and exported to other EU Member States. The information provided on the VAT returns is necessary for determining the total EU trade for those traders below the Intrastat thresholds, for maintaining the Intrastat trader's register and for identifying those traders exceeding the thresholds, who are eligible to make the detailed Intrastat return. The main data source for statistics of trade with non-EU countries is Customs Declaration, which is collected by Customs Authority via "Theseas" computerized system for imports, and exports computerised system for exports to third countries. Further to Intrastat System, customs declarations and VAT Returns, other administrative sources of information are utilized in the compilation process of International Trade in goods statistics such as data from Shipping Deputy Ministry and Civil Aviation Department etc. Shipping Deputy Ministry provide information about ship fleet and registrations and deletions of ships from Ships Register (detailed data for each ship). Civil Aviation Department provides detailed information for registrations and deletions of aircrafts from the Aircrafts Register. For special categories of goods such as ships, a direct collection of data via a special survey is carried out by Statistical Service. From the year 2022, Member States are exchanging monthly micro data for Intra EU exports (MDE data) which are used in the compilation process of Intra EU trade statistics. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Extra and Intra EU trade data is collected according to the related EU regulations and methodology and United Nations methodological manual. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Provisional data for both Intra and Extra EU trade statistics is published at 40 days after the reference month, which meets the SDDS standards. Data timeliness according to EU regulations is also achieved, as the whole process of collection, compilation and dissemination of Intra and Extra EU trade data is performed within deadlines (40 days for Extra EU data and 70 days for Intra EU data). 3.2.1 Source data assessment In the case of Intra EU trade, for which the collection of data is carried out mainly via Intrastat system, other data sources are used for checking the coverage and data response error (Customs, VAT data, ships and aircrafts registers etc). Moreover, from the year 2022, Member States are exchanging monthly micro data for Intra EU exports which are used in the compilation process of Intra EU trade statistics for coverage purposes and quality control checks. In the case of Extra EU trade, there is full data coverage by Customs Authority for all goods entering or leaving Cyprus. Additionally, other sources are used for special movement goods (e.g. ships, aircrafts) such as national and international registers. However, as from 2024 monthly data are exchanged between Member States for specific categories of extra EU data (CDE data), which are utilized in the compilation process of Extra EU trade statistics. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Extra EU trade data is distinguished by the concept of general and special trade systems as defined by European Union and United Nations methodologies. The distinction is made with the use of customs procedures (customs procedures are specifying the flow of goods e.g. imports into a customs warehouse, exports from local market etc). The variable nature of transaction is also used for excluding certain trade transactions (such as repairs trade etc.) for both Intra and Extra EU trade. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Estimation techniques are used (automatically produced via SAS programming language) for calculation of missing data (non-response and below exception threshold trade) following internationally accepted methods for estimations. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The involved Authorities in Cyprus for collection of Intra and Extra EU trade data (TAX Department and Customs and Excise Department respectively) carry out verification and validity controls to the declarations submitted by traders. Furthermore, Customs and Excise Department performs physical inspection of goods where necessary in order to assure the correctness of information submitted for trade with third countries. The Foreign Trade Department of CYSTAT is paying great importance to assuring a high quality of statistics produced. There are 6 persons who validate the data, contact traders and make corrections in the data base. Various data control programs and validation procedures were introduced. Moreover, credibility checks are performed, both automated and manual. IT programs written in SAS programming language are used by Statistical Service for validity and credibility checking of Intra and Extra EU trade data according to several logical rules. Moreover, manual checks are performed by Foreign Trade staff (including telephone contacts and personal visits to providers of statistical information where necessary) in order to enhance data quality and completeness. Data collected via different sources are compared to assure correctness and coherence elements. Additionally, all foreign trade data are checked for validation correctness by Eurostat automatic validation system at the time Member States detailed and aggregated data are submitted to Eurostat. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Data validation and credibility checks of statistical data are the primary control checks of foreign trade data. Additionally, other data sources are used for verifying the statistical data received via Intrastat system for Intra EU trade and Customs Authority for Extra EU trade. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The completeness of Foreign Trade data (statistical output) is enhanced by the comparison checks performed with data published by other Member States of the EU.From the year 2022 micro data exchanged between Member States for Intra EU exports (MDE data) are available for use by the Member States of import in the compilation process of national statistics, including assessment of discrepancies observed between Member States data. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions of data are currently performed and published when available. In addition to monthly revisions, for each reference year (Y), 3 annual revisions are carried out, in March and October of next year (Y+1) and in June of year Y+2. Furthermore, when deemed necessary revisions of previous years are carried out even after the revision of June of year Y+2. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Revisions of data are disseminated to the public and Eurostat, according to the procedures/practices followed for the dissemination of the first version of foreign trade data. A note is included in publications, indicating reference periods which were revised. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Tailor- made requests CYSTAT accepts requests from interested parties for tailor-made statistics and analyses. The requests may concern the extraction of information from the databases or from older printed publications, special tabulations, the provision of data with a specific breakdown level that is not already available, etc. with the exception of cases where confidentiality issues arise. For requests demanding significant processing time, a fee is charged. The fees applied are based on the hourly cost of a Statistics Officer for the processing of the request, as well as administrative and operational costs. The interested party is informed of the total cost and if it is accepted, then CYSTAT proceeds to satisfy the request. Interested parties can submit their request electronically, through the contact form for "Tailor-made Requests" , which is posted on the web portal, under the "Contact Us" section. Dissemination of detailed non confidential foreign trade data on request is available via Foreign Trade Department Dissemination/help desk. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs Catalogues of all publications, documents and other services of the Statistical Service are available in hard copy from: Statistical Service of Cyprus, 1444 Nicosia, Cyprus.
