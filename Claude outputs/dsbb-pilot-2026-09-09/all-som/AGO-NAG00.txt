 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The framework used in the compilation of the National Accounts is based on the United Nations System of National Accounts, Revision 3 (SNA68). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The Classification of Activities and Products [ Nomenclatura das Atividades e Produtos (NAP) ] is structured in accordance with the International Standard Industrial Classification of All Economic Activities (ISIC, Rev. 2) and subdivided into 19 branches of activity. Final household consumption expenditure has been classified using the classification of Household Consumption of Goods and Services, as per the SNA68. Scope of the data Geographic Coverage The national accounts cover all territory in the Republic of Angola. Transaction Coverage The 1985-1990 national accounts basically cover the categories of transactions involving goods and services, some distribution transactions, and transfers to and from the rest of the world. Since 1992, the series of estimates cover GDP using the production approach only and broken down into the major economic activities. Unit Coverage The national accounts cover the activity of all resident institutional units in Angola. Estimates of GDP are not calculated on a province-by-province basis. Unrecorded activity For the national accounts (1985-1990), estimates are not prepared for illegal activities; however, estimates of the informal sector (parallel market) are included. 2.4.1 Valuation Production is valued on the basis of producer prices; Imported goods are recorded CIF, whereas exports are recorded FOB; Intermediate consumption is valued at purchase prices; GDP is valued at market prices and at factor cost; 2.4.2 Recording basis Operations are generally recorded on an accrual basis, except for government operations which are valued on a cash basis; The recording period for operations is the calendar year. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The National Accounts of Angola are compiled and disseminated by the Department of National Accounts and Statistical Coordination of the National Statistics Institute (INE). Production Approach The principal sources of information for calculating the production of goods are: 1. Performance reports on activities carried out by the Ministries; 2. Statistical surveys conducted by the National Statistics Institute; 3. The INE/UNICEF 1990 Household Budget and Nutrition Survey of the city of Luanda ; 4. External trade statistics prepared from customs records and other data sources; 5. Information on grants in kind prepared by national and international organizations; 6. Balance of payments; 7. General State Budget execution report; 8. Information on firms provided by the Office of Business Restructuring [ Gabinete de Redimensionamento Empresarial—GARE ] in the Ministry of Industry. In the series to 1990, as a rule, the available statistical statements on production of goods and services are expressed in physical units, since it is on this basis that the annual production plans were prepared and evaluated. Under these conditions, the data present coverage problems with varying degrees of significance, depending on the product or the production process in question. These sources do not usually make explicit reference to information on changes in the stocks of goods with the producer, goods and services consumed internally, own-account production of fixed capital goods, and production of goods that productive units give to their workers as fringe benefits. Since 1992, preliminary estimates have been prepared using administrative sources based on annual reports from firms and from the line ministries for the different branches of activity, resulting in a decline in the quality and coverage of the national accounts. Agriculture, Forestry Agricultural and forestry production is compiled on the basis of information from the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development and the FAO. The data compiled refer to gross production estimates. Fisheries The information on the Fish Catch is provided by the Ministry of Fisheries and the Environment, and up to 1991- by the Joint Angolan-USSR Expedition. Extractive Industry Crude Oil and Gas: The data on quantities and volumes of production of petroleum and gas are compiled on the basis of reports provided by the Ministry of Petroleum and the yearly balance sheets of companies in the sector. Diamonds and Other Extractive Industries (N.E.S.): The data are obtained from reports from the Ministry of Geology and Mines, as well as from the principal company in the sector. Processing Industry This includes industry in foodstuffs, beverages and tobacco, textiles, clothing and leather, chemicals, rubber and plastics, crude oil derivatives, metallurgy, metal products, machinery and equipment, as well as other processing industries. Data are obtained from the annual reports of the ministries responsible for the industries in question and the annual balance sheets of a number of companies in each sector. Electricity and Water The reports from the Ministry of Energy and Water are the source of information. Construction The data are collected on the basis of reports from the Ministry of Public Works and the annual reports of firms in the sector. Production of Market Services Includes the branches of Business, Transport, and Communications as well as Other Business Services. The production of market services includes trade margins corresponding to the services produced by the business sector and the housing rental services produced by families who own their own dwellings. The production of financial services, banking and insurance is calculated directly based on information from the reports and accounts of the National Bank of Angola (BNA), the Savings and Loan Bank (BPC), and the Angolan National Insurance and Reinsurance Company (ENSA), respectively. Production of Non-market Services The production of non-market services includes services provided free or nearly free of charge to the community by government agencies, radio, television, and grants received. The production of services by government agencies includes the value corresponding to the costs associated with service delivery, particularly employee compensation and current expenditures to purchase goods and services, appearing in the reports on the outturn of the General State Budget (OGE). To this value are added estimates obtained for the following headings in the balance of payments: Official Technical Assistance and Expenses in Connection with Diplomatic Missions. Radio and television services are an estimate of the operating costs of Angolan National Radio (RNA) and Angolan Public Television (TPA) obtained through their respective reports and accounts. Expenditure Approach Final Consumption of Households and General Government In the process of compiling the calculations to estimate final household consumption, the point of departure is the information from the UNICEF household expenditure and income survey conducted in 1990 in the Province of Luanda; the data from the Ministry of Domestic Trade on the market fund; studies on the parallel markets of Luanda prepared by the Ministry of Planning; and the demographic structure by type of dwelling (rural and urban) obtained on the basis of statistical publications and demographic studies of the INE. Final consumption of general government is the total value of production of non-market services. Gross Capital Formation Gross fixed capital formation is primarily calculated on the basis of the estimate of production and import operations. Changes in stocks, the second component of gross capital formation, are estimated on the basis of information available for the following products: crude oil and natural gas, refined crude oil products, diamonds and coffee. The values for the remaining products are determined during the process of analyzing the balances. Imports and Exports of Goods and Services Imports and exports by product are obtained on the basis of foreign trade statistics prepared from customs records and other data sources, particularly records of goods imported by companies directly or indirectly involved in the process of importing merchandise. This also includes information on grants, prepared by both domestic and international organizations, and selected balance of payments data that make it possible to ascertain by imputation goods not recorded in the aforementioned sources. Imports and exports of services are calculated on the basis of the balance of payments. Import Duties Import values by product at the level of the balances are estimated on the basis of the Angolan schedule of import duties that sets the charges and surcharges to be applied to imported merchandise. After the theoretical value of import duties is calculated, compilers carry out a product-by-product breakdown of the total value of the headings concerned as they appear in the OGE (General State Budget) execution report, obtaining estimates of the actual values in question. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques In the preparation of the National Accounts series for 1985-1990, supply-use balances are constructed for nearly 150 basic products in the Classification of Activities and Products (NAP). The constant price calculations are performed in order to estimate the various transactions in the supply and use balances, as well as gross production and intermediate consumption for the production accounts of branches of activity at 1987 prices. Production Approach 1985-1990 Series : The process of compiling the production accounts for the branches of activity is based on the construction of the production matrix to allow the transition from production by product to production by branch of activity. This method is used because information is not available on an annual basis on production and the relevant cost structure. Intermediate consumption is estimated either by breaking down sales by end-use or by assuming that supply is used based on the nature of its key basic component products or by using indirect calculations. The estimates of intermediate consumption of activities not surveyed annually are calculated indirectly by combining global technical coefficients obtained through the intermediate consumption/gross production ratio found in the production accounts for the base year. Imputed banking services are treated as intermediate consumption from a notional branch. 1991-1996 Series: Based on the supply-use tables for the 1985-1990 series, estimates were compiled from 1991 to 1996. Owing to the instability in the country, resulting in a progressive deterioration in the production of basic statistics and serious weaknesses in the production of these estimates, this series was never published. Projections from base year 1992 : Preliminary estimates have been published with 1992 as the base year, by compiling data obtained from administrative sources based on the annual reports of companies and the line ministries for the various branches of activity. The calculation of projections for the current year is based mainly on changes in the production and prices of oil, the CPI, using the same matrix as for the 1985-1990 published series, with greater aggregation. Given the scarcity of information for compiling the accounts, it is not possible to calculate the gross value added broken down by the components of production and intermediate consumption. The assumptions for its calculation are based on the principle that gross value added increases at the same rate as production. Furthermore, the constant changes in the domestic currency also contributed to the deterioration and interruption of the series. Since 1992, the GDP estimates have been compiled using only the production approach at current prices, at prior year prices, and at constant (1992) prices, published in the press releases and via the Internet at the National Bank of Angola Website. The series of these estimates includes projections for the year. The series is estimates using only the production approach with gross value added broken down into the major economic activities. The data for calculating GDP for this period (1992-2002) are obtained from administrative sources. Expenditure Approach The calculation of gross fixed capital formation is primarily based on an estimate of production and import operations. The value estimated for gross fixed capital formation does not include estimates for goods and services added to land in order to improve it or prepare it for productive uses, especially in agriculture, because the extent of such activities is totally unknown. The change in stocks is estimated on the basis of the information available for the products in question: crude oil and natural gas, refined crude oil products, diamonds and coffee. The values for other products are determined during the process of analyzing the balances. None
