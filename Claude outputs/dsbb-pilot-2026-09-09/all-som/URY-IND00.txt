 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The production index of Uruguay is compiled by INE following the International Recommendations for the Index of Industrial Production 2009 (New York, January 2009: United Nations Statistics Division). Variables defined for surveys meet internationally established criteria, and the classification is at the 4‑digit ISIC Rev. 4 level, adapted for Uruguay. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification of institutional units and transactions follows the 2008 SNA guidelines. For classes of activities, ISIC Rev. 4 is used, adapted for Uruguay. The INE uses its own product classification, in accordance with ISIC Rev. 4. Scope of the data National coverage for companies with 10 or more people employed in the different sectors of the manufacturing industry defined as Section C of the International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC) revision 4. Exceptions to coverage There are no exceptions to the coverage. Unrecorded activity Only the activities of formally registered enterprises are recorded, and no adjustments are made to account for activities in the informal sector. 2.4.1 Valuation Production is valued at basic prices, according to the sales channel to which it is destined (place or export), at the time it is actually produced. The basic price for sales in the market is the price counted at the door of the establishment (including the price of transportation over short distances, if it is provided by the establishment and expenses for packing, packaging and other services provided by the establishment, after applying bonuses and discounts), without taxes. 2.4.2 Recording basis Output is recorded on the base period it is produced, and the same criteria apply to local sales and exports; prices refer to actual transactions in the reference month. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Weights are calculated based on annual survey data on gross value-added and personnel employed, adjusted to 2008 SNA, for 2018 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The companies included in the EIM sample are chosen at random under a complex sampling design in two selection phases (i.e. sub sample). The sample of the first phase (s1) corresponds to the companies included in the sample of the Annual Survey of Economic Activity (EAAE) of the year 2017. The companies of the sample s1, were selected under a random, stratified, systematic and conglomerate design, where the primary sampling unit is the company, which can be seen as a conglomerate of economic activities (UCAs). In the second phase (EIM sample), a sample s2 was selected, which was made up of those companies from s1 that have at least one industrial UCA (class of activity belonging to section C according to ISIC REV 4). Subsequently, within each of the UCAs that make up the EIM estimation domains and are included in s2, they were grouped into two size strata at the company-UCA level: small and large, using as a measure of size (mos) the Employed Personnel of the UCA, which comes from the sample s1. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data meet the definitions, classifications, valuation, and time of recording criteria. The source data are consistent with the valuation and time of recording of the statistics. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The periodicity and timeliness of the PVI survey is adequate for the dissemination of statistics. At the time questionnaires are sent, respondents are informed of the deadline for data reporting. In case data are not received in a timely manner, attempts are made to call and notify the respondent before imposing the penalties provided for in the regulations. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Outliers and other atypical differences in responses are analyzed and confirmed with respondents. Source data are analyzed by comparing them (when available) with sources outside the scope of the survey to correct any reporting errors (e.g., customs records, cattle slaughtering reports, etc.) 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Procedures in place, such as coding, typing, etc., minimize errors. The use of electronic forms and the automatic collection of data essentially reduce the number of typing errors. Calculations follow standardized procedures, thereby ensuring that they are always performed the same way. Adjustments to unit records are made only if absolutely necessary and clearly justified. Imputation procedures have a sound basis. Weights, rather than expanders [expansores] are used in compiling the PVI. Weights are calculated on the basis of the GVA obtained from the most recent Annual Survey and are projected to the base year of the PVI starting from the 4-digit class/stratum of the ISIC Rev. 3 and more aggregated levels. Both production and prices of nonreporting units are imputed on the basis of changes from the previous month of other units in the class/stratum, except when this imputation is not considered good or refers to high-incidence enterprises, in which cases manual imputation is used based on real data from the reporting unit itself. Coverage includes units and goods that are created, while units and products that disappear are marked as “no activity.” 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures For all the units included in the effective sample of the EIM, that is, those eligible respondents, a weight (weight or expander) was computed at the UCA level, that is, the same company can have a different weight or expander for each one of the UCAs that said company has. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Data are contrasted with external sources as available. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Unusual changes are investigated and, to the extent possible and with due regard to the existing regulations—especially concerning statistical secrecy—, a brief comment is made in the press release. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Not performed. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses The base is changed and the weights are updated when resources are available or when, based on experience, this is considered to be necessary. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Not performed. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Upon request, special arrangements might be made to meet specific needs, provided that statistical secrecy requirements are complied with. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Contact points are listed in each questionnaire and on the home page of the INE website. No document containing specific information is prepared. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs In addition to service catalogs, a number of documents prepared by the INE are available on its website. Information that is not available on the website may be obtained from the institutional library. Special requests are handled by the dissemination unit, where users are informed whether their requests are feasible and referred to the appropriate unit.
