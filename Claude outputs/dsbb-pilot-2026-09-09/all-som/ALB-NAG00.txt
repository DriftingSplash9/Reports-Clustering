 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Concepts and definitions used are in accordance with internationally accepted statistical frameworks. The compilation of the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) estimates generally follows the concepts and definitions set out in the System of National Accounts 2008 (SNA 2008) and European System of Accounts (ESA 2010). GDP - Gross domestic product Gross Domestic Product (GDP) represents the total monetary value of all goods and services produced over a specific period of resident producing units within the economic territory of the country. a. GDP Output approach From the production point of view GDP can be measured as the sum of the following components: GDP = Total gross value added (B1G) + Taxes less subsidies on products (D.21 less D.31) Gross Value Added (GVA) It is the result of output valued at basic prices less intermediate consumption valued at purchasers' prices. Output The total of products created during the accounting period is considered as output. There are three types of output such as: Market output; output produced for own final use; non-market output. Intermediate consumption Intermediate consumption consists of goods and services consumed as inputs by a process of production, excluding fixed assets whose consumption is recorded as consumption of fixed capital. The goods and services are either transformed or used up by the production services. Taxes on products Taxes on products are paid taxation per unit of some goods and services produced or transacted like the Value Added Tax, excise duties and customs' tax on imports. Subsidies on products Subsidies on products are non-reverse payments made by public administration units to the companies in the form of a certain amount of money per unit of goods or services. Subsidies on imports consist of subsidies of goods or services payable when the product surpasses the border of economic territory or if the services were made to resident institutional units. b. GDP Expenditure approach From the expenditure side, GDP can be measured as follows: GDP = Private final consumption expenditure + Government final consumption expenditure + Gross fixed capital formation + Changes in inventories + Acquisition less disposal of valuables + Net Exports. Final consumption expenditure It consists of expenditure incurred by residential institutional units on goods or services that are used for the direct satisfaction of the individual needs or the collective needs of members of the community. Final consumption of households It contains all goods and services directly used to fulfil the individual needs of resident families. Final consumption of General government and NPISHs It is the value of non - commercial services ensured by General government and non - profit institutions to the profit of communities or groups of families. It is calculated as the difference between the general government production and NPISH s and their market production value Gross fixed capital formation - GFCF It consists of resident producers' acquisitions, less disposals, of fixed assets during a given period plus certain additions to the value of non-produced assets, realized by the productive activity of producer or institutional units. Fixed assets are tangible or intangible assets produced as outputs from processes of production that are themselves used repeatedly, or continuously, in processes of production for more than one year. Disposals of fixed assets are treated as negative acquisitions. Changes in inventories It is defined as the difference between inventories of stocks in process and circulating assets by the end of the year and beginning of the other one. Inventories include raw material and others, products, works and services in process, not finished and finished goods, animals etc. They are not measured at quarterly frequencies. Net export is the difference between export of goods and services (f.o.b) and import of goods and services (f.o.b). Exports of goods and services consist of transactions in goods and services (sales, barter, gifts or grants) from residents to non-residents. Imports of goods and services consist of transactions in goods and services (purchases, barter, gifts or grants) from non-residents to residents. Imports and exports of goods are to be valued free on board (f.o.b.) at the border of the exporting country. Imports of services are to be valued at purchasers' prices and exports of services at basic prices. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization GDP calculations are based methodologically on the basic concepts of the European System of Accounts (ESA 2010) and the System of National Accounts (SNA 2008). Both these methodologies are the latest updates to the ESA 1995 and SNA 1993. Classifications used in National Accounts are: - Nomenclatures of economic activities Rev. 2 (NACE Rev. 2). - Nomenclature of products (CPA 2008); - Classification of Individual Consumption by use (COICOP); - The classification of government expenditure by function (COIFOG). 2.2.1 Scope GDP calculations are based methodologically on the basic concepts of the European System of Accounts (ESA 2010) and the System of National Accounts (SNA 2008). Both these methodologies are the latest updates to the ESA 1995 and SNA 1993. Classifications used in National Accounts are: - Nomenclatures of economic activities Rev. 2 (NACE Rev. 2). - Nomenclature of products (CPA 2008); - Classification of Individual Consumption by use (COICOP); - The classification of government expenditure by function (COIFOG). Scope of the data Annual national accounts refer to the whole economy, all the industries and the sectors of non-financial corporation, financial corporation, general government, household and NPISH. The national accounts cover the economic activity of all Albanian resident units in conformity with the 2008 SNA/2010 ESA . The geographic territory of reference is the territory of the Republic of Albania. Unrecorded activity Value added generated in the non-observed economy is measured and included in GDP estimates. To ensure exhaustiveness of GDP estimates, INSTAT compiles estimates of non-observed economy based on “Tabular Approach to Exhaustiveness”. The overall adjustment of exhaustiveness is for: a) Producer Deliberately Not Registering (N1); b) Producer Not Required to Register (N3); c) Misreporting by Producers (N6), d) Other Statistical Deficiencies (N7). Illegal activities are not included yet. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation : Output is valued at basic prices before any taxes on products (import duties, excises and VAT) are added and before any subsidies on products are deducted. Non-market output is estimated by the current expenses. Intermediate consumption and final consumption are valued at purchasers' prices. Gross fixed capital formation is valued at purchasers' prices. Owner-occupied housing is valued at comparable rental values. Imports and exports are valued on f.o.b. basis. GDP is calculated at market prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording : The general principle for time of recording is the accrual basis, following the rules of the 2008 SNA/ 2010 ESA . 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Gross/netting procedures are broadly consistent with internationally accepted standards, guidelines, or good practices. Transactions between establishments within the same enterprise are recorded on a gross basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources: The statistical sources include data gathered from various records and surveys on entities or households, as following: Business Register; Population and Housing Census; Structural Business Survey; Retail Trade Survey; Household Budget Survey; Labour Force Survey; Price Statistics Survey. Administrative sources include administrative data collected by other institutions for various purposes, among which may be mentioned: Annual financial statements; Value added tax (VAT); Monetary financial statistics; Balance of Payments; Government fiscal statistics; Foreign trade statistics; Data on electricity and energy products. For compiling GDP data at constant prices, several price indices are used such as: Producer Price Index (PPI), the Unit Value Index (UVI), the Consumer Price Index (CPI) and the Construction Price Index. Further information could be found on the INSTAT’s webpage http://www.instat.gov.al/media/333395/part_a-gni_compilation__albania.pdf http://www.instat.gov.al/media/333401/part_c-qna_compilation_albania.pdf 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The definitions, classifications, valuation and time of recording used in the data sources basically comply with the recommendations of SNA 2008/ESA2010. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data collection and processing timetables are generally adequate to meet required timeliness and periodicity. 3.2.1 Source data assessment After the integration in the system of national accounts databases, the data taken from the basic statistics are checked for completeness, coverage and plausibility at the second stage. For each source, the internal and external coherence of individual data of enterprise level is checked by ascertaining that accounts are balanced, and the data in different sources are consistent to define the most reliable data based on the appropriate coherency check. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques National accounts produce, on a regular basis, estimates of production (valued added and GDP) at current prices, at the prices of previous year and chain linked reference 2010 year (2010=100). The compilation procedure of the national accounts is described in four stages as following: 1. Integration of source data into the system of national accounts; 2. Analysis and plausibility checks of data at the detailed level; 3. Conceptual adjustments; 4. Exhaustiveness adjustments. In the first stage includes the acquisition and integration of data source into the system of national accounts. For the estimation of GDP is used the information provided by various statistical and administrative sources. Data could be a result of surveys conducted from INSTAT or administrative data from different institutions as Ministries, General Directorate of Taxes and Customs, National Registration Centre, Bank of Albania, Financial Supervisory Authority, National Agency of Natural Resources, etc. After the integration in the system of national accounts databases, the original data taken from the basic statistics are checked for completeness and plausibility at the second stage. The third stage includes conceptual adjustment due to transition from business accounts to national accounts concepts. The fourth stage of compiling national accounts concerns exhaustiveness adjustments. From the perspective of exhaustiveness, different types of adjustments are applied for each group of statistical units considering size of enterprise. Exhaustiveness adjustments are done for: N1- Producer Deliberately Not Registering; N3-Producer Not Required to Register; N6- misreporting by the producer and N7-Other Statistical Deficiencies. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Services of owner occupied dwelling are estimated using “User cost approach-UCA”. Financial intermediation services indirectly measured (FISIM) is calculated as sum of FISIM on loans and FISIM on deposits using a reference rate. Quarterly GDP is seasonally adjusted. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results While compiling annual GDP, information from various sources data are used and cross-checked, leading to an additional control of data accuracy. There is a constant exchange of information between the national accounts and data providers. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs In the process of reconciliation of the GDP data by production and expenditure approaches, the statistical discrepancy is residual and balancing items. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revision policy of GDP is done in accordance with general revision policy introduced by INSTAT in the link: http://www.instat.gov.al/media/371722/revision_policy_2016.pdf The published data are revised based on revision policies of the national accounts. The estimates of the GDP and its components are subject of two types of revisions: routine and major revisions. In year 2014, it was a major revision due to introduction of ESA 2010 and NACE Rev.2 classification in time series 2008-2012, and in year 2016 was for series 1996-2007. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses For major revision, analysis of differences between the revised and previous data is published together with the revision methodologies. It is part of the statistical publications procedure for the transparency dimension towards the data users. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Users can submit specific request for statistics data through the e-mail address: info@instat.gov.al (contacts in INSTAT webpage) The data are disseminated also to international organizations (IMF, Eurostat, national and other international organizations).
