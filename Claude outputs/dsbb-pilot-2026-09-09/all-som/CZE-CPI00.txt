 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Definition: Measure of pure price change in a fixed-market basket of goods and services of constant quality and quality bought by households. Consumer price indices are compiled according to CPI Manual 2020. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification system: ECOICOP ver 2 https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/metadata/classifications#European%20Classification%20Of%20Individual%20COnsumption%20according%20to%20Purpose%20(ECOICOP) Scope of the data Population coverage: All households including the institutional ones. Separate indices are compiled for (i) households in total, (ii) households of pensioners, and (iii) households living in Prague. Geographical coverage: The index covers the entire country, both urban and rural areas. Item coverage: All goods and services of first quality currently purchased by the reference population. Both products from domestic origin and imports are included; excluded are second hand or in any way impaired goods, but second hand cars are included. The CPI distinguishes 13 divisions; 47 groups, 115 classes, and some 400 representative items. Exceptions to coverage Second hand or in any way impaired goods are excluded, but second hand cars are included. 2.4.1 Valuation Collected prices include taxes. Types of prices: Transaction prices (mostly on an acquisition basis, except owner-occupied housing) including special sale prices offered by the selected outlet; money off coupons, loyalty cards, free gifts, trading stamps and free extra products (temporarily available) are ignored. 2.4.2 Recording basis Timing of price observation: Prices are collected at least monthly between the first and the 20th day of month. Fuels are collected 4 times per month. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Since 2024, the consumer basket for CPI calculation has covered some 400 selected representative items, of which 24 items are calculated separately (subindices): e.g. market rentals mediated by real estate agent's, imputed rentals, electricity, natural gas, medicines, new cars, second hand cars, rail transport, air transport, letter handling services, other postal services, wired telephone services, wireless telephone services, internet access provision services, package holiday, financial services, household insurance, health insurance, and motor vehicle insurance. Every subindex is calculated on the basis of individual aggregate of relevant representatives (up to 144 for natural gas). Approximately 35,000 price quotes are collected from about 8,500 selected shops. Every representative item is surveyed at 1-16 shops, according to the size of a municipality, and in total. Prices are collected at least once a month, between the 1st and the 20th calendar day of the reference month. Item selection: Purposive (based on HBS survey). Outlet selection: Purposive (based on outlet survey - source: Gfk). Sample sizes: On average 35,000 prices are collected each month, from 8,500 outlets in the Czech Republic. Price collection methods: Price collectors visit shops and record surveyed prices into electronic means of support (electronic transmission of data - handheld computers). Scanner data are used as the only source for the production of price indices of food, beverages, products for pets, paper products and beauty products. Scanner data are used as one of the sources for the production of price indices of selected goods for routine household maintenance, pharmaceutical products and other stationery and drawing materials. Centrally surveyed prices are collected from various, in most cases administrative, sources. Item/product specification: The CZSO supplies general specifications to price collectors; the collectors themselves record detailed descriptions covering characteristics such as brand, size, fabric, etc. into their electronic recorders or paper forms. Sources of weights: Expenditures of total households and the social subgroups according to the 2024 Household Budget Survey (HBS). The weights are based on household expenditures as measured by family budget statistics for 2024. The HBS defines a household as a small group of persons who share the same living accommodation and who jointly take economic decisions. The core of such households is usually a family, but it may also be an individual. Selection of reporting households for the HBS is currently done by purposive quota sampling. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Treatment of missing prices: One month "carry forward" is permitted; after that, a replacement item or outlet is sought. Selection of replacement items: In case of an inaccessible variety, a similar variety is sought in the same outlet. In case of inaccessible outlet, the same item or a similar variety is found in other outlet. Substitution policy: Once a year a substitution of outdated representative items is carried through. When the old item and the replacement item are incomparable, their prices are surveyed for an overlap month. The index is then linked. Adjustment for quality differences: Imputation of price change using the price change of a similar product. Introducing new products: New products are introduced once a year (see Substitution policy). If the new product replaces a representative item, it is assigned the weight of the replaced item. If the product is totally new, it is assigned a weight through reallocation within the existing 4-digit group. Owner-occupied housing: Imputed rent of apartment owners is included since the January 1995 revision. Basis for the imputation are prices for co-operative housing, which are neither regulated nor subsidized by government. Institutional households: Institutional households are included since January 2001. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Computation of the lowest level indices: Relative of average prices. From the surveyed prices, in most cases average regional prices are calculated (single arithmetic average) which are subsequently weighted to arrive at a domestic average price. Average regional population adjusted by regional retail turnover and expenditure on final individual consumption in 2024 serves as weights. Exceptions include e.g. average rent, which is calculated by weighting average prices recorded in municipalities of certain size groups, where the weights are taken from the structure of apartment stocks. Aggregation: Modified Laspeyres formula with the fixed base. Alignment of expenditure and base period: Weight reference period is the whole year 2024, price reference period is December 2025, index base is year 2025. Linking reweighted index to historical index: Linking of time series in consumer prices published up to 2006 and revised indices published since January 2007 have been done on the base of coefficients (re-calculation bridges), which were calculated for December 2006 and re-calculated for the previous months in 2006 from the original and revised overall indices. Reference period: The main published series take 2025 average = 100. In addition, indices are published that take the previous month = 100, corresponding period of the last year = 100, and December 2025 = 100. Moreover, a twelve month moving average CPI is published that takes the average CPI of the preceding twelve months = 100. At the time of the last revision in 2025, the sample of price items, the sample of shops, and the weighting pattern were revised Time period of current weights: calendar year 2024 Frequency of weight updates: About everytwo years a comprehensive revision of the fixed weights is carried through. Seasonal items: The selected representative items for fresh fruit and vegetables are sold during the entire year. For other seasonal items, all seasonal estimation is used in non-seasonal periods. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Verification of processing: Once a year the official fixed-weight CPI is compared with a CPI calculated using current weights (using household budget data for the last calendar year). 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Verification of prices: Before the CPI is computed, CZSO division specialists evaluate the recorded price changes and the reasons for price changes as indicated by the price collectors. In addition, CZSO division staff checks the price collectors in individual regions. Finally, the CZSO center evaluates data consistency with information on the market (top control). 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revision of 2025: The last revision of the CPI entailed (i) a review of the sample of price representatives and (ii) a change of the base period. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Information Services Department of the CZSO (phone +420 274 052 304 and fax: +420 274 054 070 , e-mail: infoservis@csu.gov.cz ). 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs Catalogue of products: https://www.csu.gov.cz/catalogue-of-products
