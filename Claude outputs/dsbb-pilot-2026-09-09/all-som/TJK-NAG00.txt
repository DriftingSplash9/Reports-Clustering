 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Statistical Agency (SA) under President of the republic of Tajikistan compiles the national accounts based on the methodology recommended by the CIS Statistics Committee, which is broadly in accordance with the 1993 SNA . National accounts are compiled annually. Since 2002, quarterly accounts (production approach) are compiled on an experimental basis. The results of these estimates are published in the annual publication “National Accounts of the Republic of Tajikistan”. The national accounts statistics consists of those annual accounts and tables which are part of the minimum requirements for the implementation of the 1993 SNA, as determined by the Inter-Secretariat Working Group on National Accounts (ISWGNA) (please, see 5.1.1). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Since 1995, the Statistical Agency classified institutional units and transactions following the methodology developed by the CIS Statistics Committee. Sectorization of institutional units following 1993 SNA started in 2002. At present, the Statistical Agency uses the national General Classification of Types of Economic Activities in Tajikistan that is largely based on International Standard Industrial Classification of All Economic Activities (ISIC). The classification used for household consumption is largely based on the Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP) and the classification for government functions is based on the Classification of the Functions of Government (COFOG). 2.2.1 Scope Since 1995, the Statistical Agency classified institutional units and transactions following the methodology developed by the CIS Statistics Committee. Sectorization of institutional units following 1993 SNA started in 2002. At present, the Statistical Agency uses the national General Classification of Types of Economic Activities in Tajikistan that is largely based on International Standard Industrial Classification of All Economic Activities (ISIC). The classification used for household consumption is largely based on the Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP) and the classification for government functions is based on the Classification of the Functions of Government (COFOG). Scope of the data The delineation of constituent units of the economy generally follows 1993 SNA . There are no free zones/bonded warehouses/factories operated by offshore enterprises under customs control. Within the most important industrial groups (in terms of value added), including the survey based estimates of the unobserved economy, almost all types of activities are covered. At present, compilation of the production account, income generation account, primary and secondary distribution of income account, use of income account, capital account and goods and services account for the years 2001-2015 has been completed. The national accounts data cover all resident units. The production boundary is generally in accordance with 1993 SNA and includes the following : own-account production of all goods for own final consumption; output of goods for own-account fixed capital formation; mineral exploration; production of entertainment, literary, or artistic originals; production of computer software. The assets boundary is also broadly in accordance with the 1993 SNA . In particular, agricultural work-in-progress and intangible assets are included in the estimates of gross fixed capital formation (GFCF). Defense related assets that could be used for civilian purposes and valuables and historical monuments are not covered. Exceptions to coverage The unobserved economy for the past several years is estimated at about 30 percent of GDP. The Statistical Agency intends to work jointly with experts in identifying the measurement object in line with respective terms such as informal economy, shadow economy, illegal activities, etc. 2.4.1 Valuation The valuation rules used for recording flows and stocks generally follow 1993 SNA : market output is valued at basic prices; value added and excise taxes are included in the valuation of intermediate consumption, excluding the deductible part of value added taxes; imports are valued on a c.i.f. basis, and exports on a f.o.b. basis; transactions in foreign currency are converted using the mid-point exchange rate prevailing in the market at the time of the transaction. 2.4.2 Recording basis Government transactions and most enterprise transactions are recorded on a cash basis 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Transactions between establishments within the same enterprise are recorded on a gross basis . 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Source data are collected through (i) monthly, quarterly and annual enterprise reports; (ii) annual enterprise balance sheets; (iii) household surveys, and (iv) annual reports of various government agencies. Enterprise reports Depending on size, enterprises submit to the Statistical Agency quarterly or annual reports on production and intermediate consumption. Data are available for both primary and, if applicable, secondary activity of the enterprise. Each industry has separate report forms that are adapted to its particularities. The most detailed annual reports consist of the following four sections: (i) production, (ii) current inputs, (iii) taxes and subsidies, and (iv) inventories. The production section includes data on total turnover, value of barter, and own-consumption of goods. The current inputs section includes data on intermediate consumption and consumption of fixed capital. The other two sections include data on taxes and subsidies, as well as on the value of inventories of different types of goods (including work-in-progress) at the beginning and end of the period. Enterprises also submit monthly or quarterly production reports. These reports contain volume and value data on produced goods for the current and previous period. All enterprises submit monthly or quarterly labour reports. The reports contain data on the number of employees and the wage bill. This concept is in accordance with category “compensation of employees” in the 1993 SNA and comprises all payments, in cash and in kind, made to employees (e.g., salaries, bonuses and premiums, in kind payments, and other benefits and social contributions). Enterprise balance sheets All state enterprises submit balance sheet data to the Statistical Agency for compiling estimates on gross fixed capital formation (GFCF) by activity and types of assets. The balance sheet data include information on total GFCF, work-in-progress of construction, software, entertainment originals, and patented entities. Household expenditure surveys The main household survey is the Household Budget Survey (HBS) that is conducted monthly on a rotating basis using stratified random sampling technique. The sample frame is derived from census data. All geographic areas and all socio-economic groups are covered. The sample consists of 3000 households (of which 1875 in rural areas) that are surveyed by the regional offices. Detailed data on income and expenditure of households are collected through the survey. Besides HBS, the Statistical Agency conducts several other household surveys to derive estimates of the unrecorded activities. The following are examples of such surveys: Paid services : Sample size of this annual survey is 9000 households who are randomly selected by the regional offices. The survey collects data on purchases of transport, medical, cultural, educational and public utility services. Students : This annual survey covers 3 percent of university and other students, who are randomly selected by the regional offices, and collects data on payments by students for education (registration fees, exams, courses, books, repairs, etc.). Annual reports of different government agencies Furthermore, the Statistical Agency collects annual reports from government agencies to estimate their output, intermediate consumption, fixed capital formation, and final consumption expenditure. Data for some of the government agencies involved in education, health care, social protection, and cultural services and for local governments are received from the Ministry of Finance (MOF). These data include local government operations and data on the stock of capital. Data on imports and exports are obtained from customs declarations provided by the Customs Service. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Data received by sectors are in line with Classification of Types of Economic Activities (harmonized based on European Classification NACE rev.1), as well as Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose and Classification of the Functions of Government. There is a need in full introduction of Classification of goods and services. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Generally, data are received timely, except for some specific cases related to administrative data. 3.2.1 Source data assessment SNA Department verifies received data across various sources (sample surveys, censuses, and administrative data) 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The data collected from enterprise reports and surveys are sufficiently detailed to allow appropriate adjustments for national accounts purposes. All source data are available on a calendar year basis. Production approach : Estimates of output and intermediate consumption are compiled at the level of 21 type of activities. The following features of compilation practices are noteworthy: the output of the owner-occupied dwellings is valued as the estimated rentals that tenants would pay for similar accommodation. These rental estimates are based on total annual costs connected to owning a dwelling; data for the work-in-progress are derived from the enterprise reports. They include data on livestock and on large construction projects; in the estimates of output, sales are adjusted for changes in inventories; data on consumption of fixed capital are derived from the enterprise reports and are therefore not estimated using the perpetual inventory method; most source data are on a cash basis and are not converted to accrual. For compiling volume measures of GDP the data are deflated using respective price indices. The enterprise reports contain information on production of the current and previous year. These data are used to derive volume indices by type of activity (on the most detailed level, i.e. 21 type of activity) which are used to compile volume measures of value added. The volume measures of net taxes are obtained by extrapolating the previous years' data using volume indices of operations subject to taxation. The value added of the trade sector is deflated using the CPI . Expenditure approach: Estimates of household final consumption are compiled based on household survey data (largely based on COICOP). Government expenditures (based on COFOG) are derived from state budget data. Gross fixed capital formation is estimated by activity and types of assets. Data on changes in inventories are derived from the enterprise reports by activity and types of inventories. The following features of compilation practices are noteworthy: expenses of residents abroad are included in household final consumption and in imports, and expenses of nonresidents in the economy are excluded from household final consumption and included in exports; expenditure on valuables is not included in capital formation; BOP data on exports and imports are used in the compilation of national account with respective adjustments. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The unobserved economy is estimated on the basis of multiple specific sources (labour force survey (2009) and regular surveys of some industries) that are closely related to the estimated variables. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Individual source data are visually checked for outliers. In case of outlier detection, the submitting unit is contacted to verify the data. The source data are analyzed and checked for dynamic consistency. At each stage in the process of aggregation, the data are validated by consistency checks. Grossing-up factors to account for the unobserved economy are derived from several surveys. For the past several years the unobserved economy is estimated at about 30 percent of GDP. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Estimates are validated at all stages of data processing. Intermediate data on major activities, main sectors are assessed against other information, such as the observed trends in volume data. Discrepancies in intermediate data are routinely assessed. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Reconciliation: On the basis of surveys results, the Statistical Agency estimates the importance of unobserved activities for several paid services (e.g., transportation services and education) and for trade activities by unregistered merchants. For those activities that are not covered by the surveys, an estimate based on unregistered employees is carried out. Therefore, the Statistical Agency estimates the number of these employees by type of activity and imputes the average cost of these employees to the corresponding industry’s value added. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Once the annual national accounts data are compiled, analysis and revisions for the previous years are carried out. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Whenever possible, the available aggregated data are provided upon request.
