 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The National Statistics Office (NSO) compiles merchandise trade statistics broadly according to the guidelines recommended in the IMF’s Balance of Payments Manual , fifth edition ( BPM5 ). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Since January 1981, the commodities imported have been classified in accordance with the ‘Papua New Guinean International Trade Classification, Revised 1980’, which is based on the Standard International Trade Classification (SITC), Revision 2. Earlier import statistics were classified under the ‘Papua New Guinea Exports and Imports Trade Classifications, Revised 1974’, which were based on the SITC, Revised. At the section (1-digit) level, there are no differences between the 1974 and 1980 classifications. At the group (3-digit) level are differences for a few groups. Currently both Import and Export classification of commodity is based on Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System, which was introduced by Customs Bureau of the Internal Revenue Commission in 1991. It groups commodities into sections and each section is further divided into chapters to give more detailed commodity description. In the published data, imports are differentiated as food and live animals; beverage and tobacco; crude materials, inedible, except fuels; mineral fuels, lubricants and related materials; animals and vegetable oils, fats and waxes; chemicals and related products, n.e.s.; manufactured goods classified chiefly by material; machinery and transport equipment; miscellaneous manufactured articles; commodities and transactions, n.e.s. And exports are differentiated as agricultural and other exports; logs and other forestry products; marine products; crude oil, gold, copper, other mineral products; and re-exports. Scope of the data Merchandise trade statistics records the movement of goods coming into the country by means of international trade. Some movements of goods are excluded because they are not covered within the scope of trade as defined by the United Nations manual on International Trade Statistics, Concepts and Definitions . In general these exclusions confine the scope of trade to goods moving across the national frontier in transactions involving a change in ownership. Other exclusions reflect the fact that coverage is limited to the scope of the documents from which the statistics are compiled, namely, the import warrants submitted by importers (or their agents) under the PNG Customs Act. Exceptions to coverage The movement of goods which are specifically excluded from the statistics includes: (a) Improvement and repair trade (b) Temporary transactions (c) Goods on lease (d) Direct transit trade via Papua New Guinea (e) Stores and fuel loaded on overseas vessels and aircraft (f) Accompanied migrants’ and passengers’ effects (g) Samples of no commercial value (h) Goods sent by parcel post (i) Bank notes and coins in circulation (j) Ships and aircraft engaged in the transport of passengers or goods between Papua New Guinea and other countries. (k) Goods exported from bonded warehouses (l) Outside packages Unrecorded activity Mineral exports are under-reported. 2.4.1 Valuation The value of imports are recorded on a “cost, insurance and freight” (c.i.f) basis, i.e. including the charges of freight and insurance up to the point of entry into the country. The values of exports are recorded on a “free on board at port of shipment” (f.o.b) basis. This means that such charges as freight and insurance incurred once the goods are on board ship are excluded. Only the price of the goods, freight to the ship, and the cost of outside packages (containers, crates, gas cylinders, etc) are included. For the purpose of compiling statistics, the transaction is recorded as at the date on which the warrant relating to it is passed by the Customs Authorities and the value recorded is the value shown on the warrant. However, the warrant date may differ from the date on which the goods arrived in the country, and the final sales value of the goods may differ from the value shown on the warrant. For both of these cases, the statistics for a given period do not generally relate to the final sales value of goods arriving in the country during that period. 2.4.2 Recording basis Imports are recorded on c.i.f. basis; exports are recorded on f.o.b. basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Both imports and exports are shown on a gross basis because imports for re-exports are difficult to identify. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The Customs Department collects customs declarations providing information on the quantity, values and country of origin (in case of imports) or destination (in case of exports). The declared values are inputted into the Automated System for Customs Data (ASYCUDA). From this system information is derived for compiling merchandise trade statistics.
