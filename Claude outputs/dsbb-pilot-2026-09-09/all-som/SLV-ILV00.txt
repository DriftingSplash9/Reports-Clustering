 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions All concepts and definitions comes from the VI Balance of Payments Manual (BOP VI) and the Guidelines of the International Reserve and Liquidity in Foreign Currency document are taken into account. Concept: It refers to the external assets of the Central Reserve Bank of El Salvador that may be used to directly finance balance of payments imbalances. Definition: Includes monetary gold; special drawing rights; foreign exchange funds in deposits, investments, and custody; foreign banknotes and coins; international securities; and other assets such as interest receivable on the aforementioned asset holdings. Relationship with international guidelines: The definition given above conforms to the stipulations of the sixth Edition of the Balance of Payments Manual y the Guidelines from the International Reserve document and Liquidity in Foreign Currency—Guidelines for a Database. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Gross international reserves are classified into gold, currency (coins and bills), deposits abroad, investments, other foreign assets, and SDRs. Scope of the data Institutional coverage: Includes only reserve assets owned by the Central Reserve Bank. The international liquidity template is a consolidated statement of the Central Bank and the General Government. Instruments Coverage: Comprise monetary gold, special drawing rights, current account deposits, time deposits, funds in custody, and foreign currency. Most resources are freely available and usable or convertible at short time. Coverage for financial derivatives: No financial derivatives that could be classified as part of the International Reserves are used. Exceptions to coverage Data are also disseminated for net international reserves, which are calculated by deducting short-term credits to the central bank from gross international reserves. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: All components are valued at market price. In the case of monetary gold its price was maintained fixed since 1989 (US$325 per troy ounce) to November 2000. Since December 2000 to October 2004 gold was valued at the end of each month at a price 5 percent below the moving average of the last hundred days on the London market (London p.m.), adjustments were only made if the resulting value was lower. Since November 2004, gold is valuated by taking as a reference the lowest value resulting from the comparison between the moving average of market prices as of the monthly close of the last 10 years and the average of daily prices in the market on said month.From November 2010, the gold price is set by "The London Bullion Market". Frequency of calculations and revaluations: International reserves are calculated daily, and revaluation for the exchange rate also takes place monthly. The liquidity template is calculated monthly. 2.4.2 Recording basis The registry of international reserves is carried out in earned values. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Analytical balance sheet of the Central Reserve Bank of El Salvador. In addition to that, the compilation of the liquidity template uses data provided by the government collected by the National Accounts Department, the Financial Department, and Securities Department. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording There is a good degree of application of definitions, scope, classification and time of the registry as prescribed in the VI Payment Balance Manual and the document International Reserves and Liquidity in Foreign Currency—Guidelines for a Database. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The production of balances from the Central Bank and the reports used are timely in relation to the date of presentation of the Reserve Database. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Data are taken directly from the balance sheet with no adjustment of any kind. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The data on international reserves are valid against any calculation carried out by the National Accounts Department. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Data reviews are carried out by primary information sources (Ministry of Finance and the Accounting Office of the Central Reserve Bank). 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Bank’s publications disclose users the reasons giving way to review statistics, although the studies explain in detail such changes dare not available to the public. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Fernando Vásquez, fernando.vasquez@bcr.gob.sv César Mateo Villamariona, mateo.villamariona@bcr.gob.sv
