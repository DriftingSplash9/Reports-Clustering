 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions National accounts statistics in the Republic of North Macedonia are compiled following the conceptual framework of the European System of Accounts 2010 ( ESA2010 ) and the System of National Accounts 2008 ( SNA2008 ). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization North Macedonian national accounts follow most of the international standards and classifications. Institutional units, transactions and other flows are in accordance with the ESA2010 . GDP by production approach is calculated in accordance with NACE Rev.2. Classification of Products by Activity (CPA) is used in Gross Capital Formation, the classification of products and the Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP) is used to classify household consumption. The Classification of Functions of Government (COFOG) is adopted as a national standard officially, as well as the COPNI, but they are not fully implemented in the process of work. 2.2.1 Scope North Macedonian national accounts follow most of the international standards and classifications. Institutional units, transactions and other flows are in accordance with the ESA2010 . GDP by production approach is calculated in accordance with NACE Rev.2. Classification of Products by Activity (CPA) is used in Gross Capital Formation, the classification of products and the Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP) is used to classify household consumption. The Classification of Functions of Government (COFOG) is adopted as a national standard officially, as well as the COPNI, but they are not fully implemented in the process of work. Scope of the data Annual Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is calculated by production and expenditure approach as independent approaches. Income approach estimation is not being made independently in the SSO. It is connected with the production approach calculations. The same data sources are used for production and income approach. Quarterly GDP is calculated by production, expenditure and income approach. In October 2014 is published the revised data of GDP according to the ESA2010 for the period from 2000 to 2012. In December the same year quarterly GDP is also available according to the ESA2010 for the period from first quarter 2000 to third quarter of 2014. Quarterly data for GDP (production and expenditure approach) for the series since 2000 at current prices and since 2001 at constant prices are available on website. The quarterly national accounts data covers the total economy. Exceptions to coverage Illegal activities are included as a part of the Household Final Consumption Expenditure (Narcotics and Prostitution). 2.4.1 Valuation Market prices are used to evaluate flows and stocks in the North Macedonian National Accounts. Output is valued at basic prices, and intermediate consumption is valued at purchase prices excluding the deductible part of the value added taxes. Total exports and imports are valued on an f.o.b. basis. Transactions in foreign currency are converted to the national currency using the monthly average exchange rate. 2.4.2 Recording basis Apart from general government income and expenditure, recording of all transactions is done on an accrual basis. Source data for government are on a cash basis, and no adjustments are made at present for their conversion to an accrual basis for national accounts compilation purposes. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Since the main data source for the compilation of GDP are the financial statements of enterprises, transactions between establishments within the same enterprise are recorded net. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs For compilation of national accounts, the SSO in the Republic of North Macedonia uses a wide range of data sources. The SSO has a regular program with monthly, quarterly, and annual surveys covering a range of economic activities. These surveys collect data in monetary terms, as well as data on quantities for numerous variables (production, inputs, inventories, number of employees, etc.). These data are processed and disseminated in the form of indices and/or absolute values (usually sample values rather than estimates for the target population). For some of the survey data, the lack of sufficient coverage (partly due to low response rates) has limited their usefulness. Compilers then use them only as alternative source data, mainly for validating the estimates. In 2007, the work on establishing the Statistical Business Register was completed. In accordance with the law, all operating enterprises and those legal entities requesting a license to initiate operations must register with the Central Register. These include the Classification of Economic Activities at the 5–digit level of the Statistical Classification of Economic Activities in the European Communities NACE Rev.1. In 2008, the Government adapted NKD Rev.2 based on NACE Rev.2. All business entities were recorded in Business Register according at the 4–digit level NACE Rev.2. Other administrative registers used in the compilation of national accounts are those maintained by the Tax Revenue Office, the Pension Fund and the Health Fund. The staff obtains information from these on the number of employed persons contributing to these funds, as well as information on taxes and contributions. Most of the samples used in the business statistics surveys of the SSO are of a cut-off type, that is, care is being taken to obtain reasonable coverage. The response rate to these surveys is not satisfactory. Among the surveys conducted by the SSO are the mining, manufacturing, and electricity survey (conducted monthly, quarterly, and annually), the monthly on internal trade, the construction survey, the transport survey, the hotels and restaurants survey, and the agriculture survey. Quarterly surveys on establishments also provide data on employment. By the end of 2017, data on wages are collected with a statistical survey filled out based on wages records for legal entities. Since the beginning of 2018, the collection of data has been executed from an administrative source (Public Revenue Office). Monthly surveys on establishments also provide data on employment and wages and salaries by activity. From the monthly mining, manufacturing, and electricity survey, which are based on a sample of establishments, statistics are compiled and published in the form of indices for production, employment, and inventories of finished goods, and quantities produced of selected products. Data sources for the production approach are mainly from monthly and quarterly enterprise surveys in SSO and from other institutions. Data sources from departments in SSO are Price and Volume indices and other indicators that are used from Social and Business Statistics Sectors. Data sources from other institutions are: Turnover - VAT data from the Public Revenue Office; Data source from financial sector where Gross value added is calculated with the data which are submitted by the National Bank of the RNM – NBRNM; Ministry of Finance data- Value added tax, Excise duties and Custom duty; Agency for Electronic Communication- number of subscribers to TV services, number of users of fixed and mobile network and number of users of internet). Data sources for the expenditure approach are mainly from monthly and quarterly enterprise surveys in SSO and from other institutions. Data sources from the departments in the SSO are: Social and Business Statistics Sectors. Data sources from other institutions Insurance Supervision Agency; Enterprises; Ministry of Finance; National Bank of the Republic of North Macedonia. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording North Macedonia's national accounts statistics are compiled following the conceptual framework of the European System of Accounts 2010 ( ESA2010 ). In general, source data are consistent with the definitions, scope, and classifications of National Accounts statistics. Data collected covers all economic activities. In total, about 90 percent of the total value added is produced by the corporate sector, which is fully covered by the data sources. Data used for calculation of GDP categories gathered from SSO statistics are in accordance with the recommended international standard classification and definitions. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness All source data are collected and processed in a timely fashion to meet the timeliness and periodicity requirements of the quarterly national accounts estimates. Deadlines for the provision of source data by other units of the statistical system, including other units of the SSO, needed for the compilation of national accounts, are clearly stipulated in the statistical program. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Sampling errors are calculated at a detailed level of the estimates for the household survey and other surveys based on random sampling methods. The coverage of the data is under scrutiny for all surveys conducted by the SSO, and remedies are taken to improve their representativeness, usually by extending the size of the samples and making efforts to obtain late responses. The staff applies logical and mathematical procedures to raw data to identify outliers. Source data are analyzed for correction of under reporting and misreporting, particularly by checking temporal consistency and other related source data. Thus, financial reports data on wages and salaries are corrected for under reporting by comparing the respective data obtained from the labor survey. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The staff validates source data by consistency checks at different levels of aggregation of the data, comparing them with other alternative source data. Also, the staff uses appropriate procedures for adjusting for missing observations in the surveys of the SSO. For some data for the expenditure approach calculation by COICOP, data are based on additional data from the electricity companies, telecommunication and heating companies, lottery, Macedonian Post Office and other institutions. Grossing-up factors for surveys based on random samples are scientifically derived from the sample design. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Some conceptual adjustments must be made after acquisition of source data into the system of national accounts. The reasons for these adjustments are the differences between business and national accounting rules. The term "non-observed economy" refers to activities that are within the production boundary defined by the SNA 2008 and the ESA 2010, but which are omitted, either partially or completely, from the estimates of GDP, because they are under-reported or not covered by the statistical surveys or administrative records of which the national accounts are constructed. In the quarterly calculations, the annual calculated data for non-observed are taken into consideration and proportionally are included. Production approach In the process of quarterly GDP compilation by the production approach, volume and price indicators at the level of division by NACE Rev.2 are used. These indicators are output indicators, because such data are not available separately for output and intermediate consumption. Previous year annual current price I/O ratio is used in quarterly GDP calculations at constant prices to obtain output, intermediate consumption and value added. In the process of balancing the production and expenditure calculations, the same indicators are used in several items on the production side and the expenditure side, which is the case in construction activity from one side and investments in construction from the other. The calculation of government consumption uses the output indicator, which is also used in NACE Rev.2 divisions 84, 85, 86 and 88. The value of goods and services for household consumption that are subject to taxation is used as a volume measure for VAT calculation. The SSO does not publish statistical discrepancy between the production and the expenditure side. The production side is considered more reliable and with more exhaustive data sources, and, therefore, the discrepancy is added to the expenditure side as part of the changes in inventories. All monthly or quarterly indicators used in the calculations are based on the previous year's average (Lasper formula). For reconciliation of quarterly GDP with annual GDP data (production method), Denton macro in Excel is used. Annual values are split according to appropriate indices, values and implicit deflators by the four quarterly observations using Denton. Expenditure approach The estimation is based on the available data sources from surveys conducted in the SSO, mainly from HBS and additional data directly obtained from enterprises concerning expenditures for gambling, electricity, heating, postal, telecommunication and insurance services. Additionally, some of the HBS data are substituted with data from more reliable sources and adjustments are made based on the analytical notices obtained across quantitative and price indicators (basis for VAT calculation, total CPI, turnover in retail trade and average net wage per employee). The procedure for constant prices estimations for each of the GDP expenditure categories is the same as household consumption expenditure, that's mean the method of deflation is used. For calculation of non-profit institutions at constant prices on quarterly level, general CPI is used. Data on government final consumption on quarterly level are calculated indirectly using the expenditures for goods and services and compensation of employees obtained from the central budget for all budgetary users and increased by the same kind of expenditures obtained the extra budgetary funds (Health insurance fund, Pension and disability insurance fund, Agency of Employment, and Public enterprise for State Roads). As there are no data on the consumption of fixed capital on quarterly level, the estimation is making based on the available annual data for the previous year and this value is adjusted with the index of investment for the previous year regarding the government bodies, health and education. After obtaining the results for government consumption on annual level based on financial reports (available only at annual level) adjustment using pro-rata is made to the quarterly data in order the sum of quarterly estimation to be equal to annual data. For general government consumption at constant prices, a weighted deflator is used. Consumer price indices (CPI), Producer price indices (PPI) and nominal wages for the NACE activities: O, P and Q are used for calculations. Structure of value added from the latest GDP final data is used as weights. The estimation of Gross Fixed Capital Formation at quarterly level mainly is based on data from the Business department, as well as on the available data from NA Department. For estimation of GFCF in the part of machinery and equipment, the commodity flow method is used. For estimation of GFCF in the part of construction, average nominal indicators for each quarter that are already used for calculating quarterly data at current prices for construction by production side, are used. The quarterly data on cultivated assets, intangible assets and other assets not mentioned previously is based on estimation by using available data. Machinery and equipment are estimated at constant prices using the commodity flow method, and corresponding deflators (PPI and UVI). Classifications and methods are the same as annul calculations because annual data on machinery and equipment at constant prices is a sum of the quarters. For investment in construction, a deflator from the production side of GDP (NACE Section F) is used. Other investments are calculated at constant prices using an implicit deflator from two other parts of investment (machinery and equipment and investment in construction). The changes of inventories by quarters are estimated in the process of balancing between production and expenditure GDP on quarterly level. For calculation of changes in inventories, PPI is used as a deflator. Data on export of goods (F.O.B.) are obtained from the Balance of payments (BOP) statistics received from the National Bank of the Republic of North Macedonia (NBRNM). Monthly data on export of goods are multiplied with the monthly data on exchange rates of US dollar to denar, to obtain the value in national currency. Quarterly data on export of goods is the sum of calculated data for three months. Data on export services are based on BOP statistics from NBRNM. Non-resident consumption of services is estimated separately by the SSO and BOP data are adjusted based on this calculation. Export of goods and services at constant prices are estimated using Export unit value indices and CPI. Data on import of goods (F.O.B.) are obtained from the (BOP) statistics received from the (NBRNM). Monthly data on import of goods are multiplied with the monthly data on exchange rates of US dollar to denar, to obtain the value in national currency. Quarterly data on imports of goods are the sum of calculated data for three months. Data on import of services (F.O.B.) are obtained from the (BOP) statistics received from the (NBRNM). Monthly data on import of services are multiplied with the monthly data on exchange rates of US dollar to denar, to obtain the value in national currency. Quarterly data on import of services are the sum of calculated data for three months. Import of goods and services at constant prices is estimated using Import unit value indices and CPI for EU countries. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The staff validates estimates at all stages of processing. Intermediate results are checked against other alternative data sources available. Output estimates for export-intensive industries are compared with the appropriate data on foreign trade. Implicit deflators of output are compared with relevant price indices. Volume indicators derived by deflating current values of national accounts estimates are compared with available volume indices. Data from administrative sources are compared with similar data from statistical surveys. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Discrepancies in intermediate data sources are assessed and investigated on an ongoing basis. The causes of discrepancies are investigated, and measures are taken for improving the data as necessary. The procedure for investigation of statistical discrepancy for quarterly calculations by both methods of calculation of GDP is improved (for government consumption, construction) using the same sources of data. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs In fact, the GDP estimates by production and by expenditure are compiled independently. No discrepancy is shown between both estimates of GDP, because the differences are assimilated in the components of the final expenditures, mainly in the estimates of changes in inventories. The process of decreasing the discrepancy is ongoing. New efforts are concentrated on the use of SUT framework as basic frame for the compilation of GDP in current and constant prices. The process is in progress. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses In accordance with the Statistical Data Revision Policy. https://www.stat.mk/en/about-us/policies-and-strategies/data-revision/ In Quarterly national accounts there are revisions because of the new information for previous quarters received or because of changes of the annual data. The explanations are present in the regular reports published. The last bigger revision in 2014 was made and data were published at SSO website. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses The large explanations of revised data are mostly present in the annual Press Release of GDP data. The explanations are also prepared in the common work on "Quarterly National Accounts Inventory on sources and methods of NA" supported by Eurostat. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request According to the Law on State Statistics (Article 41) and the Policy on Access to Anonymised Micro data for scientific purposes, data collected for the purpose of official statistics may be used for scientific purposes if there is no risk of direct or indirect identification,i.e. disclosure of data individuality.
