 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The compilation system in the Central Bureau of Statistics (CBStat) broadly follows the recommendations of the United Nations for the compilation of external merchandise trade statistics. However, improvement is needed in the external trade definitions, data collection and processing procedures principally at the General Directorate of Customs (GDC) before comprehensive, timely, and reliable statements can be produced. The National Statistics Law of 1960 and Legislative Decree issued in 1968 concerning the CBStat and their subsequent amendments regulate, among other things, the responsibility of the CBStat regarding the collection and compilation of external trade data. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The Trade and Prices Directorate (TPD) receives the data based on the Harmonized System Codes (HS) classified by sections, chapters, and commodities. Data are further reclassified according to the SITC (revision 3) and BEC classifications. The data are also classified by main countries of international blocks and countries of origin and destination. Scope of the data The external trade data cover all imports, including goods in transit and temporary admissions, exports, and re-exported items. The external trade statistics also cover goods imported or exported via parcel post. However, some imports such as those for military purposes are not compiled as these are considered confidential. 2.4.1 Valuation The GDC records export data on an f.o.b. basis and imports on a c.i.f. basis 2.4.2 Recording basis As usual for customs, the data collection system for external trade is set up to record goods as they move across borders, or when customs duties are paid. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The Customs declaration form, the major source of data, reflects the HS starting in July 2001. The data from the customs forms are compiled manually at the borders and copies of these forms are sent from all borders to the GCD in Damascus on a weekly basis. In 2004, the GCD started discussions with the UNDP, the UNCTAD, and the EU on providing assistance in the area of computer software and hardware, training of staff, and setting up of an electronic network among all the customs points in the country and the GCD. The idea was to have computerized working stations at each customs point where the data in the custom forms will be entered on an immediate and continuous basis (see also plans for improvement). This arrangement is currently in process and the Automated System for Customs Data (ASYCUDA) has recently been implemented in one customs point. Additional data on imports and exports provided by the Ministry of Oil are added to the customs data. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Generally, the GCD submits data to the CBStat in two-three months after the reference month. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The data are entered into computers manually in the GCD by the CBStat staff for further processing. The hardware and software used for data processing are adequate. Computer and manual edits are performed to ensure the accuracy of the data. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The TPD makes corrections to the data only when there are serious concerns about the value of a certain item. These corrections are discussed with the GCD. The TPD makes broad adjustments to the reported data based on recommendations developed by the interagency Statistical Committee on Foreign Trade Statistics. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request External trade data, when ready, may be provided upon request.
