 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: The United Kingdom (UK) External Debt data are compiled according to the IMF's ‘External Debt Statistics: guide for compilers and users' ( Guide ) . The data are consistent with those contained in the UK's International investment position (IIP), which are compiled in accordance with the methodology set out in the sixth edition of the IMF's ‘ Balance of Payments Manual ' (BPM6) and the System of National Accounts 2008 (SNA08). The criteria used for determining whether a liability is a debt instrument or not is the future requirement to make payments—principal and/or interest: External debt data are largely produced from survey data designed for national accounts and balance of payments purposes. These data are collected with sufficient instrument detail to determine the nature of the liability. External debt liabilities payable in goods and/or services are not included in the external debt position. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization These data are broken down by: sector; type of instrument; original maturity of instrument. Direct investment liabilities are separately identified. The data are compiled in accordance with the methodology set out in the sixth edition of the IMF's ‘ Balance of Payments Manual ' . External debt data are classified into four sectors of the BPM6 (general government, monetary authorities, monetary financial institutions, and other sectors). The sector classification is in line with BPM6 and External Debt Guide requirements. In particular: External debt liabilities of households and small private enterprises are included under ‘other sectors'. External debt liabilities are attributed to the sector of the immediate borrower. External debt liabilities are classified under the sector category of the debtor and not of any guarantor. Deviation from these international guidelines for sector classification is as follows: Monetary authorities are separately identified in the external debt data. However, at present, these data are not separately identified in the UK's Balance of Payments and National Accounts (they are contained in the general government and MFI sectors' data. These data published show gross debt liabilities to non-residents as defined in the IMF's External Debt Statistics: guide for compilers and users. In particular: Deposits of non-residents with domestic banks are included. Instruments included under ‘other debt liabilities' are: net equity of households in life insurance reserves and pension funds; prepayments of premiums and reserves against outstanding claims. Trade credits owed to non-residents are included. Intercompany lending between entities in a direct investment relationship are separately identified in the total external debt position. Arrears cannot be separately identified under ‘short-term, other debt liabilities'. Scope of the data Coverage: End period stocks of external liabilities are classified according to institutional sector (General Government, Monetary Authorities, Monetary Financial Institutions, and Other sectors); type of instrument; original maturity of instrument. Direct investment liabilities are separately identified. The international investment activity of households is partly estimated. External debt data are compiled on the basis of covering debt positions between residents and non-residents. Debt liabilities of the offshore enterprises —including banks—that are resident in the reporting economy are not included in the gross external debt position, as this is not applicable to the UK. Private sector debt are included in the gross external debt position under banks and/or other sectors (as it corresponds); as such, debt liabilities are correctly classified to the appropriate sector in line with balance of payments data. External debt data measure both domestic and foreign currency debt owed to non-residents. All data on intercompany lending between entities in a direct investment relationship that are collected by the UK are included in the gross external debt position. The coverage of short-term debt includes data on short-term loan liabilities, issues of commercial paper, currency and deposit liabilities, trade credit liabilities and miscellaneous other short-term liabilities. These data on arrears are included implicitly (in that arrears of debt will remain in the gross positions). The UK is unable to separately identify arrears. Positions in financial derivatives, equity securities, and equity capital are excluded from the gross external debt position. External debt includes securities issued in the domestic markets by residents and purchased by non-residents. 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to unit of account: Stock positions originally denominated in foreign currencies should be converted to pounds sterling at the mid-point of the buying and selling exchange rates applying at the beginning or end of the period. Survey respondents are asked to provide data on this basis, but in practice the actual rate used varies according to the source of the data. Valuation: Foreign financial liabilities are generally collected at market prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis Outstanding liabilities arising from periodic interest costs that have accrued and are not yet payable are not included in the gross external debt position. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Debt securities: Data for government bonds are obtained from administrative sources; data relating to issues by banks and monetary authorities are supplied by the Bank of England; other sectors debt security liabilities are derived from ONS inquiries. Trade credits: Limited information on trade credit is obtained from ONS surveys and administrative sources. Loans: Government loans data is taken from administrative sources; in addition to ONS inquiries, other sectors' borrowing from banks abroad is derived from data supplied by the Bank for International Settlements (BIS), and from administrative data from the European Investment Bank (EIB). Currency and Deposits: Banks and monetary authorities deposit liabilities data are supplied by the Bank of England. Other debt liabilities: Government data is taken from administrative sources; other sectors' liabilities are derived from ONS inquiries Inter-company loans: Data is derived from ONS inquiries. Direct Investments: Stocks of direct investment abroad and in the UK are collected by quarterly and annual surveys run by the ONS and the Bank of England. The surveys collect net asset values attributable to investing companies. Up to 2005 Q4, quarterly stocks data were based on accumulating flows to the last annual balance sheet, revalued to take account of exchange rate movements. From 2006 Q1, balance sheet information is collected on a quarterly basis. Portfolio investment: Foreign holdings of UK issued equity is based on a biannual Share Ownership Survey of share registers. Levels of British government stocks held by non-residents are measured from banking statistics and other Bank of England sources including the General Gilt Office. Inward investment in bonds and notes issued by UK monetary financial institutions and other sectors are estimated from information derived from the Bank of England and London Stock Exchange records of UK company bond issues. Estimates of outstanding UK equity and debt liabilities are compared with the results of the IMF Coordinated Portfolio Investment Survey. Other investment: Trade credit, loans and other assets are based on a range of Bank of England and ONS surveys together with some government administrative data. Other investment in the UK is based on banking data, Bank for International Settlement (BIS) data, information on borrowing from the European Investment Bank (EIB), and administrative data on the deposit liabilities of UK central government. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording For most instruments, data available to allow disaggregation of ‘other sectors' into three sub-sectors (non-bank financial corporations, non-financial corporations, and NPISH). A full sector breakdown of liabilities in debt securities is not available. Data are not available to allow disaggregation into public sector external debt and publicly guaranteed private sector debt 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Full details of UK concepts, sources and methods are published on the ONS website. A wide variety of verification procedures are used during data collection and compilation. Company balance sheets are checked against previous estimates for consistency; flows and levels are checked for reconciliation problems at aggregate level; balance of payments stock data are fully reconciled with UK national accounts; balance sheets and income data are validated using rates of return analysis. Debt securities: The results of the Coordinated Portfolio Investment Survey are used to validate foreign holdings of UK issued debt securities. Loans: Data supplied by BIS are used to supplement ONS inquiry data for non-bank borrowing from banks abroad. Currency and deposits: Data for the bank sector are consistent with monetary and financial statistics. Other liabilities: Estimations are made to include liabilities of pension funds and life insurance companies to their non-resident participants and policyholders. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Financial leases with non-residents are treated as loans and included in the gross external debt position. Traded debt instruments issued by residents abroad, but in hands of residents are excluded from the gross external debt position. This is achieved as follows: Total UK liabilities in traded debt securities are largely drawn from administrative sources that relate only to UK issues. Non-resident holdings are largely derived as the residual of total issues less domestic sector take-up. Domestic sector transactions are measured via surveys. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Revisions are published quarterly in the ‘ Quarterly Balance of Payments Statistical Bulletin ' and ‘United Kingdom Economic Accounts' giving information on the scale and reason for revision. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Quarterly and annual data are available. SDDS Table 2 (encouraged table on domestic/foreign currency breakdown) is not prepared, as a full currency breakdown of liabilities is not available. SDDS Table 3 (encouraged table on debt-service payment schedule) would not include payments on noncurrent and/or contingent debt liabilities in the debt service payments.
