 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions All the data have been converted and are presented in accordance with the GFSM 2014 framework as recommended by the IMF. The National Government Debt is published, with all the relevant underlying tables such as Ownership Distribution of Domestic Marketable Bonds of National Government, Redemption Schedule of Domestic Marketable Bonds, Interest Payment Schedule of Domestic Marketable Bonds, Domestic Marketable Bonds by Unexpired Maturity, Redemption Schedule of Foreign Debt as well as Interest Payment Schedule of Foreign Debt. Marketable debt of national government at market value is calculated based on the dirty price (all-in-price), which includes accrued interest and is also published in the Quarterly Bulletin. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Government debt is classified broadly according to the IMF's A Manual on Government Finance Statistics, 1986 before September 2003. From September 2003 data are compiled according to the IMF's Government Finance Statistics Manual 2014 . The monthly data reflect the total stock of outstanding contractual obligations of the national government classified according to the debt instruments used and marketability. The domestic marketable government bonds and marketable foreign debt are also classified according to the outstanding maturity of the debt. The distribution of debt among holders in different sub-sectors of the economy is partly available. The data available to the SARB is for the Central Bank, Public Investment Corporation (PIC) and Deposit-taking corporations. The non-monetary private sector includes: non-financial corporations, households and non-profit institutions servicing households; and other financial corporations for the domestic and foreign sectors. All the data are disseminated in millions of local currency (rand) and the distinction between domestic and foreign debt is made according to the currency in which debt was issued. Scope of the data The debt data include: Outstanding debt securities and loans of national government (i.e. the budgetary central government excluding social security funds and extra-budgetary institutions). From April 1994 onward, outstanding debt data include debt of the former Transkei, Bophuthatswana, Venda, Ciskei and self-governing states that was assumed by the national government in terms of Section 239 of the interim Constitution of the Republic of South Africa, Act No. 200 of 1993. From May 1997 onwards, outstanding debt data also include the liability assumed for the payment of the debt incurred by the Republic of Namibia (the former South West Africa) as contemplated in subsection (1) of Section 52 (c) of the Exchequer Act No. 66 of 1975, as amended, and From April 2002 the South African Housing Trust in terms of the disestablishment of the South African Housing Trust Limited Act, No. 26 of 2002. The balance on the Gold and Foreign Exchange Contingency Reserve Account (GFECRA) is not a debt instrument issued by the government, and is therefore not included in the debt statistics but shown as a memorandum item. The Gold Price Adjustment Account, the Foreign Exchange Adjustment Account and the Forward Exchange Contracts Adjustment Account were established and are managed by the Bank on behalf of the National Treasury. At the close of each financial year of the Bank or at such other times as the SARB and the National Treasury may determine, any credit or debit balances on these accounts are transferred to the GFECRA. In addition to its direct indebtedness, the national government is also a guarantor of certain third-party indebtedness. The national government issues formal contractual guarantees in respect of certain indebtedness of wholly or partially state-owned companies (SOCs). These guarantees are added as memorandum items in the debt tables since they only constitute debt once the SOCs fail to meet their obligations. Exceptions to coverage Data on the liabilities of social security funds are only available on a quarterly and annual basis. Unrecorded activity Data are comprehensive for the recording of the two national government debt instruments (bonds and loans) and no unrecorded activity is observed. 2.4.1 Valuation Data are disseminated in millions of local currency (rand) on the total gross outstanding debt of the National Government (that is, budgetary central government, excluding the social security funds and extra-budgetary institutions). Outstanding debt is valued at the amount the government is obligated to pay when the debt matures, that is, at its nominal, par, or face value. Debt denominated in foreign currencies is valued at appropriate foreign exchange rates as at the end of each period. Both domestic and foreign bonds of national government are revaluated on a monthly basis. The valuation is done at face value and market value. However, no Statement of other economic flows is currently compiled. 2.4.2 Recording basis Government debt is recorded at the time the borrowing takes place, i.e. when payment is made, and the removal of maturing obligations from the outstanding debt is recorded when government payment or exchange for new obligations occurs. Non-cash generation or extinction of debt is recorded when the debt is created or cancelled. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures All relevant data are recorded on a gross basis. This allows for data to be netted out where necessary. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs All debt data are compiled from administrative records kept by the National Treasury. The National Treasury maintains a Public Debt Register from which the gross loan transactions are derived. These transactions are documented in the Statement of the National Government's Revenue, Expenditure and Borrowing , which is published on a monthly frequency in the Government Gazette and forms the basis of the analysis of central government gross debt by the SARB. Unpublished debt data is also available from National Treasury to supplement SARB's analysis. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording All the data has been converted and is reported in accordance with the GFSM 2014 framework as recommended by the IMF. The data are being reported according to this framework with the exception of other economic flows . All relevant data are recorded on a gross basis. This allows for data to be netted out where necessary. The Budget Review has definitions for some of the categories. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The debt is measured as on the last working day of each month, fiscal year and calendar year. Section 32 of the PFMA requires that the data be made available within 30 days after the end of each month. An advance release calendar can be accessed through the internet on: https://www.resbank.co.za/en/home/calendar/2021-ADVANCE-RELEASE-CALENDAR . It indicates the date and time of the release of data to the public and also to be posted on the website for the SDDS. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The balancing of debt data with the deficit and financing on the Statement of the National Government's Revenue, Expenditure and Borrowing serves as a crosscheck of the data. All debt data are subject to an annual audit by the Office of the Auditor-General of South Africa(AGSA) 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques No estimations are required, as the accounts are comprehensive. All the data has been converted and is presented according to the GFSM 2014 framework as recommended by the IMF. The data is being reported according to this framework with the exception of other economic flows. All relevant data are recorded on a gross basis. This allows for data to be netted out where necessary. The Budget Review of the National Treasury has definitions for some of the categories. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Domestic debt totals are adjusted for revaluation on inflation-linked bonds. Foreign debt data are adjusted by the average exchange rates and for revaluation gains and losses on conversion of foreign loans. Foreign marketable bonds are also converted and published in market values in the Quarterly Bulletin using the dirty price (all-in price), excluding accrued interest, with debt denominated in foreign currencies converted to rand at the period end's 14:30 foreign exchange rate. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The (GFECR) as recorded in the Bank's balance sheet, is a contingent asset (-)/liability (+) of National Treasury. Since 2017, this account has been reported monthly as month-end amounts. Prior to this, month-end amounts were not available and the audited amount as at the end of the previous fiscal year was kept constant in the monthly time series for the subsequent fiscal year. For financial guarantees by the government amounts in the accounts of the National Treasury are kept constant for the following three months. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Data on central government debt are compared against amounts in the records of the National Treasury. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Data should balance to the amounts in the records of the National Treasury. The domestic marketable bonds must balance to the Interest Schedule; domestic non-marketable debt as well as the foreign marketable and non-marketable bonds should balance to the Borrowing Statement and Total state debt. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Changes and revisions to data are effected retrospectively i.e changes and revisions are done as and when they happen. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Revised data are presented immediately by the Bank to ensure that the data reported by the two agencies are the same. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Requests are received telephonically or via email by institutions such as the BIS, World Bank, private institutions, general public and scholars. The requests are placed on record and disseminated accordingly as mentioned in 0.1.3. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Details of contact persons are disseminated on the metadata pages. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs None. However, a list of all publications of the Bank is sent to the outside agencies with the application forms for inclusion on the distribution list. The list also contains the prices charged for the respective publications.
