 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Definition: External trade data are compiled according to the special trade system, i.e. goods are covered when they enter the free circulation area or are exported out of the free circulation area. The free circulation area coincides with the country's territory with the exception of customs free zones and customs warehouses. Relationship of national practices with international or regional guidelines: Basically the 1982 edition of the UN International Merchandise Trade Statistics: Concepts and Definitions (IMTSCD) is taken into consideration. In line with the 1998 edition, "returned goods" were included in coverage since 1998. No other changes according to the 1998 IMTSCD have been implemented. Since 1996, the national customs legislation has been harmonized with the customs legislation of the EU (new Customs Law, Combined Nomenclature, Single Administrative Document). The difference between Community (Eurostat) and national figures is due to different concept of coverage: external trade statistics published by Eurostat includes also data, declared on SADs by companies that are not identified for VAT in Slovenia, while national external trade statistics covers only trade of companies identified for VAT in Slovenia. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: Combined Nomenclature (9-digit code, EU CN-8 + 1-digit national extension, after the accession to the EU, CN-8 and Taric) is used for collection and dissemination of data since 1996. Prior to 1996 the national Customs Tariff Nomenclature based on Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS) was used. In 2001 a 2-digit national extension to CN was introduced to formally agree with the European Taric. Since accession to the EU, the EU Combined Nomenclature (CN-8 digit code) and TARIC are used. For dissemination of data several other classifications are also used: (i) Standard International Trade Classification; (ii) Standard Classification of Activities based on the EU NACE Rev. 1 from 1997 to 2007; NACE Rev. 2 is used from 2008 on (prior to 1997 the old National Classification of Activities); (iii) ISO 3166 standard for countries is used until 2005, since 2006 Geonomenclature is in use; (iv) ISO 4217 standard for currencies; (v) national Classification of End-use was used until the end of 2006; (vi) national Classification of the Stage of Processing was used until the end of 2006, and (vii) Classification by Broad Economic Categories is in use since 2007 (data series by BEC was recalculated for the period from 2000 on). Scope of the data Transactions coverage: External trade transactions are covered (the 1982 edition of the IMTSCD is followed) according to the special trade system (relaxed definition) with processing (also processing in free zones) included. Only transactions for which a customs declaration has been issued are covered. In Intrastat the value of intra-community trade of companies above the exemption threshold is covered. Trading value of companies below the exemption threshold is estimated. Geographic coverage: No trade with specific countries/territories is excluded. Exceptions to coverage External trade does not cover goods for or following temporary use, services, goods for and after repair, means of payment which are legal tender and securities, monetary gold, advertising material and commercial samples when supplied free of charge, goods used as carriers of customised information, software downloaded from the Internet and exports or imports carried out by foreign diplomatic representatives. Unrecorded activity Unrecorded activity: The potentially under-reported or missing trade (e.g., smuggling activity or shuttle trade) is not estimated. 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to unit of account: The data are published in euros (from 2002 on), U.S. dollars and Slovenian tolars (until the end of 2006). Extrastat: until 23 April 1999 the Customs administration [1] used the so-called weekly exchange rates to convert the invoice value into the value in the national currency. From this date on the monthly exchange rates have been used by the customs. The Statistical Office recalculates the statistical value from the customs declaration to the value at the current exchange rate valid on the day of submission of the customs declaration or the date which is used for implementing customs regulations (the date of the submission of a simplified declaration). The statistical value at the current exchange rate in national currency (EUR) is then recalculated to the statistical value in US dollars using the current exchange rate of the USD. All the rates used are average rates published by the Bank of Slovenia. Weekly rate referred to the average rate valid on Friday every week, which was used by the Financial administration for the whole week from next Monday to Sunday. Monthly rate refers to the average rate valid on Thursday the week before the last week in the previous month that is used by the Financial administration in the next month. (The terms "weekly" and "monthly" are used in the sense that "the same rate is used for a whole week, month"). Intrastat: the average monthly exchange rate of the Bank of Slovenia is used for the conversion of invoice and statistical value expressed in euros to USD. Valuation: FOB values are used for exports and CIF values are used for imports. In Extrastat the statistical value of goods in euros at the Slovenian border is calculated by the declarant by (1) converting the invoice value into euros using the exchange rate applied by the Financial office, and (2) adjusting it for the insurance and freight costs depending on the delivery terms set out in the contract. This statistical value is then recalculated in the Statistical office to the value at the daily (current) exchange rate. The Financial office adjusts invoice value when it is believed to be over- or under-valued, according to the Article VII of GATT. Also, in cases where no invoice is issued (e.g. commodity aid), the principles of the Article VII of GATT are followed. [1] Customs administration acted until 31.12.2014. After that the Financial Administration of the Republic of Slovenia was established by merging the Customs Administration of the Republic of Slovenia and the Tax Administration of the Republic of Slovenia. 2.4.2 Recording basis Timing of recording transactions: In Extrastat, transactions are recorded according to the day of submission of the customs declaration or the date that is used for implementing customs regulations. Data supplements and data corrections are recorded on the date of the original transaction. In Intrastat, transactions are included in the reporting month according to the date of the issued invoice or the date of the actual arrival of dispatch of goods. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Before the accession to the EU, the data were based on customs declarations only. After May 1, 2004, the data are based on customs declarations for trade with non EU countries (Extrastat) and on Intrastat forms for trade with EU Member States (Intrastat). The special trade system (relaxed definition) is followed. Sources: Extrastat: customs declarations (Single Administrative Document). Intrastat: data are collected directly from companies liable to report for Intrastat on statistical forms. Basis of country data: Arrivals (imports) from EU member states are classified according to the country of consignment while imports from non-EU countries are classified by the country of origin. Exports to non-EU member countries and dispatches to the EU member states are classified according to the country of destination. Due to the adjustment to Intrastat methodology in the imports data broken down by countries for the period from January 2000 to April 2004 (the period before the accession to the EU), the country of consignment is taken into account if it was an EU country and the country of origin respectively in the opposite cases. Data on imports broken down by countries are thus not comparable to the data which were published previously for these periods. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Companies reporting to Intrastat report the invoice value of goods in euros. Companies which are above the statistical value threshold also report the statistical value of goods in euros. The statistical value of goods for companies, which are below the statistical value threshold, is estimated by SURS since 2009. Type of data collected: Customs tariff nomenclature code, statistical value in euros, quantities in net mass and supplementary unit when requested by the customs tariff nomenclature (CN), nature of transaction, delivery terms, country of origin/destination/consignment, means of transport, customs procedure, etc. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Adjustments to improve coverage/classification/timing/valuation: Extrastat: no adjustments are made. The Statistical Office performs formal controls of coded data and logical controls of value and quantity data. Only some less important types of errors are corrected automatically. Each month, the customs declarations are ranked according to statistical value and those with the biggest values are controlled (usually about 1,000 declarations). If they are suspected to be erroneous, they are sent back to the Financial administration where they are additionally checked and in case of error corrected. Before the release of the final annual data, all high value transactions are checked for consistency. Intrastat: the companies liable to report for Intrastat in a given reporting year are the companies that exceed the exemption threshold in the preceding year for one or both flows of goods (flow of goods is total exports-dispatches or total imports-arrivals to/from EU member states). In a given reporting year, companies that have exceeded the threshold during that year are included. The value of the exemption threshold are provided in the methodological explanations. Companies report only for the flow of goods for which the threshold was exceeded. This method includes more than 97 percent of the total value of the trade with EU member states. Data for EU member states, which refer to the period from May 2004 on, include also estimated value of trade for business entities below the assimilation threshold. Monthly data for the current year are provisional and are updated throughout the year. Extrastat: supplements and data corrections made by the Financial office are included in the month of observation according to the date of submission of the original customs declaration or the date valid for the implementation of the customs regulations of the original customs declaration. Intrastat: transactions are included in the reporting month according to the date of the invoice or the date of the actual arrival of dispatch of goods. Annual data are published twice, the first time as provisional data with regular time delay and the second time as final data (usually six months after the end of the reference period). At this time also, monthly data become final. Processing system: Extrastat: the Financial administration uses a national automated system. The Statistical Office has developed its own computer applications for data control and other data processing. Intrastat: for collecting and performing controls, both institutions use the same IT system. Site of processing: Extrastat: Financial Administration and Statistical Office. The Financial office collects the data (mostly electronically) and prepares selected data from customs declarations in the predefined records for the Statistical Office. Intrastat: according to the agreement between Financial Administration and the Statistical Office, Financial Administration collects data and performs basic checks, while the Statistical Office performs additional more complex checks of data (e.g. checks with other comparable data sources). 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Country or commodity details excluded on confidentiality grounds: Some exclusions are made. Military goods are actively confidentialised, i.e., only the two-digit HS code without country breakdown is used. For a few other goods, the principle of passive confidentiality is applied upon special request by some companies (low number of importing/exporting companies for a given product/country combination). Seasonal adjustment: The data are not seasonally adjusted. Geographic detail: Data broken down by country of origin/destination are available for the years before the accession to the EU. For imports, data by country of consignment are also available. After the accession (introduction of the Intrastat system), data broken down by country of destination (for exports), by country of origin (for imports from non-EU countries) and by country of consignment (for arrivals from the EU countries) are available. With regard to import data for the period from January 2000 to April 2004 (the period before the accession to the EU), the country of consignment is taken into account due to the adjustment to Intrastat methodology if it was an EU country and the country of origin respectively in other cases. Commodity detail: Data broken down by the most detailed level of the customs tariff nomenclature and by the 5-digit SITC are available. Price and volume measures: Chain Fisher unit value indices of exports and imports (with previous year = 100) used to be published quarterly according to the 2-digit SITC classification. From 1999 they are published also by 2-digit Standard Classification of Activities (NACE) and by BEC from year 2001 to year 2016. The volume indices were calculated by dividing the value indices by the Fisher unit value indices. Other: Data are compiled and published in euros (from 2002 on), U.S. dollars, and Slovenian tolars (until the end of 2006). The data in U.S. dollars and euros (data before 2007) reflect strongly the change of intercurrency relations. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Further detail is available, subject to the confidentiality provisions of the Law on National Statistics, on request from the contact point.
