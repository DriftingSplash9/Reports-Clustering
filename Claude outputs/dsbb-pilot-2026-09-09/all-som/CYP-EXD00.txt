 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions External debt statistics are compiled in accordance with the standards set forth in the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6). Furthermore, specific compilation recommendations from relevant international bodies are observed. In particular, the ECB and Eurostat ( Guideline ECB/2011/23 of 9 December 2011, as amended, and related " European Union balance of payments/international investment position statistical methods " ("b.o.p. and i.i.p. book"), and Commission Regulation (EU) No 184/2005 of 12 January 2005 as amended). The same concepts that are included in the External Debt Guide (e.g. maturity, residency, accrual principle, etc) are also followed by the CBC for the external debt compilation. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification / sectorization used to compile Cyprus's balance of payments is essentially the one set in the BPM6 and ESA 2010. For institutional unit definition, the general principles on institutional units (i.e. the centre of economic interest, decision-making autonomy, and complete set of accounts) are applied. SPEs and artificial subsidiaries are treated as follows: The statistical coverage of SPEs is in line with the conclusions of the Eurostat/ECB/OECD 2013 Task Force on head offices, holding companies and SPEs as well as the recommendations of the final report of the relevant IMF's Task Force. Artificial subsidiaries, defined as entities which: Are wholly owned by a parent corporation. Lack the ability to act independently from their parent corporations and may be subject to restrictions on their ability to hold or transact assets held on their balance sheets. Are treated as an integral part of the parent, i.e. not as a separate institutional unit and their accounts are consolidated with those of the parent, unless they are resident in an economic territory different from that of the parent. Regarding geographical allocation, the general principles laid down in the BPM6 on country allocation are applied. In cases where the counterpart country is not known, the "not allocated" code is used. For the allocation to institutional sectors, the CBC and Cystat are cooperating closely to create a "Common Registry" for statistical purposes and to allocate units into institutional sectors for the compilation of external statistics and national accounts. In Cyprus, monetary authorities, other monetary financial institutions (MFIs), government, captive financial as well as non-financial SPEs (ship-owning companies) account for the vast majority of financial cross-border activity. Captive financial institutions form part of a relatively important institutional sector in Cyprus. They are categorised under sector S127, which mainly consists of SPEs, as defined in relevant IMF's Task Force. More precisely, an enterprise is usually considered as an SPE if it meets the following criteria: It is resident in an economy i.e. it is a formally registered and/or incorporated legal entity recognized as an institutional unit, with: no or little employment up to maximum of three employees1, no or little physical presence and no or little physical production in the host economy. It is directly or indirectly controlled by non-residents. It is established to obtain specific advantages provided by the host jurisdiction with an objective to: grant its owner(s) access to capital markets or sophisticated financial services; and/or isolate owner(s) from financial risks; and/or reduce regulatory and tax burden; and/or safeguard confidentiality of their transactions and owner(s). It transacts almost entirely with non-residents and a large part of their financial balance sheet typically consists of cross-border claims and liabilities. 2.2.1 Scope The classification / sectorization used to compile Cyprus's balance of payments is essentially the one set in the BPM6 and ESA 2010. Scope of the data In principle, external debt statistics produced by the CBC cover all non-equity financial liabilities positions between residents and nonresidents. Concerning residency definition, the general principles on residency, as laid down in the BPM6 manual, are applied. Special purpose entities (SPEs), with the exception of shipping companies for which a special regime is applied (economic ownership), are considered residents if registered/incorporated in Cyprus. Exceptions to coverage In practice, constraints on data sources prevent full coverage for external debt statistics in some areas. One such example is external transactions (except travel) and financial stocks of resident households which may not be fully covered although ongoing efforts aim to achieve this, such as the use of mirror data. In case debt securities from SHS are not listed (this concerns only a very small minority), then the companies report them based on: the net present value of the expected stream of future payments/receipts associated with the securities, or for unlisted securities, the price used to value securities for accounting or regulatory purposes, or for deep discount or zero coupon securities, the issue price plus amortisation of the discount, or for debt instruments issued at a premium, the issue price less the amortisation of the premium. Debt securities holdings collected directly from the reporting entities through the "External Financial Statistics" (EFS) survey as well as through their financial statements are recorded at the price depicted in their financial statements. Loans and deposits are valued in nominal terms. Trade credits and other receivables/ payables are collected based on their balance sheet value (this concerns data collected through the EFS survey, BSI, financial statements). 2.4.2 Recording basis In general, the Cyprus external debt statistics are compiled and recorded according to the accrual principle. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Grossing and netting procedures are consistent with BPM6 recommendations. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The compilation of Cyprus's external debt is based, primarily, on direct reporting by resident entities. The data collected is supplemented by surveys conducted by other organisations, relevant administrative sources, annual financial statements and estimates. External debt statistics rely mostly on actual data (either collected on a monthly or on a quarterly basis), except in some cases where data are only available on an annual frequency (mainly data extracted from audited financial statements of resident entities). The CBC applies a correction/estimation procedure for non-response whenever relevant reports are sent late or are of poor quality. The extent to which this procedure is used is limited (in terms of value) since the CBC staff is making considerable efforts to collect the required data from "big players" within the prescribed deadlines. As regards sampling and calibration, there is a complete coverage by existing data sources for a number of sectors, namely, MFIs (S122), insurance companies (S128) and investment funds (S124). For the remaining sectors, although the target is to include all relevant companies, at least the companies that constitute the substantial majority of each sector are covered by our existing data sources. A comprehensive description of the data collection system for the i.i.p. is given below. Monetary authorities sector : the Payments and Settlement section and Accounting Services Department of the CBC provides the monthly balance sheet of the CBC, from which data on reserve assets, external debt and other required items are obtained. Government : the Ministry of Finance provides data on the government external debt. As regards EMTN issues, the market values are extracted at each month's end from the CSDB. Banks: the Money and Banking Statistics and Financial Accounts Section provides MFIs' monthly balance sheet returns on a monthly basis which covers all related instruments apart from debt securities. In the case of debt securities, the stocks are provided by each reporting MFI and this is linked with the CSDB in order to extract the market value. Other sectors : the collection of data for the other sectors is obtained from various sources, such as: The CBC's quarterly survey "External Financial Statitics" A survey addressed to credit acquiring companies (census) A survey addressed to mineral exploration companies (census) Surveys and assumptions made for the shipping sector (both ship-management and shipowning). Annual financial statements. A survey addressed to investment funds (QIFS). SEC issues data for data on debt securities issued by Cypriot residents and listed on euro area's stock exchange. The Insurance Companies Control Service which provides information on data for the insurance sector (including data on insurance, pensions and standardised guarantee schemes). The investment funds survey which provides data on assets and liabilities of investment funds.Information on data for households' loans abroad is collected from the Bank of International Settlements (BIS) data as well as from SDW (mirror data). 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The source data definitions, scope, classification, valuation and time of recording are generally in line with BPM6. (For deviations please refer to 2.2.1.2. and 2.4.1.). 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Broadly speaking, the source data for compiling the external debt statistics are timely. In cases where data are not available within a certain time lag, preliminary estimates are made. The timiliness is especially an issue for data collected from financial statements as this is done on an annual basis and with a delay (mainly has to do with SPEs data). 3.2.1 Source data assessment The questionnaire forms used in the surveys are designed by the CBC and are revised to reflect developments in international standards and accounting standards. The CBC also regularly revises the reporting instructions to reporting entities. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Corrections and adjustments on the reported data are made only when required or when confirmed with the reporting entities. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data A reconciliation of the stocks, transactions and flows is performed on a quarterly basis. The behavior of back series is assessed against current related series. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Comparisons are made using mirror data. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses External debt statistics are available on the CBC's website , usually within five working days after the dissemination to the ECB and Eurostat. The initially released data are subject to revisions. The revision policy of the CBC includes three types of revisions: The routine/regular revisions, which concern quarterly and annual data and occur twice a year as follows: In March, whereby monthly and quarterly data of the previous year (t-1) are revised, and In September, whereby monthly, quarterly and annual data of the previous three years (t-1, t-2 and t-3) as well as the current year's (t) data are revised. The first revision of the provisional data of the first quarter in year t is published when data for the second quarter of year t are published (that is, in each September of year t). Subsequently, a first revision of data of the second and the third quarter of year t along with a second revision of the first quarter of year t is published when the data of the fourth quarter of year t is published (that is, each March in year t+1). Regarding annual data, a first revision of the provisional data of year t is made in September of year t+1, then the second revision, in September of year t+2 and then the third and final revision, in September of year t+3. When revised annual data is published, revisions are made on quarterly data as well. The major revisions, which may be regular, usually every five years, and aim to incorporate results of changes in basic data sources (this is currently practically done from 2008 onwards, which was the year in which Cyprus joined the euro area). The ad-hoc revisions, which originate from methodological changes or special events and usually take place in the year in which they appear.
