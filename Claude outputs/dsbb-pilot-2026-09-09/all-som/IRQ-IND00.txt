 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The Index Number of Output in the Manufacturing Industry measures the change in quantity of production in manufacturing industry. The Laspeyres formula is used in the index calculations. CSO prepares volume and revenue indices for the water and electric power industries separately based on data from the Water Utility and the National Electric Power Company. The CSO Index Numbers Department also produces an annual Agricultural Output Index based on survey information from the CSO Agricultural Statistics Department. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Industries are classified using ISIC, Revision 4 (7 digits). Scope of the data Unit Coverage The manufacturing output price index and manufacturing output index are produced using the same data on establishments and products. Transaction Coverage The manufacturing output index excludes all mining and extractive activities, including for oil and gas. However, it includes manufacturing activities downstream from extraction, for example, refining and chemical production. The indices exclude public utilities such as water, power distribution and generation, and sewage and sanitation. Exceptions to coverage · Geographic Coverage: The index excludes Northern governorates in Kurdistan. Unrecorded activity 2.4.1 Valuation Output prices implicit in the index weights are "Factory Gate." They correspond with the basic price concept of the System of National Accounts 1993 . Input prices are "purchaser prices," including taxes less subsidies on products. 2.4.2 Recording basis The recorded quantities are of the specified quarter of output which are compared with the corresponding items quantities on the base year of 2012 . 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The weights of the output index for each 2-digit industry are proportional to the total value added of output for each industry in the base year 2012 . For 2-digit industries, there is no netting of weights to produce, for example, a value added weight for each product. While the weight of each item is the relative importance for each item inside the 2-digit industry multiplied by the weight of industry. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The concerned quarter quantities by product are derived from an industrial establishment survey conducted quarterly. The survey is covering all large industrial establishments which amounted to 600 establishments where 76 are governments and 524 are private or mixed activity establishments .The sample products(the same of output price index) consists of 263 products distributed on 18 divisions of ISIC 4 after excluding one division due to its small relative importance ,the chosen products account for 98.9% of total manufacturing industries output. There are three strata in each 2-digit industrial activity: large (more than 30 employees), medium (10 to 29 employees), and small (fewer than 10 employees) establishments. The survey questionnaires and survey methodology updated on 2007. The Industrial Statistics Department of CSO undertakes the industrial survey by personal visits during each quarter. The survey methodology followed is for the enumerator to visit the establishment, leave the form for the respondent to complete, and return to pick up the form when it is completed. Respondents are asked for the simple average price and the simple average quantity over the previous quarter for each sampled product. The form collects information on output products on one side and input products on the other. Response rates are very high, but data considered to be of high quality is returned in about 80 percent of the cases. However, the prices and quantities establishments report can be subject to errors due to problems with using inconsistent units of measurement between establishments. For example, for a given product, some reporters may enter the price and quantity in units, while some may enter the same in tons. The Industrial Statistics and index numbers Departments consult with respondent establishments on issues they may have in participating in the survey. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The sources satisfy the definitions, scope, classification, valuation, and time of recording required for the output indices. The establishment and product samples support compilation of 2-digit industry output indices. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Source data are available within 2 months beyond the concerned quarter. 3.2.1 Source data assessment 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The enumerator checks the accuracy of respondent reports when returning to collect the completed form. Formal reviews of data are conducted when the forms are returned to the CSO office of the governorate. The CSO Governorate Director checks for unusual prices and quantities with the enumerator, who may in turn check with the respondent. If the report is deemed accurate, it is accepted without modification. The industry output indices for 2-digit industries use the long term relative form of the Laspeyres formula. For each product produced in each 2-digit industry, the shares are proportional to the value of output in 2012. For the current year and for 2012, the output quantities are, for each product used in each 2-digit industry, the total quantity produced across the large establishments in the industry. If a quality for the current quarter is missing, the item is deleted from the calculation of the index. None of the products is considered subject to seasonal availability. Quality adjustment methodology is employed in the output indices. There is no methodology for introducing new products into the output indices. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The base year of the output indices becomes 2012. The weights of the output price index has changed in 2012., which is the 2012 for the Laspeyres formula used in the production index. The index includes data from all establishments in the selected 2-digit industrial activities within the large size classes. There is no grossing up to cover the small establishments. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Although there are production statistics for electric power, water supply, and agriculture, comparisons of these series with manufacturing output are not considered informative. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Unusual movements in the outputs of industries, products, or establishments are investigated to ensure accuracy. However, these changes are not explained in press releases or other public disseminations. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Industry aggregates only are produced; there is no opportunity for statistical discrepancies across different breakdowns of the output indices. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses 5.1.5 Dissemination on request More detailed data on the Index Number of production in the Manufacturing Industry not routinely disseminated through CSO publications might be available to users upon request.
