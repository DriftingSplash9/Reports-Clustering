 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The LSB compiles its IMTS in accordance with the United Nations' the International Merchandise Trade Statistics: Concepts and Definitions 2010 (IMTS 2010) . Data are sourced from the Customs, which adopts “general trade system”. These documents are available at UNSD publications web site: http://unstats.un.org/unsd/pubs Concepts and Definitions: http://unstats.un.org/unsd/publication/SeriesM/SeriesM_52rev3e.pdf Compilers Manual: http://unstats.un.org/unsd/publication/seriesf/SeriesF_87Add1e.pdf 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Product classification : The LSB compiles the IMTS following product classification in accordance with the Association of South East Asian Nations (ASEAN) Harmonized Tariff Nomenclature (AHTN) 2012. The Harmonized System (HS) is a high-level international nomenclature owned by the World Customs Organization (WCO), comprising of 6 digits and used by the majority of trading nations. These exact same 6 digits form the first part of the 8-digit AHTN code. Lao PDR Customs authorities added 2 digits to the AHTN to form 10-digit national commodity code. Country classification : The LSB adopts ISO 3166 2-alpha digit country code. Exports are classified by country of last known destination, and imports are classified by country of origin. Customs Procedures Classification (CPC) : A national CPC is used to segregate different types of transactions, some of which are used to identify whether the transactions should be included or excluded from the IMTS (more detail in 2.2.1.1). 2.2.1 Scope Product classification : The LSB compiles the IMTS following product classification in accordance with the Association of South East Asian Nations (ASEAN) Harmonized Tariff Nomenclature (AHTN) 2012. Scope of the data Lao PDR’s IMTS comprises all goods that enter or leave Lao PDR's economic territory, and which add or subtract to/from the stock of the country's material resources. The economic territory corresponds to the Customs territory, in line with the UN requirements. Goods included - Non-monetary gold - Unissued banknotes and securities, and coins not in circulation - Goods traded on government account - Goods for military use (confidential) - Food and other humanitarian aid - Goods acquired by all categories of travelers, including non-resident workers to a significant scale as defined by law (except goods treated as part of trade in services) - Goods on consignment - Goods used as carriers of information and software. This category includes, for example, (a) packaged sets containing diskettes or CD-ROMs with stored computer software and/or data developed for general or commercial use (not to order), with or without a users' manual, and (b) audio- and videotapes recorded for general or commercial purposes. Tailored software to order, is not included in trade of merchandises - Goods for processing - Goods which cross borders as a result of transactions between parent corporations and their direct investment enterprises (affiliates/branches) - Goods transferred from or to a buffer stock organization - Goods under financial lease - Ships, aircraft and other mobile equipment. Note : Customs authorities register only ships or aircraft that are bought by Lao PDR companies. Once these companies have proceeded to customs clearance, they are allowed to register the ship or the aircraft in the books of port or airport. As the LSB do not use data sources for ships and aircraft other than Customs', only ships and aircraft which must be registered in Lao PDR port or airport are recorded in IMTS. No other sources such as national and international statutory instruments which govern civil aviation or the registration of aircraft or ships are used by the LSB. - Goods delivered to or dispatched from offshore installations located in the economic territory of Lao PDR. - Fish catch, minerals from the Mekong River and salvage landed from foreign vessels in Lao PDR ports or acquired by Lao PDR vessels on the Mekong River from foreign vessels - Bunkers, stores, ballast and dunnage - Empty bottles which represent a traded commodity - Waste and scrap (including those having no positive value) - Migrants' effects are included in Lao PDR's trade statistics and have a national procedure code of identification Goods excluded - Monetary gold - Issued banknotes and securities and coins in circulation - Goods temporarily admitted or dispatched (no change of ownership, limited time – duration not known, clarification required) - Goods consigned to and from territorial enclaves - Goods in transit - Goods treated as part of trade in services - Goods lost or destroyed after leaving the economic territory of the exporting country but before entering the economic territory of the intended importing country - Empty bottles which are returned to be refilled - Goods for repair - Ships, aircraft: see note in previous paragraph 'Goods included'. Exceptions to coverage - Electricity is yet to be included - Goods under operational lease may still be included (more information required to distinguish between operational versus financial lease). - Shuttle trade not already captured by the Customs Unrecorded activity No estimates are made for smuggling or other illegal transactions. 2.4.1 Valuation Lao PDR’s IMTS records the full value of the goods at the Lao PDR border. The statistical value of goods imported or exported after processing is the so-called gross value (i.e., it includes the value of exported/imported goods (materials) plus the value of the manufacturing service performed). The statistical value is sourced from the invoice value reported to the Customs. Imports are valued on a cost, insurance and freight (CIF) basis, which includes freight and insurance costs from the place of export in the exporting country to the Lao PDR border. Exports are valued on a free on board (FOB) basis, which includes only the cost of goods, and freight and insurance costs from the place of production in Lao PDR to the Lao PDR border. Quantities are in kilograms, and determined on a net weight basis. Valuation and weight are consistent with the World Trade Organization (WTO) agreement on valuation and weights. Up to three “supplementary unit of quantity” are also available for each declaration item. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording : The imported and exported goods are included in the statistics when the declarations pass the Customs formalities. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Goods are recorded as gross value. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The only source for Lao PDR’s IMTS is the customs declarations provided by the Customs Department, Ministry of Finance. The Customs uses Automated System for Customs Data (ASYCUDA) for major Customs checkpoints (including for online submission of Customs declarations) nationwide. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Same as 2.3.1 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The Customs provided data to the LSB in Excel format on a monthly basis, within one month after the reference month. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The LSB uses Eurotrace software package for IMTS database and data validation, and Comext software for IMTS data compilation and dissemination. Transactions with doubtful codes or value detected at the validation stage are reported back to the Customs for confirmation/correction. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Statistical techniques are adopted in data validation process (for example, applying proper threshold for data verification, and reclassification of unspecified, small-valued transactions, etc.). 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Not applicable 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Not applicable 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Not applicable 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Not applicable 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Not applicable 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Not applicable 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Statistical tables other than the format already disseminated can be provided upon request.
