 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Nig eria uses the general trade system for compiling foreign trade statistics and follows the guidelines of the UN "International Merchandise Trade Statistics Concepts and Definitions" (IMTS) manual published in 2010. The tables in which the data is reported quarterly include: - Table 1: Product Ranking table showing the top ranking 15 exported and imported products by trade value - Table 2: Country Ranking table showing the top ranking 10 exporting and importing partner countries by trade value - Table 3: Foreign trade summary of imports, exports and trade balance (disaggregated by year, quarter, month and oil and non-oil) - Table 4: Imports by Section - Table 5: Exports by Section - Table 6: Imports by Region and major trading partners (disaggregated by year, quarter and month) - Table 7: Exports by Region and major trading partners (disaggregated by year, quarter and month) - Table 8: Imports by SITC (disaggregated by year, quarter and month) - Table 9: Imports by BEC (disaggregated by year, quarter and month) Classification systems enable internationally comparable trade statistics for economic analysis of the data. The classification systems used by the Division include: - Section: A classification system comprised of 22 product groups by which imported and exported goods can be aggregated and analysed. - Standard Industrial Trade Classifications (SITC): A classification of goods into 10 different industrial categories created based on the materials used in production, the processing stage, uses of the products etc. - Broad Economic Category (BEC): A three digit classification of commodities with distinct groups for food products, industrial goods, consumer durables and consumer non-durables etc. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Goods are described by the Harmonized Commodity and Coding System (HS) on 10 digits classification. The data are also classified by SITC, BEC and Section for dissemination and analysis by economic group, industrial trade classification and product groups. Scope of the data Nigerian trade statistics are compiled from the declarations of imported and exported goods submitted to Customs. The declarations include the following information: commodity code and description, country of destination/origin, quantity, value, mode of transport etc. Import and export data for electricity, natural gas, and premium motor spirit which are not captured by Customs, are obtained from their relevant ministries and added to the Customs data. Exceptions to coverage Excluded from the statistics are passenger's luggage, diplomatic goods, goods in transit, monetary gold and military equipment. Unrecorded activity Informal Cross Border Trade (ICBT) is unrecorded financial, service-based, or product-based business transactions that take place across Nigeria's borders. ICBT data is important in order to supplement data collected on merchandise trade and to capture more adequately the volume and types of trade Nigeria engages in. NBS has a working proposal for an ICBT pilot study and Trade in services exercise to be carried out in the near future pending available funds/support. 2.4.1 Valuation Imports are valued at cost, insurance and freight (CIF) and exports at free on board (FOB). Values expressed in foreign currency are converted to Naira ( Nigeria currency), using the conversion rates as compiled by CBN. 2.4.2 Recording basis The dates of lodgment of the customs declaration is used as the time of recording. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The Nigeria Customs Bill of Entry is the major source of foreign trade data in Nigeria and is sourced from the ASYCUDA System, which became operational in Nigeria early 1999. With effect from the year 2000, Nigeria Foreign Trade Statistics have been sourced from the customs ASYCUDA system supplemented with oil returns. Crude oil export data is sourced from Nigerian National Petroleum Corporation (NNPC), while data for natural gas and premium motor spirit (PMS) are sourced from Nigerian Liquefied Natural Gas (NLNG), Petroleum Products Pricing Regulatory Agency (PPPRA) respectively. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Customs supplies the data by HS code, and the 43 fields of ASYCUDA used for managing raw trade data. However, organization of the data into categories such as Section, BEC, or SITC is done by the division after processing of the raw data. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Customs data is sent to NBS in the first week of each new month. There are delays, however, in the timely release of crude oil and PMS data due to the various administrative processes their organizations face. Notwithstanding the issues, NBS' ideal cycle of production within the quarterly timeframe is to publish data within 45 days of the end of each quarter. This is achieved by releasing provisional trade data on quarterly basis using crude oil and PMS estimates, which is subject to revision once the actual values are available. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Foreign trade statistics are compiled using secondary data collected from Customs and other agencies. Thus the originators of the data are responsible for resolving quality issues in the source data. NBS, however, makes corrections as needed in line with recommendations given from Customs. NBS also continues to have regular meetings and dialogue with the Custom's office and other stakeholders in order to improve data quality and ensure reliable, harmonized data. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The source data from Customs undergoes editing in line with IMTS methodology before it is sent to the Eurotrace database for processing. The data editing process entails inclusion of a national currency field, ensuring appropriate codes for HS products, taxes and other fields, arranging of column fields and population of data from other sources. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Conversion of foreign currencies to national currency (Naira) using official exchange rates from the Central Bank of Nigeria, and conversion of crude oil quantity from barrels to metric units, using an appropriate conversion factor. This is in accordance with the World Customs Organization recommendation on standard units as stated in the IMTS manual. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results NICIS is a data portal from Customs' which allows for query of single goods declaration forms for all imports and exports and is used for data verification. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data The Eurotrace software used in processing trade data has in-built systems for error correction and data validation. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs After processing of the data, data assessment is done in order to eliminate errors, including balancing of all aggregated and disaggregated figures, correction of any outlier values, and ensuring proper calculations throughout. NBS also holds quarterly stakeholders meetings to resolve data discrepancies, reach consensus on issues and streamline data outputs and processing procedures. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses NBS is in the process of harmonizing previous trade output to ensure that its historical series are in line with IMTS 2010. Upon finalization, the results will be made public. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Revised data is posted on the NBS website upon availability of crude oil export data from NNPC and PMS data from PPPRA. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished, non-confidential data can be provided upon request depending on the type of data specified, level of detail, and the period of years requested.
