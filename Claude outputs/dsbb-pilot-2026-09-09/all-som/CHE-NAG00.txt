 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: The National Accounts are based on the methodology of the European System of Accounts 2010 (ESA 2010) and the System of National Accounts 2008 (SNA 2008). Definition: All aggregates are calculated according to international standards as they are defined in SNA 2008/ESA 2010. Gross domestic product (GDP), on a quarterly basis, is defined in three ways covering the economic activity during the period: GDP = output – intermediate consumption + taxes on products, net of subsidies on production – correction for financial services indirectly measured (FISIM) = final consumption expenditure + gross fixed capital formation + changes in inventories and valuables + exports – imports. = compensation of employees + net operating surplus + consumption of fixed capital+ taxes on production and imports – subsidies 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: Classification of institutional sectors follows the recommendations in SNA 2008/ESA 2010. The National Accounts (NA) for Switzerland were revised in Summer 2012 by implementing the new statistical classification of economic activities NOGA 2008. The NOGA 2008 is modeled after the latest version of the Statistical classification of economic activities in the European Community (NACE Rev. 2). https://www.bfs.admin.ch/bfs/en/home/statistics/industry-services/ nomenclatures/noga/publications-noga-2008.html In September 2014 the NA were revised to ESA 2010. The revision of the annual data was carried out by the Swiss Federal Statistical Office (SFSO), for details see: (available only in German and French). https://www.seco.admin.ch/seco/en/home/seco/nsb-news.msg-id-54645.html Scope of the data Transactions coverage: All activities within the SNA 2008 production boundary are included except for illegal transactions that are not properly covered. Geographic coverage: The Swiss economic territory includes the mainland, Swiss embassies abroad less foreign embassies in Switzerland and Swiss ships and aircrafts in international transportation. Unrecorded activity Unrecorded activities: Unregistered activities are implicitly measured within one of the three approaches used for estimating GDP (production, expenditure or income approach). No separate calculations are made for illegal or unrecorded activities. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: GDP is recorded at market prices. Both current and constant price values are published for expenditure components and for the production account. Income components are recorded and available only at current prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: Transactions are recorded on an accrual basis. Exports and Imports of goods and services are recorded follows the definition of ESA 2010 (exchange of economic owner). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Production approach: Annual figures are more detailed than quarterly estimates (a value-added statistic is used for the calculation of the annual production account, quarterly data are estimated on the basis of indicators related to production). For the calculation of constant price data on the production side, producer and import price indices are used for manufacturing, various indices (indices based on salaries or consumer price indices) are used for services. Expenditure approach: Annual figures are more detailed than quarterly estimates (special surveys are used for the calculation of annual accounts, quarterly data are based on existing surveys or other short term indicators). For external trade, annual and quarterly data are based either on the statistics published by the Federal Office for Customs and Border Security or on the balance of payments statistics published by the Swiss National Bank (SNB). For the calculation of constant price data, consumer price indices are used for household consumption; export and import price indices, production price indices are used for compilation of exports and imports of goods; indices based on salaries, export and import prices, production prices, are used for the calculation of exports and imports of services. Price indices for construction of buildings and producer price indices are used for gross fixed capital formation. Consumer price indices and indices of salaries in the public sector are used for general government consumption. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Quarterly indicators: As far as possible, the quarterly indicators are closely aligned to the data sources and methodology used for the annual accounts. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Estimations Method: The Swiss QNA are compiled by using a so-called indirect estimation approach, based on temporal disaggregation techniques. Quarterly estimates are derived from the available annual data by using statistical methods and quarterly indicators. The temporal disaggregation methods used allow to estimate the quarterly figures of available data and to extrapolate quarterly figures when annual data are not available (in practice up to a maximum of five quarters). The accuracy of the preliminary annual estimates, obtained as the sum of the quarterly estimated values, depends to a large extent on the quality of the available and retained quarterly indicators. Annual data according to the expenditure, the production and the income approach is subject to harmonization before publication. The quarterly production account and the quarterly expenditure components are calculated independently. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures S easonal and calendar day adjustment: Raw and seasonally-calendar day adjusted QNA-data are made available online. Calendar day (not seasonally adjusted) or seasonally adjusted (not calendar day adjusted) are available on request. Since September 2014 seasonal adjustment is carried out with X13 – ARIMA-SEATS Method. Calendar effects are corrected by a national calendar for Switzerland, made available by the Federal Office for Customs and Border Security. The seasonally and calendar-day adjusted time series are retroactively recalculated every quarter with regard to the extension and revision of the original non-adjusted time series. Base year and update cycle: All aggregates at current prices are first converted into the previous year’s average prices to derive aggregates at constant prices (the previous year is always the basic year, the base is changed for every year). The chain-linking of yearly or quarterly data at the previous year’s average prices is provided in a second step by means of annual average prices (the annual overlap technique is used within the quarterly accounts). The reference year for the yearly and quarterly series is 2020 (since September 2025).The annual overlap technique is well described in the Quarterly National Accounts Manual of the IMF (chap. IX). Reconciliation: Annual data are generally subject to three revisions. The quarterly accounts are revised during the ongoing year, when annual data for the preceding years are revised or when new annual data are made available. Historical values (annual or quarterly) are revised when new national accounts systems are introduced. Usually, annual accounts are revised in a first step. The new revised annual figures are then used to estimate and calibrate the new quarterly data by means of an application of the Chow-Lin method. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses All revisions are documented in technical papers at https://www.seco.admin.ch/seco/en/home/wirtschaftslage---wirtschaftspolitik/Wirtschaftslag e/bip-quartalsschaetzungen-/concepts--en--.html , (see "Technical notes").
