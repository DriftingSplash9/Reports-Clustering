 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The methodological framework follows the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6) ( https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/bop/2007/bopman6.htm ) and are strictly applied to our data. The international investment position (IIP) reports the cross-border stocks of financial assets of all institutional units as at a specific reference date at the end of a period. Like the financial account, it can be broken down by investment purpose into direct investment, portfolio investment, derivatives, other investment and reserve assets. Stocks are broken down into: Assets: Foreign financial assets held by residents. Liabilities: Domestic financial assets held by non-residents. There are three possible causes of changes in stocks in the international investment position between two periods: transactions, revaluations (due to price changes or exchange rate changes), and other changes in volume. Together with other changes, the revaluations make up the changes in stocks other than transactions. Transactions are reported in the financial account and show the change in stocks due to transactions between two periods. Revaluations are nominal holding gains and losses in respect of stocks in the international investment position. These arise as a result of changes in exchange rates and prices, e.g. increases in share prices on exchanges in Switzerland or abroad. Other changes are changes in stocks which are caused neither by transactions nor revaluations. They arise from expansions in the reporting population or reclassifications, for instance. An example of the latter would be when a company in an SNB reporting population is reallocated to a different sector. In drawing up the integrated IIP statement, which is a breakdown of changes in stocks, the SNB follows the standards defined by the IMF (BPM6 ( https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/bop/2007/bopman6.htm ), External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users ( https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/Manuals-Guides/Issues/2016/12/30/External-Debt-Statist ics-Guide-for-Compilers-and-Users-15621 )). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization System used to classify transactions: Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6) ( https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/bop/2007/bopman6.htm ). Scope of the data The international investment position (IIP) reports the cross-border stocks of financial assets of all institutional units as at a specific reference date at the end of a period. In accordance with bilateral agreements, the Principality of Liechtenstein belongs to the Swiss customs and monetary territory. In the International Investment Position, it is considered as a domestic entity. 2.4.1 Valuation The stocks at the end of the year are generally stated at market prices. Direct investment equity positions are an exception, as they are shown at own funds at book value (OFBV). 2.4.2 Recording basis Conversions to unit of account: Stock data reported in foreign currencies are converted to local currency at the end-of-period exchange rates. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Direct Investment: Quarterly and annual surveys on cross-border capital linkages submitted by domestic enterprises to the Swiss National Bank. Portfolio investment: Quarterly surveys on securities bought and sold on behalf of residents and non-residents by domestic enterprises, banks and custodians to the Swiss National Bank. Derivatives: Quarterly surveys reported by domestic enterprises and banks to the Swiss National Bank. Other investment: Government sector: Government reports Commercial bank lending: Transactions are computed using balance sheet data reported by resident banks on their claims and liabilities vis-à-vis non-residents. Other sectors: Data reported by enterprises, international banking statistics Accounts of the Swiss National Bank Reserve assets: Accounts of the Swiss National Bank. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Direct investment: The quarterly data on equity capital are estimated by adding quarterly financial transactions to the stock at the end of the quarter and adjusting for exchange rate changes of positions in foreign currency.
