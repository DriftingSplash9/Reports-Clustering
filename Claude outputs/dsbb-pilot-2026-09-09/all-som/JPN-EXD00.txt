 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: The gross external debt of Japan is compiled in line with the conceptual framework of the External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users . The data are consistent with those contained in the international investment position (IIP) of Japan, which are compiled in accordance with the guidelines set out in the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6) . End period positions of external debt are broken down into four sectors (central bank; general government; deposit-taking corporations, except the central bank; other sectors). These are classified further by original maturity (long term/short term) and by instruments. Definition: The gross external debt of Japan is a financial statement of value and composition of Japan's external liabilities excluding equities and financial derivatives. It is compiled on the basis of covering debt position between residents and nonresidents. The only major deviation from the international guidelines is the treatment of non-depository public financial corporations. In Japan's external debt, they are classified as general government, instead of other sectors. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The gross external debt of Japan is mainly based on BPM6 classifications. Classification by type of debt instrument: 1) Deposits of nonresidents with domestic banks are included; 2) Instruments included under "other debt liabilities": Accrued amount payable, accrued interest payable, deposit payable on securities transaction, etc. are included; 3) Trade credits owed to nonresidents: Trade credits owed to nonresidents are included. The criteria used for determining whether a liability is a debt instrument or not is based on the External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and User . External debt liabilities of households and small private enterprises are included under "other sectors". Financial leases with nonresidents are treated as loans and included in the gross external debt position. Intercompany lending between entities in a direct investment relationship is separately identified in the total external debt position. Maturity classification: Data are disseminated by maturity on an original maturity basis. Reclassification: Major reclassifications are not identifiable through table footnotes. Sector attribution of external debt liabilities: External debt liabilities are attributed to the sector of the immediate borrower. Sector classification of external debt liabilities: External debt liabilities are classified under the sector category of the debtor and not of any guarantor. 2.2.1 Scope The gross external debt of Japan is mainly based on BPM6 classifications. Scope of the data Coverage of external debt: The gross external debt of Japan covers external liabilities of all economic units. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation of tradable instruments: They are valued at market value as much as possible. When marked to market data are not available, they are valued at book value. 2.4.2 Recording basis Conversion to unit of account: Positions originally denominated in foreign currencies are converted into yen using market exchange rate at the end of the reference period. Recording basis: Outstanding liabilities arising from periodic interest costs that have accrued and are not yet payable : Those for the banks are included in the gross external debt position. Those for sectors other than banks are not identifiable because they are not included in the reporting form and the data on them are not collected. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The source data for the external debt are obtained from comprehensive data programs in common with the international investment position. For details, see the BOJ website ( https://www.boj.or.jp/en/statistics/outline/exp/exbpsm6.htm/#p04 ). 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The source data are largely consistent with the definitions, scope and classifications, and time of recording of external debt data. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Overall, the source data for compiling the external debt are timely. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data are assessed and merged in the external debt statistics application database as they are received from reporters. The ITRS electronic data verification and processing has been developed. Source data submitted by reporters are automatically checked by the IT system for consistency, compared to formerly submitted data, and cross-checked between reporters having identical activities. BOPD (Balance of Payments Division) staff interact with reporters and gain adequate knowledge of their routine operations and encourage reporters to consult with BOPD staff frequently. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques In general, incorporation of the ITRS data, implementations of statistical surveys, statistics data creation, and processing are skillfully integrated into the whole process. The BOPD staff has a good data processing ability, adequate knowledge, and experience. A comprehensive electronic reporting system has been working since January 2005. Major respondents submit daily electronic reports. Data also are compiled from hard copy and electronic reports from banks and other financial institutions. The reporting software allows for electronic reporting and automated computerized checks that reduce processing errors. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Adjustments made for compiling the external debt statistics employ sound statistical techniques. Data that are not compiled by using reports or survey are estimated by adding quarterly BOP data to the positions at the end of the previous reference period. The positions at the end of the previous reference period and the BOP data are revalued with exchange rate (and bond performance index for debt securities). As for data for the end of calendar year, revised data are equal to those of IIP compiled annually based on year-end reports. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data A wide variety of verification procedures are practiced during data collection and compilation. In particular, the consistency between position data and balance of payments flow data are monitored. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Information of revision is posted on the MOF and the BOJ website. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request The decision on the disclosure of requested information is made considering the confidentiality of individual data. Therefore, requests for data not routinely disseminated are considered by the MOF on a case-by-case basis and depend on confidentiality considerations. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Statistics users are informed about the contact number and e-mail address mentioned on the MOF and the BOJ websites. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The MOF and the BOJ provides a catalog of publications to users.
