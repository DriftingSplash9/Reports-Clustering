 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The concepts and definitions of the BoP of Lithuania are based on the conceptual framework of the international standards recommended by the sixth edition of the IMF Balance of Payments Manual ( BPM6), European System of Accounts (ESA 2010), methodological requirements of Eurostat’s (BoP Vademecum), Guideline ECB/2011/23 (Recast) on the statistical reporting requirements of the European Central Bank in the field of external statistics, OECD Benchmark Definition of Foreign Direct Investment (Fourth Edition 2008), Regulation (EC) No 184/2005 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 12 January 2005 on Community statistics concerning balance of payments, international trade in services and foreign direct investment. Definition: The balance of payments is a statistical statement that summarizes, for a specific time, the economic transactions of an economy with the rest of the world. Transactions recorded in the balance of payments are interactions between resident and nonresident institutional units. An economic transaction is an interaction between two institutional units (including transactions between the parent institution and its branches abroad) and involves changes in the ownership of goods and/or financial assets, the provision of services, or the provision of labor and capital. A transaction itself is defined as an economic flow that reflects the creation, transformation, exchange, transfer or extinction of the economic value. The term of economic ownership is the main in determining the time of recording on an accrual basis for transactions in goods, for non-produced non-financial and financial assets. The concept of economic ownership is associated with risks and rewards, rights, and responsibilities. The BoP is compiled based on the double entry principle — the main principle of accounting. It means that every recorded transaction is represented by two entries with equal values. One of these entries is designated a credit with a positive arithmetic sign, the other is designated a debit with a negative sign. Lithuania’s BoP is compiled on a monthly and quarterly basis. The scope of the quarterly BoP indicators complies with the scope and composition of standard components (indicators) of the IMF Balance of Payments Manual. Quarterly and monthly BoP is compiled based on direct reporting. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The BoP components are classified according to the sixth edition of the IMF Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6), European System of Accounts (ESA 2010), methodological requirements of Eurostat’s (BoP Vademecum), Guideline ECB/2011/23 (Recast) on the statistical reporting requirements of the European Central Bank in the field of external statistics, OECD Benchmark Definition of Foreign Direct Investment (Fourth Edition 2008), Regulation (EC) No 184/2005 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 12 January 2005 on Community statistics concerning balance of payments, international trade in services and foreign direct investment (Recast). The standard components are grouped under two major headings: the current account and the capital and financial account. The current account is further subdivided into goods, services, primary income, and secondary income. The capital and financial account covers transactions in external financial assets and liabilities, further subdivided into functional categories (direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives, other investment, reserve assets) and instruments. Portfolio investment and other investment also are classified into institutional sectors. Scope of the data Transaction coverage: The data cover the entire economy's transactions with the rest of the world. Exceptions to coverage Transaction coverage: Changes in assets or liabilities that do not result from transactions are excluded from the BoP. 2.4.1 Valuation All transactions in the balance of payments are valued at the market price, i.e., the price for which the seller is ready to sell a product or service, and the buyer is ready to pay that price. Transactions in financial assets and liabilities should be recorded exclusive of any commissions, fees, and taxes. Conversion to unit of account: Transactions denominated in other currencies are converted into euro at the rates set by the LB prevailing at the time of transactions. 2.4.2 Recording basis Investment income flows are recorded at the time of accrual. Dividends are recorded when declared payable. Reinvested earnings are recorded in the period in which the related profits are earned. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Entries in the current and capital accounts of the BoP are recorded on a gross basis. Gross recording is also applicable in recording to income on reverse investment compiling FDI income. Transactions and other flows in financial assets and liabilities are recorded as net changes in financial assets and net changes in liabilities respectively. Net recording principle is applied within a given standard component of assets and liabilities. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs BoP compilation by the LB is based on the statistical data collected from different data sources and reporting agents - statistical reports, statistical registers, administrative sources, data collected by using surveys, data from the LB accounting, data published by international financial organizations, data published by other countries, mass-media, data obtained by the one-off request from economical entities and government institutions in Lithuania and from colleagues abroad. A part of statistical reporting forms is collected by the LB directly from national economic entities, and some portions of these data are gathered by the SL. All public institutions and commercial banks, besides quarterly data reporting, also furnish the LB with their monthly figures. Investment funds and pension funds submit data on monthly basis. Monthly data for other sectors are derived from a sample survey of economic entities. Economic entities are selected for the monthly survey according to their level of representation in a concrete activity or to the scope of operations with non-residents. From almost 5,000 other sectors' economic entities that provide quarterly data, about 200 entities also submit their monthly reports. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Goods: Data on trade in goods are compiled based on data provided by the SL. Data on export and import of goods are recorded in the BoP, when the economic ownership of goods is transferred from residents to non-residents, and vice versa. Data on goods delivered at the ports of transport agencies are received from the reports of transport companies. In the BoP export and import of goods are valued on a f.o.b. basis, thus allowing to make comparable data of different countries. In this respect, the LB makes some adjustments for freight. Freight is calculated for each mean of transportation individually and depending on the country of the sender. Freight transportation costs are added to transportation services, while freight insurance expenses (that are deducted) are included with insurance services. Also, the smuggled goods (cigarettes, spirit) are included under the definition of general merchandise and therefore LB in cooperation with the SL makes adjustment in goods item. Services: Data on services provided by residents to non-residents (export of services) and on services provided by non-residents to Lithuanian economic entities (import of services) are collected from different sources. The main source is a quarterly survey conducted by the SL from the non-banking sector. Since 2022 the quarterly financial services data were additionally extended by the data from the major electronic money companies. Data on services were extended by the Mini One-Stop-Shop for VAT (MOSS Scheme) based on TAX authority information since 2015. Since July 2021 MOSS scheme was transformed into OSS. As of 2021Q3 statistics on international trade in services included the provision of services calculated based on administrative data from the OSS. The LB collects quarterly data directly from banks. The data on government services is received from the Ministry of Finance, State Tax Inspectorate, and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Data on health care services is received from Patient's Fund at the Ministry of Health. Monthly data on services are collected by the LB from administrative data sources (from Interdepartmental Tax Data Warehouse of the Information Society Development Committee under the Government). Data on life and non-life insurance services were extended by the annual EIOPA data since 2017. EIOPA covers information on the activities of insurance companies of other EU Member States without their establishment in Lithuania and providing services for Lithuanian residents. Primary Income: Income covers: · compensation of employees, which is paid to non-resident workers (e.g., border, seasonal and other short-term workers); · investment income receipts and payments on external financial assets and liabilities; · other primary income. Information on compensation of employees is obtained from data on the cross-border transactions by individuals through postal offices and banks, data on social insurance contributions paid by non-exempt foreign embassies for resident employees in Lithuania and data on the expenditure of Lithuania's embassies abroad, quarterly reports of non-banking enterprises, the Lithuanian Labour Exchange for quarterly information on work permits granted to non-residents of the EU. Additionally quarterly data are extended by information from TAX authority about Lithuanian resident's received compensation of employes income from abroad. Investment income is income derived from the ownership of external financial assets and payable by residents of one economy to residents of another economy. Investment income includes interest, dividends, remittances of branch profits, and direct investors' shares of retained earnings of direct investment enterprises, income on reserve assets. Interest is recorded on an accrual basis. Other primary income cover taxes and subsidies on products and production. The main sources of data on direct investment income (expenditure) are a quarterly survey of enterprises involved in direct investment conducted by the SL and monthly and quarterly reports of commercial banks. Information from the Ministry of Finance on interest paid on government debt securities, data obtained from the Registers of securities issuers and holders of the LB and data from the ECB's Centralized securities data base are the main data sources on portfolio investment income . Since 2011, portfolio investment income is compiled on security-by-security and debtor approach basis. Income attributable to investment funds shareholders was estimated and recalculated using ECB CSDB since 2019. The main sources of data on other investment income are quarterly reports of non-financial enterprises, monthly and quarterly reports of commercial banks, loans received on behalf of the state and guaranteed by the government, the Foreign Loans Register of the LB, which includes foreign loans received without state guarantee by legal entities of the Republic of Lithuania and of loans granted to foreign economic operators. Income on reserve assets is identified separately, and the main data source is LB balance sheet. The main data source of other primary income is the Ministry of Finance, Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Secondary income: Secondary income account corresponds broadly to "current transfers". This account introduces indicator personal transfers . Workers' remittances are a part of personal transfers. The main data sources are data on international payments provided by commercial banks and the Post Office "Lietuvos Pastas". Data were extended by the Mini One-Stop-Shop for VAT (MOSS Scheme) based on TAX authority information since 2015. The retained part of the VAT receipts was recorded as D.74. Capital transfers: The acquisition/disposal of non-produced, non-financial assets (items like copyrights and patents – transactions) are no longer included on this list (they are classified under research and development services). The main sources of data are monthly data on capital transfers from EU support funds to government and other sectors (provided by the Ministry of Finance); data on debt forgiveness transactions. Financial account All components are classified according to the type of investment or by a functional subdivision (direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives, other investment, official reserve assets). Direct investment: Foreign direct investment transactions are presented on the gross (assets/liabilities) concept. Investments between fellows are also included. The value of equity capital balances in foreign direct investment is established by valuing the equity stocks of companies listed in stock exchanges at market price , and the value of equity stocks of unlisted companies at book value . It must be noted that this requirement is applied for calculating accumulated investment data but is not applied for calculating direct investment flows. Reinvested earnings consist of the direct investor's share (in proportion to the direct equity participation) of earnings not distributed as dividends. Also, these reinvested earnings are recorded as income under primary income account. Debt instruments cover borrowing and lending of funds – including debt securities, suppliers' credits, and non-participating preferred shares (which are treated as debt securities), between direct investors and subsidiaries, branches, and associates. The LB is responsible for the compilation of the final FDI data and coordinating all work related to the collection of FDI statistics. FDI quarterly and annual surveys of non-banking sector are conducted by regional State Data Agency office. Quarterly reporting data from non-banking sector are collected by sampling survey of economic entities. Entities are selected according either to their level of representation in a specific activity or to their scope of transactions with non-residents. Quarterly reports submit 1.9 thousand of non-banking sector entities. Annual FDI data are collected by census survey of 3.4 thousand of non-banking sector entities. Annual FDI data are submitted by 15 May and final FDI data for the accounting year (including quarterly data revision) are compiled in September. By using quarterly surveys, the LB directly collects FDI data from commercial banks. Moreover, the LB uses FDI data provided by the Ministry of Finance, local authorities, and Central Securities Depository. The LB compiles final FDI data using all data sources (individual FDI data from State Data Agency initial FDI database are also available). FDI data is broken down by a geographical area and economic activity (using NACE codes corresponding to the economic activities used in the Eurostat/OECD codification for economic activity groups), except the adjustments due to exchange rate changes, adjustments due to price changes, reclassification, and other adjustments (these adjustments are reported for the total FDI, without geographical breakdown). Portfolio investment: Portfolio investment is understood as foreign investment involving less than 10% of voting rights and no extensive powers in corporate management decisions. Portfolio investment covers transactions in equity and debt securities. In the case of securities with coupons, the interest accrued from the last payment of interest is included and, in that of securities issued at a discount, the interest accumulated since the issue is included. The inclusion of interest accrued is required for the financial account of the quarterly balance of payments and international investment position. Offsetting entries are made in the respective income account. Portfolio investment transactions are broken down further by instrument, sector, country (since the first quarter of 2004) and maturity. Some 90% of portfolio investment data are now based on security-by-security information. The primary data sources are: (i) the monthly security by security data from the custodians and directly from MFIs on the distribution of securities by holders (including government securities); (ii) the monthly survey of collective investment and money market funds conducted by the LB (KIS-01), which includes data on cross-border transactions of these funds; (iii) information from the Ministry of Finance on the government's cross-border transactions in debt securities and information on debt securities issued by central government; (iv) the monthly security by security data from the Central Securities Depository on the distribution of securities by issuers (including government securities); (v) the monthly survey of cross-border transactions of commercial banks, conducted by LB (B-09-02); (vi) the daily data on prices of equity and debt securities issued in the Baltic states provided by NASDAQ OMX Baltic exchanges; (vii) the monthly data on securities by issuers obtained from the ECB's Centralized Securities Database (CSDB); (viii) the quarterly SHS third party holdings on euro-area securities holdings; (ix) the quarterly survey of insurance companies conducted by the LB which includes data on cross-border transactions of these funds; (x) the ECB's Centralized Securities Database (CSDB) is used for calculation of Investment fund's reinvested earnings (since 2023 q3). Financial derivatives: Financial derivatives are financial instruments that are linked to a specific financial instrument, indicator, or commodity, and through which specific financial risks can be traded in financial markets. The valuation of financial derivatives is conducted on a marked-to-market basis. The sources of data are as follows: (i) the quarterly survey of cross-border transactions of banks conducted by the LB (B-09-01) and reports provided by pension and collective investment funds; (ii) the monthly reports (Balance Sheet data) of MFI's sector; (iii) the Balance Sheet data, received from the Financial Accounting Information System (FAIS) of the LB; (iv) the quarterly survey of cross-border transactions of government sector conducted by the Ministry of Finance; (v) the monthly data of cross-border transactions of banks about the realized profit/loss on financial derivatives; (vi) the quarterly survey of cross-border transactions of collective investment funds (KIS-01 report). Other investment: Other investment is defined as a residual category that includes all financial transactions not covered in the direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives, or reserve assets categories. Other investment covers trade credits and advances, loans, currency and deposits, insurance, pension and standardized guarantee schemes, other accounts receivable/payable, SDR. It also comprises offsetting entries for accrued income on instruments classified under other investment. The sources of data are as follows: (i) the quarterly survey of cross-border transactions of non-bank enterprises conducted by the SL (F-06) is used for calculation the trade credits, and currency and deposits items; (ii) the quarterly surveys of cross-border transactions of banks conducted by the LB (B-09-01); (iii) information from the Ministry of Finance on government loans received (and repaid) on behalf of the state and guaranteed by the government and information on loans received from non-residents directly by local government; (iv) the monthly survey of non-bank enterprises conducted by the LB (B-09-04); (v) the Foreign Loans Register of the LB, which includes foreign loans received without state guarantee by legal entities of the Republic of Lithuania and of loans granted to foreign economic operators; (vi) information from BIS Quarterly Review about stocks on currency and deposits with foreign banks; (vii) the data on external operations from the balance sheet of the LB. Breakdowns are available by sector, instrument, country (since the first quarter of 2004) and maturity. Reserve assets: Official reserve assets – highly liquid, marketable and creditworthy foreign assets controlled by the central bank that may be used for direct financing of payment imbalances and other similar purposes. Starting with the January 2015, the claims on non-euro area residents denominated in convertible foreign currencies (other than the euro), monetary gold, SDR and reserve position in the IMF are classified as official reserve assets. The LB external assets that cannot be classified as reserve assets are recorded in the data item ‘Portfolio Investment', ‘Financial Derivatives and Employee Stock Options' or ‘Other Investment' under BoP. The official reserve assets data are compiled in accordance with the methodology set out both the 6th edition of the Balance of Payments Manual and Operational Guidelines for the Data Template on International Reserves and Foreign Currency Liquidity. Official reserve assets are compiled in accordance with the gross concept, without any netting with monetary authorities' liabilities. International reserves and foreign currency liquidity data template shall mean the statistical statement that reports, with the appropriate breakdown, stocks of reserve assets, other foreign currency assets and reserve-related liabilities of the reference date and predetermined and contingent short-term net drains connected to official reserve assets and other foreign currency assets. The Balance Sheet of the LB data received from the Financial Accounting Information System (FAIS) of the LB is the major source for accounting of changes in official reserve assets (flows and stocks). 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Source data timeliness is stated in the Resolution No 241 of 24 December 2009 of the Board of the Bank of Lithuania on the approval of the procedure for submitting to the LB the statistical information and data necessary to compile the BoP and IIP. Order No. V2010/(1.7-0202)-02-42 of 25 February 2010 of the Chairman of the Board of the Bank of Lithuania on the approval of terms for submitting to the LB the data needed to compile the BoP, IIP and official international reserves. This resolution states the kind of statistical data to be provided by the various ministries, public institutions and economic units and the required frequency and timeliness. This resolution also states that BoP and IIP data must be provided to the ECB, Eurostat, and the IMF according to the required timetable, which is published on the LB’s website and in its relevant publications. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Goods: In foreign trade statistics, import value includes costs, insurance, and freight (c.i.f.), while exports are recorded on free on board (f.o.b.) basis. According to the BoP methodology exports and imports of goods are recorded on f.o.b. basis, so the statistical value of imports excludes customs duties and internal taxes. In this respect, the LB makes some adjustments for freight. Freight is calculated for each mean of transportation individually and depending on the country of the sender. Freight transportation costs are added to transportation services, while freight insurance expenses (that are deducted) are included with insurance services. Moreover, for general merchandise item LB (calculation made in cooperation with the SL) makes adjustment on smuggled goods (cigarettes, spirit). Services: Transportation: Credit and debit entries for freight and passenger services are derived from quarterly surveys (data on the services transactions between resident non-bank enterprises and non-residents). Debit entries for freight on imports include insurance on merchandise. Credit and debit entries also include international fare receipts and expenditure. Other transportation entries include services rendered by carriers, and seaport and airport services. The debit entries also include payment made for maintenance and repair of Lithuanian ships and aircrafts at foreign ports and airports. Travel: Data on travel services are calculated on the basis of the monthly data on the number of incoming foreigners from non EU countries into and departing residents from Lithuania, as provided by the State Border Guard Service at the Ministry of the Interior and on the SL selective research on incoming foreigners and on Lithuanian travelers about their average expenditure and length of stay, on the data of quarterly surveys of enterprises providing accommodation services (hotels, health spa, etc.) and of enterprises involved in sales of touring packages. For estimation of travel services, the data of quarterly surveys of enterprises providing accommodation services (hotels, health spas, etc.) and of enterprises involved in sales of touring packages are also included. Government services: Data on government services are obtained from State Tax Inspectorate under the Ministry of Finance and Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Expenditure of the Lithuanian missions abroad, based on budget execution documents, and the expenditure of foreign missions in Lithuania, by grossing up the requests of these missions for refunds of value added tax (VAT) for goods and services acquired by foreign embassies. Income: Since 2011, portfolio investment income is compiled on security-by-security and debtor approach basis. All income (or actually allocated income (dividends) or reinvested income), received from investment fund shares is allocated to holders of these shares. Income on equity securities other than investment fund shares includes only dividends. Interest on debt securities is recorded on an accrual basis and debtor approach is used for defining and measuring interest for debt instruments. Information on compensation of employees is obtained from: data on social insurance contributions paid by foreign embassies for resident employees in Lithuania; quarterly reports of non-financial enterprises (the survey conducted by the State Data Agency); Lithuanian Labour Exchange data; the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (for data on the expenditure of Lithuania’s embassies abroad); data on cross-border transactions by individuals obtained from a monthly reports of MFIs. Other primary income includes taxes on production and imports, subsidies, and rent. Secondary income and capital account: The main information for Secondary income is provided by the Ministry of Finance, Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Interdepartmental Tax Data Warehouse, MFIs, and other administrative data sources. Secondary income comprise the data on participation in international financial organisations (acquired shares, contributions for subscribed shares, entry and membership fees), data on the implementation of bilateral technical assistance projects, expenses of embassies and representative offices of Lithuania, data on EU Allowances on carbon emission trade with non-residents, data on foreign aid granted to Lithuania and abroad, data on reimbursements of the value-added tax to foreign embassies and representative offices residing in Lithuania, international transfers of cash through money transfer operators. Private transfers are estimated based on transfers from and to abroad reported by the Post Office and on data from the commercial bank statements. The main sources of Capital account are monthly data on capital transfers from EU support funds to government and other sectors (data are provided by the Ministry of Finance); data on debt forgiveness transactions (data from enterprises survey and MFI quarterly reports). · Direct investment: Net direct investment capital flows = investment in equity capital + reinvested earnings +debt instruments. Debt instruments include debt securities, loans, trade credit and other financial assets (liabilities) related to affiliated enterprises or direct investors. Data from the primary sources are reconciled and adjustments for coverage, classification and timing are made. The data on equity capital for listed enterprises are recorded at market values and at book value. The data on equity capital for unlisted enterprises are recorded at book value. FDI data in the BoP is broken down by geographical area and by economic activity (using NACE codes corresponding to the economic activities used in the Eurostat/OECD codification for economic activity groups). Since 2007, FDI data include private non-business real estate investment. Since the beginning of 2009, quarterly data (flows and stocks), collected on inward and outward FDI also cover transactions and positions between resident and nonresident fellow enterprises. · Portfolio investment: Since 2006 while compiling portfolio investment, data from ECB’ Centralised Security Data Base are being used. For compilation purpose type of security, country and sector of issuer attributes are used, and for cross-checking purpose price and currency of security issued attributes are used. · Financial derivatives: Financial derivatives cover options, traded financial futures, warrants, and arrangements such as currency and interest rate swaps. Realized profit (assets)/ loss (liabilities) are calculated when the deal is closed. Holding of gains is treated as a decrease in the asset position when the realized loss – as a decrease in the liabilities position. The premium of option is also treated as transaction. · Other investment: Other investment: trade credits, currency and deposits are estimated and recorded on a net basis – net credit (financial inflow) or net debit (financial outflow). The valuation changes are excluded from the data for the banking sector. The valuation of the data for non-banks is based on their book values in euro. Data on deposits with and loans from foreign banks are supplemented with the BIS data. New allocations of SDR are recorded as "net incurrences of liabilities" by the LB. Additionally, equity participation in international organizations are included in other investment. · Reserve assets Reserve assets are compiled in accordance with the gross concept, without any netting with monetary authorities’ liabilities. Reserve assets are valued at prevailing market prices. Gold holdings are revalued on the last business day of each month at market price at the London bullion market. Holdings of foreign exchange (cash) are revalued on a currency-by-currency basis on each business day at the official exchange rate of that day. Securities are revalued at market price on the last business day monthly. The value of securities and deposits at the reference date includes accrued interest. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Seasonal adjustment: The monthly BoP current account (5 indicators) are adjusted seasonally but is used for Euro area needs. Geographic detail: BoP data are broken down by the geographical zones in accordance with ECB and Eurostat requirements and by economic activity (NACE codes corresponding to the economic activities used in the Eurostat/OECD codification for economic activity groups). Other: Since 2010 quarterly reporting of FDI data from non-banking sector are collected by sampling survey of economic entities. Entities are selected according either to their level of representation in a specific activity or to their scope of transactions with non-residents. The monthly BoP data obtained from a sample survey of economic entities are extrapolated by the application of corresponding coefficients. As the BoP indicators for any month are calculated using the extrapolation coefficients and making respective evaluations, the sum of the three months' data may not coincide with the respective figures of the corresponding quarter. Monthly data are revised after the quarterly BoP data for the corresponding period are compiled. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results When preparing the BoP all components of respective item data are checked in terms of internal consistency and consistency within a respective period. Data obtained additionally are compared with respective data of other statistical sources. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Information on contact points for statistical information compiled by the LB is published on the LB website https://www.lb.lt/en/statistics-contacts . 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs
