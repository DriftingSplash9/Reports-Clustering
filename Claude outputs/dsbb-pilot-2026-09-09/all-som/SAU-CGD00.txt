 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Central government debt is defined as the face value amount of liabilities contracted by the central government sector with the rest of the economy and the world. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Central government debt is compiled in line with the Government Finance Statistics Manual and its compilation guide for public sector debt statistics. The central government debt is broken down by domestic and external debt by using residency criteria. Data on central government domestic and external debt are further broken down by original maturity: short term (less than one year) and medium/long term (more than one year). 2.2.1 Scope Central government debt is compiled in line with the Government Finance Statistics Manual and its compilation guide for public sector debt statistics. The central government debt is broken down by domestic and external debt by using residency criteria. Data on central government domestic and external debt are further broken down by original maturity: short term (less than one year) and medium/long term (more than one year). Scope of the data Debt statistics cover all liabilities incurred by the central government and consist mainly of government bonds/ Sukuk and medium/long term loans. The government bonds / Sukuks are publicly traded. Debt data comprises outstanding debt liabilities of the central government. This coverage corresponds to that of the total government debt. 2.4.1 Valuation The debt is recorded at face value (amounts will be repaid at maturity). Long and medium-term loans are recorded as they are drawn down. Bonds and sukuks maturities are in range of 3 to 40 years. 2.4.2 Recording basis Debt data are recorded on a cash basis. The principal and interest are shown separately. Information on bonds and interest rates may be obtained from the Lending Department. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Domestic and external debt data disseminated are recorded on a gross basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data is compiled using the accounting records of the National Debt Management Center, which manages the debt on behalf of the Government. The information on accrued interest and loans is accessible from computer records. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Detailed source data for central government debt are consistent with the GFSM 2014. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Source data are available on quarterly basis and with timeliness of about one month after the end of the quarter. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Data sources based on accounting records gives assurance that the best sources have been used in the compilation of fiscal data. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Compilation of fiscal statistics is based on comprehensive source data. There is no need to estimate or impute missing data. Statistics are final when first published. Reporting dates allow sufficient time to ensure that complete and accurate information is reported. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The likelihood of inconsistencies is minimal since the data are prepared from accounting records.
