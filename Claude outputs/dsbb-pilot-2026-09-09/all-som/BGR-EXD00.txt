 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions • Analytical Framework, Concepts and Definitions: For the compilation of the Gross External Debt data, the Bulgarian National Bank (BNB) uses the concepts, definitions and conceptual framework as set in External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users, 2013 and in the Balance of Payments Manual, IMF, 6-th edition, 2008. For the compilation of the gross external debt, the Bulgarian National Bank (BNB) follows the international standards set out in the gross external debt guides, published by the International Monetary Fund (IMF). The methodological requirements of the latest edition of the External Debt Statistics – Guide for Compilers and Users, International Monetary Fund (IMF) 2013 (The Guide) are implemented in new published data. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: The BNB compiles and disseminates monthly statistical data on the gross external debt by institutional sectors The main components of the gross external debt tables classified by sectors are General Government, Central bank, Other MFIs and Other Sectors (disagregated into three new sub-sectors: Financial corporations, Nonfinancial corporations and Households and nonprofit institutions serving households (NPISHs).). In accordance with the international statistical standards, debt liabilities related to direct investment (intercompany lending) are presented separately. The classification by institutional sectors is in compliance with the External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users, 2013 and with the Balance of Payments Manual, IMF, 6-th edition, 2008.Within the sectors the external debt liabilities are classified by maturity and by instruments. General Government : General Government debt includes: (1) central government debt, (2) local government debt, (3) debt of social security funds and (4) debt of all nonmarket non-profit institutions that are controlled and mainly financed by government units. Public corporations and unincorporated enterprises that function as if they were corporations (so called quasi-corporations) are explicitly excluded from the general government sector and are allocated to MFIs or other sectors as appropriate. The debt liabilities of the General Government sector are long-term and are classified by instruments Loans and Debt securities Liabilities for which no issue of tradable securities is involved are recorded under Loans. Loans received by the central government from the IMF are also recorded under Loans. The central government liabilities on securities issued on the international markets are recorded under Debt securities, as the item comprises the entire stock of the issue.The government securities, issued by the government on the domestic market and purchased by non-residents, are also recorded under item Debt securities. Central bank : allocated SDRs (special drawing rights) – positions and transactions in SDRs are reported as liabilities of the Central bank. IMF members are entitled to use SDRs in case of financial difficulties in exchange of convertible currency. Other MFIs : Short-term liabilities of the banks include short-term loans, repos, non-residents' deposits with domestic banks as well as other payables to non-residents. In sub-item Deposits are included deposits in foreign currencies and in leva of non-residents with domestic banks. In accordance with the External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users, deposits related to contingent liabilities are excluded. Long-term liabilities include loans and debt securitiesissued by the MFIs and held by non-residents. Loans between financial institutions are recorded as deposits, not as loans. Other sectors : In the Other sectors item the debt of private and state-owned non-bank enterprises (incl. Government guaranteed debt) as well as external liabilities of the households are recorded. Short-term liabilities of the Other sectors include short-term loans, trade credit and advances as well as other payables to non-residents. Long-term liabilities include loans and debt securitiesissued by the companies and held by non-residents. The sector is disagregated into three new sub-sectors: Financial corporations, Nonfinancial corporations and Households and nonprofit institutions serving households (NPISHs). Direct investment: Intercompany lending : In accordance with the international methodology on external debt statistics a distinction is made between liabilities owed to direct investors (Intercompany Lending) and such owed to other creditors. Liabilities related to direct investment relationship are separately identified (equity liabilities arising from direct investment i.e. equity capital and reinvested earnings except nonparticipating, preferred shares are excluded from external debt). Intercompany lending is disagregated into three new items: Debt liabilities of direct investment enterprises to direct investors, Debt liabilities of direct investors to direct investment enterprises and Debt liabilities between fellow enterprises. Scope of the data • Coverage of external debt : The Gross External Debt Data cover external actual current, and not contingent liabilities of all resident entities in respect to all non-residents. Exceptions to coverage 2.4.1 Valuation • Conversion to unit of account: The Gross External Debt of Bulgaria is reported in Euro. The debt comprises, however, liabilities, denominated in foreign currencies and in Bulgarian leva which are converted into Euro as follows: using the exchange rate as of the last working day of the reporting month - when compiling the debt stock and using monthly average exchange rate - for debt transactions compilation. Securities issued by residents and held by non-residents are valued at market price (including accrued but unpaid interest) instead of nominal value, which is now published as supplementary information. Thus, gross external debt data are synchronised with the International Investment Position data. Also, along with the implementation of the requirements of The Guide interest on loans (including intercompany loans) is now accrued monthly on the basis of loan terms and conditions as directly reported by respondents. In accordance with the accrual principle, interest is reported as an increase in loans liabilities' positions (including intercompany loans) and in the gross external debt position. 2.4.2 Recording basis The distinction between short-term and long-term debt is based on the original, not residual maturity of the liability. The long-term debt includes all liabilities with original maturity of more than a year, and the short-term - those with original maturity of one year or less. All arrears of principals and interests are recorded as short-term debt. All intercompany lending is recorded as long-term liability. In addition, the short-term remaining maturity by sector and instrument is presented. It is divided by short-term debt with original maturity, which includes liabilities with maturity of one year or less and long-term maturity including all liabilities with remaining maturity of one year or less. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures All external debt is recorded on a gross basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs • Government debt: The source of data is the Register of Government and Government-guaranteed Debt of the Ministry of Finance. Other sources are Government and Government Guaranteed Debts Depository Directorate with the BNB and the MFIs. • Central bank : The data is derived from the Register of Government and Government-guaranteed Debt of the Ministry of Finance. • MFIs : Data on the amount and transactions on external loans is received monthly directly from the banks through a statistical form B-7 on their external liabilities. Monetary and FinancialStatistics Division with the Statistics Directorate of the BNB provides monthly data for the Deposits and Other liabilities items. Source for the data on Debt securities item is the Central Depository. • Other sectors : Data on financial loans is collected through the statistical Form SPB-4 Quarterly Report on the Financial Credit Liabilities of Residents to Non-residents and Form SPB-4A Anunal Report on the Financial Credit Liabilities of Residents to Non-residents that cover data on the stock and the various types of transactions on the credits. As the Foreign Exchange Law envisages quarterly reporting of these liabilities, for the first two months of each quarter the BNB publishes estimations on the non-bank enterprises, based on the debt service schedules provided by companies on the loans extended to them and on estimations. Under the Trade credit and advances sub-item are recorded the respective liabilities of residents to non-residents excluding trade credit liabilities towards foreign direct investors. Data are collected from the BNB through Form SPB-6B Report on the liabilities of residents to non-residents. As the Foreign Exchange Law envisages quarterly reporting of these liabilities, for the first two months of each quarter the BNB publishes estimations for the stock of trade credit and advances. Data on liabilities of the households is collected through the annual statistical Form SPB-8 Report on the Assets and Liabilities of Resident Physical Persons to Non-residents. The source for the data on Debt securities item are the Central Depository, the Ministry of Finance and MFIs. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The source data are broadly consistent with the definitions, scope, classifications, and time of recording required in the Guide and in the BPM6. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data collection and processing are adequate to meet timeliness and periodicity for disseminating the external debt statistics. Respondents are made aware of the deadlines set for reporting through detailed instructions. 3.2.1 Source data assessment • Source data are analyzed in the context of revisions. All material changes from available up-to-date data are incorporated into the Gross External Debt according to the ECB revision cycle that prevents the accumulation of revisions over long periods of time. • The source data are analyzed for underreporting/misreporting, in particular to check for: - temporal consistency, - consistency with related data sources. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures During the data compilation process the IT system does not allow processing errors such as coding, editing and tabulation errors. External debt data are not seasonally adjusted. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results • Cross-checking techniques are implemented for verifying the accuracy of the data. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data • Intermediate Gross External Debt items are analyzed reporter by reporter for the data behaviour (e.g., unusual growth patterns) and, if necessary, the BNB contacts the reporting units to obtain additional information. The behaviour of series is cross-checked with related series. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The difference between the beginning and end-period value of external debt data for each liability category is equal to the sum of transactions, price changes, exchange rate changes, and other adjustments for that category. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses • Internal studies of the initial estimates against the revised and final estimates are implemented. • Findings from revision studies are used to refine preliminary data and data collection programs. • Adequate documentation on revisions is maintained. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses • Revisions are measured, assessed, and explained in the Gross External Debt publication. • Analysis of differences between the revised and preliminary data is published for major aggregates to allow an assessment of the reliability of the preliminary data. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Prompt and knowledgeable support is available to users of Gross External Debt statistics. The email address and telephone number of the Press Office are available to users for enquiries. Responses are provided regularly depending on available time resources. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs Publications and other services are available to users of statistics on the BNB website free of charge (except for printed materials).
