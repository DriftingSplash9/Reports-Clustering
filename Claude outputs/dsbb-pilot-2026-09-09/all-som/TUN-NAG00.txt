 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical Framework: The Tunisian system of national accounts closely resembles that of SNA 1968 . GDP is calculated for all goods and services, including production for own use by households, oil prospecting, imputed rents, and household services by domestic employees. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: The system uses three types of nomenclatures: Institutional sectors: Nonfinancial corporations, financial institutions, general government, households, and rest of the world. Private nonprofit institutions are regarded as part of the households sector. Operations: Three types of operations (goods and services, split, financial). Activities-products: local version, which can be converted to SITC rev.3 and CPC. Public consumption is not yet singled out and is not subject to functional classification. Scope of the data Transactions coverage: GDP at constant prices is calculated on the basis of trend indicators such as the industrial production index, market supplies of agricultural products, consumption of materials for construction and public works, transportation receipts, tourist bed-nights, civil service wages, etc. The conversion to current prices uses price indicators such as the index of industrial sale prices and the household consumer price index. Military spending, included in the intermediate consumption of general government, is taken into account in the production of nonmarket services. Geographic coverage: Geographic coverage includes the entire national economic territory, including all resident units regardless of activity. The economic territory corresponds to the geographic territory other than extraterritorial enclaves and including territorial enclaves (Tunisian embassies abroad). Unrecorded activity Unrecorded activity: The estimates cover the modern and informal sectors as well as a number of illegal activities relating to the employment of domestic help and to trade which affect the comprehensiveness of GDP data. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: The valuation system adopted calculates production inclusive of all taxes and intermediate consumption at purchaser’s prices, yielding a value added at market prices. Conversion to value added at factor costs is carried out by deducting taxes and indirect fees net of subsidies, in the knowledge that the latter are determined in terms of cash entitlements. The other variables of the supply and use equilibria are valued: in c.i.f. terms for imports; in f.o.b. terms for exports; at purchaser’s prices for other uses. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Production approach: The basic data are derived from a combination of various sources, namely: annual enterprise surveys; business surveys; agricultural statistics; updated data on small enterprises; administrative information for financial institutions and general government; customs records for imports and exports; the national enterprise registry; household surveys (employment, consumption). 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Production approach: The accounts are drawn up on the basis of the production approach. Accordingly, a production matrix is prepared on the basis of information from various sources. The accounts of the institutional sectors are checked against the output accounts so as to synthesize production and intermediate consumption and subsequently the value added by sector of activity at current prices. Application of a battery of price indexes to the various variables of the supply-use equilibria makes it possible to identify value added by the double deflation of output and intermediate consumption.
