 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The analytical framework used for compiling monetary and financial statistics is based on the MFSM, which the BOM has applied since June 2003. The DCS is a consolidation of the accounts of the Bank of Mauritius and other depository corporations (ODCs), which comprise banks and nonbank deposit taking institutions. In addition to the monthly DCS, its two component surveys, the CBS and the ODCS are also disseminated, along with the underlying sectoral balance sheets for the central bank and for the ODCs. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification of financial instruments and the principles of sectorization applied in the monetary statistics compiled by the BOM are fully consistent with the guidelines of the MFSM. 2.2.1 Scope The classification of financial instruments and the principles of sectorization applied in the monetary statistics compiled by the BOM are fully consistent with the guidelines of the MFSM. Scope of the data As at end-September 2025, the financial corporations sector of Mauritius comprises the BOM (the central bank), 19 banks, 6 non-bank deposit-taking institutions (NBDTIs), the Development Bank of Mauritius (DBM), 5 money-changers, 7 foreign exchange dealers, 5 payment service providers as well as pension funds, global business corporations, investment companies, insurance and re-insurance companies, asset management companies, and other providers of financial services. BOM compiles the Depository Corporations Survey and the Financial Corporations Survey. The institutional coverage of the DCS includes BOM and all 19 banks and 6 NBDTIs. Exceptions to coverage Unrecorded activity Not Applicable 2.4.1 Valuation In line with the general recommendation of the MFSMCG 2016, the valuation of financial assets and liabilities is based on market prices or, for financial assets and liabilities that are not traded or traded infrequently, on market-price equivalents (fair values). Loans and deposits, however, are included in the statistics on the basis of book value, which includes the nominal value of the principal augmented by the accrued interest. This practice is consistent with the MFSMCG 2016 guidelines. In line with the MFSMCG recommendations, loans are reported on a gross basis, without netting of provisions for expected losses. These provisions are reported in liabilities as part of other accounts payable, and are identified in the new reporting template. Financial instruments denominated in foreign currency are converted into national currency values at the mid-point market exchange rate prevailing at the balance sheet date or, for transactions, at the transaction date. This practice is consistent with the recommendations of the MFSMCG 2016. 2.4.2 Recording basis In the DCS and its component surveys, in accordance with the accrual accounting principles recommended by the MFSMCG 2016, interest accruing on deposits, loans, and securities other than shares is incorporated into the outstanding amount of the underlying financial asset or liability. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Data are collected and compiled on a gross basis. Assets and liabilities of the banks are reported on a gross basis in their monthly returns and are aggregated into a statement of assets and liabilities without consolidating interbank positions. Reciprocal asset/liability positions between the BOM and the depository corporations, as well as those among the depository corporations, are separately identified. Consistent with the standard recommended in the MFSMCG 2016, netting and consolidation adjustments are made only in presenting the analytical survey (the DCS). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The source data for compiling the other depository corporations accounts are their financial statements, called Sectoral Balance Sheets, submitted to the BOM on a monthly basis. These reports are sent electronically. In addition to the Sectoral Balance Sheets, banks report detailed breakdown by activity (industry) of claims on private sector in a new reporting template which separately identifies loans to Other Nonfinancial Corporations, Households and Other Sectors. With the emergence of new types of economic activities, the return on sectorwise distribution of credit to the private sector has been replaced by a new template based on the United Nations International Standard Classification (ISIC) of all economic activities, Rev. 4, built on a set of internationally agreed concepts, definitions, principles and classification rules. The new reporting template provides details on loans disbursed by banks and NBDTIs to other nonfinancial corporations, households and other sectors. The data reported are based on the accrual accounting concept, in line with the IMF's MFSMCG 2016. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data used for compiling monetary statistics provide a reasonably good approximation of the concepts, definitions, scope, classifications, and recording principles for compiling sound monetary statistics that are recommended in the MFSMCG 2016. Report forms for depository corporations are based on sound design principles and are reviewed periodically to take account of changed circumstances and needs. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The data collection system allows sufficient time for the compilation of monetary statistics within the timeframe that is necessary to meet the SDDS timeliness guidelines. The accounting records of the BOM are provided to the Monetary and Financial Statistics Section within a week after the end of the month for purposes of monetary data compilation. The returns of the banks and non-bank deposit taking institutions are at present submitted via electronic transmission by the 10th working day after the end of the reference month. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Automated procedures are used to facilitate the monitoring of the accuracy of data reported by individual financial corporations. The data received from ODCs are verified using built-in consistency checks. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Compilation procedures rely on automated consistency checks to minimize processing errors such as coding, editing, and tabulation errors. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Given that data are fully based on accounting records, no estimation techniques are employed to adjust data for missing balance sheet items. Also, no seasonal adjustments are applied to monetary aggregates. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The quality of monetary statistics is monitored throughout the compilation process, which is automated. The source data from banks are subject to a number of built-in internal consistency checks and validation routines. Any inconsistencies and unexplained large data variations are investigated with the reporting bank. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data The accuracy of the monthly balance sheets reports submitted by banks and Non-Bank Deposit-Taking Institutions is cross-checked for consistency with other banking returns. The MFS Section reviews the data and discusses any problem areas directly with the bank and non-bank deposit taking institutions concerned. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Large changes in monetary or credit aggregates are very rare, and likely to occur only in the event of a significant change in the methodology. The procedures for ensuring internal and intertemporal consistency that are built in the data processing and compilation system provide adequate safeguards against unexplained fluctuations and large distortions in monetary statistics. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Monetary statistics are considered final when published and rarely subject to revisions. Studies and analyses of revisions are carried out infrequently or ad hoc. The more recent data revision took place in 2012 after the Bank changed its methodology of adjusting the net foreign assets of other depository corporations for foreign currency deposits of resident global business entities. A Methodological Note was released in the Monthly Statistical Bulletin as well as appropriate footnotes. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Given the infrequent revisions to monetary data, no formal revision studies and analyses are prepared and made available to the public. Even in the case of a major revision of the broad money data in the DCS as of the end of 2006, which was related to the modification of the national definition of broad money, no specific commentary was provided to explain this change in the definition of broad money and resulting data revisions for the past period. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished and non-confidential data are made available to users upon request. This policy, however, is not publicized and the BOM only occasionally receives user requests for historical and non-published data. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Data users can contact the Director of the Economic Analysis & Research and Statistics Department as well as the staff of the Monetary Financial Statistics Section for assistance. They are guided through phone or e-mail. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The Monthly Statistical Bulletin is posted on the website of the Bank as from December 1998. Hard copies are no longer available since July 2011. Monetary statistics data are also disseminated in Annual Report and Monetary Policy Report.
