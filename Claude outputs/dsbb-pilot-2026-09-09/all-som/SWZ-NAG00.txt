 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The national accounts for Swaziland are compiled and disseminated by the National Accounts Section of the Central Statistical Office (CSO). The framework is largely based on the 1968 "System of National Accounts" (SNA), but an exercise is currently ongoing to update to the 1993 SNA. The main data published are annual GDP at current and constant 1985 prices for nine major activity groups, and by expenditure for all major expenditure categories, with private final consumption taken as a residual. Estimates of GDP are available up to the year 2002. Estimates of GNP (GNI), disposable income and gross saving are also compiled. Institutional accounts are not compiled, but with the implementation of the 1993 SNA the CSO plans to introduce these accounts. Estimates of GDP at factor cost are available up to the year 2003. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Economic activities of producer units are classified according to the International Standard Industrial Classification of All Economic Activities (ISIC Rev 2). The nine major activities distinguished are: (1) Agriculture and forestry; (2) Mining and quarrying; (3) Manufacturing; Electricity and water; Construction; Wholesale and retail trade; Hotels and restaurants; (4) Transport; (5) Communication; (6) Banks, Financial institutions and insurance; (7) Real estate; (8) Other services; and (9) Owner occupied dwellings. Imputed bank service charges are subtracted from these to arrive at GDP at factor cost. The components of the expenditure method, are; (1) Private final consumption expenditure; (2) Government final consumption expenditure; (3) Public gross fixed capital formation; (4) Private gross fixed capital formation; (5) Changes in stock; (6) Balance of payments for goods and services; (7) Exports of goods and services; (8) Imports of goods and services and (9) Gross domestic product at market prices. For national accounts purposes, the Harmonized System classification used in the foreign trade statistics in respect of imports and exports of merchandise is converted to the Standard Industrial Trade Classification (SITC). Classifications that will be implemented in the medium term are: Imports by Broad Economic Categories (BEC) in the analysis of production and foreign trade data; ISIC Rev.3 classification is used for industry classifications; the CIOCOP will be used for private consumption expenditure breakdowns; and COFOG will used for the classification of Government expenditure. Scope of the data The national accounts cover the whole country. All transaction categories including financial transactions and excluding illegal transactions. The national accounts cover all units residing in Swaziland. Unrecorded activity No direct estimates are made for the informal sector. Indirect estimates, such as small scale services, are not reliable because of the high rate of non – response. There is thus an urgent need for an informal sector survey. 2.4.1 Valuation Output is recorded at producers' prices, domestic expenditures at purchasers' prices, while both imports and exports are valued at free on board (fob). 2.4.2 Recording basis In principle, all transactions are recorded on an accrual basis; however, the basic data for general government, which are on a cash basis, are not adjusted to an accrual basis. All transactions in goods and services are recorded at market prices. Output is recorded at producers' prices, domestic expenditures at purchasers' prices, while both imports and exports are valued at free on board (fob). The recording periods refer to calendar years where most of the production or activity takes place for most transactions. For example if a respondent’s financial year ends in March the record is for the previous year. Agricultural production is allocated to the harvest year. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Production approach: The CSO maintains a Central Register of Establishments (CRE), which has been constructed on the basis of the census, administrative records on licenses from district councils and municipalities, the Swaziland Income Tax Department, the Swaziland National Provident Fund (SNPF) and the Register of Companies. Agricultural, fishing, and forestry establishments are included in the CRE. The CRE is updated with information received annually. Agriculture: Volumes and prices on cash crops are collected from the Agriculture section of the CSO as well as the Ministry of Agriculture and Cooperatives (MAC) from cooperative unions and processing enterprises. Main food crops: volume and prices are collected from the Agriculture section of the CSO. These are collected annually. The sources of information are the following annual publications: Report of individual tenure farms, Swaziland Annual Agricultural Survey, Swaziland livestock census report, the Early Warning Unit report, foreign trade statistics. In the estimation of the Agriculture sector we separate two different types of producers. The first one is the Individual Tenure Farms (ITF) who farm commercially. The second category is Swazi nation land (SNL), which consists of land owned by the King and administered through the Chiefs to citizens, without any charge involved. In the second category the dominant mode of production is subsistence farming, although this category is undergoing a gradual change to a more commercial mode of production. Just over the half of the area of the country is allocated to SNL farmers. Major crops are sugarcane, citrus fruits, cotton, maize and tobacco. Minor crops are vegetables, groundnuts, potatoes, sweet potatoes, rice beans etc. Livestock is mainly kept for beef and milk. In addition there are minor holdings of poultry, sheep, goats and pigs. Livestock production is independently estimated from agricultural production covering both the ITF and the SNL. The output of the ITF and SNL is taken as the volume of production of different crops multiplied by their unit value. Regarding minor crops, no specific price information is collected but the price information in the Consumer price index is used after deducting 15% to cover for transport and retail margins. The estimation of production output of livestock is taken as the volume of production multiplied by the unit value. The collection of price information is limited to cattle and milk and for other livestock holdings no specific price collection is undertaken. The Consumer price index and South African price data are used for deflation. In all cases, double deflation is used. Multiplying the current output with the base year price yields constant price output. Forestry: The estimation of Forestry covers all timber plantations in the country irrespective of size. The area of man-made forests amounts to 99,000 hectares in Swaziland. The estimation of forestry in the informal sector, grown on Swazi nation land (SNL), is not covered by the survey. The timber is mainly exported both as raw material and as manufactured products after processing in Swaziland. The dominating timber growers also hold processing facilities for the raw material. The sources of data are the annual Survey on timber statistics and the annual Industrial census. The estimation of intermediate consumption is based on benchmark estimation and taken as a ratio to the output. The timber statistics also include annual price information on output prices, this is utilized to arrive at constant prices. Single deflation is being applied assuming that the price movements in the inputs follow the outputs. Mining and quarrying: There are only two companies in this sector and information from the annual census is supplemented and verified with data on sales and produced quantities from the Geological Survey and Mines Department. Current price estimates are derived from the annual census returns and volume indicators are used to derive the constant price estimates. Manufacturing: Manufacturing consists of a small number of large establishments that dominate the each industry. Larger firms in the country are the sugar millers, the timber and pulp producers and the beverage producers. In contrast to the large firms we find a number of small entities mainly in the textile and handicraft activities. The sources of information for this estimation are the quarterly data collected for the production index and the annual census of manufacturing industries, which covers all the registered manufacturing companies. A less detailed questionnaire is sent out to the smaller firms mainly in the textile and handicraft industry. Electricity and Water: includes the generation, transmission and distribution of electric energy. These activities are carried out by the Swaziland Electricity Board (SEB). Electric power is both generated in the country as hydropower and imported from South Africa. Imported electricity makes up about 85 percent of the total supply, on average for the latest 3-4 years. All source data are available from the Annual Report of SEB. Output is defined as total sales of electricity. Intermediate consumption includes imports of electricity. Constant prices are derived by projection with quantities sold. The collection, purification and distribution of water is carried out by a parastatal, Swaziland Water and Sewerage Board, from 1995 turned into a corporation, Water Services Corporation (WSC). As the name indicates, WSC is also engaged in sewage disposal services. However, it is not possible to extract separate data for sewerage so it is treated as a secondary activity. Financial source data are available from the Financial Statement of WSC. Output, at constant prices is calculated by deflation with a price index calculated on the basis of water rates, available from WSC. Both output and intermediate consumption are projected with quantities produced from the source mentioned above. Thus, the assumption is that the ratio of intermediate consumption over output is constant. Construction: Construction in Swaziland is undertaken mainly by private contractors. There are a handful of major construction companies in the country responsible for the bulk of the construction activity. The estimation covers all the registered companies involved in construction activity. No estimation is made to cover for own-account construction in the informal sector. The source of the estimation is the annual census of construction, civil engineering and allied industries. This census covers all the registered construction companies in the country. The census returns are however in some cases too late for timely estimates and the quality of response low. Estimates of output at current prices are therefore also based on information on imports of building materials that are used to project earlier estimates. If census data are not available, estimates of intermediate consumption at current prices and of compensation of employees are based on the assumption that its ratio over output is the same as the last year when census returns were available. Single deflation is applied. The South African index of building and construction has been used as deflator. Wholesale and Retail Trade: Wholesalers and retailers are considered as supplying services rather than goods to their customers by storing and making a selection of goods easily available. The output of such services is measured by the trade margins realised on goods purchased for resale. Outputs of other services produced by wholesale and retail trade are valued as the revenue from the services. Intermediate consumption consists only of goods and services needed to run the trading establishments, such as packaging materials, electricity, office supplies, and rent. Value added of wholesale e value of imports of goods, c.i.f., while output of retail trade is projected with an average of the development of the same imports and revenue of sales tax. The latter has been adjusted for changes in the tax rate. Intermediate consumption and compensation of employees are assumed to make up the same ratio of output as for the benchmark year. Output is deflated with the CPI. Hotels and Restaurants: includes hotels, other types of accommodation like rest camps and caravan parks, and restaurants. Many hotel enterprises also operate a restaurant and a few also have a casino. Therefore, the output of hotels, besides accommodation services, also includes restaurant and casino services. For hotels the CSO collects data on total receipts, number of bed nights sold and number of room nights sold. Current price output for the benchmark year is projected with total receipts. Intermediate consumption is assumed to make up the same percentage of output as in the base year and compensation of employees the same percentage of value added. Constant price output and intermediate consumption are projected with a volume index calculated as the average of the index numbers for bed nights and room nights sold. For restaurants the annual changes at constant prices depend on the number of tourists but also on restaurant expenditure by residents. A volume index is constructed as the average of bed nights sold (expenditure by tourists) and an assumed annual increase of 1 percent for expenditure by residents. Both output and intermediate consumption is projected with that volume index. For current prices both output and intermediate consumption are reflated by a price index calculated as the average of CPI for food, alcoholic beverages and tobacco. Compensation of employees is assumed to make up the same percentage of value added as in the base year. Transport and Communication: Transport includes estimates for land transport and air transport. Land transport comprises railway transport, freight road transport and passenger road transport. The source of information for the estimation is the Ministry of transport and communications annual bulletin and the annual census of the transport industry. Air transport Current Prices: The source of information for air transport is the financial statement of the Royal Swazi Airline, the only company involved in air transportation in the country. Constant Prices: To obtain the estimation at constant prices single deflation is applied by considering the number of passengers transported as volume indicator. Railway transport Current Prices: The source of information for railway transport is the financial statement of the Swaziland Railway company as published in the annual bulletin of the Ministry of transport and communication. Constant Prices: Single deflation is being applied where output in real terms is extrapolated by the number of tons hauled. Passenger road transport Current Prices: Passenger road transport comprises estimation of buses and taxis. The annual census of transport is used to derive annual output and inputs coefficients per vehicle in use. This information is multiplied with the number of vehicles operating in the country. This information is obtained from the Central Motor Registry. Constant Prices: The estimation of output at constant prices is obtained by projecting the base year estimation with the number of vehicles operating. Intermediate consumption is assumed to consist of fuel and spare parts. Thus the deflation is carried out by using a composite fuel and non-fuel intermediate consumption by vehicle. Communication: includes national post activities and telecommunications. These activities are carried out by one parastatal, Swaziland Posts and Telecommunications Corporation (SPTC). All required source data are available from SPTC: the Annual Report, returns to the CSO survey, the Annual Transport and Communications Sector Bulletin published by the Ministry of Transport and Communications Output. In terms of the Annual Report of SPTC output is equal to turnover plus other income. The latter is a very minor item (rentals). Details of turnover are available as well as quantitative data. On the basis of the latter a Laspeyres volume index is calculated and used for estimates at constant prices. Finance, Insurance: This industry comprises financial intermediaries, such as the central bank, commercial banks, other financial institutions and financial services insurance institutions, pension funds, and insurance brokers. Most source data are available from annual reports and the annual census Real Estate: This industry comprises real estate and business service activities. Real estate encompasses the renting, management and operation of buildings. The source of information for current price estimates is the annual Census of real estate companies' agents, property owners, landlords etc. and the 1995 Household income and expenditure survey. For constant prices single deflation is applied were the number of real estate entities in the estimation frame are used to project the base year estimate. Government Services: comprise all establishments of general government that are classified as "other non-market producers". There are two levels of general government in Swaziland. Central government includes, firstly, all ministries and departments that are financed through the government budget. All these are non-market producers. The government budget also includes a few trading accounts recording market activities, notably the Central Transport Administration; these are not included in producers of government services. Secondly, central government includes activities financed through technical assistance from donor countries, often employing expatriate experts. Local government includes the city councils and the town councils. Value added of local government make up only 2-3 percent of total value added of general government. Activities of general government are not classified into industries. Output of producers of government services is measured as the sum of their costs of production. For ministries and departments the basic data for financial years ending on March 31 are available from the Treasury Annual Report and analysis sheets for the Ministry of Finance. Total expenditure of local government is available from the Central Bank and annual statements from the city councils. Constant prices are estimated by deflation with the same indices as for departments and ministries and compensation of employees with CPI, all items. For estimating compensation of employees at constant prices we deflate the current price estimate using a Paasche salary index. The source for the index is CSO's statistics on employment and wages. Technical assistance: The total amount is available in the balance of payments. Intermediate consumption and compensation of employees is assumed to make up 50 percent each. The first item is deflated by the production price index for imported goods from South Africa. Regarding compensation of employees the assumption is that the salaries of expatriate experts increase with 5 percent every year. Ownership of dwellings: is treated as fictitious establishments, which produce housing services for their own consumption. Current price estimates are derived by taking residential buildings completed in the two major cities and using their value to estimate value added. For constant prices the benchmark is the number of owner occupied dwellings in 1985, which is projected forward with an index of the number of residential buildings completed in the two major cities. Other Services: The estimation of social, community and personal services covers Radio and television broadcasting Electrical repair shop etc. The source is the annual census for community and social services for current prices. Constant prices are projected with the growth rate of the other sectors from the 1985 benchmark year. Expenditure approach Only current price estimates are derived on the expenditure side. Household Final Consumption Expenditure: Household final consumption expenditure is calculated as a residual, taking GDP from the production side and subtracting the directly estimated components of the expenditure method that are described below. Government Final Consumption Expenditure: Derived from government accounts and information on aid-funded expenditure outside government budgets using the same sources as for production. Gross Fixed Capital Formation: Construction estimates and Investment in machinery and equipment are derived from imports data. The imported inputs to the construction industry are used as a proxy for construction and selected imported machinery is assumed to be capital formation. Changes in Inventories: The methodology for estimating this is still being developed. Previous estimates are to be revised because they reflected a continuous growth, which is not realistic. Foreign Trade: Compiled by the Central Bank and used as they are with no modification. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Production approach: The benchmark data are extrapolated at current and constant prices with the indicators discussed under section IV. GDP at factor cost is then calculated as the sum of all value added estimates. Expenditure approach: GDP has not been calculated from the expenditure approach as a result of poor results from previous Household Income and Expenditure surveys and the prior deficient coverage of foreign trade. With the improvements in the household income and expenditure survey results and trade data then GDP by expenditure can be calculated. Revision and dissemination policy: For every year two estimates are prepared. Provisional estimates are ready by end May of the year following the reference year. One year later, the final estimates are available. Overall revisions of the national accounts methodology are made when there is evidence that the current sources and methodology do not lead to data that reflect actual activity. CSO also publishes them in the Annual Statistical Bulletin. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Reconciliation: No reconciliation takes place, as all components of the expenditure method are not estimated separately.
