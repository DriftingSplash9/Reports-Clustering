 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: Canada's external debt statistics are a subset of the international investment position which is the statistical statement that presents the value and composition of financial assets and liabilities with non-residents at period-end. Canada’s gross external debt presents the market value of Canadian debt instruments held by non-residents by sector of Canadian issuer, by type of debt instruments and by maturity. Supplementary data on Canada’s gross external debt at nominal value and by currency are also produced. Data are published quarterly and are estimated from a combination of quarter-end position data sources as well as from flows and other changes, in particular currency and price changes, and annual benchmark estimates based on surveys. Relationship to international guidelines: The Canadian statement is compiled according to international standards and conventions described in the sixth edition of the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6) of the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and the External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (EDG). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: External debt statistics show the debt liabilities by institutional sectors (general government, monetary authorities, banking sector and other sectors) broken down by maturity (short/long term) and by type of debt instruments (money market instruments, bonds and notes, loans, trade credits, deposits, other debt liabilities and intercompany debt liabilities on a net and gross basis). Scope of the data Coverage of external debt: In comparison to the liabilities side of the International investment position the quarterly external debt data do not include any financial liabilities arising from participation rights, i.e. shares, equity capital and reinvested earnings regarding direct investment. It only includes liabilities in the form of debt instruments. Geographical coverage: Data cover the entire Canadian territory and include financial debt liabilities vis-à-vis all countries of the world. 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to unit of account: Positions denominated in original currencies are converted to Canadian dollars using the closing rate of exchange at period-end. Valuation: Canada's external financial liabilities are recorded at market value. Nominal (book) value is also available as supplementary information. 2.4.2 Recording basis Recording basis: Canada's external financial liabilities include accrued interest. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Direct investment: Enterprise surveys are mainly used to compile direct investment debt instruments data. A number of administrative and public sources are also used to complement the survey results. Portfolio investment: Annual surveys, administrative and public sources are used for portfolio debt liabilities. Other investment: Enterprise surveys, banking statistics and other public and administrative sources are used for other investment debt instruments. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Direct investment: Annual surveys of the value of direct investment form the basis of the benchmark estimates for the year-end positions. Quarterly positions between these benchmarks and for periods subsequent to the latest benchmark data are estimated using quarterly flows at market value. Portfolio investment: A detailed system is used to process data on Canadian bonds and money market instruments. Positions are derived on an issue by issue basis, at market value. Other investment: Loans from foreign banks are derived from annual surveys and quarterly flows. Deposit liabilities are also available from the banks on a quarterly basis. The other miscellaneous accounts are based on annual surveys adjusted to quarterly positions using quarterly flows. Currency fluctuations: In addition, outstanding positions for all accounts denominated in foreign currency are re-evaluated each quarter to reflect changes in exchange rates. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Major changes to the estimates resulting from the implementation of new concepts and methodologies are explained to users through special studies (example: Revisions to Canada's International Investment Position ). Users are also informed of reference periods that have been revised at the time the data are disseminated.
