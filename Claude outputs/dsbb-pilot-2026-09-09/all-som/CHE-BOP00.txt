 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The methodological framework follows the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6) ( https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/bop/2007/bopman6.htm ) and are strictly applied to our data. The balance of payments is a systematic presentation of the economic transactions between institutional units in Switzerland and the Principality of Liechtenstein (i.e. residents) and those of other countries (i.e. non-residents) during a specified period. The balance of payments consists of the current account, the capital account and the financial account. The statistical difference reported in connection with the balance of payments covers discrepancies arising from errors and omissions in the statistical data collected. The current account reports transactions which result from the exchange of goods and services as well as from primary income (labour income, investment income) and secondary income (current transfers). The transactions are broken down into: Receipts (credit): Receipts report the value a resident receives from a non-resident in a transaction. Expenses (debit): Expenses report the value a resident provides to a non-resident in a transaction. The current account balance is calculated from receipts (credits) minus expenses (debits). A positive balance is referred to as a current account surplus, a negative balance as a current account deficit. The capital account reports transactions involving non-produced and non-financial assets as well as other transfers (e.g. debt reduction measures, settlement payments). The transactions are broken down into: Receipts (credit): Receipts report the value a resident receives from a non-resident in a capital transfer. Expenses (debit): Expenses report the value a resident provides to a non-resident in a capital transfer. The capital account balance is calculated from receipts (credit) minus expenses (debit). The financial account reports transactions that involve the exchange of financial assets. It can be broken down by investment purpose into direct investment, portfolio investment, derivatives, other investment and reserve assets. The transactions are broken down into: Net acquisition of financial assets: Purchases less sales (debit minus credit) of foreign financial assets by residents. Net incurrence of liabilities: Purchases less sales (credit minus debit) of domestic financial assets by non-residents. The financial account balance is calculated from the net acquisition of financial assets minus the net incurrence of liabilities. The statistical difference is calculated from the financial account balance minus the sum of the current account and capital account balances. Theoretically, it should be zero as every transaction is entered twice in the balance of payments. The statistical difference is the result of the discrepancies that arise in practice from errors and omissions in statistical data. A positive value indicates either that current account receipts/net incurrence of liabilities have been underestimated or that current account expenses/net acquisition of financial assets have been overestimated. A negative value indicates either that current account receipts/net incurrence of liabilities have been overestimated or that current account expenses/net acquisition of financial assets have been underestimated. Constant changing between positive and negative values may be an indication of difficulties in allocating data to the appropriate period. Scope of the data The balance of payments is a systematic presentation of the economic transactions between institutional units in Switzerland and the Principality of Liechtenstein (i.e. residents) and those of other countries (i.e. non-residents) during a specified period. In accordance with bilateral agreements, the Principality of Liechtenstein belongs to the Swiss customs and monetary territory. In the balance of payments, it is considered as a domestic entity. 2.4.1 Valuation Transactions are valued at market price. 2.4.2 Recording basis Conversions to unit of account: Transaction data reported in foreign currencies are converted to local currency at transaction point exchange rates or, if not possible, at period average exchange rates. If stock data in foreign currencies are used to calculate transactions, the change in stock in the original currency is converted to local currency at period average exchange rates. Recording of income: Accrual basis with offset recorded in the underlying instrument (implicitly). 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Current account: Goods: General merchandise according to the foreign trade statistics of the customs authority;non-monetary gold, merchanting and different supplements to trade. Services: Survey of transport operators for transportation, survey by the Swiss Federal Statistical Office (accommodation survey etc.) for travel, enterprise survey for telecommunication, computer and information services, manufacturing, maintenance and repair services as well as other business services, surveys for financial and insurance services, administrative information for government services. Primary income: Primary sources for compensation of employees: Estimates of labour income of cross-border commuters (based on number of workers and on average income earned) and information from international organisations and consular representatives. Estimates of income from securities: Surveys of securities holdings and their yields. Survey of enterprises for direct investment income, survey of banks for lending and deposits income, information from the Swiss National Bank for receipts on reserve asset. Secondary income: Primary sources for the public sector: Government reports. Primary sources for the private sector: Surveys of private aid agencies, estimates of the remittances of immigrant workers (based on average income earned) and surveys of insurance companies (premium income and payments). Capital transfers: Primary sources for debt forgiveness: Government reports. Primary sources for non-produced nonfinancial assets: Reports from other institutions and surveys of enterprises. Financial account: Direct investment: Quarterly and annual surveys on cross-border capital linkages submitted by domestic enterprises to the Swiss National Bank. Portfolio investment: Quarterly surveys on securities bought and sold on behalf of residents and non-residents by domestic enterprises, banks and custodians to the Swiss National Bank. Derivatives: Quarterly surveys reported by domestic enterprises and banks to the Swiss National Bank. Other investment: Government sector: Government reports. Commercial bank lending: Transactions are computed usingbalance sheet data reported by resident banks on their claims and liabilities vis-à-vis non-residents Other sectors: Data reported by enterprises, international banking statistics. Central bank: Accounts of the Swiss National Bank. Reserve assets: Accounts of the Swiss National Bank. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Current account Goods: Additions: Unchecked goods trade, small consignments, goods procured in ports. Subtractions: Goods under manufacturing, returned goods, CIF/FOB adjustment on imports. Income: Adjustments to improve coverage: Estimates for reinvested direct investment income not covered in the quarterly survey. Financial account Direct investment: Transaction data are verified by comparing them with changes in stocks, quarterly transaction data are verified by comparing them with annual transaction data. Adjustments to improve coverage: Estimates for transactions in real estate assets based on SNB's collective capital investment statistics and for transactions in real estate liabilities based on actual changes in ownership according to Federal Department of Justice statistics. Other investment: Changes in stocks due to valuation changes or statistical changes are excluded to compute transactions. For balance sheet data reported by resident banks, the concept of remaining maturity is used. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The data are not seasonally adjusted. None
