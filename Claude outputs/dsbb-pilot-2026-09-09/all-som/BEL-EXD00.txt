 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The overall structure of concepts and definitions follows internationally accepted standards, guidelines, or good practices. Concepts and definitions are in line with the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual ( BPM6) . The methodology is described in various documents available on the website of the NBB . 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification/sectorization systems used are consistent with internationally accepted standards and guidelines (the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual ( BPM6 ) and ECB Guideline on the statistical reporting requirements of the European Central Bank in the field of external statistics. Total liabilities owed by residents are broken down in a three-tier system as follows: by debtor sector (general government, monetary authorities, monetary and financial institutions, other sectors), by original maturity (short term up to one year and long term), by instrument (money market instruments in the short term or bonds and notes in the long term; loans, currency and deposits; trade credits; intercompany loans under direct investment)."The exclusion (from direct investment) of debt positions between affiliated financial enterprises is specified as being for deposit-taking corporations, investment funds, and other financial intermediaries except insurance companies and pension funds" (BPM6). It should be noted that intercompany loans under direct investment show up neither at the debtor sector level nor at the maturity level. Those loans are treated as a separate instrument. Intercompany lending is included in IIP under direct investment or under other investment. Private sector debt is to be found under banks and under other sectors. Data for banks and for other sectors are collected separately. External debt liabilities are allocated to the sector of the immediate "borrower", except for debt securities. Indeed, for debt securities, positions are shown as debt of domestic issuers vis-à-vis non-resident end-investors even if these positions do not originate on the occasion of the issue of these domestic securities by the issuer (original "borrower") but on the occasion of transactions on secondary markets between a resident seller (immediate "borrower") and a non-resident buyer or vice versa. In principle, the external debt liabilities are classified under the institutional sector of the debtor and not under the institutional sector of the guarantor. Nevertheless, some respondents will report, under their own issues, securities that they have not issued but that they have guaranteed. Arrears cannot be separately identified. Scope of the data Gross debt positions or residual approach Most data sources are directly collected as gross debt positions between residents and non-residents. There are two major exceptions on this rule: portfolio liabilities of the general government and of the other sectors. In both cases, the residual approach is applied, combining data collected about the outstanding amounts of issued domestic securities and data collected about the holdings of these securities by residents. The holdings by non-residents are then computed as the difference between the outstanding amounts of issued domestic securities and the holdings by residents. Comprehensiveness of the scope of data Coverage of households might be partial because of limited data accessibility. Currency External debt data measure debt liabilities irrespective of currency, so including both debt liabilities denominated in domestic currency and debt liabilities denominated in foreign currencies. Arrears Arrears are included insofar as they are included in sources. Short position "Short positions occur when an institutional unit sells securities that it does not own. [...] The short position is shown as a negative asset, rather than a liability." (BPM6) . Therefore, short positions are not taken into account for EXD. Exceptions to coverage Financial derivatives for the households' sector are not included. Unrecorded activity Direct investment income: accrued interest is estimated in the income account but not included in the financial account. Accrued interest is not included in the reported investment positions of debt securities. In order to reach consistency with BPM6 and SDDS Plus Guide, an IT project on the securities database has started to include the accrued interest. The first results are planned to be ready in 2026. Registration of financial lease: no registration is made neither in the BOP financial account, nor in the IIP and gross external debt, for the lump sum of a financial lease contract representative of the underlying capital of the contract; only a proxy is computed based on registered flows related to financial leasing. Financial derivatives for the households' sector are not included. 2.4.1 Valuation Positions are valued at end-of-period market prices when available. If a market price is not available, the nominal value or a best estimate is used. 2.4.2 Recording basis The conversion of the original currencies into euros for outstanding amounts is based on the monthly end-of-month exchange rate. The conversion of the original currency into euros is based on the monthly average exchange rate for transactions and other flows. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Grossing-up techniques are marginally used in portfolio investment, in the scope of security-by-security reporting (see 3.1.1). They allow to extrapolate from the annual frequency to the quarterly frequency if need be. Grossing-up techniques are used to compute the trade credits component, based on the use of a monthly sample survey on trade credits stock data. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Source for monetary authorities Data are provided by the National Bank of Belgium based on a balance sheet approach. Source for public external debt On the one hand, data for loans, currency, and deposits and for other debt liabilities come from administrative records. On the other hand, data for debt securities come from security-by-security positions reporting surveys. Source for monetary and financial institutions Data for monetary and financial institutions originate from their assets and liabilities statistics. Source for the other sectors for debt securities data come from security-by-security stock surveys loans, currency, and deposits, for trade credits and for other debt liabilities are compiled on the basis of stock surveys 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data reasonably approximate the definitions, scope, sectorization, classifications, valuation, and time of recording required. The definitions used in the data sources are, when concerning surveys organized by the National Bank of Belgium, specific to the surveys by domain and input oriented (mostly also with reference to accountancy) but in line with the international definitions and standards of the outputs to be produced. For other sources, the definitions of the input are related to the reference frame of the source and when necessary, adjusted to be in line with the international definitions and standards. The valuation of the source data is mostly made according to accountancy rules and adjusted afterwards in the compilation process to fit with the international standards. The same is valid for the time of recording. All source data are real stock data. Debt securities Data come from the National Bank of Belgium, from assets and liabilities of the banking sector and from the security-by-security data collection system (for the general government and for the other sectors). Loans, currency, and deposits - Trade credits - Other debt liabilities Data come from the National Bank of Belgium, from the Treasury department, from assets and liabilities of the banking sector and from the new survey system for the other sectors. A comparison is made with BIS & BSI statistics for the other sectors. Direct investment Stocks are based on a survey system. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness All source data are available on time for quarterly compilation. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data are routinely assessed, e.g., for coverage, sample error, response error, and non-sampling error; the results of the assessments are monitored and made available to guide statistical processes. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Sound statistical techniques are used in dealing with the data sources. ECB guideline 2004 / 15 The new approach for debt securities is an application of "model 3" out of the Guideline of "The European Central Bank of 16 July 2004 on the statistical reporting requirements of the European Central Bank in the field of balance of payments and international investment position statistics, and the international reserves template" (ECB/2004/15). Model 3 encompasses the collection of monthly security-by-security stock data (at least 85 % of total) and the derivation of net transactions, net exchange rate changes and net price changes (and possibly net other adjustments). Residual approach The residual approach is applied to the general government and to the other sectors for debt securities. Outstanding amounts of issued securities are retrieved from the security-by-security reporting system for portfolio investment, with exchange rate and price valuation. Holdings of the same securities by residents also flow from the reporting system, with exchange rate and price valuation. These holdings are provided, just as on the assets side of IIP, in a mixed approach combining direct reporting and custodian reporting. This mixed approach seems only very marginally sensitive to the problematic of repo's and of similar "mobilization transactions". The difference between outstanding amounts of issued securities and holdings by residents produces estimates of holdings by non-residents. Sample surveys Data for the trade credits component in other investment is computed using an extrapolation process based on the outcome of a sample survey on trade credits (assets and liabilities). The reference population based on external trade statistics is stratified referring to different levels of trade distinguishing imports and exports. Cut off surveys The FDI components and the other investment components of the other sectors are provided by ad hoc surveys addressed to a limited number of enterprises selected on the basis of different balance sheets/accountancy items for which a threshold has been defined. The selected enterprises report different surveys (FDI/Foreign Other Investment (FOI) flows by instrument, FDI/FOI stocks, by instrument, income, results, group structure...) by individual counterpart (shareholder, investment enterprise). 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Automated and manual quality checks are applied to all steps of the production process, such as formal correctness, coherence with accounting data, coherence with other reported data and outlier detection. In case of wrong or implausible data, the reporting agent is contacted. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Statistical discrepancies in intermediate data are assessed and investigated. Statistical discrepancies which show up in the intermediate data are traced back to the primary source data in order to identify possible reasons. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The discrepancies, anomalies or other issues in statistical output are analyzed and solved as much as possible. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Additional non-confidential statistics can be obtained from bop@nbb.be 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Contact information is published.
