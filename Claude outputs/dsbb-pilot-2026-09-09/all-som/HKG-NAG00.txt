 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The System of National Accounts 2008 ( 2008 SNA ) is closely followed in compiling the GDP statistics. In particular, the 2008 SNA recommendations regarding (a) adoption of the change of ownership principle in recording goods sent abroad for processing and merchanting, (b) capitalisation of research and development expenditure, (c) estimating the output of financial intermediation services indirectly measured using the reference rate method, (d) recording the value of employee stock options in compensation of employees, and (e) adoption of new classification and enhanced estimation methods for trade in services statistics have been implemented. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classifications used in the compilation of GDP statistics are in conformity with internationally recommended systems and follow the 2008 SNA . For household final consumption expenditures, classification of goods and services is based on the Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose ( COICOP ), with local adaptations. Government consumption expenditures are classified according to the Classification of the Functions of Government (COFOG). International transactions of goods are classified according to the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System. International transactions of services are based on the recommended classification given in the 6th Edition of the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual ( BPM6 ). Sectoral classification of economic activities is based on the Hong Kong Standard Industrial Classification (HSIC) Version 2.0, an adapted version of the United Nations' International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC). 2.2.1 Scope The classifications used in the compilation of GDP statistics are in conformity with internationally recommended systems and follow the 2008 SNA . For household final consumption expenditures, classification of goods and services is based on the Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose ( COICOP ), with local adaptations. Government consumption expenditures are classified according to the Classification of the Functions of Government (COFOG). International transactions of goods are classified according to the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System. International transactions of services are based on the recommended classification given in the 6th Edition of the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual ( BPM6 ). Sectoral classification of economic activities is based on the Hong Kong Standard Industrial Classification (HSIC) Version 2.0, an adapted version of the United Nations' International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC). Scope of the data The following GDP statistics are compiled and disseminated: annual expenditures of GDP at current market prices and in volume terms; annual value added by economic activity at current prices and in volume terms; annual value added components at current prices by economic activity; quarterly expenditures of GDP at current market prices and in volume terms; quarterly value added by economic activity in volume terms. The delimitation of the constituent units of the economy is in accordance with the 2008 SNA . The geographic coverage includes the territory administered by the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region. Transactions with Chinese Mainland are treated as external transactions. The production boundary and the asset boundary generally follow the 2008 SNA . Fixed assets include building and construction, costs of ownership transfer, machinery, equipment, computer software (including databases) and research and development. Mineral exploration is insignificant and is not covered. Valuables and entertainment, literary or artistic originals are currently not included in the fixed assets. Deviation from the scope is kept under review. Exceptions to coverage Transactions in military goods are excluded. Unrecorded activity With the exception of the retail of goods by unlicensed hawkers, illegal production is generally not recorded in the GDP. 2.4.1 Valuation The data are mainly valued at market prices. Final consumption expenditures are generally valued at purchasers' prices. Owner-occupied housing services are valued at comparable rental values. Non-market services are valued at cost. Estimates of gross domestic fixed capital formation, at purchasers' prices, are derived by adding transfer costs (in the case of land and buildings) or distributors' margins (in the case of machinery and equipment) to the cost of fixed assets. Exports and imports of goods are valued at f.o.b. Market output is valued at basic prices, and output for own final use and intermediate consumption at purchasers' prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis Transactions are generally recorded on an accrual basis, except for government expenditures which are largely recorded on cash payment basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Transactions between establishments within the same enterprise are recorded on a gross basis, as recommended in the international guidelines. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The expenditure measure of GDP which is more well established, is presented as the single measure of GDP. The difference with the production measure is presented as the statistical discrepancy. Expenditure approach: Private consumption expenditure: refers to the total value of final consumption expenditure on goods and services by households and non-profit institutions serving households. Data sources include external trade statistics, administrative statistics, economic surveys, household expenditure surveys and other sample surveys conducted by the C&SD. These are supplemented by information from other miscellaneous sources where appropriate. Government consumption expenditure: Data sources include government accounts data and income and expenditure data of quasi-government institutions. Gross domestic fixed capital formation: The main data sources for various components of the gross domestic fixed capital formation are: Public sector building and construction: government accounts data and administrative data from public corporations. Private sector building and construction: annual and quarterly surveys on construction activities. Costs of ownership transfer: government accounts data (for stamp duties in connection with property transactions), and administrative data on number of property transfer cases, average property transaction value and average rate of charge (for estimates of legal fees and agents' commissions). Machinery, equipment and intellectual property products: external merchandise trade statistics, annual economic surveys, government accounts and administrative data from public corporations. Changes in inventories: Data are mainly collected from annual and quarterly economic surveys. Exports and imports of goods: external merchandise trade statistics, trade surveys, economic surveys and administrative records. Exports and imports of services: The basic data sources include (a) Tourism--statistics from the Hong Kong Tourism Board (for expenditure of visitors), data on household surveys (for per capita expenditure abroad of Hong Kong residents), and administrative data on number of Hong Kong residents travelling abroad; (b) Annual and quarterly surveys on services covering transportation services, trade-related services, insurance services, and financial services; and (c) Administrative data e.g. for government services. Production approach: Quarterly GDP by economic activity is compiled in volume terms only. Current price GDP by economic activity is compiled annually, based on data collected from the Annual Survey of Economic Activities, supplemented by data from administrative sources. The basic data for estimation of quarterly value added in volume terms include business receipts, producer price indices (PPI) and statistics on the output of various economic activities which are obtained from monthly and quarterly surveys, viz. the Monthly Survey of Retail Sales (MRS), Quarterly Survey of Service Industries (QSSI), Quarterly Survey of Restaurant Receipts and Purchases (QSR), Quarterly Survey of Industrial Production (QSIP) and Quarterly Survey of Construction Output (QSCO). Financial statistics are also required. Statistics on banks' financial assets and liabilities, foreign currency positions and income from commissions and other fees are obtained from the Hong Kong Monetary Authority. The Hang Seng Composite Index is obtained from the HSI Services Limited. Other statistical data include trade statistics, insurance statistics, consumer price indices and various administrative returns from government departments and related indicators. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data, particularly data from statistical surveys conducted by the C&SD, generally are consistent with the definitions, scope, classifications, valuation and time of recording of the GDP statistics. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Most of the monthly and quarterly source data are produced in a timely manner that enables the dissemination of GDP statistics according to the SDDS timeliness requirement. In some cases, advance/preliminary estimates based on partial data collected from statistical surveys are used. Subsequent revision to the source data will be effected in the GDP figures. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Statistical surveys are developed and conducted based on methodologically sound approaches. Quality indicators of source data such as sampling errors, response rates, micro and macro editing are monitored on a regular basis. Analysis of (a) the revision of the source data and (b) comparison between quarterly and annual estimates is also done regularly. All source data are reviewed to ensure correctness and continuity in the time series before being used. Updated data from various sources are incorporated into the GDP statistics according to a revision policy. For administrative data, their compatibility and approaches for adaptation to meet the national accounts requirements are reviewed regularly. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Statistical surveys are designed to harmonise with the concepts and definitions of national accounts statistics as far as possible. Regular meetings between survey statisticians and national accounts statisticians are held to communicate changes and adaptations arising from change in international standards, developments in good practices and emerging users' need. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Expenditure approach: Private consumption expenditure, government consumption expenditure, gross domestic fixed capital formation and changes in inventories are derived independently. 1. Estimation of current price data: Private consumption expenditure is estimated by first compiling estimates of consumption expenditure on goods and services in the domestic market, then adding residents' expenditure abroad for non-business purposes, and deducting expenditure of non-residents in the domestic market. For consumption expenditure in the domestic market, the benchmark estimates are largely derived using the commodity flow method. The quarterly estimates are derived by extrapolation of the benchmark data using value indicators (e.g. retail sales, restaurants receipts), volume indicators (e.g. population, employment in selected services industries), and administrative records (e.g. rental of public housing). The estimate of residents' expenditure abroad is derived by multiplying the number of residents' outbound trips (based on administrative records) for non-business purposes by the estimate of per resident person's expenditure abroad (based on household survey data). Government consumption expenditure is computed as the sum of compensation of government employees and purchases of goods and services by the government units, less sales receipts for goods and services provided by these government units. Government purchases of goods and services include the consumption of fixed capital which is estimated using the perpetual inventory model. Gross domestic fixed capital formation: Gross domestic fixed capital formation in building and construction including dwellings, at purchasers' prices covers the value of work put in place, real estate developers' margins and transfer costs incurred on purchasers in acquiring the assets. Real estate developers' margins of new buildings is estimated by taking the sales value of property less the sum of the current cost of the land which the property stands on and the project development outlays (including payments to contractors, architectural, engineering, other professional fees and project related expenses). Adjustments are made to discount holding gains on land over the course of project development. The margins covers all newly developed properties, irrespective of whether they are completed during the period in question or are still under construction; whether they have been fully sold or partly sold, awaiting sale, or have in fact been sold before the period in question. Building which is not yet put on the market for sale, their current market values are estimated based on information on the assessment values provided by real estate developers who report data to the economic survey. For acquisition of existing buildings, only the transfer costs are included. For private sector, the quarterly estimate is based on the quarterly survey on construction output. For public sector, the quarterly estimate is based on government accounts data and administrative records from public corporations. Gross domestic fixed capital formation in machinery and equipment is estimated using the commodity flow method. The estimate at purchasers' prices is derived by adding the distributors' margins to the value of retained imports of capital goods. The latter is based on external merchandise trade statistics. The estimate also takes account of domestically produced machinery and equipment based on data from economic surveys. Gross domestic fixed capital formation in computer software is the sum of the business and government expenditure on computer software purchased and developed in-house. For the public sector, the estimate is mainly based on government accounts data. For the private sector, the annual estimate is based on data collected through economic surveys and the quarterly estimate is compiled with reference to available indicators such as imports of computer hardware and software. Gross domestic fixed capital formation in research and development is the sum of the business and government expenditure on research and development activities that are performed on own account and contracted out to other organisations. For the public sector, the estimate is mainly based on administrative data. For the private sector, the annual estimate is based on data collected through economic surveys and the quarterly estimate is compiled with reference to quarterly business receipts indices. Changes in inventories are derived as the difference in book value of inventories between the beginning and the end of the reference period, with adjustment for appreciation (or depreciation) in the value of inventories within the period. The adjustment is made by revaluing the opening and closing book values of inventories at the average current prices of the period by means of appropriate price indices. Exports and imports of goods on f.o.b. basis: Exports and imports of goods are based on monthly external merchandise trade statistics, data collected from the Quarterly Survey of Merchanting and Other Trading Activities and estimates on insurance and freight costs, which are based on the data from establishment surveys, administrative records and relevant market information. Exports and imports of services: Data on expenditure of incoming visitors and travellers are based on visitors survey conducted by the Hong Kong Tourism Board. The estimate of residents' expenditure abroad is compiled from data from household surveys (for per capita expenditure abroad of Hong Kong residents) and administrative records (for the number of outbound trips by residents). For services other than travel, the benchmark estimates are based on data from annual surveys on imports and exports of services. The quarterly estimates are compiled by extrapolation of the annual benchmark data using quarterly value and volume indicators from quarterly surveys and administrative records. 2. Estimation of chain volume measures: The annually re-weighted chain linking approach is adopted for compiling the chain volume measures of GDP and its expenditure components. In compiling the volume measure of GDP for a particular year, volume estimates of major expenditure components revalued at preceding-year prices are first derived by deflating the current price values of sub-components by the relevant price indices at the most disaggregated levels. The volume measure of GDP for that particular year is then obtained by aggregating the volume estimates of its components. Private consumption expenditure: The volume estimates of consumption expenditure on goods and services in the domestic market are obtained by deflation of the values at current prices, at disaggregated levels, by the respective goods and services components of the consumer price indices. Government consumption expenditure: Compensation of employees--deflation by the tailor-made salary rate index for government employees. Purchases of goods and services by government units and quasi-government institutions--deflation by the tailor-made government purchases price index. Sales by government to household and enterprises--deflation by the tailor-made price index. Gross domestic fixed capital formation: Building and construction work put in place deflated by type-specific construction output deflators derived with reference to the appropriate tender price indices (for buildings) and the input-based indices on labor and material cost (for civil engineering works). Real estate developers' margins extrapolated using the real growth rate of the output in respect of private sector buildings as the volume indicator. Costs of ownership transfer--Stamp duties deflated by the property price index and the indices on stamp duty rates. Legal fees extrapolated using the number of property transfer cases as volume indicator. Agents' commissions deflated by the property price index. Machinery and equipment deflated using the relevant unit value index on capital goods. Computer software and research and development deflated by appropriate price indices. Changes in inventories: Petroleum products--quantities of petroleum products revalued at base year prices. Others deflated by the price indices of domestic exports and/or price indices of retained imports of the respective commodity groups. Exports and imports of goods: deflation of the values at current prices, at disaggregated levels, by related price/value indices. For some commodities, the volume estimates are obtained by deflating the current price value by the specification price indices compiled from data obtained from the monthly specification price survey. Exports and imports of services: Receipts from inbound tourism--deflation of the values at current prices by a tailor-made tourist price index derived based on relevant price measures for various tourist products and services. Other services--deflation by the relevant producer price indices, other input-related price indices etc. Base year and update cycle: The base year of the price weights for the chain volume measures of a particular year is always the preceding year in principle. The chain volume measures are referenced to the current price values in the second last complete year. Production approach: For current price estimates, gross output and intermediate consumption are compiled for over 200 industrial breakdowns. For the chain volume measures of GDP by economic activity, data are compiled for around 100 industrial breakdowns. Specific issues in the compilation of GDP by economic activity are addressed, as follows: Ownership of premises: Ownership of premises includes (a) leasing services assumed to be provided by residential owner-occupiers to themselves; and (b) leasing services provided to tenants by owners of premises in their individual capacity. An imputed figure on rentals for such properties is added to the GDP calculations to reflect the value of the services rendered. The estimate of ownership of private domestic premises includes both the rentals of private domestic premises received by households and the imputed rentals of households occupying their own premises. It is estimated based on the rateable value of all private domestic premises supplied by the Rating and Valuation Department (R&VD), after discounting the related rentals received by companies from households. Inventory valuation adjustment: The major activity involved in import and export, wholesale and retail trades is the distribution of goods. Output in respect of sales of goods is measured by the gross margin realised on trading, i.e. the sales value less the cost of goods sold. The cost of goods sold is obtained by adjusting the value of purchases for changes in inventories net of price appreciation (or depreciation). To remove the effects of price appreciation (or depreciation) included in the book value of inventories collected in the economic surveys, special price indices are applied to re-value the book values at the beginning and the end of the reference year at the average prices of the year. The difference between the re-valued figures then gives the value of physical changes in inventories for calculating the cost of goods sold. Financial intermediation services indirectly measured (FISIM) For estimating the value of output of financial intermediation services, Hong Kong has adopted the reference rate method. The "reference rate" of interest refers to the rate that should contain no service element and duly reflect the risk and maturity structure of deposits and loans. The rate prevailing for inter-bank borrowing and lending which does not include element of financial intermediation services provides a suitable choice as a reference rate as mentioned in the 2008 SNA . The value of FISIM, together with other explicit services charges, rentals, commissions and other fees received, constitute the gross output of financial intermediaries. Using the concept of reference rate of interest, the output of the financial intermediation services and the value of subsequent consumption of these services by different types of depositors and borrowers (e.g. households, business establishments, non-residents) can be estimated as follows: (i) For each type of depositor, the value of financial intermediation services consumed is equal to the stock of deposits multiplied by the average interest margins of that type of depositor; (ii) Similarly, for each type of borrower, the value of financial intermediation services consumed is equal to the stock of loans multiplied by the average interest margins of that type of borrower. The amount of financial intermediation services consumed by various economic sectors are included in the intermediate consumption of the respective sectors, and subsequently deducted from their gross output in calculating the value added of the respective sectors. The amount of financial intermediation services consumed by households when they make deposits at banks and obtain loans from banks (other than dwelling loans) and the amount of financial intermediation services provided by resident banks to non-residents are included in private consumption expenditure and exports of services respectively in the GDP by expenditure component. Financial intermediation services relating to dwelling loans of households are included in the intermediate consumption of the item "Ownership of premises" in the GDP by economic activity. As for the compilation of chain volume measures of GDP by economic activity, four methods are used: (a) single extrapolation of previous year value added by deflated gross output measures e.g. business receipts deflated by PPI; (b) single extrapolation of previous year value added by volume/quantity measures such as volume of trade and physical units of outputs; (c) single extrapolation of previous year value added by input indicators such as number of hours worked; and (d) double indicators methods. Single extrapolation of previous year value added by deflated gross output measures: In calculating deflated gross output, relevant price deflators are needed. For construction, price deflators employed include type-specific construction output deflators (e.g. offices, residential buildings, civil engineering works), the Civil Engineering Works Index (CEWI), building services tender price index (BSTPI) and other price indices. For accommodation services; air, land and water transport activities; selected real estate services and selected business services, respective PPIs are also used. Single extrapolation of previous year value added by volume/quantity measures: The method of single extrapolation by volume/quantity indicators is commonly applied under two circumstances. The first circumstance is where the service prices of economic activities are both theoretically and practically very difficult to measure, as in the case of services provided by wholesale, retail and import and export trades and financial intermediation services indirectly measured (FISIM). The second circumstance is where quantity indicators are highly correlated with volume of output and hence provide good proxies for estimating value added in volume terms (e.g. statistics on outward external telephone traffic volume is used as an indicator to extrapolate the value added of international telephone services in volume terms). Single extrapolation of previous year value added by input indicators: In extrapolation by input indicators, the commonly used indicators are deflated wages and hours worked. Deflated wages indicators are applied to economic activities of which the output is estimated by cost approach, viz. the output of government and private non-profit bodies in Hong Kong. For indicators of hours worked, they are at present applied to selected business service industries and other information and communications industries for which PPIs are not available. Double indicators methods: The double indicators method requires detailed data on the outputs and inputs of respective economic activities and their prices. Owing to data availability problem, in Hong Kong, double indicators method is applied to the electricity and gas supply, and food and beverage services only. Seasonal adjustment: At present, seasonally adjusted data series is compiled and published for the quarterly series of the aggregate GDP and major expenditure categories including private consumption expenditure, government consumption expenditure, exports of goods, imports of goods, exports of services and imports of services. The seasonally adjusted series of GDP and its components are compiled by the X-12 ARIMA method. The X-12 ARIMA method and its related versions are the standard method commonly employed in compiling seasonally adjusted data series. The method can be used to detect whether outlier(s) is present in the original series for estimation of seasonal factors. Geographical detail: In view of the small size of the economy, geographical breakdowns of national accounts statistics are not produced. Benchmarking technique: The Denton method is adopted for interpolation of benchmark estimates such as data from the Household Expenditure Survey which is conducted at a five-year interval. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The main source data are thoroughly checked before they are used for GDP compilation. In particular, attention is given to the following aspects: (a) changes in value, price and volume from previous year; (b) reversion of the direction of change; and (c) consistency with related indicators. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data The compilers of the national accounts are in close contact with producers of the source data and regularly review and assess the source data together with the producers. Thus, the accuracy of administrative data, international trade, survey data etc. used to compile national accounts statistics is routinely assessed. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The causes for significant discrepancies, if any, are investigated. The scope for harmonisation is explored. Methods are sought to come to the most accurate description of reality. Reconciliation between the production measure and expenditure measure of GDP is conducted regularly. Statistical discrepancy between the expenditure measure and the production measure of GDP at current prices is explicitly shown in the report "Gross Domestic Product by Economic Activity". 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses The GDP figures are subject to routine revisions when more data become available until they become finalised around the third February/March after the reference year. Summary of revisions to the GDP figures is stated in the report. In the course of the routine compilation of the GDP statistics, the direction and magnitude of revisions between the previously published data are looked into. Any significant changes arising from the revision are investigated and the underlying reasons for such changes are studied. The findings from the revision studies are used to refine the advance/preliminary data and the data collection programs for the subsequent periods. Track records of revisions of the GDP figures are maintained to identify the pattern in the data revision. Systematic revision pattern, if any, will be taken into account in refining the advance/preliminary GDP data as appropriate. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses The nature and effects of non-routine technical revisions for major changes are explained in statistical publications. In the routine compilation, the revision of the figures is due to the availability of more data for the compilation of the statistics. The revision between the advance estimates and revised figures is generally small. The direction and size of the revision are regularly measured, assessed and published in the GDP reports and statistical tables accessible by users. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Requests for additional statistics which are not available from the existing tabulations will be considered on a case-by-case basis having regard to such factors as the nature of the request, data relevance and precision, and data confidentiality. Where applicable and in consultation with users, customised tabulations will be provided at a charge under the cost recovery principle. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Details of contact for enquiries, including phone numbers, fax numbers, email addresses and office addresses, are clearly given in the GDP reports, the Performance Pledge of the C&SD and on the C&SD website ( https://www.censtatd.gov.hk/en/ ). The information is readily available to the public. Information leaflet on Gross Domestic Product is available to the public to raise the general understanding of the GDP. Prompt and knowledgeable support is provided in accordance with the performance pledge of the C&SD. The timeliness of response to enquiries from the public is regularly monitored. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The availability of the report "Gross Domestic Product by Expenditure Component" is publicised in the press release on the latest economic situation and updated economic forecasts issued by the Government Economist. The availability of the report "Gross Domestic Product by Economic Activity" is publicised in the press release on chain volume measures of GDP by economic activity issued by the C&SD. Such information is also available on the C&SD website ( https://www.censtatd.gov.hk/en/ ). Lists of publications, feature articles and other services available are also obtainable from the C&SD website ( https://www.censtatd.gov.hk/en/page_1273.html ).
