 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The foreign trade statistics are prepared according to the definition of general trade system and guidelines contained in the United Nations manual “International Merchandise Trade Statistics: Concepts and Definitions, Rev.3 2010” 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classifications adopted are those of the Harmonized System, the SITC, national accounts, and by usage groups. 2.2.1 Scope The classifications adopted are those of the Harmonized System, the SITC, national accounts, and by usage groups. Scope of the data Moroccan trade statistics are compiled according to the general trade system. The methodology for compiling International Merchandise Trade Statistics has gone through three stages before adopting the general trade system: Until 1997, adoption of the special trade system, strict definition: imports include all goods entering the free circulation area of the country, which means cleared through customs for home use. Exports include all goods leaving the free circulation area of the country. From 1998, integration of goods placed under the customs procedures for inward processing. In 2005, recording goods that enter or leave an industrial free zone. Thus, Morocco adopt the special trade system, relaxed definition. Since 2014, adoption of the general trade system in accordance with the guidelines contained in the United Nations "International Merchandise Trade Statistics: Concepts and Definitions 2010": integration of goods entering or leaving a warehouse. These statistics are compiled by the Foreign Exchange Office on the basis of customs declarations filled by economic transactors at all customs offices in the Kingdom. Geographical Coverage: The statistics are expressed in dirhams and broken down by country of origin and consignment or destination, as well as by geographic, economic, and other area. Unrecorded activity Unrecorded Economy: No adjustments are made for unreported trade (smuggling). Unrecorded activity Unrecorded Economy: No adjustments are made for unreported trade (smuggling). 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: Foreign trade transactions are recorded at their declared value when the merchandise clears customs. Imports are valued c.i.f. and exports f.o.b. Currency Conversion: Domestic currency is converted by the declaring party and the exchange rate is indicated on the customs declaration. The declared exchange rate is corroborated by the exchange certificate provided by the bank. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: the date of customs clearance entered in the declarations. Recording Period: The data are recorded according to calendar month. No adjustment is made. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The data are compiled from the declarations signed by economic transactors in customs offices at the time of each import or export operation. These declarations are forwarded each day to the Foreign Exchange Office by the Customs Administration via an IT system linking the two departments. The foreign trade statistics are compiled in terms of value, weight, and any supplementary units. Customs declarations are given a serial number by each customs office and any irregularity is automatically indicated. None None
