 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The responsibility for compiling international trade statistics lies with Central Statistical Office (CSO). The CSO publishes international trade statistics in accordance with the Special Trade System (STS). The CSO follows recommendations of the UN "International Trade Statistics Concepts and Definitions" and COMESA Common Statistical Rules and Regulations. All these rules, regulations and practices are built into a statistical software called EUROTRACE used to generate trade statistics. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Goods are classified by the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS) of the World Trade Council, with seventh and eighth digit extensions for national purposes. Partner countries are classified by the ISO coding established by the UN Statistics Division. Imports are classified by country of origin and exports by country of destination. Scope of the data The CSO regularly compiles the following trade statistics: Total exports and imports; Trade by harmonized Commodity Description (HS) at eight, six, four and two digit levels; exports and imports by trading partner (direction of trade), exports and imports by port of entry or exit, exports and imports by trader (exporter/importer), international transport statistics (exports and imports by mode of transport and nationality), export and import quantities and weights. Exceptions to coverage Trade indices are currently not being generated because of the unreasonable quantities in ASYCUDA data. However, a Trade Indice Module (TIM) is available and trade indices will be generated later this year as soon as data are edited. 2.4.1 Valuation Mineral exports and floricultural products are valued at their pre-auction or preshipment values. Hence there is need to make valuation adjustment for these exports. However, ZRA states that both traditional and non-traditional exports are on f.o.b. basis. Data for Zambia's major mineral exports, copper and cobalt, are currently being obtained from ASYCUDA system of the ZRA. Exports in ASYCUDA data are indicated to be valued FOB. However, there is need for further investigations with ZRA. Data for electricity exports are submitted on a monthly basis by the Zambia Electricity Supply Corporation (ZESCO). All imports data are collected from ZRA. Imports are valued on c.i.f. basis. Values expressed in foreign currencies are converted into Zambian Kwacha using the average monthly exchange rate. In general, the Brussels definition of value is employed. For certain goods, the CED has defined minimum acceptable values. Shipments without invoice, such as barter and aid goods, and intra-company are recorded at the values assessed by Customs officers and declarants on the CED. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The major source of trade data is the Automated System for Customs Data (ASYCUDA) system of the Zambia Revenue Authority (ZRA). Following the introduction of the ASYCUDA at most of the border entry and exit points and some inland offices, the data from these stations are transferred electronically to the Central Statistical Office for analysis. The non-computerized stations send third copies of the CED forms to CSO on regular basis, which are then keyed into Eurotrace software. At the end of the month, all data are loaded into a database, validated and processed again using the same software.
