 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The Production Index of Manufacturing Industry (PIMI) measures developments in the production in manufacturing establishments that on average have a labor force of 50 persons or more. Methodology Data collection is done four times in a year. The data collected are quantities produced each month. Starting from the first quarter 1996, computation of the index has followed the chained Laspeyre’s formula instead of the usual Laspeyre’s fixed base version so as to incorporate any structural changes e.g. entries or exits of industries/commodities that may have taken place in the sector over a specific period of time. To facilitate the formula, the old activity/commodity list was revised and the entire series re-weighted accordingly using 1995 information. In the exercise, the ex-factory commodity values were used as new weights while the average quarterly commodity quantities of 1995 were used as new base year for the production in the calculation of subsequent quarterly indices (1995 = 100). The results were then chained with those of the previous respective annual 1995 indices (1985 = 100) by simply multiplying the two binary indices results to obtain the corresponding chained indices (1985=100). In addition, the questionnaire asks for the establishment's number of employees, and total labor cost and the turnover during the quarter. The accuracy of the responses is verified both at the regional and central and also by verification inspection visits. Scope of the data Scope The survey is limited to the manufacturing industry and covers all manufacturing establishments engaging 50 persons or more. These, according to the available information contribute about 85 percent of the total manufacturing gross output and therefore can provide reliable estimates of the performance of the manufacturing industry. To ensure that each activity is covered to at least 85 percent of its gross output, smaller establishments with substantial outputs are also included in the survey. However, these constitute less than 10 percent of the total number of establishments covered. Data for the Quarterly Survey of Industrial Production is collected by Questionnaires, which are sent at the end of the reference period to the regional Statistical Offices and later distributed to respective establishments. Collections of the questionnaires are done in the first month after the end of reference period. Follow-ups are made by physical visit and telephone calls. Where necessary, a team of supervisors is sent to speed-up the process. Data processing takes place centrally in Dar-es-Salaam. The survey collects monthly data on quantities produced with standard and measurement units for all major commodities produced by the establishment, together representing at least 85% of output. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Coverage The basket weights refer to annual output valued at ex-factory selling prices for each establishment separately. From the second Quarter of 2004, the weights have been taken from the output of the 2000 annual survey of Industry Production. The lowest level indices are calculated as the production of the current quarter, expressed in units like kilograms, square meters, liters, etc., divided by production in the base year (divided by four to bring it to a quarterly basis). These lowest level indices are aggregated according to the Laspeyres formula to obtain group and overall indices. There are various methods to deal with missing basic data on quantities produced. If the figure for the one-month is missing from the quarterly questionnaire, it is usually estimated on the basis of the data reported for the two other months of the same quarter. If no data are reported, the production changes for the corresponding period in the previous year are applied on the last quoted production. If no other method is applicable, the missing production data is carried forward. Discontinuation of an establishment or a product line leads to problems, which are largely addressed by the introduction of annual re-weighting in 1996. This also facilitated the introduction of new establishments and new products. For instance, with the introduction of chaining, fish fillet, malt and pharmaceutical products were incorporated in the index, while canned meat, baby food, and razor blades were eliminated either because they were no longer not produced anymore or had insignificant output values in 1995. The index is compiled at 4-digit ISIC levels, but published in 3-digit ISIC detail. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Coverage The accuracy of the responses is verified both at the regional and central and also by verification inspection visits.
