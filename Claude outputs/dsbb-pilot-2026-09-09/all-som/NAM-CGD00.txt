 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Debt statistics on domestic debt and external debt are derived with respect to residence and nonresidence of debt holders. Debt is defined as the outstanding stock of recognized, direct liabilities of the government to the rest of the domestic economy and the world generated by government operations in the past and scheduled to be extinguished by government operations in the future or to continue as perpetual debt. External debt occurs when payment is owed to a nonresident. Domestic debt occurs when payment is owed to residents. All the reported data has been transformed and is presented in accord with the GFSM 2014 guidelines as published by the International Monetary Fund (IMF). The data has been reported in line with the requirements of data compilation and dissemination procedures, tables and statements are being reported. The valuation of reported stock debt is face value. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Debt by instruments Domestic Domestic Currency and deposits Loans Debt securities External Currency and deposits Loans Debt securities Debt by maturity Domestic debt by Maturity Short Term (<1 year) Medium and Long Term debt (>1 year) External Debt by Maturity Short Term (<1 year) Medium and Long Term debt (>1 year) External Debt by currency Euro Rmb Yuan Usd Zar Chf Japanese Yen Kwd Total Government Debt Guarantee Domestic guarantee External guarantee Banking and Nonbanking 2.2.1 Scope Debt by instruments Domestic Domestic Currency and deposits Loans Debt securities External Currency and deposits Loans Debt securities Debt by maturity Domestic debt by Maturity Short Term (<1 year) Medium and Long Term debt (>1 year) External Debt by Maturity Short Term (<1 year) Medium and Long Term debt (>1 year) External Debt by currency Euro Rmb Yuan Usd Zar Chf Japanese Yen Kwd Total Government Debt Guarantee Domestic guarantee External guarantee Banking and Nonbanking Scope of the data All central ggovernment debt is included in the reporting of debt statistics by the for domestic debt and the for external debt. Sources of funding of development expenditure, as shown in the annual development budget - the Three-Year Rolling Development Budget for the Financial Year - are broken down between internal and external funding. According to the Three-Year Rolling Development Budget for the Financial Year, internal funding includes only resources financed by the government. External funding corresponds to financial resources provided by external funding agencies. This source of funding can be either in grant or loan form. External grants are channeled inside or outside the State Revenue Fund, while the external loans are supposed to be channeled through the State Revenue Fund. However, this is not yet fully realized. Therefore, substantial portions of external grants and external loans may be channeled outside the State Revenue Fund and thus would not appear to be included in the accounts of budgetary central government. Debt reporting covers all government debt for the entire country. Debt of the government includes all debt obligations undertaken by the government, specifically including all debt that derives from government on-lending to parastatals (non-financial public enterprises and public financial institutions). Central government debt indicators reported quarterly are debt instruments (domestic & external) currency and deposits, loans, debt securities, debt by maturity, by currency, banking and nonbanking as well as guarantees. A given transaction may be valued in Namibian dollars, using the foreign exchange rate of the date of the transaction, as provided by the Bank of Namibia (BON). Alternatively, some foreign donors calculate the value of each transaction in Namibian dollars and provide that information to the authorities, along with the foreign-currency value of the transaction. Debt stock is valued in local currency at the BON official exchange rate at the end of each month, when the CDMD transmits that valuation information to the BON. The valuation of reported stock debt is face value 2.4.2 Recording basis All transactions, of both principal and interest, are recorded and reported on a cash basis. Domestic debt is valued in Namibian dollars at face value. Reporting of foreign currency denominated debt is valued in Namibian dollars according to the prevailing exchange rate on the day of the transaction. Outstanding domestic debt is measured on the face value, in Namibian dollars. All government transactions, including central government debt, are reported on cash basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures All debt-related data are recorded on a gross basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Cash and Debt Management Directorate (CDMD), within the Ministry of Finance, are moving from utilizing the Commonwealth Secretariat Debt Recording and Management System (CS-DRMS 2000+) and instead plan to use the Meridian system, which will maintains records of foreign debt by maturity, by type of debt holder (bilateral, multilateral, commercial bank, export credit), and by type of debt instrument. Domestic debt is comprised solely of treasury bonds and bills. In its maintenance of the annual budget and the revised budget (at three months into the fiscal year), the CDMD monitors all future debt repayment obligations (principal and interest) by date, amount, denomination, and creditor. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The source data broadly follows the definitions, classifications and valuations required for producing debt statistics in accordance with the GFSM 2014 and so minimal adjustments are required.
