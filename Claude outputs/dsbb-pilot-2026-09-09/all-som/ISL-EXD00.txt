 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: The external debt is compiled by Central Bank of Iceland (CBI) in accordance with the standard set out in the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position manual, Sixth edition (BPM6). The data are derived from the IIP statistics which are compiled at end-of-quarter. The data covers the four major institutional units: Central Bank, general governments (central and local governments), banks (deposit money banks) and other sectors (other financial and non-financial public and private companies). The data is further broken down by original maturity: short-term and long-term debt and by instruments: debt securities, loan, trade credits, currency and deposits, other debt liabilities, data on intercompany lending under direct investment relationships are separately identified without sector attribution and maturity structure. Definition: Gross external debt is defined as the outstanding amount of all actual current, and not contingent, liabilities that require payment(s) of principal and/or interest by the debtor at some point(s) in the future and that are owed to non-residents by residents of an economy. External debt liabilities payable in goods and/or services are not included in the external debt position, as it is not applicable to the Icelandic economy. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The external debt is classified in accordance with the standard components of the BPM6 manual. The classification of external debt data into four sectors (Central Bank, monetary authorities, banks, and other sectors) fully follows the guidelines set out in the BPM6 and IMF’s External Debt Statistics Guide ( the Guide ). There are no deviations from these international guidelines for sector classification include. For example; external debt of the monetary authorities are reported separately from those of the central government, and external debt liabilities of households and small private enterprises are included under "other sectors" as far as possible. External debt liabilities are attributed to the sector of the immediate borrower. External debt liabilities are classified under the sector category of the debtor and not of any guarantor. External debt data can be classified by type of debt instruments as set out in BPM6 and the Guide . In particular, deposits of non-residents with domestic banks are included since early 2000; instruments included under "other debt liabilities" are trade credits, loans, deposits and other liabilities. Intercompany lending between entities in a direct investment relationship are separately identified in the total external debt position. Scope of the data The data covers the four major institutional units: Central Bank, general governments (central and local governments), banks (deposit money banks) and other sectors (other financial and non-financial public and private companies). External debt data is compiled on the basis of covering debt positions between residents and non-residents. Coverage of external debt: Repurchase agreements and gold swaps are treated as collateralized loans; financial leases are treated as other debt liabilities. Geographical coverage: No inclusion/exclusion of territories/regions of the resident/non-residents entities. Debt liabilities of the offshore enterprises—including banks—that are resident in the reporting economy are not included in the gross external debt position, as it is not applicable to the Icelandic economy. There is a full coverage of the private sector debt in the gross external debt position under banks and/or other sectors (as it corresponds). There is a full coverage of intercompany lending between entities in a direct investment relationship in the gross external debt position. There is a full coverage of short-term debt included in the gross external debt position. Positions in financial derivatives, equity securities, and equity capital are excluded from the gross external debt position. External debt include securities issued in the domestic markets by residents and held by non-residents. Financial leases with non-residents are treated as loans and included in the gross external debt position. 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to unit of account: The external debt data are compiled in millions of Icelandic krónur (ISK) at the end of reference period. Foreign currency denominated assets and liabilities are converted into ISK using end of period exchange rate. Valuation: The external debt is compiled at market value or equivalents were possible. In the absent of market value debt is valued at own fund book value (FDI) or nominal value. 2.4.2 Recording basis In general, external debt is recorded on accruals basis or close approximations to the accruals principle. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The compilation of external debt data is based on the foreign positions of both financial and non-financial direct reporting entities. Debt securities: Data on foreign issued securities and other long term debt is collected quarterly from both financial and non-financial reporting entities. Domestic securities held by foreigners are collected daily from The Icelandic Securities Depository. Trade credits: Data on trade credits is both collected quarterly from large direct reporting entities and estimated using import figures on small businesses. Currency and deposits: The data is collected monthly from deposit money banks in Iceland. Other debt liabilities: The data is collected from large enterprises and could either be unallocated loans or financial lease. Intercompany lending: Data are collected and compiled in accordance with the annual FDI-statistics. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Data is available to allow disaggregation of "other sectors" into three subsectors (non-bank financial corporations, non-financial corporations, and NPISH), with the exception of NPISH which would be classified under non-financial corporations. 3.2.1 Source data assessment 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The quarterly data and the first compilation of annual data may partly be based on estimations and marked as preliminary data in CBI's publications. Data are not seasonally adjusted. Iceland participates in the Co-ordinated Portfolio Investment Survey (CPIS). Iceland participates in the Co-ordinated Direct Investment Survey (CDIS).
