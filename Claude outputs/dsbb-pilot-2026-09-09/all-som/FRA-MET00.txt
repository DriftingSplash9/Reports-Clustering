 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions In general, the definitions used are in accordance with the UN International Trade Statistics Concepts and definitions. However, French external trade statistics are mainly based on a "special trade" concept by opposition with "general trade". External trade statistics measure the movement of commodities across frontiers between France and the rest of the world. Imports are goods crossing the borders entering French statistical territory, and intended for a domestic consumption, and also for processing before reexportation. Exports are goods crossing the borders exiting French statistical territory for a foreign consumption, and also for processing before reimportation. Aside from movements of commodities of small amounts, all movements of incoming or outgoing commodities are recorded. Relationship of national practices with international or regional guideline : The foreign trade statistics are compiled in accordance with the UN guidelines on International Merchandise Trade Statistics and with the Balance of Payments Manual of the International Monetary Fund, except for a few difference such as : Balance of payment does not take into account : the movements of goods without transfer of property ; movements of goods for processing ; Balance of payment takes into account ship and aircraft fuelling. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Quantities and values of imported and exported commodities are classified and described primarily by commodity types and countries in foreign trade statistics. The classifications of commodities to be used for reporting imports and exports are the Customs tariff and statistical nomenclature 8 digits (Combined Nomenclature CN8) based on the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS). The classification of countries and regions used for Foreign Trade Statistics is in accordance with the European Geonomenclature. French foreign trade statistics are composed of "extratrade statistics" and "intratrade statistics". Extratrade statistics measure the flows of goods with countries outside the European Union (third countries), intratrade statistics contain information on commodity transactions with EU partner countries. The results of these two statistics are however presented to data users as a whole in the description of foreign trade. 2.2.1 Scope Quantities and values of imported and exported commodities are classified and described primarily by commodity types and countries in foreign trade statistics. Scope of the data Transactions coverage : gold, aid goods, goods sent by courier services, goods for processing, waste and scraps, foreign bunkers, goods under financial lease, goods from/to offshore sites, electricity, gas, water, ships and aircraft. Geographic coverage : the economic territory of France and overseas departments. Exceptions to coverage Transactions exceptions to coverage : excluded are migrants' and personal effects, monetary gold, issued banknotes and coins in circulation, goods temporarily admitted or dispatched, transit trade, goods from/to territorial enclaves, services, goods under operational lease. Among others, the movements of commodities of minor importance (for example, removal goods) or those which are difficult to record (for example, diplomatic goods) as well as transient commodity imports and exports are exempted from collection. Unrecorded activity No estimates for smuggling are made. However, trade made by corporations which have not been selected by External Trade statistics and analysis division of the French Directorate General of Customs and Excise (DGDDI) to complete the survey concerning intra-EU trade in goods, is estimated. 2.4.1 Valuation The so-called statistic value is published as the commodity value according to international standards. Generally it is derived from the billed payment for a commodity in purchase in an import transaction or for sale of a commodity in an export transaction. The freight and insurance costs between the borders are not included in the value of exports (FOB), but they are included in the value of imports (CAF). The General custom administration carried out a survey to estimate this part in the total of the imports However, a CIF-FOB correction cannot be performed in detailed statistics by commodity or country. Transactions are to be reported in local currency (euro). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Since the completion of the European internal market on January 1, 1993, and the associated elimination of customs examination of commodities on the internal borders of the member states of the European Union (EU), data on movements of commodities across borders have been recorded either classically via the customs authorities or by a monthly survey on trade in goods between France and other European Union Members States . The different collection systems can be outlined in simplified form as follows : Collection via Customs Offices : The classical collection system via the customs authorities is always used for extra UE trade. Declarations for each transaction are filed through the customs IT system and after being controled by the customs authorities, they are sent to the data center Monthly survey on trade in goods between France and other European Union Member States : Since February 1rst 2022 this compulsory survey has replaced the statistical part of the Intrastat declaration of trade in goods (DEB) for all intra-EU trade in goods. Corporations must complete this survey when they have been selected by External Trade statistics and analysis division of the French Directorate General of Customs and Excise (DGDDI) due to significant value of its intra-EU trade (goods imported from other EU member states to France, or exported from France to other EU member states) in the previous year . Type of data collected : for extra-EU trade the main variables collected are : party responsible ; period ; declaring 3rd party ; country of consignment, origin or country of destination ; nature of transaction ; mode of transport ; (air)port of unloading ; commodity code ; type of flow (imports/exports) ; statistical procedure ; net mass ; supplementary unit ; invoiced amount (breakdown by type of goods) ; currency ; preference code ; tariff code ; statistical value ; affiliates. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Basis of country data : The country of origin is the country in which the commodity has been entirely obtained or produced or in which it has acquired its last significant and economically justified processing. But for commodities for which a so-called preferential certificate has been submitted, the country of origin is considered the country in which the commodities acquired the property of a commodity of origin based on the preference regulation. This concept is the one used in French external trade statistics, by opposition to the country of consignment which is the community concept. The country of destination is the country into which the commodities have been physically transported from France and are to be consumed or used and processed. Reporting period : The reporting period is fundamentally the calendar month. All commodity movements completed in a certain calendar month are assigned to it. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Timing of recording transactions: For extra EU trade, declarations are recorded all along the month of reference and the data are recorded very early in the different regional centers before being sent to the national direction of statistics and external trade (NDSET). For intra EU trade, companies must send their export and import declarations ten opened days after the end of the reference month. The data are captured in the regional centers and sent to the NDSET, which compiles the whole information. French external trade statistics are monthly published 40 days after the end of the month of reference. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Processing system: Both intra-EU and extra-EU trade is mostly declared electronically. A small portion is disseminated on paper and is keyed in at Customs or at the statistical office. All further processing takes place at the statistical office. Adjustment for undercoverage : trade made by corporations which have not been selected by External Trade statistics and analysis division of the French Directorate General of Customs and Excise (DGDDI) to complete the survey concerning intra-EU trade in goods is estimated. In addition, adjustments for late response in intra-EU trade are made. These adjustments may be based on historical data or VAT-information. Valuation adjustments are made to convert reported invoice values in respect of intra-EU trade into statistical values. Following the recommandations of Statistical Office of the European communities, estimates for the aforementioned exemption elements have already been considered there in the corresponding calculation. Estimates for small transactions are also included in the published results. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Calculation of Volume and Indices : for national accounts needs, imports and exports are calculated in real terms. Prices used for the calculation are index value units. Theses indexes are calculated monthly, by country and commodity. Seasonal adjustments : the import and export of goods are influenced by seasons and trading days. To take these effects away the series of imports and exports of the French national accounts are seasonally adjusted. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Reconciliation with partner country data occurs on an ad-hoc base. There is no standardized procedure to perform reconciliation exercises on a regular basis. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Data using SITC (UNO's International Trade Classification ) rev. 3, harmonized system and other product classification are available on request to the External Trade statistics and analysis division of the French Directorate General of Customs and Excise (DGDDI)'s contact person.
