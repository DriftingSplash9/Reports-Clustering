 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Methodology: I. Analytical Framework, Concepts, Definitions and Classifications Considerable efforts are made to adopt and adapt, to the extent possible, internationally accepted standards and best practices. External Trade Statistics are presently compiled according to international standards i.e. as per UN concepts and definitions of merchandise trade (International Merchandise Trade Statistics Rev 2). Most of the IMTS 2010 recommendations are being followed and work is in progress to implement as far as possible the remaining recommendations of IMTS 2010. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Trade statistics are compiled as per U.N concepts and definitions of international merchandise trade statistics (IMTS 2010). Classification: presently using Harmonised Nomenclature (HS 2022) and Standard International Trade Classifications Rev 4 (SITC Rev 4) Tables are disseminated in SITC for economic analysis purposes. 2.2.1 Scope Trade statistics are compiled as per U.N concepts and definitions of international merchandise trade statistics (IMTS 2010). Classification: presently using Harmonised Nomenclature (HS 2022) and Standard International Trade Classifications Rev 4 (SITC Rev 4) Tables are disseminated in SITC for economic analysis purposes. Scope of the data Trade statistics are compiled according to the General Trade System of the U.N. Using the national boundary as the statistical frontier, the General Trade System is a record of all goods entering (imports) and leaving the country (exports). Activities of the Freeport are also included. Exceptions to coverage All imports and exports valued below Rs 1,000 are excluded from trade statistics. Monetary gold, banknotes and coins, empty containers and personal effects are excluded as well. Unrecorded activity Trade in illegal goods (drugs and narcotics are excluded). 2.4.1 Valuation All imports transactions are valued at Cost, Insurance and Freight (CIF) basis and exports transactions are valued on a Free on Board (FOB) basis. Information on insurance and freight for merchandise imports is available. Concerning currency conversion rate, the Trade Statistics Unit uses the average buying and selling rate from the Mauritius Commercial Bank on a monthly basis. 2.4.2 Recording basis Methodology: III. Accounting Conventions Collection of trade data is on an accrual basis that is at the time the declaration is lodged at Customs. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Administrative data (declarations lodged through the “Customs Management System”) are obtained from Customs Department on a monthly basis on electronic media. Supplementary data for some commodities such as petroleum products, rice, flour and sugar are obtained from Government controlled bodies and other institutions. Usually customs data and data on sugar and petroleum are obtained on the second week of preceding month of the reference month. Close collaboration is sought between the data sources and Statistics Mauritius to obtain revised figure as soon as it is ready. Data relating to aircraft spare parts are also collected from Air Mauritius on a quarterly basis. Data on bunkering are obtained from the Mauritius Ports Authority (MPA) on a monthly basis. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Customs data obtained from the Customs Department are classified using Harmonised Nomenclature (HS 2022) and Standard International Trade Classifications Rev 4 (SITC Rev 4). The commodities are coded as per a list of CPCs. All imports transactions are valued at C.I.F basis and exports transactions on the other hand are valued on a F.O.B basis. All imports and exports transactions are priced in Mauritian Rupees and unit of measurement in terms of net weight in kilogram and the supplementary units (metre, litre, etc.) are also captured on the bills of entry. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data are supplied by the Customs Department with a time lag of 10 days at most after the reference month. Data from other sources are obtained with a one-month lag. The monthly External Merchandise Trade Statistics is released on the website not later than the eighth weeks after the end of the reference month. The timeliness is in conformity with that prescribed for Special Data Dissemination Standards (SDDS). 3.2.1 Source data assessment Unit values are scrutinized by statistical officers/senior statistical officers and there are also in-built validation checks in the computer system e.g. customs procedure codes are checked against country, currency and mode of transport. All outlying unit values are queried. The in-house processing has improved the quality of trade data and there has also been an improvement in the timeliness of data dissemination following the implementation of the in-house system. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Compilation procedures minimize processing errors such as coding, editing and tabulation errors. An appropriate balance between accuracy and timeliness is targeted. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Prior to publication, the data are analyzed and if there are discrepancies in the figures, the providers of inputs are queried and corrections are made accordingly. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Unusual movements in trade are investigated. Regular meeting with concerned stakeholders are conduted to better understand the current status and future trends in imports and exports of goods. In case the unusual movements are genuine, resulting outputs (published figures) are provided backed with reliable explanations. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished non-confidential trade statistics are supplied upon request. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points All publications (excluding monthly trade bulletin) include the name and contact details of the responsible person.
