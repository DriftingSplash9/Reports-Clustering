 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Definition: Government debt presents the comprehensive stock of recognized, direct liabilities of the government to the rest of the economy and the world as defined in A Manual on Government Finance Statistics, IMF, 2014; (GFSM 2014). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: As prescribed by the SDDS, the debt data are broken down by the following characteristics: foreign and domestic, maturity and currency. The breakdown of debt by foreign and domestic components is provided according to the residency criteria and it is consistent with the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual, Sixth Edition. The classification by maturity is done by residual maturity broken down into three ranges: up to one year, one to five years and over five years, that is, short-term, medium-term and long- term debt respectively. Scope of the data Data cover consolidated budgetary government and National Insurance Institution 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation practices: The debt is valued at nominal value in the following way The domestic debt (including government marketable securities), linked to the Consumer Price Index or to the foreign exchange rate, is revalued at the end of the reported period according to the change of the CPI or the exchange rate as relevant and includes the premium/discount and accrued interest to be paid in redemption. The foreign-currency-denominated debt is valued, using the representative market exchange rates. 2.4.2 Recording basis The debt is recorded according to the accrual accounting principals. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Gross vs. net: The debt data are recorded on a gross basis and consolidated according to the GFSM instructions. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The detailed data on the domestic currency debt are provided by the Government Debt Management Unit (GDMU) of the Ministry of Finance. The data are classified by the residual maturity. The source of the data for the foreign currency debt is the Bank of Israel (BoI). The data are classified by the original maturity. The data on domestic currency debt held by non-residents and data on foreign debt held by Israelis abroad are provided by BoI to compile the breakdowns of debt by the residency. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The net issuance figures given are net changes in the stock of the given debt elements from previous year to the current year. This also includes the change in value resulting from the movement of the exchange rates in the case of foreign currency debt elements and other changes such as debt assumptions. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Revisions for past years’ quarterly data, when necessary, are made once a year. During the current year, revisions are made on an ongoing basis, as necessary. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Consolidation : The obligations eliminated in consolidation include government debt held by the National Insurance Institute and by the internal funds managed by the Treasury.
