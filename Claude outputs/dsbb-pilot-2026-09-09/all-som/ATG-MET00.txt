 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Merchandise trade statistics are compiled in accordance with recommendations on International Trade Statistics Concepts and Definitions of the United Nations, Statistical Papers series M No. 52 of 1970. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Data are compiled by commodity classification, volume, value, supplementary quantity where applicable and by country or origin/destination. Commodities are classified using both the Harmonized System (HS) and Standard International Trade Classification (SITC) Rev.3. The HS comprises of 98 chapters ranging from 0 - 97 with a sum of 5000 plus commodity classifications. The SITC on the other hand has 10 major classifications from 0 - 9. The HS classification is used mainly for trading purposes while the SITC is used for analytical purposes. Thus official trade statistics are published in SITC but HS format is available upon request. Scope of the data Merchandise Trade covers the movement of goods with Antigua and Barbuda and the rest of the world via parcel post (goods passing through the Post Office), air and sea. Items traded include the value of improvements and repairs to goods, non-monetary gold, goods leased for a period exceeding one year. Excluded however, are monetary gold, notes and coins in circulation, goods transmitted for diplomatic purposes, temporary imports (e.g. empty cylinders and goods on lease for less than one year), goods short-handled and human remains. Trade statistics are compiled using the general system of trade. Hence goods are considered imports after they enter the economic territory e. Imports therefore include all goods brought into the country whether or not they are held in Customs Bonded Warehouses, likewise goods re-exported from customs warehouses are included as exports. 2.4.1 Valuation The valuation of imports is c.i.f. (cost, insurance and freight) and total exports, the sum of domestic exports and re-exports, are recorded at f.o.b. (free on board), i.e., exclusive of insurance and freight charges. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The primary source of the data is the system of customs declarations, which are manually keyed into the data capture module of the EUROTRACE computerized trade processing system.
