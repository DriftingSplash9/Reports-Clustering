 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Manufacturing production index is calculated as a composite index of the volume of output of manufactured products in South Africa. The calculation of the monthly manufacturing indices is based on the value of products and articles manufactured after the effect of price changes has been eliminated through deflation by using appropriate sub-indices of the producer price index. An index number is a statistical measure of the change in a single or composite variable. The index is a chronological comparison over time of a composite index (major group indices combined to the division and an industry total index). The index number is the ratio between the production output in the reference month compared with its corresponding value in the base period. The value of products manufactured according to manufacturing major groups is calculated separately. Indices of 38 of the 44 manufacturing major groups are calculated in this way. In the case of the tobacco, coke and refined petroleum products, basic iron and steel products, non-ferrous metal products, motor vehicles and the parts and accessories for motor vehicles industries, the volume indices are calculated on the basis of physical quantities manufactured. Statistics SA adopts the broad definition that manufacturing is the physical or chemical transformation of materials or compounds into new products, whether the work is performed by power-driven machines or by hand. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification of the manufacturing industry is in accordance with the 1993 edition of the Standard Industrial Classification of all Economic Activities (SIC) , Fifth Edition, Report No. 09-90-02. The SIC is based on the 1990 International Standard Industrial Classification of all Economic Activities (ISIC). 2.2.1 Scope The classification of the manufacturing industry is in accordance with the 1993 edition of the Standard Industrial Classification of all Economic Activities (SIC) , Fifth Edition, Report No. 09-90-02. The SIC is based on the 1990 International Standard Industrial Classification of all Economic Activities (ISIC). Scope of the data The index covers all the manufacturing major groups (SIC 3-digit), divisions, as well as total manufacturing. The most important manufacturing major divisions are the basic iron and steel, non-ferrous metal products, metal products and machinery; petroleum, chemical products, rubber and plastic products; food and beverages; wood, paper, printing and publishing; and motor vehicles, parts and accessories and other transport equipment industries. Data regarding the value of sales and inventories and the products manufactured are obtained from a sample of 3 046 manufacturing enterprises, drawn from a comprehensive Business Register of units derived from and linked to the South African Revenue service (SARS) administrative data. The sample is refreshed annually. No regional breakdown is available. Exceptions to coverage Sample frame is constructed from VAT registered units and no informal sector units included. No sample drawn from units that have an annual turnover smaller than R3 984 745– compensated for by adjustment factor to size 4 units. Unrecorded activity Only formal manufacturing activity is recorded. Informal activity is not recorded in this survey. 2.4.1 Valuation The local producer price index is used to deflate the value of sales and inventories before indexing. 2.4.2 Recording basis Data in respect of the value of sales and the products manufactured refer to the whole of the reference month, while value of inventories refers to the end of the reference month. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs For seven of the ten SIC divisions in manufacturing, the index of production are calculated from the value of sales of own manufactured products and change in inventories of manufactured products obtained from the monthly survey of manufacturing enterprises. More direct indicators are used for the major groups of production of tobacco, coke and refined petroleum products, basic iron and steel products, non-ferrous metal products, motor vehicles and parts and accessories for the motor vehicle industry. The volume indices for these major groups are calculated on the basis of physical quantities manufactured. The monthly survey of manufacturing production and sales is conducted mainly by email but the requirement of timeliness sometimes necessitates that questionnaires are collected telephonically. The data are collected monthly based on a stratified random sample covering 3 011 manufacturing enterprises. The latest sample was drawn in April 2025 from a population then of 27 305 manufacturing enterprises. Each manufacturing major group is divided into four size groups. The sample is drawn at the SIC three-digit level. All large enterprises (size group one), which comprise 81,9% of the enterprise turnover in the current sample, are completely enumerated. The Swedish JALES sampling technique is applied for size groups two, three and four enterprises. The total value of sales of manufactured products of large enterprises (size group one) is added to the weighted totals of size groups two, three and four of that major group to reflect the total value of sales of the major group. Similar procedure is followed with the data on inventories. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The survey is run according to the prescriptions as required. Data are published within six weeks (42 days) after the reference month. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Completed survey questionnaires are required to be returned to Stats SA within 10 days after the end of the reference month. Telephone and email reminders are used to follow up with non-respondents. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Raw data received are assessed on an ongoing basis based on specified editing checks. In some cases it is also verified against information provided to other sources (SARS; other relevant surveys within Stats SA). 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Regarding the value of sales and inventories and the products manufactured, major group weighting is used to compensate or correct for under-coverage and unit non-response. These weights are updated annually from the value added data compiled by Stats SA National Accounts division. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The calculation of monthly production indices is based on the value of products and articles manufactured and change in the monthly value of inventories of own manufactured products, after the effect of price changes has been eliminated through deflation using appropriate sub-indices of the production price index (PPI). For indices, a weight is calculated for every major group according to the value added of the major group (i.e. output of a major group minus intermediate consumption) relative to the total value added of the manufacturing industry as a whole, based on the results of the most recent census of manufacturing, large sample survey (LSS) of the manufacturing industry or national accounts (NA) value added data. The production indices of all divisions are multiplied by the applicable weights and aggregated to produce the index for the total volume of manufacturing production. Seasonally adjusted estimates of all major groups are generated each month, using the X-12 Seasonal Adjustment Program developed by the US Bureau of the Census, 1968. Seasonal adjustment is a means of removing the estimated effects of normal seasonal variation from the series so that the effects of other influences on the series can be more clearly recognised. Seasonal adjustment does not aim to remove irregular or non-seasonal influences, which may be present in any particular month. Influences that are volatile or unsystematic can still make it difficult to interpret the movement of the series even after adjustment for seasonal variations. Therefore the month-to-month movements of seasonally adjusted estimates may not be reliable indicators of trend behaviour. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Data evaluation and validation takes place through the whole period of collection and processing up to publication. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Outliers and discrepencies are followed up with individual enterprises. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions analysis are performed and published annually.
