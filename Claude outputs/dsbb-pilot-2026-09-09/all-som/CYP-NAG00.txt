 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The System of National Accounts for Cyprus is nowadays in full harmonization with the European System of Accounts (ESA 2010), which is binding to all Member States of the European Union. This reflects the ongoing work to satisfy European regulations, which is observed in the area of official statistics as well. Annual and quarterly data series for Cyprus, according to ESA 2010, are available from the year 1995 onwards. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The breakdown of economic branches in the Cyprus NA is based on the ESA 2010, but only the broader groupings are published in the web-site. The industrial classification used is the Statistical Classification of Economic Activities in the European Communities NACE Rev.2; For annual national accounts , the details in the publications are presented up to the two digit level (A64 breakdown) at maximum. Products are classified by the Classification of Products by Activity (CPA). The Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose ( COICOP 2018) serves to break down household final consumption, and the Classification of the Functions of Government ( COFOG ) is used to classify functions of government. For quarterly national accounts , the details are presented at A10 breakdown (NACE Rev.2) for Production approach. For the Expenditure approach though, no breakdown is presented for Private (COICOP) or Government (COFOG) Consumption Expenditure. Concerning the institutional sector breakdown, ESA 2010 distinguishes five mutually exclusive domestic institutional sectors: (a) non-financial corporations (S11); (b) financial corporations (S12); (c) general government (S13); (d) households (S14); and (e) non-profit institutions serving households (S15). The five sectors together make up the total domestic economy (S1). Each sector is also divided into subsectors. 2.2.1 Scope The breakdown of economic branches in the Cyprus NA is based on the ESA 2010, but only the broader groupings are published in the web-site. Scope of the data GDP (Gross Domestic Product) calculations are compiled using both the production and expenditure methods; the independent calculation of GDP using the income method is for the moment incomplete since at present reflects the compensation of employees and capital consumption which are calculated directly whereas the gross operating surplus is obtained as a residual for the given period. The resulting aggregates - above all the gross domestic product - are important for assessing and shaping economic policy. On CYSTAT's website, which has become the main source of statistical information, the following tables are published: For the annual national accounts data: GDP and gross value added (A21 breakdown) at current prices and at volume measures (reference year 2019 - Chain Linking Method), expenditure of GDP (main categories of expenditure) at current prices and at volume measures (reference year 2019- Chain Linking Method), gross fixed capital formation by type of investment at current and at volume measures (reference year 2019 - Chain Linking Method), GNI (Gross National Income) and per capita GNI. For the quarterly national accounts data: GDP and gross value added (A10 breakdown) at current prices, previous year prices and at volume measures (reference year 2019 - Chain Linking Method), working day and seasonally and non-seasonal adjusted data, expenditure of GDP (main categories of expenditure) at current prices, previous year prices and at volume measures (reference year 2019 - Chain Linking Method), working day and seasonally and non-seasonally adjusted data. Exceptions to coverage Due to the Turkish invasion of Cyprus in July 1974 and the subsequent occupation of nearly 40% of its territory, the calculations of Gross Domestic Product and Gross National Income are limited only within the government controlled areas of the island. Unrecorded activity The Eurostat’s Tabular Approach to Exhaustiveness is continuously adopted to measure informal activities, underreporting and any other possible GDP under-coverage, in order to achieve maximum exhaustiveness of GDP. 2.4.1 Valuation Flows and stocks are measured according to their exchange value, i.e. the value at which flows and stocks are in fact, or could be, exchanged for cash. Thus, market prices are the basic reference for valuation. In the case of monetary transactions, the values required are directly observable. In most other cases, the method of valuation is by reference to market prices for analogous goods, services or assets (e.g. services of owner-occupied dwellings). In the case of non-market services (government), valuation is made according to production costs. 2.4.2 Recording basis All transactions are recorded on accrual basis, except for some imports and exports of services. Work-in-progress is recorded in the period it is produced. Where necessary, adjustments are made to some government transactions, to transform them in accrual basis (VAT, Income Tax). 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The gross/netting procedures are consistent with European and International standards (e.g. interest, changes in inventories). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Main Sources The main data sources used in Cyprus for the compilation of the national accounts aggregates are listed below. administrative sources, the overwhelming majority of data used is seen to be available at such a level of analysis that the consistency with national accounts conventions (definitions, scope, classifications, valuation etc) can be adequately identified. Where necessary, adjustments are made. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness In general, the timetables for data collection and processing are adequate to meet timeliness and periodicity for dissemination of national accounts statistics. A standard procedure informs respondents of the deadlines set for reporting to the statistical offices. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Information about sampling errors for each of the surveys conducted is monitored on a regular basis. Source data is analyzed in depth in the context of major revisions which are performed every five years. New statistical sources and their statistical data are carefully reviewed before incorporation into the national accounts during major revisions. Surveys/censuses are audited to verify the accuracy of the individual survey data. The source data is analyzed to correct for underreporting/misreporting, in particular to check for temporal consistency and consistency with other related source data. In the framework of National Accounts compilation, the various data sources are assessed, namely the government finance statistics, merchandise trade statistics, volume and price statistics etc. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The Statistical techniques employed conform to sound statistical procedures. During the processing stage of the various economic surveys, great care is taken for minimizing the errors in coding, editing and tabulations, using sound compilation procedures. Grossing-up factors are derived scientifically, based on sample design. Non-response in annual economic surveys does not appear to present a cause for concern in Cyprus, since the level recorded is very low (below 5%). The adjustments made for non-response (adjusting grossing-up coefficients and imputation) are adequate. Although the questionnaires of the annual economic surveys are designed in a large extend to meet the requirements of ESA2010, they are also designed in a way to accommodate as much as possible the private accounting framework in order to reduce the responding burden from the business. Therefore, it is necessary to make transitional adjustments of the survey data to meet the needs for National Accounts (conceptual and exhaustiveness adjustments). 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures In estimating Quarterly National Accounts, the Statistical Service follows the so-called “indirect approach” instead of relying on direct estimates based on quarterly ad hoc surveys. The lack of the same sources used at annual level does not permit to employ the same methodology also at quarterly level. The annual data is temporally disaggregated by means of related indicators observed at quarterly frequency. The Chow-Lin’s approach (1971) is used to this purpose, a well-known temporal disaggregation technique. According to this method, quarterly national accounts are generally constructed on the basis of short term indicators. Each account series is linked to one or more related quarterly series, available at the time of compilation of the account series themselves. Apart from the estimation of single series, statistical methods are also used in order to achieve the accounting relationships. For seasonal adjustment, the model-based approach in the estimation of quarterly national accounts is also adopted. The process of seasonal adjustment is carried out on related indicators through the programs TRAMO-SEATS. The preferred indicators are seasonally adjusted and then, the SA indicators are used to obtain seasonal adjusted versions of the NA aggregates. The working day adjustment is applied for two effects associated to the calendar structure: the working day and the leap year. The Easter effect for the case of Cyprus is not applicable since it always falls during the 2nd quarter of the year. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results For countries adopting an indirect approach in estimating Quarterly National Accounts, the ability of short-term indicators to give reliable and timely information on the evolution of the phenomena under study is an essential feature to be addressed at the very preliminary stage. It is important to stress that the quality of the final results in the estimation of quarterly national accounts depends on a number of factors, the most important one being the quality of the indicators used. The quality of indicators in this respect refers to the ability of the indicators to be good proxies of the information one obtains from all those sources used at the annual level for estimating national accounts. In fact, annual data are generally estimated by considering sources generally not available (with the same level of the detail and accuracy) at the quarterly level. Annual sources are generally more exhaustive and complete than those observed and sampled at higher frequencies. Therefore, the first step when constructing the data-producing system was to check for the existence of indicators (related time series observed at least on a quarterly basis) and analysed their reliability, timeliness and coherence with the sources generally used in estimating annual national accounts. In some cases, the indicators considered as the more appropriate for quarterly disaggregation purposes were not directly available and needed to be constructed. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data All short-term indicators assessed as being appropriate for quarterly disaggregation purposes are systematically checked as a matter of validating procedure. The main target is to verify their relative ability in explaining the corresponding annual figures through standard econometric tests derived from the annual relationships obtained from the quarterly disaggregation program. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs In the case of Annual National Accounts, the Supply and Use framework is used to investigate any discrepancies and make the statistical outputs consistent. In the case of the Quarterly National Accounts compilation, GDP is estimated from the production-side and the inconsistencies with the expenditure and income sides are reconciled through a balancing procedure where the adjustment is applied to Changes in Inventories (expenditure) and Operating Surplus/Mixed Income. For the validation of National Accounts data, Cyprus also applies the validation rules as set by Eurostat and provides metadata with respect to major revisions. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses The analysis of revision is the most important tool to validate the accuracy of preliminary estimates in quarterly accounts. Comparing the preliminary GDP estimate with the one obtained at T+2 months permits to evaluate the reliability of sources and methods employed at 45 days. This analysis is made regularly into two specialised procedures accordingly addressed at T+45 days (flash estimate) and T+2 months (regular estimate). During the first procedure the programme saves the results coming out from each compilation of GDP and value added for the twenty one sectors of A21 breakdown (at volume measures, in raw and working and seasonal adjusted forms) and stores them for further analysis in the future. During the second procedure the programme produces a tabulation of comparisons between the current estimate of GDP (and value added at A21 breakdown) with the previous one. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Information on revisions (routine or major) is provided to users through the relevant news releases accompanying data dissemination from CYSTAT's website. https://library.cystat.gov.cy/NEW/National_Accounts-Revision_Policy-EN-070521.pdf Cyprus performed a major revision for National Accounts data in 2024. The requests may concern the extraction of information from the databases or from older printed publications, special tabulations, the provision of data with a specific breakdown level that is not already available, etc. with the exception of cases where confidentiality issues arise. For requests demanding significant processing time, a fee is charged. The fees applied are based on the hourly cost of a Statistics Officer for the processing of the request, as well as administrative and operational costs. The interested party is informed of the total cost and if it is accepted, then CYSTAT proceeds to satisfy the request. Interested parties can submit their request electronically, through the contact form for "Tailor-made Requests" , which is posted on the web portal, under the "Contact Us" section. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The website provide adequate information to users with respect to the ways in which they can contact the Statistical Service and obtain further material or assistance. Contact persons are assigned for the most important topics and their names are disseminated to the main users. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs Catalogues of all publications, documents and other services of the Statistical Service are available in hard copy from: Statistical Service of Cyprus, 1444 Nicosia, Cyprus.
