 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The definition of international reserves follows, in principle, the recommendations of the Balance of Payments Manual, 6th edition (BPM6). Those foreign assets held by SAMA that do not qualify for inclusion in the official reserves are excluded from reserves. However, certain departures exist; in particular, the international reserves are not disseminated with the instrument breakdown recommended by BPM6 (i.e. a breakdown of foreign exchange into currency and deposits, and securities). The definition of residency is consistent with the BPM6 and 2008 SNA. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Foreign positions are distinguished based on residency criterion. Classification of Financial Instruments The principles underlying the classification of financial instruments in Saudi Arabia's monetary statistics are based on legal characteristics and the liquidity of financial instruments. The classification of financial instruments is, notwithstanding the differences in terminology used, generally consistent with the MFSM recommendations . The following groups of financial instruments are identified in the data compiled by SAMA: (i) monetary gold and SDR, (ii) currency and deposits broken down into demand deposits, time and savings deposits and other quasi-monetary deposits, (iii) marketable securities, (iv) bills, (v) loans and advances, (vi) overdrafts, and (vii) shares. Other quasi-monetary deposits comprise foreign currency deposits, marginal deposits for letters of credits, outstanding remittances, and banks' Repo transactions with private parties. 2.2.1 Scope Foreign positions are distinguished based on residency criterion. Scope of the data The reserves cover SAMA's foreign assets transactions and their positions with nonresidents. Exceptions to coverage No exception of coverage exists. 2.4.1 Valuation SAMA accounts are compiled on the basis of cost valuation for holdings of all securities other than shares and other equities. Where no observable market price is available for a security, accepted accounting standards for fair value are applied. The exchange rate against US dollar does not change, because the Saudi Arabian currency is pegged to the U.S. dollar, therefore assets and liabilities in U.S. dollar are not revalued and are based on the fixed exchange rate. Foreign currency denominated assets and liabilities other than the US dollar are revalued at the market exchange rate at the end of the month. According to the Royal Decree No. 38, monetary gold is valued at the fixed price of 0.20751 grams for one Saudi Riyal (equivalent to about US$35 per ounce) in SAMA's accounting balance sheet and analytical accounts. 2.4.2 Recording basis SAMA accounts, including data on official reserves, are recorded on cash basis. In general, transactions are recorded at the time the transaction occurs by means of simultaneous electronic recording, which ensure intra- and inter-sectoral consistency in the compilation of monetary data. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures In line with the general principles of the MFSM, the accounts of the central bank are collected and compiled on a gross basis. Claims on particular transactors are not netted against liabilities to those transactors. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The source data used for the compilation Reserve Template is SAMA's balance sheet. Moreover, Data & Statistics General Department(DSGD) is responsible for compiling the analytical accounts of the central bank in line with the standard presentation recommended by international practices. Besides, the source for IMF-related accounts is the IMF's International Financial Statistics (IFS). 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The arrangements of the flow of the data between SAMA's related departments for compiling the official reserves provides for the timely receipt of data by the DSGD, ensures the adherence to the timeliness of dissemination of the data. SAMA's accounting balance sheet is available three days after the end of the reference month. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Issues concerning necessary revisions of source data are addressed jointly with the related SAMA's departments. In the process of cross-checks, in the event that discrepancies are found, problems are addressed by the staff of the DSGD and, as a result, reported data are requested to be revised. Further, all the source of data used are assessed to check the consistency with international guidelines, concepts and methodologies. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques No adjustments are made to the source data. SAMA accounting balance sheet covers all the necessary source data. Therefore, no estimation methods are used for data compilation. In addition, the data compilation procedures are fully automated, which eliminates data processing errors, such as data coding and editing errors and tabulation errors. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Data on monetary statistics are not seasonally adjusted. Complementary data are obtained from SAMA's related Department for the purpose of IMF related account such as SDR valuation. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Reserve data are validated with the IMF-related accounts available in the IMF's website and in its International Financial Statistics (IFS) publication. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Rarely, discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs are found because the monitored SAMA's balance sheet is the only source. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Information are made available on request. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points All SAMA publications and the website identify DSGD's contact points for enquiries by mail, telephone, facsimile, and by e-mail.
