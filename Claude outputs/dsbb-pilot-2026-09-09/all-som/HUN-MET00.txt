 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Foreign trade statistics is compatible with UN Statistics Division methodology. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization External trade data is collected by the 8 digits of the Combined Nomenclature as defined in the yearly updated Annex 1 of the Council Regulation (EEC) No. 1106/2012, which is based on the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS) maintained by the World Customs Organization (WCO). Concerning dissemination, figures are available by the Combined Nomenclature, HS, and the Standard International Trade Classification (SITC Rev. 4) of the UN. Countries are classified according to the yearly updated version of the Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2020/1197 on the nomenclature of countries and territories for the External Trade Statistics of the Community and Statistics of Trade between Member States (Geonomenclature). Scope of the data Transactions coverage: The majority of transactions recorded are sale or purchase transactions. However, external trade statistics also includes transactions without change of ownership and/or compensation. This way, statistics include operations with a view to and after processing under contract (at the gross values of products in both directions), financial leasing transactions (at the market value of the product without additional expenses), returns of goods, as well as movements of some special goods (e.g. natural gas and electric energy). Geographic coverage: The economic territory of Hungary. Merchandise trade within the EU (called intra-EU trade) is established on the basis of a data collection in which only VAT-liable establishments with EU imports of more than HUF 250 million and/or EU exports of more than HUF 140 million on a yearly basis are obliged to provide data. Exports and imports below these thresholds are estimated on the basis of VAT. Merchandise trade with non-EU countries (called extra-EU trade) is primarily registered within a customs framework, in which all importers and exporters are obliged to provide data. Data collection is organised for some transactions under simplification measures, the full datasets of which are missing at the national customs administration (Single Authorisation for Simplified Procedures). Exceptions to coverage Goods treated as part of trade in services (e.g. repair and operational leasing), direct re-export, fuel sold on the territory of Hungary to foreign motor vehicles and purchased abroad by Hungarian motor vehicles etc. are excluded from the statistics on trade in goods. A detailed list of exclusions is available in the Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2020/1197. Unrecorded activity Illegal trade is not recorded. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: The statistical value for exports is a FOB-type value (free on board) which means the value of the goods as they leave the territory of Hungary.. The statistical value for imports is a CIF-type value (cost, insurance, freight). This means that incidental expenses (e.g. transport, insurance) incurred from the place of exports in the partner country to the national border of Hungary are included in the value 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: In intra-EU trade the reported month of a transaction is the month in which the physical movement of goods across the Hungarian border is performed, in extra-EU trade the time of recording is the month when the declaration is accepted by the customs authority. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Inward processing is recorded on a gross basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Electronic data provision is mandatory from 2016. Data transmitted from administrative source: VAT data (for managing an intra-community register, in order to identify the parties responsible for providing information, data checking and estimation purposes) Recapitulative VAT statements (for data checking and estimation purposes) VAT recapitulative statements from other EU countries (VIES) (for data checking) Additional data sources for specific movements: Volume of trade in electricity data provided by MAVIR grid operator Traded value/prices of electricity provided by the Hungarian Energy and Public Utility Regulatory Authority Sources: Regarding intra-EU trade only VAT-liable establishments with EU-imports of more than HUF 500 million and/or EU-exports of more than HUF 200 million are obliged to provide data. Companies with a European Union import turnover not exceeding HUF 4.5 billion and achieving excellent data quality in the previous year — based on a comparison between the Intrastat arrivals report they provided and the data received from other Member States under the MDE framework — are exempt from the obligation to submit Intrastat arrivals reports. MDE stands for Micro-Data Exchange on intra-EU exports between the Member States. Regarding extra-EU trade, datasets of declarations submitted to the national customs authority are collected and transmitted from the national customs, whereas declarations submitted to EU customs authorities other than the Hungarian one are collected from non-resident economic operators. The International Trade in Goods Statistics related to Non-EU countries (Extrastat), compiled by the Hungarian Central Statistical Office (HCSO), is traditionally based on customs data provided by National Tax and Customs Administration of Hungary. However, if the supplementary declaration for goods located in Hungary is submitted in another Member State permitted by an authorisation for centralised clearance (or Single Authorisation for Simplified Procedures), the statistical data source for HCSO would be lost. Therefore, these customs authorisations can only be used in Hungary if the economic operator has contacted HCSO, in order to fulfil its obligation of statistical data provision. Eurostat provides this data to member states, including Hungary, as part of its CDE data. CDE stands for Customs-Data Exchange on extra-EU exports between the Member States and third countries. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Type of data collected: the following variables are collected within Intrastat: reference month, reference year, VAT-number of transactor, country of consignment (imports), country of destination (exports), country of origin, commodity code (according to the Combined Nomenclature, at 8-digit level), net mass/quantity in supplementary unit (if applicable), statistical value, invoice value and partner VAT-number (exports). The following variables are collected within Extrastat: date of acceptance, customs identifying number (EORI) of transactor, country of consignment (import), country of destination (export), country of origin (import), nationality of transport unit at destination, container, delivery terms, nature of transaction, mode of transport at border, location of goods, mode of transport in Hungarian territory, commodity code (according to the the Combined Nomenclature at 8-digit level from TARIC code), TARIC sub-heading (10-digit level), preference code, customs procedure code, net mass/quantity in supplementary unit (if applicable), statistical value, invoice value, centralised clearance authorisation number, currency, place of storage of the goods, nature of transaction code, declaration part, customer VAT identification number. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Concerning Intrastat, the 15th working day after the end of the reference month is the deadline of submission for monthly Intrastat-reports. Concerning Extrastat, the date of accepting the customs declaration for the goods submitted to the national customs administration determines its assignment to the reference period. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Data quality is verified in a data checking procedure. At production stage foreign trade input data are subject to automatic and manual, formal and logical check before statistical datasets are produced. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Adjustments to improve data: adjustments for missing data are made in Intrastat: missing trade (data of both exempted enterprises and non-respondents) is estimated by methods of mathematical statistics. There is a minor adjustment concerning data collected in Extrastat system based on preliminary data of Customs by the time of closing the statistical processing of the reference month. Processing site and system: since 2016, only electronic declarations have been accepted. Customs declarations are processed by Customs and the files are sent weekly to Hungarian Central Statistical Office (HCSO) electronically. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Geographic details of data: on a monthly basis, information is published by country-groups and main partners; however, data are available by countries. Country-groups are EU-15, new Member States (joined in 2004, 2007 and 2013), EU-27 (excluding UK from 2020), third countries, OECD (by the 2013 membership). Export figures are broken down by countries of destination. In the case of import data, since May 2004, the formerly applied country of origin has been replaced by the country of consignment. Calculation of price indices: for measuring the changes in volume and price levels of foreign trade turnover, price indices based primarily on actual market prices have been calculated since 2003. Price indices of homogeneous commodity groups (food, beverages, tobacco, crude materials, fuel) are worked out on basis of HUF values and quantity data (unit value indices). As for the price indices of heterogeneous commodity groups (manufactured goods; machinery and transport equipment) data supplied by enterprises are used. Indices are aggregated by total turnover weights of commodities. Since 2003 price indices have been calculated on a monthly basis by the Fisher formula. Price indices may change during the reference year. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results All incoming INTRASTAT and EXTRASTAT declarations (statistical survey) and all administrative data relating to EXTRASTAT trade submitted by the customs authorities are checked for validity and credibility. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Intrastat data are checked and compared with VAT and VIES data. In case of large discrepancies, data providers are consulted. Reconciliation exercise between trade data compiled by HCSO and financial data compiled by the National Bank of Hungary is carried out quarterly. Occasionally foreign trade data are also checked in mirror exercises with other EU Member States. All discrepancies during these reconciliation exercises are analysed in order to find reasons for discrepancies and to avoid them in the future. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revision analyses are also a part of Quality Report produced for EUROSTAT: https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/international-trade-in-goods/quality 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished and non-confidential data are disseminated on request, before the dissemination the confidentiality of data is checked. These requests can arrive by e-mail or telephone. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Information on contact points is available at the end of each publication: kommunikacio@ksh.hu .
