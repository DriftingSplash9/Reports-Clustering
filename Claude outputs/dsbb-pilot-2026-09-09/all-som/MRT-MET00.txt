 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The General Directorate of Customs in the Ministry of Finance is responsible for the compilation, processing, and dissemination of customs data. The National Statistics Office (ONS) in the Ministry of Economic and Development Affairs is responsible for processing and publishing trade statistics. Trade statistics are compiled in accordance with the “special” and “general” trade systems defined in the United Nations manual International Trade Statistics: Concepts and Definitions (1998 version), by group of customs regime codes corresponding to the definitions of special trade and general trade. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Exported and imported goods are classified in accordance with the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS10). (HS10 = International HS6 + 2 European Community positions + 2 national positions) Scope of the data In the trade statistics, exports are attributed to the “country of destination” on their exit from the port of shipment, and imports are attributed to the “country of origin.” The country of destination is the country in which the goods will be used, and the country of origin is the country in which the goods were produced or last processed. If the country of destination/origin is unknown at the time of customs recording, the exports/imports in question are shown as being of uncertain origin. All entries and exits of merchandise are recorded, except in the case of informally traded goods. The net weight of the merchandise traded is expressed in kilograms. Some categories of merchandise are, in addition, expressed in specific units of measurement: number, pair, linear meter, liter, etc. The value of merchandise is expressed in ouguiyas. In customs declarations, amounts shown in foreign currency are converted into ouguiyas at the official exchange rate provided by the Central Bank of Mauritania (BCM). Unrecorded activity All entries and exits of merchandise are recorded, except in the case of informally traded goods. 2.4.1 Valuation To evaluate goods, the statistical value base to be considered is the transactional value as defined in the regulations. Based on this transactional value, the statistical value to be calculated is the f.o.b. value for exports and re-exports, and the c.i.f. value is the one used for imports. Whenever transactional value cannot be established because of the absence of a trade transaction (for food assistance and grants to the government, assistance provided by private organizations or individuals, etc.), the value of the goods in question is determined on the basis of the value of similar traded goods. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The statistical sources for collecting data are customs declarations completed for all merchandise crossing the country’s customs territory. Declarations are processed by on-site customs offices, if the latter are computerized, or centralized within the General Directorate of Customs, through its IT and Statistics Division, which is responsible for the input and tracking of such declarations. Declarations are managed through an automated system of customs data (ASYCUDA), with the data processed in ORACLE. The data are applied to the month corresponding to the date on which the customs declaration in question was recorded. None None
