 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Definition: Australia's international investment position (IIP) is a statistical statement summarizing the level of Australia's foreign financial assets and liabilities at a point in time, together with details of changes in these levels, since the previous reference point, as a result of financial transactions, price changes, exchange rate changes and other adjustments. Analytical framework: The conceptual framework and classification structure for Australia's IIP statistics published by the ABS follows both the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual ( BPM6 ) and the System of National Accounts 2008 ( SNA08) . The IIP is fully integrated with Australia's balance of payments statistics. Summary analytic reports of the data present Australia's net IIP as the difference between the levels of gross foreign financial assets and gross foreign liabilities; as well as the sum of net foreign equity and net foreign debt. More detailed IIP data are presented according to the fully articulated framework prescribed by BPM6 and are presented in both a time series format and in a reconciliation format. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification system: Australia's IIP statistics are classified according to BPM6. Assets and liabilities are classified into direct investment, portfolio investment (showing a breakdown of equity and debt), financial derivatives, other investment and reserve assets. These are further classified by instrument of investment, institutional sector and original contractual maturity. To satisfy known user requirements, transactions and levels of debt are published with a more detailed sector classification than required by BPM6, together with additional detail on the domicility of the debt. 2.2.1 Scope Classification system: Australia's IIP statistics are classified according to BPM6. Assets and liabilities are classified into direct investment, portfolio investment (showing a breakdown of equity and debt), financial derivatives, other investment and reserve assets. These are further classified by instrument of investment, institutional sector and original contractual maturity. To satisfy known user requirements, transactions and levels of debt are published with a more detailed sector classification than required by BPM6, together with additional detail on the domicility of the debt. Scope of the data Coverage of activity : Australia's IIP attempts to cover all of Australia's foreign financial assets and liabilities, together with all related financial transactions. All institutional sectors are covered, although the international investment activity of households may not be fully captured by existing sources and methods. Geographic coverage : In Australia's IIP statistics, the external territories of Christmas Island, Cocos (Keeling) Islands and the Antarctic Territory are regarded as part of the Australian economy. 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to unit of account : Transactions and stock positions originally denominated in foreign currencies are converted to Australian dollars using prevailing market rates of exchange. Transactions are recorded on the basis of conversion using the mid-point of the buying and selling exchange rates on the day of the transaction. Opening and closing stocks data are recorded on the basis of conversion at the mid-point exchange rate applying at the beginning or end of the reference period. Valuation : Transactions and positions data are recorded at market prices. In practice the values recorded in business accounts may be used. These are considered to be a close practical approximation to market prices in Australia's case. In cases where significant distortion may occur to the valuation of direct equity, adjustments are made on the basis of total market capitalisation. 2.4.2 Recording basis Recording is on the basis of accrual accounting. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures All flows (transactions, market price changes, exchange rate changes, and other changes) are collected on a gross basis except for financial derivatives within the reserve assets functional category which are recorded on a net basis (assets - liabilities). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The main source of Australia's IIP data is the Survey of International Investment (SII) conducted quarterly by the ABS. This is an electronic form dispatched quarterly which collects data from enterprises. Enterprises consist of all related legal entities that are classified to the same resident institutional sub-sector. The SII obtains information about foreign financial assets and liabilities acquired as principal by financial intermediary enterprises, corporate trading enterprises and financial auxiliary enterprises, as well as any foreign financial assets and liabilities acquired using funds managed by the enterprise on behalf of other Australian enterprises, governments or individuals. Details of the international investment of the Commonwealth Government and the Reserve Bank of Australia are collected using separate forms which capture all of their balance of payments and international investment activities. Information is collected about foreign portfolio investment in Australian assets where the investment is held by a nominee enterprise in Australia on behalf of a foreign investor. Most of these data are collected on a security by security basis (through nominee collection and form 85L). In most cases, transactions and levels data are reported by transactors within the reconciliation format of the IIP. However, in some instances, such as foreign investment in tradable securities registered with domestic nominees, the transactions information is estimated by the ABS based on nominee holdings on behalf of non-residents which is reported quarterly, together with public information on price changes for the relevant security portfolios. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data is collected in a manner that aligns with the definitions, scope, classifications, valuation and time of recording required by the BPM6. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The SII provides quality data in sufficient time to meet SDDS requirements. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures In most cases, transactions and levels data are reported by transactors within the reconciliation format of the IIP. However, in some instances, such as foreign investment in tradable securities registered with domestic nominees, the transactions information is estimated by the ABS based on nominee holdings on behalf of non-residents which is reported quarterly, together with public information on price changes for the relevant security portfolios. A wide variety of verification processes are adopted. As the source data are collected within the IIP framework, self-verification by the transactors is part of the reporting to the ABS. The data for cross-border transactions and investment positions are also confronted with data reported on total balance sheet positions and financial transactions in other ABS collections (for flow of funds and balance sheet presentations in the national financial accounts, including summary market statements) and in prudential reporting. Data are also routinely compared with company reports and press reports. Historical annual series, commencing in 2001 are released with each issue. Quarterly IIP data are shown for the full quarterly time series (from September quarter 1988). 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results All data submitted by businesses in surveys or through administrative sources are subject to rigorous editing procedures, verifying the validity of the data. Where available, data are also confronted with regulatory prudential information, company reports, and information gathered from the financial press 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Aggregates and individual business data are assessed for their plausibility and may result in further investigation of individual business data. These assessments include alignment with concepts as well as confrontation within the BOP framework and alignment with accounting identities and sensibilities. All information from other sources is brought together prior to the compilation of investment income, financial account and IIP. This external information is used to compile an "expectation" of these components to confront against survey results when they are compiled. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs There is an ongoing program to confront reported data and aggregates to identify and remove causes of statistical discrepancies. This includes ensuring consistency across the macroeconomic accounts regarding the recording of large transactions. Staff involved in compiling the financial account monitor errors and omissions to confirm that no consistent biases are becoming apparent and that the magnitude of errors and omissions is not increasing over time. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Summary revision tables are included in the quarterly publication, Balance of Payments and International Investment Position, Australia . The publication is available on the ABS website. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request More detailed quarterly, annual statistics, and time series data are available on request or via subscription from the listed contact person.
