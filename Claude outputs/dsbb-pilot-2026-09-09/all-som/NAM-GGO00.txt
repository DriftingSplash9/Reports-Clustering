Scope of the data The aim of GFS compilation is to capture comprehensively the General Government sector and to ensure that a list of public sector institutions by sector, accordingly to the GFSM 2014 and SNA 2008 sector classification principles, is being drawn up. This list will guide data collection and compilation. The GGO comprises of the Budgetary Central Government, Extra-budgetary Units; Local Governments; State governments and Social Security fund. It is worth mentioning that while total expenditure is largely comprehensive, the lack of data sources for delegated functions and Extrabudgetary units means that estimates are currently being used for the economic breakdown of expenditure related to intra-government grants for delegated functions and to Extrabudgetary units. Also, currently missing are own revenues of Extrabudgetary units and their related expenditure. Work is ongoing to secure data to resolve these issues. Exceptions to coverage GGO data coverage compilation and dissemination currently does not include extra-budgetary units as a separate sub-sector due to the need to identify all such units and establish data collection for these units. It is planned to include these units in the statistics by September 2023. Unrecorded activity No data are currently recorded for small local government units, such as village councils and town councils that fall under local authorities mandate but the activities of these units have been assessed as not material. Any other instances of unrecorded activities that are identified will be promptly referred to the Statistics Committee for resolution. 2.4.1 Valuation The accounting standards valuation of assets and liabilities implies that GFS compiled data sources for BCG and SG are valued at exchange prices (based on cash flows) while the data for LG and SSF currently use data compiled based on accounting standards and so are valued at fair value / market value. Where necessary data are netted, and all relevant data are recorded on a gross basis. 2.4.2 Recording basis The recording of budgetary central government data and State Governments data is cash. The Local governments and Social Security Funds data are recorded on accrual basis. SG data is based largely on the cash data from the management reports. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures All stock data are recorded on a gross basis, while flows are recorded as net basis. Tax repayment is deducted from revenue amount. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Budgetary central government: Data are directly collected from the administrative records of Offices, Ministries and Agencies (OMA's). Extra-budgetary units: Data are from annual financial statements records of the entities where the latest data are not available, estimates based upon the last available audited financial statements are made. Annual data are taken from the annual audited financial records of the funds. This year 2021 reporting does not include Extra-budgetary units. Social security funds: Data are taken from the annual financial records of the funds. Local government: Data are collected from the local authorities (Municipals) and when data not available estimates are done through annual survey in collaboration with Namibia Statistics Agency (NSA). The Budget has been classified to the GFSM 2014 codes and the Chart of Accounts are under review to be aligned. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The final data for central budgetary are available by July after end of financial year ending March. The draft PFMA requires that the final data be made available within 3 months after the end of March each and for reporting general government two quarters or six months respectively. The Advance Release Calendar is line with SDDS general government data reporting requirements. - GGO data are disseminated annual - BCG data are disseminated monthly - CGD data are disseminated monthly/quarterly 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data assessment for GGO is to improve source data in a number of areas (LG, EBUs, and grants) and that the work is being coordinated by the Statistics Committee. Of course, the momentum needs to be maintained on all these aspects. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques All data sources follow national classification structures which are then mapped to GFSM 2014 codes using bridge tables. The recording method for central government operation is cash-flow basis, and the plan is to move from pure cash to modified cash, modified accrual and to full accrual in future. One exception is the treatment of foreign loans which are being serviced by budgetary central government but where the disbursements are made directly to the projects they are supporting and so to units outside of budgetary central government. In these cases, a cash flow is imputed with the loan being treated as an external loan to the budgetary central government and an onwards grant from the budgetary central government to the project. Data are improved if more correct data become available. The country data compilation procedures are documented and updated, if necessary, after each processing period. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures As noted in 3.3.1, all data sources require mapping to GFSM 2014 classification structures. In the case of budgetary central government, the source data are generally sufficiently detailed to allow one-to-one mapping. Although some additional information is required to facilitate consolidation. State government's data are compiled from cash-based administrative reports following a standardized format. However, the revenue data are in some cases insufficiently disaggregated to allow a detailed bridge to GFSM 2014, and in these cases adjustments and assumptions are made based on the report descriptions and more detailed data reported in other periods. Local governments and social security funds data are compiled from annual financial statements. For some local governments the financial statements are insufficiently detailed to fully disaggregate the components of revenue and expenditure and so estimates are made based on the more detailed statements in other years, or by other local governments. There are some gaps in the data sources for both state governments and local governments which have been filled using estimation techniques, including the use of proportions of specific revenue and expense components in other periods or regions, and the use of interpolation/extrapolation based on time series trends. Where estimates are used these are documented and the estimates are replaced by actual data when it becomes available. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The intermediate outcomes and statistical outputs are frequently evaluated and confirmed alongside other data and annual reports, and mid-year budget reviews, as well as government announcements, are compared with source data to ensure an accurate picture of GFS. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data The GFS compiler do some data assessments for internal consistency. The expense and nonfinancial assets are reconciled with the total expenditures by functional categories. The statistical discrepancy between above-the-line and below-the-line transactions is also examined. Work is currently on-going to source more detailed data on transactions in financial assets and liabilities which is expected to lead to improvements in the current statistical discrepancy. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Currently the reported data are only based on the transactions "vertical" above and below the baseline, means some flows are not covered especially the other economic flows and stock (balance sheet). Full system of recording flows and stocks (vertical and horizontal) are not integrated at this point in time. As noted in 3.4.2 the compilation procedures include assessments of any discrepancies between above-the-line and below-the-line transactions. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses No revision studies analysis 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses No revisions studies and analysis 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Data requests are received telephonically or via email by institutions such as the FAO, World Bank, SADC, AfDB, private institutions and researchers.
