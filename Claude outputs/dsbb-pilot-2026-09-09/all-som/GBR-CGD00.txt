 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Definition: Sterling Central Government Gross Debt (CGCD) is the stock of sterling UK central government liabilities to the rest of the economy and the world. It is the sterling denominated central government component of General Government Gross Debt (GGGD).GGGD is as defined in the protocol annexed to the 1992 Treaty on European Union ('Maastricht Treaty'). Definition: All sterling CGGD components are recorded at original maturity. The total of domestic sterling debt with less than one year's residual maturity is shown separately (as is total sterling CGGD). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: Central Government includes all UK government departments and other central agencies whose competence extends over the whole economy. Also included are those non-profit institutions which are controlled and mainly financed by central government and whose competence also extends over the whole economy. The central government sector is defined in accordance with the regulations of ESA 2010 and the rules and clarifications provided in the Eurostat's Manual on Government Deficit and Debt (MGDD), which are also consistent with the 2008 SNA and GFSM 2014. The ONS economic statistics classifications process is used to determine which units belong within the government sector and which transactions are taxes. https://www.ons.gov.uk/methodology/classificationsandstandards/economicstatisticsclassific ations/ukeconomicstatisticssectorandtransactionclassificationstheclassificationprocess Scope of the data The institutional coverage of the data is the same as that for central government operations. Institutional coverage: Data represent market holdings of sterling CGGD, defined as all holdings outside the central government sector. But holdings by local government, public corporations (government organisations with a degree of management and financial autonomy) and the Bank of England are included as part of market holdings. Sterling CGGD consists of the following financial instruments: British government stocks ('gilts'): marketable, long-term, interest-bearing instruments; Treasury bills: Short-term instruments issued at variable maturity; National Savings and Investments products: a range of non-marketable debt products available to retail investors; Certificates of tax deposit: non-marketable debt available to tax payers that may be used in payment of most taxes; Other sterling debt: includes Ways & Means advances (short-term lending by the Bank of England to the government); Debt Management Office (DMO) outstanding transactions with the market; NILO gilts (non-marketable gilts issued directly to the National Investment and Loans Office, whose terms reflect those on existing gilts);Temporary Deposits: deposits by central government and public corporations with the National Loans Fund and local government deposits with the DMO; Northern Ireland government debt ( mainly Ulster Savings Certificates); Balances of certain public corporations with the Paymaster General; Insolvency Service deposits; Funds held on behalf of the European Commission: Guaranteed Export Finance Corporation (GEFCO) net liabilities (guaranteed by government) since its reclassification to the central government sector in 1987; Coin in circulation; Court funds: deposits with the National Debt Commissioners of funds lodged in courts of law. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation practices: All data are recorded at nominal values, amounts outstanding at the end of each month. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Gross vs. net: All data are recorded on a gross basis. The nominal value of index-linked gilts within sterling CGGD has been raised by the amount of accrued capital uplift. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources: Gilts data are provided by HM Treasury and the Debt Management Office. National Savings data are provided by National Savings and Investments. Data on sterling Treasury bills, certificates of tax deposits, court funds and other sterling debt (except for NI government debt, which is provided by the Northern Ireland Department of Finance and Personnel) are also provided by HM Treasury. Coin in circulation data are provided by the Royal Mint. None None
