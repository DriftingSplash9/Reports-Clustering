 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Data on general government operations (GGO) are compiled and released in accordance with the Government Finance Statistics Manual (GFSM) 2014 framework, specifically the Statement of Government Operations. The source data are transmitted from the Czech system of national accounts, which is based on the European System of Accounts (ESA 2010). For this purpose, the "GFS2014-ESA2010 transmission bridge" provided by the IMF is applied. The methodology for compiling national accounts - covering nonfinancial accounts of general government (revenue, expenditure and net lending/net borrowing) as well as transactions in financial assets and liabilities (net acquisition of financial assets and net incurrence of liabilities) - is based on ESA 2010 (Regulation (EU) No 549/2013) and the Manual on Government Deficit and Debt. There are some differences between ESA and GFS in the treatment of certain activities: FISIM Adjustment: In GFS, service fees related to interest are not separately identified, as they can only be estimated indirectly using economy-wide data from national accounts. In SNA, values for FISIM are derived by partitioning interest. For this reason, the items "Use of goods and services", "Interest", and "Other revenue" are adjusted for FISIM. Own-Account Capital Formation: In GFS, compensation of employees, use of goods and services, and consumption of fixed capital incurred in own-account capital formation are excluded from expense and recorded as part of the acquisition of the nonfinancial assets. In SNA, these costs are recorded in full alongside the acquisition of the nonfinancial assets. Therefore, the items "Compensation of employees", "Use of goods and services" as well as "Consumption of fixed capital" on the expenditure side and the item "Other revenue" on the revenue side are adjusted by own-account capital formation. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The general government sector and its subsectors are defined in accordance with the ESA 2010 guidelines and the ESA 2010 Manual on Government Deficit and Debt, which are consistent with the 2008 SNA and GFSM 2014. In the Czech Republic, the general government comprises central government, local government, and social security funds. The CZSO compiles and disseminates government finance statistics for each subsector as well as for the general government sector as a whole. Sector classification of institutional units is reviewed annually and updated as necessary. One of the CZSO's priorities is to maintain consistency between government finance statistics and sector accounts, particularly with respect to sector delimitation. Scope of the data The institutional coverage corresponds to general government sector according to the methodology ESA 2010. In the Czech Republic, the CZSO is responsible for the delimitation of general government sector and its updating. This section describes the coverage of the general government sector and its sub-sectors. The general government sector in the Czech Republic is composed by 3 subsectors: " S.1311 - Central government subsector, " S.1313 - Local government subsector, " S.1314 - Social security funds subsector. Central government subsector (S.1311) The central government subsector comprises the following units, grouped into budgetary and extra budgetary categories. 1. Budgetary: Central budgetary organizations (CBO) - ministries, central authorities, and organizations established and managed by ministries or other central government bodies, whose revenues and expenditures are part of the State budget. 2. Extra-budgetary: a) State Extra-budgetary Funds (SF) - legal entities established by the relevant ministry under specific legislation. b) Central (state) semi-budgetary organizations (SBO) - entities created by a central budgetary organization to perform selected government functions, particularly in the fields of culture, education, and social services. c) Non-financial central government institutions (e.g., Railway Administration, Road and Motorway Directorate). d) Financial government institutions (e.g., Czech Export Bank, National Development Bank). e) Non-profit non-financial institutions (e.g., public universities, public research institutions). Local government subsector (S.1313) The local government subsector comprises the following units: 1. Local budgetary organizations: a) Territorial Self-governing Divisions and Offices, i.e., regional offices, municipalities, and town councils. b) Voluntary Associations of Municipalities. Regional Councils of Cohesion Regions were abolished as of 31 December 2021. 2. Other local organizations: a) Local semi-budgetary organizations - entities established by a local budgetary organization to perform selected government functions. These organizations operate across various branches of the national economy and are linked to local budgets. b) Non-profit institutions that provide services exclusively to local government units or are primarily financed from local budgets. c) Public non-market corporations - companies providing technical services and services in culture and sports. d) Public health facilities with a legal form other than semi-budgetary organizations, owned by a general government entity (primarily municipalities and regions). e) Public financial corporations. For example, government debt is valued at face value for Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP) purposes, while individual items in the national accounts are valued at market prices. Other components are also valued at market prices as required by the applicable methodology. 2.4.2 Recording basis Data are expressed in millions of the national currency and recorded on an accrual basis. The accounting year (fiscal year) corresponds to the calendar year. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The CZSO follows the rules set by the regulation (ESA 2010). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Statistical data are collected by the CZSO in accordance with the relevant decree (https://csu.gov.cz/vykazy/program_statistickych_zjistovani). The general policy of the CZSO is to use statistical data for the general government sector as supplementary information to fill gaps in administrative data sources. Administrative data - such as profit and loss statements and balance sheets for budgetary organizations, state funds, and semi-budgetary units - are collected and processed by the MoF, including budgetary reports on revenue and expenditure of budgetary organizations and state funds. Data for financial institutions classified as government units are primarily provided by the CNB. Where necessary, these administrative sources are supplemented by statistical questionnaires, which mainly survey indicators such as natural wage and investments, etc. Data for other entities - such as public universities, health insurance companies, public research institutions, public hospitals, Czech Television and Radio (public broadcasting companies), the Financial Market Guarantee System, and other non-profit institutions and public corporations - are collected directly by the CZSO. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The time of recording fully adheres to the established rules: revenues and expenditures are recorded in the period in which they occur, regardless of the timing of cash transactions. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The primary data sources for compiling the quarterly national accounts for the main government institutions are available within t+50 or t+70 days after the end of the previous quarter. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data are primarily based on budgetary reports and financial statements, which cover the majority of government units, ensuring a response rate of 100%. Non-profit institutions provide information through statistical questionnaires, and these surveys are exhaustive, with response rates typically reaching 100%. Data assessment is carried out during the processing of statements. Results are monitored, and any significant errors are discussed directly with the respective respondent. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Since the compilation of government accounts relies primarily on budgetary reports and financial statements, the response rate is typically 100%. In some cases - such as semi-budgetary organizations - statistical surveys are not exhaustive. However, the CZSO has access to aggregate figures (e.g., total assets and their structure), so only the breakdown of certain sub-items is surveyed. Budgetary classification of revenue and expenditure is highly detailed, and supplementary accounting data for budgetary organizations, state funds, and semi-budgetary organizations (from the Auxiliary Analytical Overview - AAO) are also comprehensive. As a result, the CZSO has complete data coverage for all units classified within the general government sector. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Data adjustments are carried out in accordance with the ESA 2010 methodology. The following list summarizes the main regular and exceptional methodological adjustments: " Payment for non-life insurance services - P.2 and D.71 divided for direct insurance, i.e., D.711 and reinsurance D.712; calculation is carried out in compliance with ESA 2010 methodology. " Acquisition of combat aircraft (Gripen) - recorded in 2005 and in 2015 (to reflect the extension of the contract) as gross fixed capital formation (P.51g); consumption of fixed capital is reported under P.51c; repayments for the aircraft are recorded under D.41 and F.42. The leasing arrangement is treated as financial leasing and included in government debt. " Deliveries of other military equipment - classified either under P.51g or P.52. " Accrual adjustments for changes in inventories of budgetary organizations - applied to items P.1, P.2 and P.52. " Adjustments to output due to increases in standing timber - recorded under P.1. " Financial leasing - reflected in P.2 (negative entry), D.41 and F.42. " Allocation of travel expenses between wages in kind and intermediate consumption (P.2, D.11) - 95% of travel expenses is classified under P.2 and 5% under D.11 as a catering allowance. " Wages in kind (D.1) - included in accordance with ESA methodology. " Imputed interest (D.41) - allocated to the resources of the corresponding sectors in the same amount as included under wages in kind. " Imputed social contributions and sickness benefits paid directly by employers to employees - for the first 14 days of sick leave (from 2008), for the first 21 days in 2011-2013, and again for the first 14 days since 2014, in line with sickness legislation; recorded under D.1222, D.6122 and D.6222. " Imputed subsidies to employers - reimbursement of sickness contributions at 50% (D.39) from 2008 until the end of 2011, introduced to mitigate the sudden increase in employers' costs. " FISIM - recorded under D.41 and P.2. " Fixed capital formation (P.51g) o Information on fixed capital formation is not directly available from financial statements used in the Czech accounting system. Therefore, the calculation of P.51g for year T-1 is based on: (i) budgetary reports for budgetary organizations (BO) on a cash basis, and (ii) data on stocks of non financial assets obtained from balance sheets for T-1, which are used for quarterly accounts compilation, GDP estimation, and the April EDP notification. For the October EDP notification, the data are derived from annual statistical questionnaires, which provide more detailed information, including the breakdown by individual types of acquired fixed assets. o Since 2013, fixed capital formation has been based primarily on data from AAO statements for all central BO, state funds, state semi-budgetary organizations (SBO), and for selected (but significant) local BO and SBO. The AAO provides detailed information on individual types of tangible and intangible fixed assets and inventories, as well as on forms of acquisition (purchases, sales, acquisitions free of charge) and on counterpart sectors. o For other government units, information on fixed capital formation is obtained from statistical questionnaires (VI 1 01, NI 1 01 or Zdp 5 01; P 5 01, Pen 5 01 and Poj 5 01). " Fixed capital consumption (-P.51c) o Fixed capital consumption is calculated using the Perpetual Inventory Method (PIM), as accounting or tax depreciation data are not used in national accounts. " Neutralization of the impact of EU grants on B.9 o Transfers to final users classified in other sectors are removed from government revenue and recorded under the corresponding national accounts items (D.73, D.74, D.75, D.9), in order to neutralize the effect of EU grants on the net lending/borrowing balance (B.9). Significant adjustments are applied when converting cash data of budgetary organizations to an accrual basis. These include adjustments to output and intermediate consumption, taxes, interest, social and health insurance contributions, and penalties within health insurance companies. The time?adjustment method is used, linked to receivables/payables (F.8). Additional methodological adjustments are applied to balance sheet items (stocks) and to financial or revaluation accounts. These include, for example, the imputation of government shares or adjustments to government debt arising from debt forgiveness or the assumption of liabilities of non?government units. These adjustments concern data not available from administrative sources. Within the accounts of the State Agricultural Intervention Fund (SAIF), any effect of EU subsidies on products is eliminated. The amount of SAIF inventories is excluded and the Fund is treated as a fictitious unit classified under S.11001 (public non?financial corporations) in items AN.12 and P.52. The same amount is recorded as other equity (AF.5, F.5) of the central government sector. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Validation of intermediate results is performed automatically during data processing, as information on non-financial and financial transactions for each unit is obtained from sources such as Profit and Loss Statement and Balance sheet. Comparisons with previous reports and individual questioning are used to ensure accuracy. For data collected through statistical questionnaires, the CZSO Regional Administrations are responsible for the collection, processing, and control of quarterly and annual statistical questionnaires. To support this process, "Technical Projects" are developed, which define technical linkages between indicators and highlight anomalies such as extreme or negative values. During compilation, annual changes in both relative and absolute terms are checked and verified. Where possible, information from annual reports of reporting entities is also used as supplementary validation. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Assessment and investigation of statistical discrepancies between B.9 and B.9f are performed during the compilation of government accounts for each institutional group (e.g., central budgetary or semi-budgetary organizations) as well as for individual units classified under sector S.13. Items of exceptional value are subject to additional verification, and, where necessary, direct inquiries with the respective unit are conducted. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Statistical outputs are systematically compared with data from banking statistics (e.g., on interest or deposits) and with information from the Final State Budget. Regular consultations with the CNB and the MoF take place during the compilation of government accounts. Additionally, comparing the statistical output of individual unit with its annual report plays an important role in ensuring data accuracy. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Regular revisions primarily concern data for year t-1 and, in some cases, previous years (t-2, t-3). These revisions reflect more detailed or updated information obtained for the October EDP notifications compared to the April EDP notifications. Regular revisions also incorporate decisions and recommendations issued by Eurostat. Extraordinary revisions are carried out mainly in connection with methodological changes. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs CZSO Website The primary source of statistical information for users is the CZSO website https://csu.gov.cz/home.
