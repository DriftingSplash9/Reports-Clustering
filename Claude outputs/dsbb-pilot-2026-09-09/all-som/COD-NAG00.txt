 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions In the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), the national accounts work is the province of the National Commission on Statistical Studies and National Accounts (CESCN), which includes the Ministry of Planning, the National Institute of Statistics (INS), the Central Bank of the Congo (BCC), the Institute of Economic and Social Research of the University of Kinshasa, the Federation of Companies of the Congo, and the National Agricultural Statistics Unit (SNSA) of the Ministry of Agriculture. 1. Analytical framework : Until the 2001 accounts, the national accounts framework was based on the 1968 System of National Accounts (SNA68). But the ongoing 2002 accounts compilation work is based on SNA93. 2. Type of data produced : Based on surveys conducted, the CESCN annually produces and disseminates the following data in the BCC’s annual report and in a document published annually titled Les Comptes légers de la RDC (Summary Accounts of the DRC), with an annual lag: Estimated gross domestic product (GDP) by economic sector and by type of expenditure (final consumption, gross fixed capital formation, changes in stocks, exports minus imports of goods and services), at current and constant prices; General government accounts (up to the capital account); and Consolidated accounts of the nation. The document titled Les Comptes légers de la RDC also contains the following information: Principal indicators of the DRC economy; Data on the total resident population, the labor force, and wage earners (in the civil service and private sector enterprises affiliated to the National Social Security Institute); and The production structure of the various economic sectors. 3. Concepts : The concepts and methodology used are based on SNA68. However, since the decision to adopt SNA93, the concepts and definitions used are based on SNA93. GDP is the final result of the production activity of resident productive units during a specific period. It is also the sum of the end uses of goods and services in the economy as a whole, minus the value of imports of goods and services during the period. Productive units are grouped on the basis of their principal economic function. The end uses of goods and services are final household consumption, final public consumption (all general government), gross fixed capital formation, changes in stocks, exports, and acquisitions minus transfers of valuables. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Previously, up to the compilation of the 2001 accounts, ISIC, Rev. 2 was used for the economic sectors, and SNA68 was used for the institutional sectors. ISIC, Rev. 3 and the SNA93 classification of the institutional sectors are now being used in the compilation of the 2002 accounts. - The Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System is used for international trade. Scope of the data Geographic Coverage The national accounts are supposed to cover the entire country. However, given the war situation that the DRC has been experiencing for several years, data on the areas formerly under rebel occupation are estimated. General government data cover only those areas under government control up to the time of reporting. Unit Coverage National accounts data cover all resident units, including producers in the informal sector, that is, producers that do not have proper accounting. Unrecorded activity No activity is excluded from coverage. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation : Production is recorded at base price; imports at c.i.f. prices; exports at f.o.b. prices; and domestic uses at purchase prices. The prices and exchange rates used are market-based. Within the framework of SNA93, imports are also valued f.o.b. 2.4.2 Recording basis Most transactions are recorded on a cash-disbursements basis. Recording Period : January 1 to December 31 (fiscal year). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Production approach · Food crops and livestock: production is estimated based on data from the SNSA of the Ministry of Agriculture, obtained after reconciling the Ministry’s estimates with the results of field surveys. · Cash crops (coffee, cotton, tobacco, para rubber, and tea): the data come from the National Coffee Office, the Central Bank’s economic surveys of producer companies, from the SNSA, and the Congolese Control Office. · Lumber and forestry production is estimated on the basis of data from the Ministry of the Environment and estimates of the national consumption of fuelwood and vegetable charcoal based on international standards. · Fishing: fishing production estimates are provided by the National Fisheries Development and Promotion Service. · Petroleum: production data are provided by the industry (producers and distributors). · Mining products: production data are furnished by sector companies during the Central Bank’s economic surveys, the Permanent Accounting Board of the Congo , the National Precious Materials Appraisal Center with respect to diamonds and gold produced in the crafts industry; and the United Nations report on looting in the DRC for 2000). · Manufacturing: production is calculated on the basis of data provided by sector companies in economic surveys conducted by the Central Bank and the Permanent Accounting Board of the Congo . · Modern sector construction and public works: production is estimated indirectly, based on certain intermediate consumption, particularly of cement. · Informal sector: data are estimated on the basis of ratios calculated following the 1989 and 1992 surveys of the informal sector. · Wholesale and retail trade and transportation: production is based on estimates of the distribution margin rates for marketed products and company financial statements. · Insurance and Financial Institutions: production is based on accounting records from the insurance company, banks and other financial institutions. · General government: production is calculated based on data from budget documents (execution of the national budget) and the accounting document from the National Social Security Institute. Expenditure Approach · Public final consumption data are based on government budget documents. · Changes in stocks, generally assumed to be nil, was recently calculated based on the data from companies’ tax returns, and this facilitated the adjustment of a number of aggregates such as household final consumption in the publication of the accounts that gives the series for 1990–2000 and in the last series containing 2001 data. · Exports and imports: the documents used are statistics from customs, the banks and the OECD (balance of payments) · Household final consumption is obtained as a balance. INSTITUTIONAL SECTOR ACCOUNTS The institutional sector accounts are limited to general government. Transactions with the rest of the world are also described. · General government sector: the data are based on government budget documents. · Rest of the world: detailed data from the balance of payments 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Data are compiled monthly and annually. The accounts are set up on an Excel spreadsheet, but the unit does not have software to assist with national accounts compilation. Production Approach The methodological approach used is based on analysis, adjustment, and aggregation of the data obtained from the following sources: · Manufacturing: a value index is calculated for a sample of companies and then applied to the previous year’s accounts to evaluate production at current prices in the year in question. The structure of the production accounts is calculated based on the financial statements of the companies in the sample. For accounts at constant prices, a price index is calculated for about twenty of the products for which price data are available for the base year and current year. The index is thus used as a deflator. · Construction and public works: the evaluation of production is based on a calculation of the consumption of construction materials. The structure of the technical coefficients of production allows us to estimate production based on the value of construction materials consumed, particularly cement. The estimate of village construction is added to this production. · Wholesale and retail trade uses the same procedure as is used to estimate the manufacturing accounts at current prices. The constant price accounts are obtained by deflating the current price accounts using the consumer price index. · Financial institutions: the accounts are prepared based on the financial statements of the banks and the national insurance company. · Other market services: the same methodology is used as that used to estimate the wholesale and retail trade accounts. · Given the war situation, some data are estimated based on information contained in the United Nations report on looting in the Congo (mining industries). For products such as coffee, cacao, and logs, a corrective coefficient is applied to declared production in the portion of the country controlled by the government. Expenditure Approach · The data on household final consumption are obtained as a balance. · Changes in stocks are calculated on the basis of data from companies’ tax returns, at least for the latest publication, which covers the 1990–2000 series. · For other aggregates, the data at current prices are calculated based on information available on product values. The data at constant prices are obtained by deflating the data at current prices using consumer price indices. None
