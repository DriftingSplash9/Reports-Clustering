 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The references for the national accounts are the former standards, namely the "1993 System of National Accounts", (1993SNA). The national accounts were compiled with the software ERETES. The classification of activities contains 50 industries at the level 2 and 24 activities at the level 1. The goods and services classification contains 121 goods and services at level 3 and the same details with activities at level 1 and level 2. Each year, since 2007, a supply and use table (SUT) has been compiled at current prices and also at last year constant prices. These supply and use tables provide the required aggregates like annual Gross Domestic Product (GDP); final consumptions, gross fixed capital formation (GFCF), changes in inventories. A hundred and twenty one (121) product balances were compiled each year since 2007. These product balances helped to set up a SUT with twenty four (24) products (the level 1 of the classification). This SUT provides the GDP with the three different approaches recommended by the 1993SNA. The SUT were back casted to 1999. From 1999 to 2015, the current SUT and the volume SUT (2007 is the base year) were compiled. The institutional sector accounts (non-financial corporations, financial corporations, general government, non- profit institutions serving households, households and rest of world) are estimated annually in a manner consistent with the estimation of GDP at current prices since 2007. The integrated economic account table was compiled since 2007 from the production accounts to capital accounts. For 2014, the financial accounts had been compiled. Since 2015 INSAE has begun the process of compiling quarterly national accounts. All the methodology has been set up with the technical assistance of International Monetary Fund (IMF) through its regional technical assistance center (West AFRITAC). This process was supposed to end this year and the quarterly national accounts could be published during this year 2017. This metadata deals essentially with only the annual national accounts. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization There is a bridge table between the industrial classification used and the former UN classification, namely the International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC Rev. 2). The aggregates are divided into 11 groups: Plant Production, Animal Production, Mining, Manufacturing Industries, Electricity, Construction and Public Works, Trade, Transportation and Communications, Other Market Services, Public Administrations and Import Duties and Taxes, and Household Services. The product classification is consistent with the industrial classification. In terms of external trade, the Harmonized System (HS) is used for the basic data and a bridge to the national accounts product classification is constructed. Scope of the data Geographic Coverage The national territory of Benin. Transaction Coverage All operations are covered. Unit Coverage All resident institutional units are covered. Unrecorded activity Some illegal activities, particularly re-exports and non-monitored imports of certain products, such as refined petroleum products, are taken into account. 2.4.1 Valuation Domestic transactions are valued at market price, while imports are valued c.i.f. (cost, insurance, and freight included) and exports are valued f.o.b. (free on board). Production is valued on the basis of production prices, which, in certain cases, are obtained from consumer prices in Cotonou, less the margin specific to the industry or product. 2.4.2 Recording basis In general, transactions are recorded on an accruals basis. Government revenue data are recorded on a cash basis but the government's expenditure are recorded on a basis price. Final consumption and GFCF are at acquisition price. Owner-occupied housing is valued at the comparable rental price. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Several types of data sources are identified. They include the following: Agriculture: The estimated data on food crops are generally sourced from Rural Development ministry (statistical unit) and the National Food Security Office (ONASA). The data on industrial crops come from the National Agricultural Promotion Corporation (SONAPRA) for cottonseed, from processing plants (for palm and shea), and from port statistics (for cashew nut). Forestry: The basic data for production of official undressed timber are provided by the National Timber Office (ONAB). Estimates of uncontrolled timber production are based on extrapolation of past trends. The production of firewood, charcoal, and household wood is estimated by the National Accounts Units on the basis of a per capita consumption ratio and of the population in rural and urban areas. Collection of corporation data source . The statistical and fiscal statements (DSF) of enterprises helped to have all data sources about the non-financial and financial corporations. The formal individual enterprises' statistics were also collected through the statistical and fiscal statements of enterprises. The INSAE quarterly survey of formal enterprises based on a form of collecting data on production, turnover, staff, and the wage bill. It is a sample survey. The INSAE household surveys, like EMICOV helped to have detail data on household's expenditure for different products of the classifications. Nonmarket Services of Public Administrations: Estimation is based on data from the Government Flow of Funds Table (TOFE), Local Government Revenue and Expenditure, and the accounting documents of the Beninese Social Security Office (OBSS). These data sources are supplemented with the Revenue and Expenditure Development Ledgers of the Public Investment Program (PIP) and the UNDP report on Development Cooperation. Gross Fixed Capital Formation (GFCF): The data from the Public Investment Program (PIP) and the UNDP report on development cooperation are used to estimate public GFCF, while private GFCF is valued on the basis of the table of investments of modern enterprises, supplemented by the PIP for certain public enterprises. An increase rate is applied in the case of livestock. Imports and Exports of Goods and Services: The basic data here come from the external trade statements (INSAE) of port statistics and the monthly and annual enterprise surveys. Non-monitored trade with neighboring countries is estimated on the basis of domestic demand. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Agricultural production by volume of food crops: When the data from the National Food Security Office (ONASA) do not coincide with the data from the Statistical Unit of the Ministry of Rural Development, the differences are offset based on the trends indicated by each source (related to the year before) and on the basis of the changes observed in prices. The general principle is that a sharp rise in prices results in a drop in production and the opposite, a considerable decline in price, indicates a rise in production. Basis prices: The National Food Security Office (ONASA) collected also the basis prices for the agricultural product on primary markets used by cultivators to sell their products. This prices helped to determines basis prices for agricultural product. For the principal agricultural product (cotton), the price fixed by the Government to cultivators is used as basis prices. Price Indices: They are calculated on the basis of the positions of products in the national accounts, using the average prices of two successive years. These price indices are used partly to supplement changes in quantities to arrive at the new value at current prices. They are also used to deflate the current values obtained directly, with a view to estimating the accounts at constant prices. When there's no idea about basis prices of given product, they were supposed to have the same evolution with the basis prices. Data from accounting documents: Statistical and tax statements of business (ou Déclarations statistques et fiscales, DSF) remained one of the principle data sources for the compilation of national accounts. This data source helped to well address the national economic structure. Since 2012 a business register was set up on the base of the general census of enterprises did since 2008. Even the business register was fully updated, the collect of statistical and tax statements each year helped to update the data base of businesses used to compile each year national accounts. Public Administrations: Any change in the wage bill of one year relative to the previous year covers: recruitment of new staff (volume effect) increase or decrease in the salary scale (price effect). advancement or promotion of senior staff (volume effect) retirements (negative volume effect) Household final consumption is valued by the supply and use balances. The population rate is commonly used in absence of a good indicator Changes in stocks: Generally on the base of data sources. Meanwhile, the stocks for livestock were estimated in application of the rule given by the AFRSTAT in his methodology note about accounts. Data on official external trade (imports and exports): are adjusted by the explained adjustments (data on enterprises in a monopoly situation) estimates of unrecorded trade with neighboring countries None
