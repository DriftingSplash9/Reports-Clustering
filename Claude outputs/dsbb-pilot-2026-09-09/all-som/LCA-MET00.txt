 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The general trade system is used to record trade statistics, Imports therefore include all goods brought into the country, including those in bonded (customs) warehouses. Goods re-exported from customs warehouses are included in exports 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Data are available by commodity classification, quantity, value, and country of origin/destination. Goods are classified using the Harmonised System (HS), Standard Industrial Trade Classification (SITC) Rev. 3, Broad Economic Classification (BEC), International Standard Industrial classification (ISIC) however data is published only in SITC Rev. 3 format. Data in HS format is available upon request. Scope of the data The merchandise trade statistics include parcel post (goods passing through the post office), the value of improvements and repairs to goods, non-monetary gold and goods leased for a period exceeding one year. It however excludes monetary gold, notes and coins in circulation, goods transmitted for diplomatic purposes, temporary goods (eg. Empty cylinders and goods on lease for less than one year) and body remains. 2.4.1 Valuation Imports are recorded inclusive of insurance and freight charges (c.i.f.) and domestic exports and re-exports are recorded at the f.o.b. value, ie. exclusive of insurance ad freight charges. Import data are adjusted to the f.o.b. valuation by applying ratios of 10 percent for freight and 2 percent for insurance. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The data are compiled primarily from the customs declaration forms (trade warrants) from the Customs and Excise Department, however information on banana exports is obtained directly from the exporter. These trade warrants are edited, coded and processed by the Statistics Department using the EUROTRACE computerised trade processing system. None None
