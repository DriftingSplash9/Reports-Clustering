 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The overall conceptual framework used to compile the reserve assets statistics is in broad conformity with the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6) and the 2013 International Reserves and Foreign Currency Liquidity Guidelines for a Data Template. Reserve Assets are those external assets that are readily available to and controlled by the monetary authorities for meeting balance of payments financing needs, for intervention in exchange markets to affect the currency exchange rate, and for other related purposes. Other Foreign Currency Assets refer to liquid foreign currency assets of the monetary authorities that are not included in reserve assets, as well as such assets of the central government (excluding social security funds). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Official reserves comprise the following assets denominated in foreign currency: securities, currency and deposits, IMF reserve position, Special Drawing Right (SDRs), gold, and other reserve assets. 2.2.1 Scope Official reserves comprise the following assets denominated in foreign currency: securities, currency and deposits, IMF reserve position, Special Drawing Right (SDRs), gold, and other reserve assets. Scope of the data Institutional coverage: the BCB. Instruments coverage: Official reserves comprise the following assets denominated in foreign currency: securities, currency and deposits, IMF reserve position, Special Drawing Right (SDRs), gold, and other reserve assets. Exceptions to coverage None. Unrecorded activity None. 2.4.1 Valuation Assets denominated in SDR are valuated using the exchange rate given by IMF; gold assets, by the London PM Fixing price; assets denominated in currencies other than the USD are converted into USD according to the parities of the date of the position. Up to October 2000, assets are recorded at their historical purchase cost; from November 2000 on, at market price. Securities are valuated using the market prices. Credits related to Reciprocal Credit Agreement (CCR - included in reserve assets up to August 2012) are valuated using their nominal value.Valuation and Re-valuation Frequency: assets are valuated daily. 2.4.2 Recording basis Records are based on the cash basis, that is, the maturity data of the operation is taken into account for the reserves. The exception to this rule is the interests on securities, which are recorded on an accrual basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Grossing/netting procedures are broadly consistent with BPM6. The BCB records transactions on a gross basis. The inflows and outflows are clearly and separately identified. The valuation changes and the accrual of interests (in the case of securities) are also clearly identified. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The main sources are the BCB's accounting system. IMF provides data on reserve position in the IMF. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data obtained from the accounting system are consistent with the definitions, scope, classifications, and time of recording of the reserve assets statistics. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data collection and processing timetables are adequate to meet timeliness and periodicity for disseminating the reserve assets statistics. The tables and the accounting system, which provide the source data, are available on a daily basis, covering the position of the last business day. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Quality and comprehensiveness of data sources are continually assessed. Reserve asset data are routinely examined for coverage and classification errors. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Compilation procedures minimize processing errors such as coding, editing, and tabulation errors. Data sources are received and processed electronically through IT systems. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Intermediate results are checked across a wide range of data sources. Both the ITRS system and the accounting system, e.g., provide data that are used for crosschecking the consistency of reserve assets fluctuations. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data The behavior of series is routinely crosschecked with related series and indicators. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs As a rule, all significant discrepancies must be investigated. Every month the stock is crosschecked against data obtained on the balance sheet of the BCB. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Unless factual errors are identified, there are no revisions. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses The general policy of the Central Bank of Brazil is that major changes in methodology are announced at the time they are introduced. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Observed data disclosure rules, statistics not routinely disseminated can be made available upon request. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Prompt and knowledgeable service and support are available to users. Contact points for enquiries are available on the metadata section of each statistics series available at the BCB website (www.bcb.gov.br), including e-mail addresses of the staff in charge. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs All Brazilian reserves template data are available to the public on the BCB website at no charge.
