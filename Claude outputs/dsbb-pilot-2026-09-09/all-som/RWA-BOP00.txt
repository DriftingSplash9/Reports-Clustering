 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The compilation of Rwanda’s balance of payments is performed in compliance with the concepts, definitions, and methodology recommended and published in the IMF’s sixth edition of Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual . The preparation of Rwanda’s balance of payments currently is based on the sixth edition. The agency responsible for compiling Rwanda’s balance of payments statistics is the National Bank of Rwanda (the central bank or BNR ). The legal basis for gathering and publishing balance of payments statistics is Article 68 of Law No. 11/97 of July 26, 1997 on the Charter of the BNR. In compiling its balance of payments estimates, the BNR makes use of a number of different sources, in particular the foreign exchange settlement balance, data provided by government agencies, and the annual survey of enterprises, embassies, NGOs and international organizations. The surveys are annually conducted on FDI, services and transfers. The BOP publication is annual. The balance of payments data are published in BPM6 standard format on the BNR website and disseminated to IMF Statistics Department for publication 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization With regard to the classification (analytical presentation) of the balance of payments accounts, the BOP is published on the BNR website as well as the one disseminated to IMF is in BPM6. The BPM6 formats starts from 2010 onwards. 2.2.1 Scope With regard to the classification (analytical presentation) of the balance of payments accounts, the BOP is published on the BNR website as well as the one disseminated to IMF is in BPM6. The BPM6 formats starts from 2010 onwards. Scope of the data Unit Coverage In principle, the recorded data cover all resident units engaging in transactions with nonresidents. Rwanda’s balance of payments records all transactions between residents and nonresidents during a given period. In this regard, the BNR performs the tasks of recording trade data using data provided by customs. Some exports data are collected from exporters using exports contracts and invoices especially for the case of tea and coffee). Some BOP figures include information that is obtained on the basis of operations that pass through the banking system between residents and non-residents and not found elsewhere. Rwanda’s balance of payments is compiled on annual basis in accordance with concepts, definitions, and methodology recommended by the IMF’s 6th edition of the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual . Compilation of the balance of payments statistics involves gathering information from several sources, in particular administrative data sources by different government agencies, (administrative sources, including customs data , data from ministries and other public agencies ), and survey of enterprises, embassies, international organizations, and nongovernmental organizations , International transactions reporting system (which aggregates the daily information on payments to and from abroad made or received through local commercial banks and central bank), data provided by the ministries and public. For details on the sources for each component, please refer to Table A on the Balance of Payments (Sections and V). For some balance of payments components, the collecting entity either does not have or has insufficient information to complete the entries. This is particularly so in the case of in-kind remittances. Adjustments in respect of freight insurance and other services are made on the basis of figures from the customs agencies, which represent FOB values only after the country joined the East African Community Customs Union in July 2009. Since that period the CIF communicated by customs does not include transportation cost from the EAC first port to Kigali. Nevertheless the cost is still paid and the calculation of freight and insurance is made using the shares that are based on international transportation survey that was conducted in 2011 which are applied on FOB values to get the CIF value. The mode of transport helps to calculate the details on transport, insurance and other services related to imports (air, road and sea). Imports from neighboring countries are taken as imported by road transport. 32% by Rwandans and 68% by nonresidents. Transport of goods by air is made by resident and nonresidents carriers. Combined transport (road and sea) is 1/3 by navigation which is 100% foreign and 2/3 by road applying the local and foreign transport shares. The balance of payments publication (BPM6 version) is contained in the BNR ’s website and the cut off point for BMP6 version is 2010. These data are submitted annually to the IMF, which publishes them in its website and in hard copy in its publication International Financial Statistics . In 2010, BNR produced the first report (for 2009) and this activity is to continue for the following years, what will highly require and lead to improving the quality of BOP data published. The BOP data are compiled in USD Millions. Transaction coverage Rwanda’s balance of payments records all transactions between residents and nonresidents of the economy of Rwanda during a given period. In this regard, the BNR performs the tasks of recording trade data using data provided by customs). All border customs operates online and data submitted to BNR includes all transactions captured by the system. Information on the operations which pass through the banking ) is captured in its entirety, but sometimes very hard to be taken as such in the BOP due to the problem of the correct purpose of some operations. This is especially why the BNR with partner institutions such as NISR have in recent past started to rely more on surveys. Since 2010, a survey on foreign assets and liabilities is conducted and results are used in compilation of BOP. The survey is conducted with a year lag but with intention of conducting it on quarterly basis. Secondary income and capital transfers are sourced from administrative data sources. The distinction between grants and loans is now clear especially on government side. Within the BNR, there is a unit in charge of External private debt that captures the movements on disbursement and repayments on private external borrowing. Geographic coverage Rwanda’s balance of payments covers the entire national economic territory, as well as its diplomatic missions abroad. Exceptions to coverage Exceptions to coverage include goods consigned to military bases, consignment of goods in direct transit, goods imported by diplomatic corps, commercial samples, returned merchandise and returned packing material Unrecorded activity From 1993 to the present, Rwanda’s balance of payments does not record the effects of migrants’ activities. It has been difficult, for example, to gauge the effects of the return of former Rwandan refugees who came back to the country in 1994, 1995, 1996 and thereafter, since they were not registered by customs upon their return. However, the assets repatriated by these Rwandan nationals were of sufficient magnitude to have a significant impact on the country’s balance of payments. Similarly, the BNR has not been able to obtain data on the supplies and equipment left behind at no charge by the United Nations Mission for Rwanda at the end of its mandate. Illegal transactions are not covered. Under the BPM6, migrant transfers have been reclassified to international investment position in other changes in volume. 2.4.1 Valuation Transactions are valued at market prices. Exports are valued in f.o.b. (free-on-board) terms, and imports on a c.i.f. (cost, insurance, freight) basis, and converted into f.o.b. imports at the point of shipment by subtracting the related freight, insurance and other fees identified on the basis of customs documentation. For exports from Rwanda, products such as tea and coffee are transported to the port of lading (Mombassa, Kenya), and the sales contracts for the most part are written f.o.b. Mombasa. Related charges are calculated based on the information obtained from National Agricultural and Export Development Board (NAEB) and export companies especially coffee and tea exporters. 2.4.2 Recording basis Balance of payments operations are recorded at the time of the transactions, except in the case of revenue wherein recording is done when proceeds are received or paid. The balance of payments statistics are normally expressed in the national currency (Rwanda franc), since they are used together with other national statistics. However, to enable international organizations (such as the IMF, the World Bank, the AfDB, etc.) to compare the data from Rwanda with those of other countries, the BNR uses the U.S. dollar, a currency used in international transactions. The data are first compiled in millions of U.S. dollars at the transaction exchange rate, and then converted into Rwanda francs on the basis of an annual average exchange rate (simple average). Borrowing and lending are expressed in nominal terms, while for direct investment, assets are recorded at their historical value (i.e., at their purchase cost) and liabilities at their nominal value. Income is recorded at the time of payment. Arrears are recorded at the time they are identified. The double entry accounting system is used, in which each transaction is represented by two entries in the same amount, one on the debit side and the other on the credit side. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The current account of the balance of payments are recorded on gross basis; both credit and debit entries are recorded on gross basis but net the balances by subtracting debits from credits. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs In compiling its balance of payments estimates, the BNR makes use of a number of sources, in particular the administrative data provided by different government agencies, surveys on different service categories, ITRS and the enterprises survey particularly the foreign private capital survey, surveys on embassies, NGOs and international organizations. The FPC survey is conducted annually in collaboration with Rwanda Development Board, National Institute of Statistics and Private Sector Federation in respect of the Memorandum of Understanding signed between the four institutions in 2009 (BNR-RDB-NISR-PSFR). The MOU is renewed every after 3years, The BOP publication is annual but with intentions of doing quarterly BOP which will ultimately change the frequency of most surveys. For the trade balance, the BNR uses customs data after some treatments excluding services. For some exports (coffee and tea) BNR uses contracts and invoices values. A number of adjustments are made by BNR to complete customs data (imports of electricity, estimates of some border transactions, informal cross border trade survey, goods procured in ports by carriers and imports via post offices). This results in differences between the BNR and Customs data. All border customs operate online using ASCYUDA ++ and data submitted to BNR includes all transactions captured by the system. The international transactions reporting system is compiled on the basis of information gathered by the BNR’s Statistics Department on the daily bank declarations of BNR, commercial banks and forex bureaus’ exchange operations. Additional surveys are conducted to gather information not collected by the banking system and to correct for a number of methodological errors in the compilation of balance of payments statistics; this is particularly so with respect to compliance with the residency criterion, which is often not taken into account in compiling the settlement balance. The variables captured by means of these surveys are as follows: a. Goods Data on trade in goods / merchandise trade is obtained from the customs captured through the automated customs data ASCYUDA++ by the customs statistics services. Official border posts are automated and the information is captured and immediately transmitted on line. Exports valued at FOB Kigali and imports are valued as CIF at the first point of entry within EAC. Exports are adjusted to include informal cross border trade data, goods procured in ports by carriers, exports of electricity to neighboring countries Imports are also adjusted by deducting insurance and freight at CIF value to arrive at FOB value. The deducted components are reclassified as transport services and insurance and pension services. A number of other adjustments are made by BNR and these include informal cross border imports, imports of electricity and goods procured in ports by carriers. b. Services Transport services include passenger and freight subcomponents and other transport, each of these categories is broken down by the mode of transport such as air, see and road. Passenger transportation services include transportation of nonresidents by resident road transport companies, sea transport companies as well as national airlines or transportation of residents by nonresident transportation companies. Freight transportation services include transportation services by residents to nonresident merchandise or transportation services by nonresident to resident merchandise by resident and nonresident road transport companies, sea transport services and resident and nonresident carriers. Data are collected after classification of imports by port of entry (airport and physical borders). The calculation of the balance of payments estimates takes into account the fact that Rwandan resident transporters have a share of 38 percent of road transport cost and the nonresidents have 62 percent. The BNR regularly updates this ratio to estimate the amount of freight earnings by residents and nonresidents. Insurance and pension services: the data on insurance and pension services obtained by addressing a questionnaire to 12 local insurance companies and two private pension funds. The variables of interest are premiums, claims and insurance technical reserves. Travel services: travel data is jointly gathered on both credit and debit side by National Institute of statistics of Rwanda, National Bank of Rwanda and Rwanda Development Board, the Tourism and Parks Office through travel expenditure survey which gives average expenditure per day and average length of stay, these two components are grossed by the volume on arrivals and departures from Directorate General of immigration and migration. Communications, computer, and information services: the survey is focused on enterprises offering these services to nonresidents or receiving such services from nonresidents. The data on telecommunication, computer and information is collected on a quarterly basis where BNR sends questionnaires to enterprise offering these kinds of services to fill the questionnaire and the output is recorded in BOP under that category of service Other business services formerly called technical services in BPM5 under the heading “miscellaneous business, professional, and technical services”: the survey targets the major enterprises deemed likely to provide or receive these kinds of services. For this particular functional category of service, the data is obtained from different sources ; data on research and development, consulting , professional management consulting services and technical, trade related and other business services are obtained from international transactions reporting system while rental of equipment without crew is obtained from survey on resident and nonresident carriers Government, n.i.e. this information is collected primarily through surveys. Amounts entered as credits cover the services relating to s technical assistance and the expenditure of foreign diplomatic missions and international organizations in Rwanda as well as funds from the peace keeping missions (PKOs). Amounts entered as debits relate to the expenditure abroad of Rwandese diplomatic personnel and missions. c. Primary Income The compensation of nonresident workers (e.g., local personnel working in Rwandese embassies abroad) is estimated on the basis of the exchange records and the results of surveys of the Rwanda Social Fund ( Rwanda social security fund RSSB ). The BNR also draws data on compensation paid to Rwandese residents employed by foreign embassies, international organizations, and other international assistance agencies from the exchange records and the survey results of the CSR and other agencies. Compensation of employees: the survey covers entities that may employ nonresident foreign workers (taking the one-year criterion into account), in particular direct investment firms. Direct investment. The BNR derives the data on income on direct investment from the foreign private capital survey. The amounts entered as direct investment assets relate to data on Rwandese investments abroad while foreign direct investment liabilities relate to data on foreign companies investment in Rwanda. The information on Rwandese investments abroad, foreign companies investments in Rwanda comes with a big lag Portfolio investment. The NBR compiles data on income from portfolio investment on the basis of the foreign private capital survey results Other investment. The BNR also compiles the data on income from other investment on the basis of the foreign private capital survey results as well as administrative records. Especially from external debt unit at the ministry of finance and BNR’s bank supervision Department. BNR compiles external private debts by un related parties. Direct investment income: the survey is addressed principally to direct investment firms. The direct investment income composed of Dividends and reinvested earnings is obtained from the foreign private capital survey results d. Capital and financial operations Data on the capital account is obtained from Ministry of Finance and Economic Planning particularly capital transfers while the financial account components are obtained from different sources: direct investment, portfolio investment and other investment are obtained from the foreign capital survey results that is conducted every year. Currency and deposits are obtained from the central bank’s monetary survey and Reserves are obtained from the cash flow plan. External private borrowing is obtained from foreign private capital survey and the list of companies is compiled by the bank supervision Department at the bank. A specific survey instrument is conceived and addressed on the concerned companies A list of entities or enterprises to be surveyed (or sampled) has been prepared using various sources: The list of companies is updated each year using list of new registered foreign companies by Rwanda Development board (RDB). This done every time there is plan to conduct a new survey or if there is change in reporting requirements. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques 1. CURRENT ACCOUNT GOODS The data on exports of Rwanda’s two primary export commodities (coffee and tea) are compiled on the basis of sales contracts and invoices. Exports of other products are evaluated on the basis of customs data. Exports are valued f.o.b. border or f.o.b. airport. Moreover, in order to cover all export transactions between residents and nonresidents, the BNR adds the following to the exports surveyed: trade of electricity (source: EWSA) and parcel post exports (source: National Post Office ( ONP ) and its competitors). Estimates of merchandise imports are compiled on the basis of declarations of entry for consumption. However, the customs statistics do not cover certain official imports, in particular imports of electricity and imports via parcel post. Information on these imports is gathered separately from the entities with access to the relevant information. Grants in kind are included in the customs figures to the extent that they have been declared. SERVICES Transportation Passengers: Transportation services covering the travel of nonresident passengers by national air carriers and of resident passengers by foreign air carriers. In its initial stage, the data are based on the settlement balance (airfare tickets and other services to passengers). In the balance of payments, when the data become available, the figures from the settlement balance are replaced by those reported by resident carriers and foreign air carriers through the questionnaires they receive during survey. Freight: According to the system of imports transport, using the figures collected by customs office, BNR calculates the air, navigation and road transport services. The calculation of the figure to be included in BOP take into consideration the fact that Rwandan resident transporters have a share of 32% of road transport cost. Nonresidents have 68%. For Rwandan exports of tea and coffee, the commodities are shipped to the ports of lading (Mombassa and Dar Es Salaam) and sold f.o.b. Shipping is provided by nonresident and resident carriers, and the freight costs are paid by the foreign importers. Each exporting company specifies the carrier of the product (resident or non-resident or a percentage of each). A breakdown is made between transport, insurance and other costs. Other transportation services (port and airport services): transportation services cover the services rendered to foreign air carriers by the Rwanda Civil Aviation Authority (credit) and those provided to Rwandan air carriers abroad (debit) in respect of landings, take-offs, maintenance, and navigation. These data are got by the results of the survey of national and foreign air carriers and the Rwanda Civil Aviation Authority. Fueling and provisioning of aircraft is classified under the item “goods procured in ports by carriers.” Travel The amounts on the credit side under the travel item are provided by RDB for tourism travel and banks settlement balance, supplemented by information provided by the Ministry of Education. For the items on the debit side, the information on official missions abroad and on business travel are derived from the tables of foreign exchange operations of the banks, the BNR , and the Ministry of Education. Nevertheless, the amount given by banks exchange operations is not used as such on debit side. Due to the problem of correct motif of the operations, a large amount classified as travel may have been used for imports. Other services Insurance: At present, during the annual survey, a specific questionnaire requiring this information is addressed to all insurance companies. The variables of interest are premiums, claims and insurance technical reserves. Communications services: during a quarterly survey, a specific questionnaire requiring this information is addressed to all telecommunication companies. Other business services : the amounts recorded for other services include commissions, management fees and representatives’ commissions, professional charges, equipment rental, and film rentals without crew. All these data are derived from the international transactions reporting system (ITRS) and from surveys on resident and nonresident carriers especially on rentals on transport without crew. Government services n.i.e.: the data are established on the basis of the information extracted different administrative sources especially Ministry of Finance and Economic Planning. The amounts posted to the debit side relate to the expenditure of Rwandan diplomatic personnel and Rwandan diplomatic missions abroad. The amounts recorded on the credit side include the expenditure of foreign diplomatic missions, international organizations in Rwanda, and funds from peace keeping operations as well as units related to aid programs (technical assistance costs received in the form of aid). PRIMARY INCOME Compensation of employees The amounts recorded on the credit side are from the results of the survey of the Rwanda Social Security Fund ( CSR ) and enterprises. These amounts reflect the compensation of residents working for enterprises abroad, and the compensation of the local staff of foreign embassies and international institutions. The amounts recorded on the debit side are also derived from the settlement balance and are supplemented by information from the survey of the CSR and enterprises. These data also concern the compensation of the local staff of Rwanda's embassies abroad, information that is provided by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Cooperation. Investment income Direct investment income The data on direct investment income are derived from the foreign private capital survey. The amounts reported on the debit side relate only to the data on the dividends and distributed profits of branches. Other investment income The data on other investment income are also established on the foreign private capital survey results and information provided by the bank supervision Department of BNR (in particular, private external borrowing ) as well External Debt Unit of the Ministry of Finance and Economic Planning for the public external borrowing. SECONDARY INCOME General government The data are compiled on the basis of information provided by the Ministry of Finance and Economic Planning (MINECOFIN). They cover grants in cash and in kind received for development, humanitarian aid, and central government staff training (credit). They also cover Rwanda’s contributions to the administrative budgets of international organizations (debit). Other sectors The data (including those on workers’ remittances), non-governmental organizations and churches and associations are compiled on the international transactions reporting system. CAPITAL ACCOUNT AND FINANCIAL OPERATIONS ACCOUNT CAPITAL ACCOUNT Capital account records estimates on the gross acquisition/disposal of non-produced financial assets, debt forgiveness and project grants. The recording follows the BPM6 sectoral breakdown Capital Transfers General government The data on capital transfers include debt forgiveness, as well as capital grants in kind and in cash (e.g., grants for financing the Public Investment Program (PIP)). They are compiled on the basis of information provided by Ministry of Finance and Economic planning Other sectors The data on the capital transfers of the other sectors include the transfers in kind and in cash for housing construction, water supply, and other projects carried out by nongovernmental organizations (NGOs) in the economic territory of Rwanda. They are compiled on the basis of information provided by the international organizations as well as by NGOs. FINANCIAL ACCOUNT In the financial account, the value of current financial transactions between residents and non-residents. The entries are recorded with headings net acquisition of assets and net incurrencies of liabilities for each of the components such as Direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives and other investment. The estimates are derived from foreign private capital survey , monetary survey as well as administrative data on public external borrowing from MINECOFIN Direct investment The data are derived from the annual foreign private capital survey results. Other investment: other investment is obtained from the foreign private capital survey and some administrative data on both public and private external borrowing from MINECOFIN and BNR Monetary authorities The data is sourced from the BNR ’s monetary survey. The data on the liabilities of the monetary authorities relate to changes in the accounts held by foreign official entities (such as the IMF). General government The information under this heading is obtained from the Public Debt Division of the Ministry of Finance and Economic Planning. This heading currently covers project support lending (support for the Public Investment Program), loans to support the balance of payments, and subscriptions to the capital of non-monetary international organizations. Banks The data relate to changes in the foreign assets and liabilities of the commercial banks. They are derived from the monetary survey tables prepared by the Monetary Statistics Division in the BNR Statistics Department. Other sectors The information under this heading is obtained from the Bank Supervision Department of the BNR . This heading currently covers private residents borrowing from nonresident. The other investment heading covers also data on trade-related credit and the deposits of nonresidents with banks. The figures are extracted from Bank Supervision Department of the BNR and the foreign exchange operations statements.
