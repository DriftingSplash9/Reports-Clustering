 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions National accounts of Botswana are based on the concepts and definitions of the 1993 System of National Accounts (1993 SNA). In estimating the Gross Domestic Product (GDP), the production and expenditure approaches are used. A complete set of accounts is compiled using enterprise and establishment data. Production accounts are compiled for each industry. Income and outlay accounts and capital-finance accounts for the full set of institutional sectors were last compiled in 2000/01. Annual estimates: Production approach: The production approach is used to estimates the following aggregates for almost all the industries; Gross output, intermediate consumption, value added and GDP at current and constant 2006 prices. Expenditure approach: GDP is also calculated using the expenditure approach and the following are components compiled and published; Government and private final consumption expenditure; Gross capital formation; Exports and imports of goods and services at current and constant2006 prices. Quarterly estimates: The aggregates disseminated quarterly are: Gross Output, Intermediate Consumption, Value added by type of economic activity, Gross Fixed Capital Formation and Final consumption (Private and; Government), Exports and Imports at current and constant (2006) prices. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization For all national accounts series, economic activities have been classified according to Botswana Standard Industrial Classification (BSIC3). In the national accounts statistics, Classification Of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP) is used to classify the final consumption expenditure of households which is also classified according to marketed services and non-marketed services and Non-Profit Institutions Serving Household (NPISH). Government Finance statistics is compiled according to Government Finance Statistics (GFS) manual. GFS 2001 manual is the one currently used. Classification of Functions of Government (COFOG) is used to classify government finance statistics. The Standard International Trade Classification (SITC) is used to aggregate import and export data from the detailed Harmonized System (HS). Scope of the data National Accounts cover all units residing in Botswana and their transactions with the rest of the world. All transactions are covered in principle except for illegal transaction like drug trafficking. Transactions are recorded on an accrual basis, except for government accounts, which are on a cash basis. Exceptions to coverage No estimates are made for illegal transaction such as drug trafficking. Benchmark information based on the Informal Sector Survey of 2007 and the 2009/10 Botswana Core Welfare Indicator Survey (BCWIS). Annual agriculture survey and Annual Economic Survey (AES) have been used to extrapolate the data from one year to the other. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: Output is recorded at basic prices while intermediate consumption; final consumption expenditure and Gross Fixed Capital Formation are recorded at purchaser's price. Imports and exports are recorded at fob (free on board). 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: Transactions are recorded on an accrual basis except for government accounts, which are on a cash basis. Recording period: National accounts follow calendar year. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Annual data are compiled from the Annual Economic Survey (AES) and administrative records. The surveys are on an enterprise/establishment basis. Data on production accounts, income and outlay accounts and capital finance accounts for establishments operating in Botswana are obtained from AES. Imports and exports of goods and services are obtained from Balance of Payments. Government accounts are sourced from the government financial statement and expenditure accounts returns from the Ministry of Finance and Development Planning. AES AES covers all the Economic activities except General Government which is sourced from Government accounts. A Sample of establishment/enterprise is taken from the Enterprise and Establishment Register (EER). The EER is a register, which keeps a list of enterprises and establishments in Botswana. The major sources of the EER are the registrar of companies, the registrar of societies, the department of Road Transport and Safety and the Commercial Affairs units of the various cities, towns and districts councils, Botswana Unified Revenue Services, Industrial affairs. The sample selection for the AES is based on employment size. Large establishment (employees >=50), multi-establishment and public enterprises are fully enumerated. Establishments with less than 50 employees are sampled. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Concepts and definitions used are according to System of National Accounts (SNA) 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Quarterly national accounts gross domestic product estimates are released with a time lag of 90 days; while annual accounts at 9-12 months 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Government financial year is from April to March, while national accounts year follows the calendar. Before computing government final consumption, gross capital formation and intermediate consumption for government, government accounts are adjusted to calendar year. Gross capital formation is usually derived from the Annual Economic Survey (AES), however AES suffers from poor response. Estimates from respondent companies are weighted by a certain factor to account for the non-respondents. Another way of overcoming this problem is to use the administrative records of the Customs Department at Botswana revenue Service (BURS) and establish the amount of imported plant, equipment and construction materials and come up with a more reliable estimate of total investment in the economy. Exports and imports of goods and services are obtained from Balance of Payments section at the Central Bank. Because of these other sources and variation in reference period, an adjustment is made to align these to calendar year. Imports of goods are adjusted for duty content, in accordance with the Southern African Customs Union Agreement (SACU). There is a concern that value of imports of goods suffer from under-estimation due to under-declaration of goods at custom frontiers to avoid taxes. However, no attempt is made to adjust for under-declaration since the degree of under-declaration is unknown. Private Final Expenditure consists of private non-profit services, household marketed and household non-marketed. The household marketed part is estimated directly from Household and Income Expenditure Survey (HIES). In most cases, consumption of some commodities like beer, alcohol and cigarettes were under reported, therefore an upward adjustment was undertaken to account for the under reporting. The non-marketed part is difficult to deal with. A value is normally imputed for agricultural products, which are produced by households for their own consumption. The Agricultural Statistics Unit provides information on quantity while prices are computed based on prices offered by the Botswana Agricultural Marketing Board. HIES does not cover the institutionalized population, i.e. army barracks, prisons, hospital etc. A value of consumption expenditure is imputed on the basis of the proportion of the institutionalized population to the total population. Rentals for owner-occupied dwellings are also imputed based on the stock of houses in the economy. In urban areas, the rental value of owner-occupied dwellings is imputed based on the similar housing structures, which are rented out. CONSTANT PRICES: Method: The "double deflation" method is adopted, so that for each industry one deflator for production and another for the intermediate consumption are computed separately, and the value added at constant prices is derived as the difference between the two. Deflation of intermediate consumption has been done at the commodity level. The breakdown of each commodity is determined from the results of the Annual Economic Survey. Appropriate price indices from South Africa are used or, where possible, Botswana prices information for each commodity group. The different categories of end use, including household final expenditure, are deflated separately. The household final consumption of marketed commodities is derived at current prices from HIES and extrapolated. The constant price estimate is obtained by deflating current price figures by the national consumer price index. The procedure results in a statistical discrepancy between GDP as the sum of each industry's value added at constant prices, and that obtained by summing all final uses less net import. The deflation of external trade (exports and imports) are deflated by the consumer price index. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Seasonal Adjustments: The Quarterly GDP estimates are unadjusted for seasonal variations. Benchmarking: Benchmarking is done in the context of quarterly estimates using the statistical package based on Denton 4 least squares minimization technique. The technique is used to combine quarterly data with annual data for the same target variable into consistent time series. Re-basing: Gross Domestic products estimates are at constant 2006 prices None
