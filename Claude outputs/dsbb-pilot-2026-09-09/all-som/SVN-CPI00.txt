 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Definition: The indices measure monthly changes in the level of retail prices of goods and services that individual residential households purchase for final consumption. Consumer Price Indices are compiled according to CPI/HICP Manual. The CPI has been the official measure of the inflation in Slovenia since January 1, 1998. (Prior to that date the Retail Price Index [RPI] was the official inflation measure.) 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: Since the beginning of 2017 the European Classification of Individual Consumption according to Purpose (ECOICOP) at 5-digit level is used (it is consistent with the UN version at 4 digits COICOP (HICP version) classification). The indices using COICOP - version HICP are available also from the beginning of 1997, however they were the results of pre-classification done still on the draft version of the mentioned classification. Starting in 2026, the new COICOP 2018 (or ECOICOP ver. 2) classification will be implemented. Scope of the data Population coverage : The indices cover all individual residential households without reference to the number of household members. Geographical coverage : The index covers the entire Slovenian economy. Item/product coverage : The basket for calculating CPI contains 785 representative goods and services (elementary aggregates), divided into various sub-headings of ECOICOP (the European Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP) at 5-digit level. Units described by the published data are representative products (goods and services) or elementary aggregates (EA). Selected goods and services represent the most important share in the total consumption of an average consumer. Their price movement also best reflects the price movement for similar goods or services. Traditional price collection: Prices of the representative non-food goods and services (division from 03 to 12 according toECOICOP classification) are collected by price collectors in Koper, Ljubljana, Maribor and Novo mesto. Some prices are also collected in other places in Slovenia and centrally, mostly by phone, via the Internet, by scanner data and from other databases of individual sellers of goods and services. The total number of collection points is around 1,350. A collection point is a business entity where goods and services are sold to potential customers. It can be a store, a market place, a crafts workshop, a service organisation, a retailer's website, etc. In each town on average five prices per non-food product or service are collected, which is about 11,000 prices per month. More than a third of the prices of goods and services are collected centrally (ECOICOP 03 – 12); i.e. newspapers and periodicals, post and telecommunication services, package holidays, accommodation services, electricity,fuel prices, prices for some medicaments and vehicles, bank services, municipal services, etc. Web scraping: For computer equipment products, we use prices obtained through web scrapping. About 25,000 prices are used monthly to calculate the price index. Data from retailers' databases (scanner data): for products from divisions 01 (Food and non-alcoholic beverages) and 02 (Alcoholic beverages and tobacco) by ECOICOP classification we use prices from supermarket scanner data. Monthly around 70,000 prices are used for the price index calculation. We also receive scanner data from pharmacies for some pharmaceutical products. 2.4.1 Valuation Types of prices (ECOICOP 03-12): Prices at which retail sales outlets and other enterprises sell their products or charge their services to users. At the same time these are final prices, at which users pay for purchased goods and services. For ECOICOP 01-02, types of prices are unit value prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis Prices of manufactured products and services are observed once a month in 3 to 10 trading enterprises. The prices are inclusive of taxes, and are collected from the 1st to the 25th day of the month. As regard Scanner data, prices of the first two weeks of a month enter the index production. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Sources of weights: Weights for calculating the index in a certain year are based on expenditure data of individual households from household budget surveys (HBS). The latest HBS data are for 2022. Data on consumption are continuously monitored, which enables merging of samples of several years according to the new classification (ECOICOP version HBS) and the new method, i.e. with the help of diaries and direct interviewing. These data are supplemented and verified with other statistical and non-statistical sources. Product/item selection: Items are selected on the basis of HBS data, price collectors information, retail trade statistics, producers' and wholesaler' information, etc. Items are selected judgmentally by price statistics staff. By this selection priorities are given to those with most important share in total consumption and to those whose changes of prices reflect best the changes of prices of related products. Price collectors within the limitation of the items' specifications select the varieties of items. Scanner data: within predefined elementary aggregates, items are selected according to supermarkets turnover. Establishment/outlet selection: Geographical localities have been selected in the past according to their geographical position and number of inhabitants. The outlets in each location are first selected on the basis of turnover data from retail trade statistic and then further supplemented on the basis of price collectors' information and proposals.The main criteria for selection of outlets in each locality are the coverage of the available shopping areas within each location and the representation of different types of outlets. The number of outlets sampled varies by size of locality and by the type of item being priced. Once a year, usually in November and December, the sample of outlets is reviewed and updated. The sample covers different types of outlets from crafts undertaking, traditional shops, single-line retail shops, big shopping centres, restaurants and hotels, as well as Internet shops and catalogues. Prices for used cars, airplane tickets and some books are collected via Internet shops, and prices for profit actual rentals for housing are provided in electronic form. Scanner data are obtained from the largest supermarket chains (for food, beverages ad tobacco), all of them have a single pricing policy at the national level. Prices of some pharmaceutical products are also obtained by scanner data. Web scraping is the source for monitoring Information processing equipment prices (PSs and alike) and package holidays. Retail prices of goods and services are collected in four big cities. Price collecting is organised by the Interviewing of People and Households Section (prices are collected by interviewers). Price collectors collect prices by tablet computer (software based on Windows universal application). These data are sent by the http protocol to the SQL server at the central office. Furthermore, scanner data are obtained from some supermarket chains (mainly covering food and non-alcoholic beverages products, alcoholic beverages household maintenance products, personal hygiene products, pets related products etc.), and pharmacies, some data are provided in electronic format and some are web-scraped. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Product/item specification: The list of representative items include detailed description and measurement characteristics of goods and services, some specifications are more tight and include brand and model (e.g. technical products) other are more loose. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Timing of price observation: For the purpose of organising the observation time in individual months and to cover the whole month, items are classified into the following related groups for which prices are observed in a certain period of the month: • Food, beverage and tobacco (the first two weeks in a month, scanner data) • Non-food products (between 1st and 15th of the month) • Services (between 1st and 25th of the month) • Fuels (between 1st and 25th of the month). 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Treatment of missing prices: Prices for items which are temporarily not available are imputed according to the diffrence of price developments within an elementary aggregate. Permanently not available items are usually replaced by most similar and representative items. For Ecoicop 01 and 02, GTINs are being imputed for 14 months; prices are imputed for the difference of price developments within an elementary aggregate. Selection of replacements: An item that is in constant absence is replaced with comparable or similar and representative item in the same outlet or with comparable/similar item in other similar outlet. Replacement is done by the price collector who monitors the prices of the missing product. Quality adjustments: Annual updating of the list of products or their specifications is used as the main method to handle changes in the quality of products. In December of each year, the products that become important are added or used as replacements for products that are no longer representative. Correction of prices due to quality changes is also done during the year. Price adjustments due to quality changes during a year, are most frequently made for cars, major household appliances, clothing and technical products. Following methods are used: option pricing (new cars ­– as required); direct price comparison (e.g. clothing – as required); bridge overlap (e.g. technical products, household appliances – as required); package size adjustment (e.g. medicaments); judgmental QA (e.g. cars – as required); mixed approaches – as required. Introducing new products: New items are introduced when their sales become significant or it is judged that they are gaining on significancy. Mostly this is done once per year in December of each year. Main sources for identifying new products are information from price collectors, from media etc. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures - Period of current weights: Weights for calculating the index in a certain year are based on expenditure from household budget surveys as a main data source. These data are supplemented and verified with other statistical and non-statistical sources. In 2025 weights are based on the average of expenditure data for consumer goods from HBS survey for 2022 recalculated (indexed) to the prices of December 2024, which is also the index base month. We also took into account major changes in HICP weights, which were made on the basis of national accounts data on household expenditure for 2023. - Frequency of weight updating: Since 2000 they are revised every year. - Indices: Traditionally collected data: after all prices are collected in a particular month, we calculate average prices and indices. The average price for each individual non-food product or service in the locality is calculated from prices collected in all places of observation in that locality with geometric mean. The average national price of each non-food product or service is calculated with weighted arithmetic mean from previously calculated average prices in the locality. From average national prices in each current and base month (December of the previous year) we calculate individual index for each elementary aggregate. Data from retailers' databases (scanner data ) for food, beverages and tobacco: an average unit value price for an item at a GTIN code per individual retailer is calculated by dividing the turnover per item by quantities sold per item. From unit value prices at a GTIN code, that compose an EA, we calculate a geometric mean of price relatives per individual retailer. Monthly price of an item is compared to the price of that same item in a previous month at a GTIN code. We use the unweighted Jevons formula. For every EA per retailer we calculate a chain-linked index. From the monthly chain-linked EA indices per individual retailer we calculate an indiviudal index per elementary aggregate with weighted arithmetic mean. Weights of retailers represent the market share (turnover) of individual retailer per individual EA. Aggregation of indices: from individual indices we calculate with weighted arithmetic mean aggregate indices, i.e. indices of groups (by ECOICOP) and the total price index. Each aggregate index (December of the previous year = 100) calculated in this way and all other indices derived from this index and calculated with weights of the weight base period and with the same coverage of products are Laspeyres' indices of fixed type. Indices calculated on the basis of weights from various periods and with different coverage of products are chain indices. Alignment of weight and price base: Weights from the reference weighting period are annually harmonised with the prices of December of each year and normalized to 10000. December of each year is thus the price reference period, one of the index reference periods, and the linking month for the calculation of other indices. - Linking updated indices: Index series, starting in January of each year are calculated using new weights. They are linked with index series of the previous years, which used old weights by chaining of indices, using December as the linking month. For this reason two set of prices are collected in each December. - Reference period: The long term reference period was the average of 1992 and then also the average of 2000. In 2006 also the new long term reference period was introduced (the average of 2005 = 100). Since 2016 indices have been linked through new index reference period year 2015 (average 2015 = 100). All indices are now derived and calculated through average 2015. Starting in 2026, we will switch to the new index reference period year (average 2025=100). - Otherwise, in our practice, all price changes are presented in the form of indices and growth rates. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Verification of prices: First controls are built into the tablet application indicating bigger discrepancies in monthly price change of particular item. In addition every price change must be explained with the special codes defined in the tablet application. Data can be exported to MS Excel where prices can be checked and compared in several ways. Later, individual checkings are done in the office also (telephone contacts). In the case of scanner data, the process fails (reports an error) at the entrance in case data arrive in a different standard, structure or date to which data relate. SURS's application META SOP (SAS) is designed for logical controls of scanner data and data management. It removes duplicates, zero values, detects outliers, etc. Verification of processing: The process of data entry and first checking of prices is performed on tablets run on Windows universal application (for tablet computer) developed with MS Visual Studio, MS Excel, MS Access, META SOP (SAS) and SAS. Further on PC with Excel 2010 prices can be checked and the causes and reasons for price changes can be analysed. Calculation of indices is performed on a PC with SAS. All bases of prices and of indices are standardized in relation base of ORACLE system.This phase of processing is checked before publishing the indices manually. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request All requested data and methodological explanations can be sent by e-mail or mail.
