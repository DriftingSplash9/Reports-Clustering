 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The Index of Domestic Production is a quarterly indicator showing changes in the volume of production over time of resident manufacturing establishments at the national level. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The Index of Domestic Production follows the Trinidad and Tobago System of Industrial Classification (TTSIC) which is essentially a modified version of the United Nations International System of Industrial Classification (ISIC). 2.2.1 Scope The Index of Domestic Production follows the Trinidad and Tobago System of Industrial Classification (TTSIC) which is essentially a modified version of the United Nations International System of Industrial Classification (ISIC). Scope of the data The Index of Domestic Production is a sample survey of 151 establishments selected from the 1995 register of business establishments. The survey collects data on the revenues, employment, hours worked, and earnings as well as the quantities and values of the main products produced and sold for each of the establishments in the sample. 2.4.1 Valuation Calculate the Index from the Value of Goods Produced or Sold. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs A stratified random sample of one hundred and fifty-one (151) firms from the Annual Register of Establishments employing 10 or more persons across various industries 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The index is presented on a 1995 weight and reference base, using the Laspeyres formula. The quantities of the products within industries are weighted with their respective 1995 average unit values and the industry estimates are then aggregated using the 1995 value added weights derived from the annual national accounts estimates. Calculate estimates for non-response by using a year on year ratio of similar quarters to move the figure received for the relevant quarter in the previous year to obtain the relevant quarter in the current year or on the growth in the current quarter of production for similar firms for which a response was available.
