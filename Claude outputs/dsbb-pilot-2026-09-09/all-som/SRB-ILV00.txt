 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Gross international reserves comprise external assets that are readily available to and controlled by the National Bank of Serbia (NBS) for direct financing of payments imbalances, for indirectly regulating the magnitude of these imbalances through foreign exchange markets, for smoothing excessive daily fluctuations of the EUR/RSD exchange rate, and/or for other purposes. Gross international reserve assets are the reserve holdings of the NBS. The concept and classification applied by the NBS in the presentation of data are compiled on the basis of definitions contained in the fifth edition of the IMF`s Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) and recorded in accordance with the Guidelines for a Data Template on International Reserves and Foreign Currency Liquidity. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification is in accordance with European System of Accounts (ESA 2010). Instruments included within the definition of gross international reserves are classified into: Gold – the value of gold in the Treasury of the NBS and gold held abroad. Special Drawing Rights (SDR) – the balance on the SDR account with the IMF Reserve Position in the IMF (currently none). Foreign currency cash holdings in the Treasury of the head office of the NBS and in its branches. Foreign exchange held in current and deposit accounts with banks abroad. 2.2.1 Scope The classification is in accordance with European System of Accounts (ESA 2010). Instruments included within the definition of gross international reserves are classified into: Gold – the value of gold in the Treasury of the NBS and gold held abroad. Special Drawing Rights (SDR) – the balance on the SDR account with the IMF Reserve Position in the IMF (currently none). Foreign currency cash holdings in the Treasury of the head office of the NBS and in its branches. Foreign exchange held in current and deposit accounts with banks abroad. Scope of the data Gross international reserves include only assets held by the NBS. All official reserve assets held as deposits in foreign exchange, securities, gold and cash are readily available. 2.4.1 Valuation All reserve assets are recorded at market prices for the reporting day using the following sources: London Bullion Market – afternoon fixing price for gold valuation; IMF basket valuation for SDR holdings; Bloomberg-closing bid prices for foreign securities; Official middle exchange rates set forth by the National Bank of Serbia for the valuation of foreign currencies. 2.4.2 Recording basis Data are derived from the NBS balance sheet which is based on accrual accounting. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Data are compiled on a gross basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The data source for NBS gross international reserves is the balance sheet data of the NBS. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Source data are received from the NBS’s Accounting and Financing Department not later than 14 days after the end of the reference month. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Automated data validation procedures are employed to check the internal consistency of reported data. In addition, source data are also reviewed against those of previous periods to identify out-of-trend items. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques No statistical adjustments are made to the data. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures No further adjustments are made. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Balance sheet data are reconciled with operational data. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Official reserve assets data compilers identify statistical discrepancies and in collaboration with various NBS units investigate their causes. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Procedures are in place to investigate large and unexplained movements to detect possible scope, accounting, classification and sectorization errors.
