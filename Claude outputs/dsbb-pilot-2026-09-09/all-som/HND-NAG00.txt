 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The National Accounts of Honduras are compiled by the National Accounts Section (SCN) of the Office of the Deputy Manager for Economic Research of the Central Bank of Honduras (BCH). The national accounts are compiled using methodology of the UN System of National Accounts, rev. III of 1968, maintaining some components from the previous revision. The base year for the national accounts is 1978. Revision IV of the System of National Accounts ( SNA93) is currently being implemented, using the computerized compilation methodology designed by the United Nations Statistics Division under the coordination of an international consultant. The new national accounts series, using 2000 as the reference year, will be published at end-2005. In the current series, with base year 1978, GDP is published by expenditure components and by economic activity at current and constant prices. The production accounts contain data on gross output value, intermediate consumption, and value added for activities that produce goods and services. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Economic activities are classified using the International Standard Industrial Classification, Rev. 2 (ISIC rev.2). GDP by branch of activity is published in millions of lempiras in current and 1978 constant values, comprising 11 activities: Agriculture, Forestry, Hunting and Fishing, Mining and Quarrying, Manufacturing Industry, Electricity and Water, Construction, Transport, Storage and Communications; Wholesale and Retail Trade; Financial Institutions; Insurance and Real Estate, and Business Services, Home Ownership, Public Administration, Defense and Communal, Social and Personal Services. GDP by expenditure component is published at current and 1978 constant prices, with the following composition: Private Consumption Expenditure, General Government Consumption Expenditure; Private and Public Gross Fixed Capital Formation; Changes in Stocks, Exports and Imports of Goods and Services. Net income by factors of production received from the rest of the world (receipts and payments by factor); Indirect Taxes net of Subsidies and Allocation for Fixed Capital Consumption, which are used to calculated National Product and Income, National Disposable Income, and its use in consumption and saving, and Gross Fixed Capital Formation and its financing. Exports of goods and services are classified as key products and other products and goods imports are classified under the broad headings of the Central American Tariff System (SAC). Scope of the data The national accounts statistics of Honduras cover all transactions in goods and services, generation of income and its uses, based on the availability of data. As a result, for example, earnings by resident workers who work for part of the year abroad are not included. Other aggregates, such as saving and disposable income are provided upon request. All resident units in the economic territory of the country are covered The base year of the accounts is 1978. GDP is published by branch of economic activity and by expenditure component, at current and constant prices. To obtain GDP by branch of activity, the gross output value at current prices is calculated using volume or quantity and price indicators. Technical coefficients for the reference year are then applied to the values obtained to estimate intermediate consumption and to obtain value added at current prices as the difference. Constant values are obtained by extrapolation or deflation, as relevant. Using the expenditure approach, government final consumption, exports, imports, and gross capital formation are obtained exogenously. Unrecorded activity Illegal activities are not usually included, nor are some informal sector activities. 2.4.1 Valuation GDP is valued at market prices, value added of activities at factor cost, exports at f.o.b. prices, and imports at c.i.f. prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis An accrual basis is generally used. Government revenue and expenditure, as well as some foreign transactions, are estimated on a cash basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs PRODUCTION APPROACH Agriculture, Forestry, Hunting and Fishing Output is estimated for 32 agricultural products. Coffee growing : The production of nurseries, production for final home use and for the market are valued. Nurseries and estates (gross fixed capital formation) : Their estimates are based on the information contained in the catalog of private investment opportunities in Honduran agriculture, a publication that shows the investment plans detailing the cost structure for the first two years of a coffee plantation. The gross output value is estimated through the costs. Market production is estimated on the basis of information provided by the Honduran Coffee Institute (IHCAFE) concerning purchases that yield profits, which are adjusted for smuggling and increased by 2 percent or the estimated amount that producers leave for home consumption, according to the 2002 study Análisis Competitividad de la Cadena Agroindustrial de Café en Honduras [Analysis of Competitiveness in the Agroindustrial Chain of Coffee in Honduras]. Basic Grain Cultivation. Basic grains are produced in two cycles (first and last). In the first cycle, sowing takes place in May-July and reaping in September-December and in the last cycle, the sowing takes place in August-December and harvesting in January-April of the following year. To estimate production in year N, the total harvest of the first cycle is added to the output harvested in the last cycle of year N-1, since both sowing and harvesting take place in the same accounting period, in addition to changes in stocks of growing products. The details of this procedure are as follows: The quantities produced in each of the cycles, the monthly distribution of sowing and harvesting and producer prices are obtained from the Encuesta Agrícola Básica [Basic Agricultural Survey] by the National Statistics Institute (INE), which is conducted twice a year. In May, the last cycle of the previous year is certified and a prognosis is made for the first cycle. In September, the first cycle is certified and forecasts are made for the last. The monthly distribution of seeds and harvests is sued to determine the stocks of growing products that are used to adjust production for the year. Once the value of production is determined, the components of intermediate consumption and value added are broken down using the cost structures resulting from the agricultural surveys conducted by the BCH in various areas of the country, which have been evaluated against the investment plans of other institutions and agencies linked to the agricultural sector. Banana growing. Data provided by the two transnational companies and their subsidiaries, as well as special surveys that the BCH conducts annually of on independent producers that sell their production to these companies, are used to calculate the production account. The companies’ financial statements are used to break down production costs and the components of value-added. These financial statements also record the value of purchases from independent producers, which are used to adjust and/or validate the information obtained in the surveys of those producers. The companies also provide data on acreage under crop, crates produced, crates exported, export prices (transfer prices) and personnel employed. The value of the production of banana companies is calculated on a preliminary basis by multiplying the number of crates produced by the constant price, which is adjusted for the international market price (these prices are adjusted by deducting insurance and freight both on the domestic market and internationally, to obtain producer prices). The value of the production of independent producers is obtained, in principle, based on special surveys and checked against the value of the purchases reported by export companies, making adjustments for production for domestic consumption. African palm cultivation. Production is obtained from incorporated companies, independent producers, and producer cooperatives. The BCH conducts an annual survey of the activity of producers of this crop. The data compiled are used to determine gross output value, intermediate consumption, and value added. Each of the subsectors is calculated separately, in other words, there is an account for independent producers, another one for cooperatives, and another for corporations. This procedure differentiates cost structures for each subsector and assesses the consistency of the production values, since the companies that grow and process palm kernels have data on their purchases from other producers. The special surveys also provide information on the acreage of plantations and these data are used to calculate production for final home use and gross fixed capital formation for the activity. Sugarcane cultivation: The production account is based on data compiled in sugarcane factories, through agroindustrial surveys, and in the BCH special survey of independent sugarcane producers, who then market their production to the sugarcane factories. The financial statements of these companies are used to break down production costs and the components of value added. In addition to financial information, the companies provide data on acreage, their own sugarcane production and that of independent producers, as well as the price paid to the independent producers. They also report data on personnel employed divided into permanent and temporary workers. The value of the production of companies is calculated on a preliminary basis by multiplying the quantity produced by the unit price. The value of the production of independent producers is estimated using data from the special surveys, cross checked with the purchases reported by the companies, and adjusted as necessary to make the figures consistent. Production account for permanent crops. Estimates of production of permanent crops include market production and production for final home use. This latter corresponds to the amount spent until the plants begin to produce, during which stage the expenditure constitutes gross fixed capital formation. Market production is calculated using the information from the agricultural surveys published by the INE, which include production volumes, acreage under crop, yield, and prices. These data are assessed against the different types of demand (for household consumption, for export, and intermediate demand). Production is valued at producer prices, which are crosschecked with the wholesale and retail prices recorded in the Economic Indicators Section. Poultry: Separate accounts are compiled for farming poultry and producing eggs. Poultry farming: The poultry farming activity is calculated on the basis of chicken meat production, which is concentrated in three large enterprises that process 98 percent of domestic demand; the other 2 percent represents home-based poultry farming. It is estimated that 2.5 pounds of meat are obtained from each chicken, which, based on the total number of pounds of chicken meat produced, is used to calculate the number of chickens slaughtered, and this figure is used to estimate production. The price of live poultry is estimated by interviewing qualified reporting entities and this information is checked for consistency with the price of chicken meat. Once production is valued, the value of intermediate inputs and the components of value added are determined using the financial statements of the three large enterprises. Eggs: Production is estimated on the basis of BCH surveys. Information is also available directly from the large producer enterprises. This production is compared with the amounts reported by members of the agricultural exchange for poultry. The prices are obtained by consulting enterprises and persons involved in the production of eggs and the data so derived are checked against the consumer and wholesale prices used in calculating the CPI and WPI. The cost structure used in the production account is obtained from the statistical bulletins of enterprises involved in this activity. Livestock : The production of livestock (cattle, pigs, goats, sheep, mules) is calculated with information on the stock of livestock obtained from the agricultural censuses conducted by the INE and surveys conducted by the. The BCH . The biological cycle method is used, whereby the initial stock is increased by the number of births (calculated by birth rates) and additions owing to changes in category less stillbirths, natural deaths, losses owing to changes of category and slaughter and dissection. Once these data have been validated, values are attributed using separate prices for each category. Constant values are extrapolated with the index of the number of heads of final stock. Forestry: For this activity, the production account is calculated for roundwood (pine and colored), firewood, natural resins (pine and liquid amber) and other forestry products, the information is obtained basically from the Statistical Yearbook of the Honduran Forestry Development Corporation (COHDEFOR). In the case of firewood, the reference point is household consumption and activities that use firewood as an input. The production of roundwood is valued at different prices, as colored wood is more expensive than pine. The same is true for natural resins, for which the price differential between liquid amber resin and pine resin is considerable. Constant values are calculated by extrapolation using the volume indices for each of the products in the activity. Fishing : This activity includes the production of shrimp and fish, farmed and wild-caught, and lobster. Monthly information is available on the production volumes of shrimp and tilapia farms and data are available on purchases by lobster and wild-caught shrimp packing plants. Production of other fish and seafood is estimated on the basis of household consumption, restaurant demand, and exports reported by the Balance of Payments Section. Mining and Quarrying The activity includes establishments devoted to the mining of nonferrous metals (gold, zinc, silver, lead) en broza [mixed with sand] as well as the extraction from quarries and rivers of nonmetallic minerals, such as sand, stones and gravel, and salt. In order to measure the sector, information is provided directly by enterprises engaged in mining nonferrous metals and nonmetallic minerals, and information from the enterprises themselves. The Directorate of Mining Development (DEFOMIM) provides production volumes, prices, and exports monthly, and all information related to production costs annually. The information is used and analyzed jointly with the balance of payments. The production value of the minerals mentioned is calculated by adding the product of the quantities mined according to the corresponding prices. Salt production is calculated on the basis of a producer survey. There is no direct source of information on mining nonmetallic minerals, such as gravel, sand and stone, therefore performance is measured based on the increase in square footage under construction, which is obtained from the construction permits authorized in the main municipalities of the country. Manufacturing Industry The main data sources for this activity are: The BCH Statistical Bulletin, which is obtained annually from companies through the Executive Directorate of Income (DEI), and is designed for the national accounts. It requests annual data on the main items of the income statement and the balance sheet of each enterprise; the physical volume index of manufacturing activity (IVOFI); foreign trade data on manufactured products, which are used mainly to test the consistency of the data reported by other sources. For agroindustrial activities, such as sugarcane, African palm, farmed shrimp, joint surveys are conducted to meet national accounts and balance of payments needs. Maquiladora activity is measured separately as a total, with all enterprises surveyed to gather data and a special publication prepared every year. Constant prices are measured by extrapolation for the items for which there are production volume indicators. For activities with no volume indicators, estimates a constant prices are obtained by deflating the current values by the appropriate price indices. Estimates for 63 activities are provided up to four digits of the ISIC, in both current and constant values. Construction This activity comprises two major components: private and public construction, which includes new works and extensions. Private activity covers mainly housing construction; industrial, commercial and other construction. Public construction covers civil engineering works by state agencies, including electrical wiring and communication lines. The main data sources are: the municipal mayors’ offices - for data on square footage under new construction and extensions, and the corresponding monetary values, and the Honduran Chamber of Construction (CHICO). To calculate the production value of private construction at constant prices, the value for the reference year is extrapolated with a quantity index of the square footage constructed (surface area built). Intermediate consumption is obtained with an input/output coefficient and value-added is obtained as the difference. The value of public construction at current prices is estimated on the basis of information provided by the Finance Secretariat (SEFIN), which includes general government and public enterprise construction. This value is then deflated with the average weighted index of prices of construction materials and minimum wages paid in the sector. Electricity and Water Information on electricity is provided by the National Electrical Energy Enterprise (ENEE), which reports information on the generation, consumption, and distribution of electrical energy in KWH, exports and imports, transmission losses, and prices. The information on private production is obtained directly from enterprises on the monthly basis. The production of electricity is obtained at constant prices extrapolating the value of the annual reference by means of an energy production index. Information on water is provided by the public utility the Aqueducts and Sewerage National Autonomous Service (SANAA), Aguas de San Pedro, and Aguas de Puerto Cortés. Production at constant prices is measured by extrapolation using a quantity index of in cubic meters of water consumed by purpose. Transport, Storage and Communications Rail transport, urban, suburban, and interurban transport of passengers and cargo by road. Rail : Includes only the national railway and information for calculating gross value-added is taken from the company’s financial statements. Urban, suburban and interurban passenger and cargo transport : The list of enterprises providing this service with the respective number of units and corresponding capacities is obtained from the Secretariat of Works in Public Transport and Housing (SOPTRAVI). A sample of enterprises is selected from the universe, and these are asked to provide financial statements and balance sheets, data on the fleet of vehicles and their capacity, and other indicators for calculating gross value, intermediate consumption, and value-added. Other data sources are: BCH Statistical Sheet, lists of income tax filers, list of sales volumes reported to the main mayors’ offices. To calculate the output of the taxi transportation service, information on income and expenditure is requested on an annual form prepared by the Accounts section and sent to transport cooperatives and associations (COTRATAX and ASOMOPROH). Values at constant prices are estimated by deflation. Values at current prices of the different types of passenger transport are deflated with corresponding CPI components. Storage : The data source is the National Banking and Insurance Commission (CNBS), which provides the financial statements of supervised bonded warehouses. Values at constant prices are estimated by extrapolation using a volume index of the movement of imports and exports. Communications : Data sources are the Honduran Telecommunications Enterprise (HONDUTEL) and the cellular communication companies (CELTEL and MEGATEL), which provide monthly data on current prices and number of minutes of use billed by type of call. The financial statements of these enterprises are also available. Trade, Restaurants and Hotels Wholesale and retail trade: Production at current and constant prices is estimated indirectly, using value and volume indices of flows of domestic and imported goods, respectively. Restaurants and Hotels: These are monitored with data collected in the Statistical sheet , deflating for constant values with the respective component of the CPI. Financial Istitutions, Insurance, Real Estate and Business Services Financial Institutions include commercial banks, development banks, central bank, savings and loan associations, and finance companies. The production of services sold directly is estimated by the value of the commissions and other payments collected from customers. The production of financial intermediation services that are not sold directly it estimated as the difference between the income from assets received from and interest paid to depositors. The data source for this activity is the BCH and the National Commission on Banking and Insurance (CNBS), which provides detailed financial statements. value-added is deflated with an index of average compensation, prepared on the basis of the number of workers and salaries in the financial sector. Insurance: Includes insurance companies, insurance agents and brokers, and pension funds, the main source being be the CNBS. Production is quantified by the difference between actual premiums received less the value of compensation paid plus of the income intrinsic to the activity. Value at constant prices is obtained by deflation with an index of average compensation. Real Estate and Service Provision include leasing, management and use of real estate for own account, for example, leasing nonresidential buildings and apartments. Data sources of service provision to businesses, such as engineering, legal and other services are: the BCH Statistical Bulletin, financial statements, household surveys, income statements. Production is measured by the value of sales of the various services provided, deflated with the CPI. Home Ownership This is the imputed value of the production of services of owner-occupied housing and housing occupied by tenants who do not pay rent. The imputed production value for 1978 is obtained separately for urban and rural areas, extrapolating with volume indices for housing to obtain values at constant prices and housing rental prices from the CPI, with a view to estimating values at current and constant prices, respectively. Public Administration and Defense Covers the activities and General Government, namely: central government, autonomous agencies, nonfinancial public enterprises, institutions, and local governments (municipalities). Data sources are: the Finance Secretariat, decentralized public institutions, Ministry of Defense, and other secretariats and ministries. Production values are obtained from state income and expenditure budget execution. Intermediate consumption is incorporated into purchases of goods and services and value added into compensation, which is deflated with the CPI since there is no appropriate index of compensation paid by the government. Communal, Social and Personal Services These activities conducted by various entities and individuals involved in providing market or nonmarket services, with a view to meeting the personnel needs of social nuclei or community groups in the country. They include: professional, domestic, and personal care services, pensions, education, hospitals, and leisure services. Private and public services are estimated separately then aggregated after the results are analyzed. The data sources for private services are: the Statistical Bulletin, administrative records, universities, secondary education institutions, private schools in the country, hospitals. They provide figures on the number of students enrolled, professionals graduated, personnel employed, private hospital beds, tickets sold at cinema box offices, etc. For education, health, and other public services, the respective ministries and institutions provide the data. The in the information available, indices of volume or quantity, price and/or value are constructed to estimate aggregates at current and constant prices. EXPENDITURE APPROACH The expenditure components corresponding to GDP are: private consumption, public consumption, private and public gross fixed capital formation, changes in stocks, exports, imports. Private consumption: This is estimated as a coefficient of the average propensity to consume, which in Honduras is about 85 percent of GDP, which percentage may change depending on the performance of other variables. Public consumption : This is estimated at current prices with information on compensation and current purchases less current sales of goods and services by general government units. These data are provided by the Finance Secretariat. Expenditure on public consumption at constant 1978 prices is obtained by deflating current expenditure with the CPI. Gross fixed capital formation: Includes investment in machinery and equipment, construction and land development, and permanent crops. Private and public gross fixed capital formation are estimated separately. Private construction uses changes in square footage under construction. Constant values are obtained by deflating with the average weighted price index of construction materials and minimum wage in the sector. Public construction data are obtained directly from the Finance Secretariat (SEFIN), deflating with a construction material price index. Machinery and equipment use the nominal data in dollars in the balance of payments, which correspond to imports of machinery, electrical appliances and materials, transport, replacement parts, etc., multiplied by the average exchange rate, deflated with a price index for machinery and equipment, to yield constant values. Exports and Imports of goods and services: Exports and imports of goods and services are obtained from the balance of payments. Their value in lempiras is calculated using the average exchange rate of the national accounts. The value at current prices is deflated with an index of the unit value of exports and imports, to obtain values of constant prices. Changes in stocks : These are obtained as a difference, by analyzing consistency and adjusting for private consumption when necessary. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Price adjustments are made for some exported goods in order to convert transfer prices to market prices. This is the case for exports of bananas, shrimp, coffee, and nonferrous minerals. Quantity adjustments are also made, including estimates of coffee and shrimp. Smuggling of merchandise and livestock is estimated as well, based on the calculation of statistics for the countries with which these products are marketed. None
