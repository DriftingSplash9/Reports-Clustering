 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Data are consistent with the European System of National and Regional Accounts ( ESA 2010 ) regarding definitions, accounting rules and classifications. ESA 2010 is fully consistent with SNA 2008 and so broadly in line with PSDSG 2011 and GFSM 2014. However, ESA 2010 and GFSM 2014 differ in some points. For example, the liabilities of unfunded government defined benefit employer pension schemes are excluded under ESA 2010 but are included under GFSM 2014. In addition, provisions in the field of Government Finance Statistics follow Eurostat's Manual on Government Deficit and Debt and "A Guide to the Statistical Treatment of PPPs ". These manuals aim at a fully harmonized implementation of ESA 2010 for government finance statistics in the European Union. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification of units into the general government sector and its subsectors is in accordance with ESA 20210 regulations. The general government sector consists of four subsectors, namely central government, state government, local government, and social security funds. The sector classification of units is reviewed continuously. A list of units classified in the government sector is published online on the National Accounts Institute website and updated twice a year. The debt instruments shown in the table are the following: currency and deposits, securities, loans, insurance, pension and standardized guarantee schemes as well as other accounts payable. The distinction of the debt by currency and by residence of the creditor is also available. The coverage and evaluation of these categories are in line with ESA 2010, and so with GFSM 2014 and PSDSG 2011 definitions (except as regards the inclusion of unfunded government defined benefit employer pension schemes). 2.2.1 Scope The classification of units into the general government sector and its subsectors is in accordance with ESA 20210 regulations. Scope of the data The scope is consistent with internationally accepted standards, guidelines, or good practices. The scope is consistent with ESA 2010. Government finance statistics cover the four general government subsectors: central government, state government, local government, and social security funds. These subsectors consist of main units and extra-budgetary units. The general government sector includes all institutional units which are non-market producers whose output is intended for individual and collective consumption and financed by compulsory payments made by units belonging to other sectors, and institutional units principally engaged in the redistribution of national income and wealth. Institutions belonging to the sector of general government are not identified by their legal form but are classified according to qualitative and quantitative criteria as defined in the methodological manuals. More explanation on the sectorization of the general government' units can be found in the CIS application on the National Bank of Belgium's website . Other instruments are valued at the market value. Liabilities denominated in foreign currencies are converted into the national currency at the representative spot market exchange rate prevailing on the last working day of each quarter. Maastricht debt is recorded ‘after swaps'. 2.4.2 Recording basis For Maastricht debt instruments, accrued interests are not included (concept of the face value). 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The Belgian government gross debt is compiled according to the ESA 2010 requirements, on a gross basis recording. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs For the compilation of government financial accounts and of the Maastricht debt , the building blocks reporting is composed of purpose-built templates for collection of financial data. This statistical survey provides information on the stocks (with multiple valuations) and flows for the various financial instruments, on an instrument-by-instrument and whom-to-whom basis. The collection of data is executed by the administrations themselves and transmitted to the compilers. The reporting template is designed to ensure full consistency of opening/closing positions and flows during the reporting period as well as, if possible, the counterpart information. The reporting period is on a quarterly basis for the main states (at the central and state governments levels) and on an annual basis for other units belonging to the general government sector, except for the building blocks reporting on shares which is also on an annual basis for the main states. For local government data , the compilation of the debt relies on their annual reports available at an annual frequency. These data are cross-checked with NBB data sources (Securities Settlement System, Central Corporate Credit Register, Securities Holding Statistics). 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The building blocks reporting has been designed, developed, and adapted to meet the definitions, accounting rules and classifications of the ESA 2010 requirements and of the Protocol on the Excessive Debt Procedure of the Maastricht Treaty. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The quarterly financial data transmitted via the building blocks reporting are available 45 days after the end of the reference period. The annual financial data are available 45 days after the end of the reference period for currency and deposits, debt securities, loans, and transactions in shares, and on August 15 for other instruments and stocks of shares. The time lag for most data sources is shorter than the requirements for the transmission of the debt to international organizations and for the publication deadlines. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data are closely monitored, in collaboration with the General Documentary Base. Single point of contact for statistical purpose (SPOC) has also been set up in each government to facilitate the transmission of statistical information. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Data compilation is in line with international recommendations. Sound statistical techniques are used in dealing with the data sources. Estimates of missing data are based on annual historical data or other data sources. All data are adjusted when more accurate data source become available. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Consolidation is established within the general government sector. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Plausibility checks and controls are carried out at all stages of the production process. Cross checking with other data sources is applied. Intermediate results are assessed during the production process at several moments. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Statistical discrepancies in intermediate data are assessed and investigated. In particular, stocks-flows adjustments explaining the difference between the change in government Maastricht debt and the government deficit/surplus are closely monitored. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs See 3.4.2. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses The changes in General Government Debt statistics are carefully monitored and investigated. Revisions can occur due to new data sources, changes in perimeter or changes in methodology. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses In April and October, a note with the major methodological changes is available, if any. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished statistics are normally not disseminated, but some requests can be taken into consideration. The requests can be addressed at Governmentfinance.Na@nbb.be . 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Contact points are published. A dedicated mailbox is used ( Governmentfinance.Na@nbb.be ). 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs Catalogs and calendars of publication, requirements for source data, etc. are available on the website of the National Bank of Belgium .
