 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The Lesotho Bureau of Statistics (LBOS) compiles and publishes the Foreign Trade Statistics (FTS) according to International Merchandise Trade Statistics recommendations as well as the classifications and definitions in the Balance of Payments Manual 5 as well as classifications in 1993 System of National Accounts. The imports are classified using HS codes level 6 and defines concepts and uses classifications as per UNSD recommendations through different institutions given responsibility over international trade data. According to recommendations imports valued c.i.f and exports f.o.b. In addition by nature IMTS is part of Official statistics and as such is guided by the Fundamental Principles of Official Statistics to safe guard all processes from data collection to dissemination. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The data from all these sources are edited and coded according to the International Trade Classifications, definitions and concepts. Goods are classified according to the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS) of Customs Co-operation Council (CCC). In the classification imported goods are valued at cost, insurance, freight (cif) while exports are at free on board (fob). In processing, values are in Lesotho currency Maloti and in case of declarations in foreign currencies, conversions are made to the maloti using the exchange rate which prevailed during the time of the transaction. SITC Revision 3(Rev. 3) classification is also used. Scope of the data The law of Lesotho requires that all international trade is recorded, that is, importers and exporters of goods must report particulars of all their transactions in goods to the Customs Officials for the purposes of collection of value added tax and other trade related taxes and duties. This is not only for taxes but for other control purposes and for statistical purposes which require volume, quantities, weights and value of individual commodities that get into the territorial boundaries of Lesotho. Bureau of Statistics (BOS) is generally authorized, by law, to collect the documents and data elements necessary to compile FTS. Lesotho Revenue Authority (LRA) constitutes the main source of FTS by virtue of the fact that customs records are readily available and generally reliable source of data. The benefits of using customs records include, for example wide coverage particularly in the case of imports. However, there are other organizations keeping records relevant to trade statistics e.g. records of imports and exports of electrical energy, pipeline shipments of water, maintained by specialized governmental agencies. The LRA supplies BOS with the following; data on customs declaration forms and bills of entry, which contain description of goods imported, their monetary value and country of origin, with expectations that quantities or weights are recorded in the declaration forms for imports. However, full information is recorded for exports only, the description, the quantity, the value and the country of destination. The incomplete records of imports make it difficult to compile other imports related statistics like imports price indices without volume measures. It is neither possible to derive volume indices without quantities. The BOS is using Eurotrace system for data capturing and processing. The Eurotrace system is a software which is able to take basic transactions data generated by ASYCUDA or any other computerized system (including data set compiled from keying in data from customs records) and integrate it into a data base to facilitate in-depth analysis and checking of the data and production and dissemination of international trade statistics. The full merchandise trade imports and exports data that include HS product detail, country of origin or destination (direction of trade table). The published time series data covers the period 1991 - 2008 for both imports and exports. In the publication a full commodity detail (6 digits level of aggregation) and a geographical breakdown is given. Exceptions to coverage Some international commodity flows like trade of foreign missions and goods consigned by government to armed forces are not captured at all or inadequately captured by Customs Trade flows from the border shops are not recorded Trade in Service is not within the scope of work. Monetary gold, banknotes and coins are excluded as well. Trade indices are currently not being generated because of the unavailability of quantities and weights Unrecorded activity The LRA has deployed customs personnel in all 13 official border gates with Republic of South Africa (RSA) and it is worth noting that for Lesotho unlike other countries it is completely landlocked by RSA which makes it difficult to control illegal cross border trade. Therefore, the level of unrecorded trade is huge; however, the magnitude has not yet been determined. 2.4.1 Valuation In the classification, imported goods are valued at cost, insurance, freight (cif) while exports are at free on board (fob). In processing, values are in Lesotho currency Maloti and in case of declarations in foreign currencies, conversions are made to the maloti using the exchange rate which prevailed during the time of the transaction. 2.4.2 Recording basis Trade flows are recorded according to internationally accepted standards, guidelines, or good practices 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Apart from the Department of Customs Excise, the other sources of data are Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security (MAFS), which supplies information on exports and imports of livestock and livestock products and supplementary information on numbers, weights and quantity. Since information from LRA also includes the information from MAFS it is possible to cross check the reliability of data sources and make use of the missing variables in LRA data. The other information source which is more or less like the MAFS as a source is the Department of Mines and Geology (DMG), which provides information on exports of diamonds and other precious stones and building stones. These data sources supply all the necessary information. Other important suppliers of detailed information are the individual dealers or importers of some specialized import goods like petroleum products (petrol, diesel and paraffin and other lubricants), liquor distributors and car dealers. The Transport and Traffic Department (TTD) provides information on registration of new imported vehicles and machinery. Finally BOS receives the government records on donations and other goods. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The BOS is using Eurotrace package for data capturing and processing through the assistance from EU. The published data classification by HS codes (HS6 1996 format), country of origin and destination (direction of trade table). The published time series data covers the period 1991 – 2008 for both imports and exports. In the publication a full commodity detail (6 digits) and a geographical breakdown is given. In the classification, imported goods are valued at cost, insurance, freight (cif) while exports are at free on board (fob). In processing, values are in Lesotho currency Maloti and in case of declarations in foreign currencies, conversions are made to the maloti using the exchange rate which prevailed during the time of the transaction. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data are published twelve months after the reference year. This is because of the high backlog that surrounded foreign trade data due to late data supply from some data suppliers. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The non-customs sources of information described above are useful to supplement customs data and to assist in the crosschecking of customs data; they are not generally recommended as substitute for customs data 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Prior to publication, the data are analysed and if there are discrepancies in the figures, the traders are queried and data corrections are made accordingly. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Unusual movements in trade are investigated. If they are genuine, explanations are given and no adjustments are made. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Hard copy published annually
