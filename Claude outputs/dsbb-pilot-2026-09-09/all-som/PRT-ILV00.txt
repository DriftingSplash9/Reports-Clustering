 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The definition of Eurosystem's reserve assets, approved by the ECB Governing Council in March 1999, which is in line with the guidelines outlined in the sixth edition of the Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6), published by the IMF, and the IMF's Operation Guidelines , of the Data Template on International Reserves and Foreign Currency Liquidity. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification of Monetary gold, SDRs, reserve position in the Fund (IMF), foreign currency assets and other assets are in line with the sixth edition of the Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6), published by the IMF. Scope of the data Institutional coverage: Banco de Portugal , for reserve assets, and Banco de Portugal /Central Government (excluding social security) for the Data Template on International Reserves and Foreign Currency Liquidity. Instrument coverage: Besides the components listed under classification, the template on international reserves and foreign currency liquidity also includes information on: Other foreign currency assets, mainly deposits, securities, loans and financial derivatives not included in official reserve assets, held by both Banco de Portugal and the Central Government; Predetermined short-term drains on foreign currency assets, namely those related with the public debt, the encashment of promissory notes, the reimbursement and interest payment of long term loans, the settlements on forwards, futures and swaps, and the flows associated with repos and reverse repos contracts; Contingent short-term drains on foreign currency assets related to contingent liabilities in foreign currency, which include state guarantees of export credit insurance to developing countries backed by sovereign guarantees of the respective governments; and Related items, mainly the identification of financial derivatives included under section I. of the Template, the market value of gold repoed and included under the aggregate of Reserve assets, and the currency composition of reserves (by groups of currency). Financial derivatives coverage: Since December 1999, financial derivatives are included in the scope of Banco de Portugal reserve assets (foreign currency assets). 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: Prevailing market prices, unless otherwise indicated. From December 1986 to December 1998, data were disseminated with the gold valued on a book value basis and on a market price basis; the market price of the gold was calculated using the valuation principle employed by the EMCF/EMI: that is, the average of the two daily gold quotations on the London market for the last six months is used when it is lower than the average of quotations on the penultimate working day of the month; otherwise the latter value is used. From January 1999 onwards, data is disseminated with the gold monthly valued on a market price basis. Conversion rates: End-of-period exchange rates for stocks and daily exchange rates for flows. Frequency of calculation and revaluation: Monthly. 2.4.2 Recording basis Broad consistency with the IMF's Operation Guidelines , of the Data Template on International Reserves and Foreign Currency Liquidity. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Broad consistency with the IMF's Operation Guidelines , of the Data Template on International Reserves and Foreign Currency Liquidity. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The data on international reserves are based on the accounts of Banco de Portugal , which are available for statistical purposes (until December 1998, the data were based on the accounts of Banco de Portugal and the Directorate General of the Treasury and Finance ( DGTF ). The Common Template includes information provided by Banco de Portugal , the Directorate General of the Treasury and Finance ( DGTF ) and the Public Debt Management Office ( IGCP ). Data on Central Government are compiled according to the Portuguese public accounting rules. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Accounting registries are broadly consistent with statistical requirements. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data collection and processing timetables are arranged to meet timeliness and periodicity for disseminating the international reserves statistics. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Financial innovations are accommodated through the creation of additional accounting items so that data sources cover all relevant information. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Gold is treated as collateral in all reversible transactions (such as gold repos, gold swaps, gold loans and gold deposits) for the compilation of the Eurosystem's international reserves statistics, thus implying that holdings of monetary gold have to remain unchanged during the life of such operations. This treatment corresponds to current European banking accounting directives and practices. Reserve positions in the IMF, including the part denominated in SDR's, are classified under Reserve position in the IMF, rather than under Special Drawing Rights (SDRs). Claims arising from PRGF (former ESAF) commitments are disclosed under foreign exchange/currency and deposits, following the IMF recommendations. According to the Eurosystem's definition, foreign exchange includes foreign currency and deposits/securities/financial derivatives on non-Euro area residents, denominated in a non-euro currency. Financial derivatives falling into the category of reserve assets are netted out, being recorded on a net basis with no distinction between asset and liability positions. Loans to non-bank institutions located outside the euro area are classified under other assets. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Studies and analysis of revisions are carried out on an on-going basis. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Prompt and knowledgeable service and support are available to users of statistics.
