 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The Finnish BOP is a statistical statement summarizing the economic transactions between the residents of Finland and the residents of the rest of the world during each month, quarter and year. The data are compiled in accordance with the methodology set out in the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6), which was implemented in 2014. In addition, guidance from the ECB (financial account), Eurostat (current account) and the OECD (direct investment) is also taken into consideration. Organization of the data follows the instructions by the ECB , which are presented in the Guideline of the European Central Bank (ECB/2004/15, amended by ECB/2007/3). Data is organized in eleven broad categories under three main headings: Current account covers credit and debit entries for transactions in goods, services, income, and current transfers. Capital account includes credits and debits of capital transfers, and acquisition/disposal of nonproduced, nonfinancial assets. Financial account consists of asset and liability transactions in direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives and other investment, and transactions in reserve assets. A separate item for errors and omissions is also presented. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification and sectorization follow the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6). The recommendations by the ECB presented in the Guideline of the European Central Bank (ECB/2004/15, amended by ECB/2007/3) are also followed. The sector classification fulfils the needs of the SNA, and the Balance of Payments and the Rest-of-the world-account are consistent in this respect. As for geographical breakdowns, balance of payments items are classified according to the country of the foreign counterparty (when relevant). However, due to confidentiality reasons, country classifications for some items may not be published. 2.2.1 Scope Classification and sectorization follow the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6). The recommendations by the ECB presented in the Guideline of the European Central Bank (ECB/2004/15, amended by ECB/2007/3) are also followed. The sector classification fulfils the needs of the SNA, and the Balance of Payments and the Rest-of-the world-account are consistent in this respect. As for geographical breakdowns, balance of payments items are classified according to the country of the foreign counterparty (when relevant). However, due to confidentiality reasons, country classifications for some items may not be published. Scope of the data In principle, the Finnish balance of payments attempts to record all resident − non-resident transactions. The compiled data are intended to cover the economic territory of Finland. The concepts of "residency" and "centre of economic interest" that are applied in the data collection forms are consistent with those of the BPM6. Nonetheless, because of difficulties in obtaining reliable data sources, some transactions are not covered (see below: 2.2.1.2. Exceptions to coverage). The amounts involved are, however, believed to be relatively minor. Geographic coverage: The Åland Islands are part of Finnish territory. Exceptions to coverage Transactions coverage: Current account: Worker's remittances are not adequately covered. Capital account: Migrants' transfers are not covered. Financial account: Transactions by the Finnish private household sector in foreign securities conducted directly with non-resident asset managers/custodians are missing from portfolio investment data (assets). Similarly, loan/deposit transactions by the domestic household sector with non-resident banks are not covered in other investment. 2.4.1 Valuation The main principle is to use market values. Data collection is mostly carried out by means of surveys designed for balance of payments purposes, where respondents are instructed to report their transactions using market values (where available). In the monthly BOP-survey carried out by Statistics Finland, the respondents are required to reconcile changes between the opening and closing positions (stocks) into transactions, exchange rate changes, and other valuation changes. Non-euro transactions are instructed to be converted into euros using the ECB's middle rate quoted on the transaction date. The asset and liability flows of the MFI-sector are derived from stock data and are adjusted to remove exchange rate and other valuation changes. Similarly, portfolio investment flows are derived from stock data, which is collected on a security-by-security basis. Goods exports and imports are valued on f.o.b. and c.i.f. basis, respectively, in the merchandise trade statistics compiled by the Finnish National Board of Customs. The import valuations are adjusted to f.o.b. basis for balance of payments statistics using fixed freight and insurance factors. In the financial account surveys, respondents are instructed to convert positions (stocks) denominated in non-euro currencies into euros using the middle rates quoted by the ECB on the last day of the reporting period. In the International Investment Position, direct investment equity stocks of listed direct investment enterprises are valued at market value (as from 2004), and of unlisted direct investment enterprises at book value. Equity stocks both at market value and at book value are available for listed direct investment enterprises. Portfolio securities are at market values (dirty price in the case of debt securities), as are securities held as reserve assets. Loans/deposits in other investment are recorded at nominal values (incl. accrued interest). Monetary gold is valued at market prices based on the p.m. fixing in the London bullion market. 2.4.2 Recording basis In general, the Finnish balance of payments data adopt the accruals basis for the time of recording. Data supplied by the respondents to balance of payments surveys are typically based on business accounting (book-keeping) records, and are considered to be close approximations to the accruals principle. If book-keeping data is not readily available, estimates may be provided in some cases (e.g. services). Regarding the recording of interest, respondents to the monthly and annual financial account surveys are requested to report interest on an accruals basis, with offsetting entries to be included in the relevant financial account items. Intragroup dividends (direct investment) are also reported on an accruals basis. Data on reinvested earnings are collected on an annual basis, and the transactions are apportioned equally over the months/quarters. Portfolio investment dividends are reported on a cash basis, in other words as of the date they are paid to investors. As for merchandise trade, the data on trade in goods with the non-EU countries are recorded according to the time of customs clearance by the Finnish National Board of Customs. The trade with other EU member countries (Intrastat data) is based on enterprise reporting to the National Board of Customs, and the change of ownership is approximated at the time when the transaction is recorded in the books of the reporter. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Grossing/netting procedures are broadly consistent with the BPM6 recommendations. Current and capital account items are mostly recorded on a gross basis. Financial account transactions are recorded on a net basis, separately for asset and liability components. Direct investment is also recorded on a directional basis. Drawings of loans are reported gross, including related fees and expenses. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Goods: The Finnish Board of Customs collects and compiles the trade statistics. The extra EU trade data are acquired employing the customs declaration system and the intra EU data using the EU Intrastat statistical system. These data are available on a monthly frequency (the statistical system of the Finnish Customs is described in detail: https://tilastot.tulli.fi/en/information-on-statistics Other items in the current account: Statistics Finland collects travel, transport, insurance services and other services data through various surveys. Travel and transport data are collected on a quarterly frequency with a range of surveys and the other services annually and quarterly by a stratified sample survey. Moreover, when compiling various current account data, Statistics Finland makes use of official administrative records as well as SNA expert estimates. The compilation of the financial account in the balance of payments statistics is based on following surveys: Monthly BOP-survey on foreign assets and liabilities. Statistics Finland and the Bank of Finland collect data from non-financial corporations, financial and insurance corporations, municipalities, and the government monthly on their balance of payments on foreign financial assets and liabilities. Non-group and intra-group items are reported. Annual BOP-survey on foreign assets and liabilities. Statistics Finland and the Bank of Finland jointly collect data from non-financial corporations, financial and insurance corporations, municipalities and the government on their balance of payments on foreign financial assets and liabilities. The Bank of Finland's monthly survey by institutional sector for foreign security holdings and international securities' issues. Data is reported on a security-by-security level. The Bank of Finland's Monthly custodian survey for the holdings of securities (data is reported on a security-by-security level). The Bank of Finland's survey on Investment fund balance sheet collects data from investment funds on a monthly basis on balance sheets and subscriptions and redemptions of investment fund shares. Monetary financial institutions data collection. The Bank of Finland's collects balance sheet data monthly. The foreign assets and liabilities surveys are cut-off census collections where the most important respondents report monthly and most of the reporting population annually. Data for the small enterprises exempted from reporting are estimated on the basis of the balance sheet data acquired from the Tax Authority. The statistical unit employed to collect data for the balance of payments statistics is an institutional unit. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording CURRENT AND CAPITAL ACCOUNT Goods: Trade account (including adjustment items): Foreign trade statistics are compiled by the Finnish Customs, also covering additional information for adjustment items and transport costs. The statistics cover the commodity trade between Finland and other Member States of the European Union (Intrastat) and between Finland and third countries (Extrastat). Foreign trade statistics are compiled in accordance with the statistical value concept. Exports (third countries)/deliveries (internal) are on an f.o.b. valuation basis and imports (third countries)/acquisitions (internal) are on a c.i.f. basis. Statistics Finland adjusts the aggregated import data to an f.o.b. basis for BOP purposes. In the BOP breakdowns by country, merchandise exports (covering both exports to third countries and intra EU deliveries) are compiled according to the country of destination. As for merchandise imports, acquisitions from the EU area are recorded according to the consignment principle and imports from non-EU area to the country of origin principle. Services: Transport: Transport services comprise of goods and passenger transport broken down according to the mode of transport, that is, sea, air, road and rail transport. In addition to actual transport services this item also contains auxiliary transport activities and services. Sea transport auxiliary activities include piloting, stevedoring, shipping route and harbour services; air transport auxiliary services include airport services, compensations for air traffic control services, repairs and maintenance. The data are based on multiple sources. Sea transport receipts and expenditure are collected for a special Foreign sea transport receipts and expenditure statistics by Statistics Finland. In addition, foreign sea freight expenditures are estimated employing the market shares of foreign shipping operators as well as commodity and country breakdowns of imports. Air transport receipts are reported by domestic airlines. Air transport expenditure is estimated employing airline information and market shares of foreign carriers in the international air traffic to and from Finland. International rail transport receipts and expenditure are reported by the Finnish State Railways company. Road transport receipts and expenditure are expert estimates by Statistics Finland based on the information from the Finnish truckers association. Travel: Travel receipts comprise the consumption by the foreign tourist in the country producing the services. Correspondingly, the travel expenditures comprise the consumption by the residents of the compiling economy abroad. Consumption related to tourism refers to all expenditure that arises from a trip abroad excluding the passenger transport. Consumption involves hotel and restaurant services, transport services in the destination as well as cultural and recreational services. The main source for travel receipts was the border interview of foreign visitors in Finland until 2013. New method which is mainly based on credit card data is currently being developed and will be introduced in 2015. Figures concerning year 2014 are estimated based on various data sources. For compilation of travel expenditure the primary source is the quarterly Finnish travel survey conducted from resident population of age 15 - 74. The data are supplemented by travel volume indicators such as hotel bookings, package tours, passenger statistics of international sea and air traffic as well as Frontier Guard data on EU border crossings. Other services: Insurance services include direct insurance and reinsurance. Receipts are the difference of the premiums from non-residents less claims of non-residents. The expenditures are defined accordingly. Insurance service receipts and expenditure data are based on surveys to resident insurance enterprises selling insurance services in Finland. The commodity insurance data are compiled by Finnish Customs employing a survey of freight and insurance costs in the commodity trade. In addition, the purchase of foreign insurance services is included in the survey on foreign trade in services conducted by Statistics Finland. Other services cover communication services, construction services, financial services, computer and related services, other business services, and personal, cultural and recreational services, as well as immaterial rights such as royalties and license fees. The detailed classification follows the Eurostat BOP Vade Mecum requirements which are based on the Extended Balance of Payments Services classification as presented in the Manual of the International Trade in Services. Complete breakdowns by country are obtained from the survey on foreign trade in services. Also an annual, stratified sample survey on the foreign trade in services (other than transport, travel and insurance) was used at Statistics Finland since the reference year 1999 until the reference year 2023 (See for more detail: Statistics Finland: Internationalisation of enterprises, Enterprises 2004/5, pp. 86 - 89). Income: Compensation of employees cover remuneration for professional services and personal wages and fees. Receipts and expenditures in compensation of employees are estimated by Statistics Finland employing the volume information on Finnish workers abroad and foreign workers in Finland as well as the tax information on wages paid abroad (employment period lasting 6 months or longer). Investment income is based on the financial assets and liabilities in equity or in other instruments. Income is broken down by instrument similarly as is the financial account. It comprises earnings of direct investment equity, interest on direct investment, dividends and interest on portfolio investment and interest on other investment as well as earnings of central bank's reserve assets. As from 2004 all the data are broken down geographically according to the country of the foreign counterpart. As for direct investment income, the dividends paid by the investment object are deducted from the earnings on the equity and the difference is recorded as reinvested earnings. The offsetting entry is recorded in the direct investments in the financial account. Capital gains and – losses are not included in the return of the equity. The main source for the compilation of investment income is the survey system (see "FINANCIAL ACCOUNT, General characteristics of the survey system", below), where the income is surveyed for other investment together with asset and liability stocks and flows and valuation adjustments by investment type. Current and capital transfers: Private current transfers cover items such as private grants, donations, inheritances, taxes and pensions. The EU subsidies for the agriculture are recorded in private transfers. More than half of the public current transfer expenditure is product and import taxes and fees paid to the EU. In addition, public transfers include grants from central and local governments, membership subscriptions to international organisations and development assistance. Public transfers receipts are all EU grants to general government. Private current transfers are compiled using administrative and central government budgetary sources, various estimates and the insurance survey. Capital transfers comprise ownership transfers of fixed assets and debt forgiveness. In the Finnish balance of payments private capital transfers are acquisitions and disposals of intangible assets. The public capital transfers comprise mainly debt forgiveness to developing countries and capital transfers from the EU funds. Debt forgiveness by the central government is recorded by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. FINANCIAL ACCOUNT General characteristics of the survey system: The basic principle of the survey system is to retrieve the information from the source, i.e. from the institutional units undertaking financial or investment decisions. Domestic institutional sectors (enterprises, monetary financial institutions (MFIs), other financial institutions, central government, local government and social security institutions) receive monthly or annual survey on foreign assets and liabilities. The respondents give the net flows, valuation adjustments and stocks valued to market prices as well as related incomes (accrued if appropriate) in millions of Euro with two decimal places. As a whole, the foreign asset and liability survey system is benefited by the consistency of the flow, stock and income data on the respondent level. Similarly, the monthly, quarterly and annual time series are consistent with each other. The census type collection allows for immediate adjustments in surveys due to changes in the enterprise structures, and it is flexible to accept both enterprise and consolidated responses. For more details regarding collection techniques, etc., see 3.2.1 Source data assessment. The foreign asset and liability survey system is completed with indirect collection from domestic custodians, who report holdings of securities of both resident and non-resident customers. Data on securities holdings is collected monthly on a security-by-security basis. On the asset side the custodian survey is used to cover the household sector's investments in foreign securities and to complement the information received from the direct surveys to institutional investors. On the liability side the custodian survey is used to capture the foreign investment in securities issued in Finland. The same definitions and concepts are used in the monthly survey and in the annual survey. The direct investment data based on the monthly survey are revised according to the results of the annual surveys. The breakdowns of direct investment by geographical location and economic activity are based mostly on the annual surveys. Data on the statistically important new mergers and acquisitions, in which the domestic party is not included in the monthly survey, are requested in detail employing a special form. Data on reserve assets and other BOP items of the central bank are based on the accounting records of the Bank of Finland. The collection of these data is integrated with the general reporting system. The balance sheet data collection and the BOP data collection of the MFI-sector are carried out employing a common survey form. Data pertaining to foreign assets and liabilities of mutual funds registered in Finland are obtained from a monthly survey conducted by the Bank of Finland. Specific remarks on functional categories: Direct investment: The components of direct investment capital are equity capital, reinvested earnings and other capital. Equity contributions without payment (i.e. the provision of real or financial assets) are also covered. The item other capital consists of inter-company loans, trade credits, bonds and money market papers. Trade credits have been recorded in direct investment as from the beginning of 1999. Earlier they were recorded under other investment. Both short-term and long-term inter-company loans are included in direct investment capital (including foreign assets and liabilities between fellow enterprises). To be more precise, the survey respondents are asked to report non-marketable loans and leasing credits and those deposits in intra-group accounts that the respondent classifies as inter-company assets and liabilities in its own accounting as inter-company loans. If both the resident and the foreign counterpart are non-insurance financial corporations only perpetual loans are recorded in other capital. The 10 % -principle is applied for the direct or indirect ownership of the equity of an enterprise as the criterion for direct investment. So-called special purpose entities are defined as direct investment enterprises. Cross-border transactions in real estate by households are included, based on an estimate. Direct investment capital transactions are recorded on a directional basis. In other words, all assets and liabilities of resident direct investors vis-à-vis their affiliates and fellow enterprises are recorded in net terms (assets minus liabilities) as direct investments abroad (foreign assets) in the financial account and in the IIP. All liabilities and assets of resident investment enterprises vis-à-vis their foreign parent companies and fellow enterprises are recorded in net terms (liabilities minus assets) as Direct investments in Finland (foreign liabilities) in the financial account and in the IIP. Portfolio investment: The portfolio investment statistics include shares, participations in mutual funds, bonds and money market instruments. Private placement bonds are also classified as portfolio investment. Portfolio investment is broken down by the sector of issuer (liabilities) and end-investor (assets). Country breakdowns are available for portfolio investment assets, but not for liabilities. Portfolio investment data is collected on a security-by-security basis and ECB's Centralised Securities Database is utilized in valuation and in making the classifications needed. Monthly stocks are used to derive flow data for BOP. Other investment: Other investment includes loans, trade credits, currency and deposits, and other assets and liabilities. Loans include promissory note (non-marketable) loans (excluding private placements), financial leasing and repurchase agreements. Trade credits include import-related supplier credits, export advances, import advances and export claims. Credit accounts of companies are classified under other assets and liabilities, except for inter-company accounts, which are classified under direct investment. Short-term claims and liabilities between affiliated enterprises are also recorded under direct investment. In the Finnish BOP, genuine repos and sell/buy-back transactions are recorded under collateralised loans. Under the current reporting system, reporting agents are not required to separate repos from other short-term activities. Financial derivatives: Transactions in derivatives cover, in principle, all payments related to option-type contracts as well as forward-type contracts (including swaps): payments at inception (option premiums), during the life of and at the close of contracts. Both exchange-traded derivatives and OTC-type derivatives are included. The difference between the contract (or strike) price and the prevailing market price in the case of delivery of the underlying instrument in a contract is also classified as a transaction in derivatives. Non-repayable margin payments are included to the extent possible. Payments related to interest rate swaps and forward rate agreements (FRAs) are classified as transactions in financial derivatives (and are not recorded in investment income flows). Instrument breakdowns are, however, not available in the monthly surveys. The compilation is carried out following the basic conceptual framework and methodology originally set out in the BPM6 and subsequently revised by the IMF. The valuation of stock positions for derivatives collected in the monthly surveys is based on gross market values. Assets are defined as the sum of contracts with a positive market value (including purchased options), and liabilities as the sum of contracts with negative market value (including written options). Reserve assets: Reserve assets comprise gold, special drawing rights (SDRs), the reserve position in the IMF, foreign exchange and other claims. The BPM6 definition and the ECB recommendations are followed. The monthly flow data are based on the accounting records of the Bank of Finland. 3.2.1 Source data assessment CURRENT ACCOUNT Goods: Data on intra EU trade in goods, compiled by the Finnish Customs, are based on the EU Intrastat statistical system. For the collection of these data thresholds have been established in order to reduce respondent burden. Firstly, there is an annual, so-called assimilation threshold, both for deliveries and acquisitions , below which enterprises are not required to report. On the other hand, the reporting of information on the statistical value is voluntary for enterprises within annual thresholds . Enterprises that do not exceed these limits may report their deliveries and acquisitions according to the invoice value. The National Board of Customs makes estimates for the share of trade that falls short of the threshold values and adds them to the registered statistical figures. The data on statistical values are assessed by using chapter/country specific coefficients, which have been calculated on the basis of data where both statistical value and invoice value are indicated. Services: The main data source for other services (than travel and transport) is the quarterly survey on the foreign trade in services conducted by Statistics Finland (see 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording). Until the reference year 2023, the sample size of the quarterly inquiry on international trade in services and foreign flows of goods was around one thousand units, and that of the annual inquiry about two thousand units. The quarterly inquiry was raised to correspond to the population, based on the shares of the previous finalized annual inquiry. Stratified random sampling (SRS or PPS) was used in the annual inquiry depending on the stratum. From the reference year 2024 onwards, the sample size of the quarterly inquiry on international trade in services and foreign flows of goods has been approximately 1.500 units. The data collection is supplemented by a method based on administrative VIES data. FINANCIAL ACCOUNT: THE SURVEY OPERATIONS In its survey operations, Statistcs Finland and Bank of Finland undertakes multi-frequency surveys for foreign assets and liabilities. The starting point in the process of selecting enterprises for these surveys is Statistics Finland´s Business register of enterprises and establishments. It contains information on around 300,000 enterprises and establishments. The frames targeted for BOP surveys (monthly and annual) are, however, structured on the basis of multiple sources. The official register contains a sub-set of consolidated enterprises and an indication of foreign ownership. Balance sheet data are acquired from the Finnish Tax Authority and are used for maintaining the survey frames. In addition, mergers and acquisitions are monitored in order to keep the survey frames up to date. The Financial Supervision Authority maintains the register of financial institutions. The properties of the respondent distributions in the Finnish economy do not suggest sampled survey solutions but rather cut-off census surveys. The respondents for the monthly BOP-survey are selected using a quantitative measure of the relative importance of enterprise in BOP statistics. The overall cut-off point (cumulative coverage) of the list of enterprises in the frame is set at some 85 % in descending order by size, which results in approximately 130 consolidated monthly respondents. The coverage in each item varies between 50 % and 100 %, and in regard to flow data, the coverage is in practice higher than in the stock data. The smaller enterprises with a nonzero coefficient within the frame are surveyed annually with a simpler questionnaire than in the monthly survey. The coverage of the monthly BOP/IIP data collection is relatively high. The MFI sector and investment funds are fully covered. All major insurance companies and pension funds are included in monthly survey. General government is covered monthly to almost full extent. For non-financial corporations, the monthly collection is estimated to cover some 80% of the sector's foreign assets and liabilities. Regarding the annual BOP-survey, the frame of the survey is based on the information on foreign ownership of resident enterprises in the Business register as well as on the register of enterprise groups. The annual survey is targeted to around 1,100 units (parent companies in Finland). The cut-off point is set at slightly above 90 per cent of the cumulative coverage of the main target stock variable in the frame. The cut-offs allow flexibility in the definition of the statistical unit, and the respondents may choose whether they prefer to report on a consolidated basis or by single enterprise. They are required to state which enterprises are covered by the consolidated report. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques CURRENT AND CAPITAL ACCOUNT Goods: The merchandise imports are valued according to the f.o.b. basis in the balance of payments statistics. The freight and insurance outlays paid to non-residents included in the c.i.f. value of imports in the foreign trade statistics are transferred to transport and insurance services in the balance of payments. The foreign trade statistics are adjusted for the value of the fuel used by the vessels and aircrafts. Services: The preliminary monthly data on international trade in services produced by Statistics Finland are based on quarterly forecasts which are produced on the basis of structural time series models (STMs). The quarterly values for services are estimated on the basis of their historical share in total foreign trade. In addition, time series modelling is applied in the process to judge the recent development of the trade in services. The aggregated quarterly estimates on services are divided to the monthly level. Data are revised when the quarterly data becomes available. The preliminary estimates relating to transportation and travel are replaced by the actual data, and estimates concerning other services are revised accordingly. Income (excl. investment income), current and capital transfers: The data on compensation of employees are available on an annual basis. The monthly and quarterly figures are estimated. The same applies to private current transfers and capital transfers. Owing to the small weight of these items, the simplest possible technique is used: they are assumed to be at the same level as in the previous year. Neither trends nor seasonal effects are calculated. The preliminary data on public transfers are available monthly in the central government accounts maintained by the State Treasury The estimates for seasonal effects in the final figures for public transfers are calculated on the basis of the aforementioned preliminary monthly data originally supplied by the State Treasury. FINANCIAL ACCOUNT (INCL. INVESTMENT INCOME) Direct investment and direct investment income: The results of the direct investment enterprises are estimated for the preliminary monthly statistics. The annual estimates are spread evenly to months. The figures related to the results of enterprises for the previous year are considered to be a proxy for the results in the current year. The developments in the global economy and any noteworthy changes in the population of direct investment enterprises are taken into account in the estimation as well. The monthly estimates of reinvested earnings are calculated by deducting monthly dividend payments from the monthly estimates of the results of direct investment enterprises. Since the data from the annual FDI surveys for the previous year are available in September, the estimates of reinvested earnings for the first six months of the current year are based on the annual survey information covering not the previous year but the year before that. The estimates for the current year are revised in September at the same time as the monthly estimates of the FDI income are revised to correspond to the annual data. As for the dividends distributed by foreign-owned enterprises in Finland, the monthly survey suffers from undercoverage. For this reason, the data on dividends of foreign-owned enterprises for May and June (traditionally, dividends are distributed in these months) have to be supplemented with estimates based on the new results of the annual survey of foreign direct investment in Finland for the previous year. Estimation of cross-border real estate transactions by households is based on historical annual data. The volume of this type of transaction has thus far been relatively insignificant in the Finnish BOP. Portfolio and other investment: The annual frame survey includes portfolio and other investment stocks (excluding trade credits) at the end of the year with an instrument but without a geographical breakdown. The stocks are disaggregated into monthly flows by subtracting the previous year's stock and dividing the residual evenly. A geographical distribution is derived from the monthly survey by using the data for the corresponding sector and month. Reporting agents are required to report trade credits broken down by euro area/extra-euro area. A more detailed country breakdown is estimated based on goods' export and import data. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Seasonal adjustment: Not done. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results CURRENT AND CAPITAL ACCOUNT For the compilation of investment income, a comparison is made of the estimated accrued interest on non-resident holdings of domestically issued central government bonds with data on coupon payments paid to non-residents on these securities by domestic custodians. The comparison is typically carried out at an annual level or even for longer periods, if necessary. Private current transfers data are cross-checked with EU transfer data made available by Eurostat. Most capital transfers statistics are based on the same source data. FINANCIAL ACCOUNT For the compilation of direct investment, the financial press and press releases issued by listed enterprises on large share purchases are monitored for merger and acquisition activity and other direct investment transactions. Where takeover transactions are identified, a special survey form is sent to obtain information on the transaction. The overall valuation changes for the securities, which can be derived as a residual item, are compared with changes in stock prices and interest rates. Regarding assets, securities holdings data reported by resident end-investors in the monthly survey can be verified using balance sheet information published by these respondents, although in most cases at longer intervals (quarterly/annually). 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revision studies are conducted routinely to inform statistical processes. Special attention is paid to the impact that the results of annual surveys have on the compiled monthly data, which may necessitate modifications to estimation methods for the following 12-month period in order to improve accuracy. The revision practice has been harmonised within the EU. Following the harmonised revision cycle, different vintages of time series are stored to facilitate revision analysis. Four vintages of key variables are available: first preliminary observations, second preliminary observations (data revised after one month), data revised nine months after the end of the reference year, and the current final observations. Time series of the impact of revisions on the published data are stored to monitor long-term trends in the revision patterns. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Service and support is available from the Statistics Finland on request.
