 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The analytical framework used by the NBRNM in compiling Central Bank Survey reflects concepts and principles from the IMF's Monetary and Financial Statistics Manual and Compilation Guide from 2016 (MFSMCG - IMF, 2016). Starting from June 2008, NBRNM applied a new methodology (IMF standard report forms) for compilation of monetary statistics. The time series of data for the period from January 2009 onward has been revised for the purposes of applying a new methodology in accordance with the new IMF Monetary and Financial Statistics Manual and Compilation Guide, 2016. The monetary base of the National Bank of the Republic of North Macedonia (NBRNM) is compiled according to the methodology set forth in the IMF "Monetary and Financial Statistics Manual and Compilation Guide". The definition of reserve money refers to money supply M0 and is composed of currency in circulation, banks and savings houses' accounts in NBRNM, banks' cash in vaults, banks' and savings houses' reserve requirements in Denars, banks' reserve requirements in foreign currency, compulsory deposit with the NBRNM as well as other financial corporations' accounts in NBRNM. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The sectorization adopted for Central Bank Survey is in line with the MFSM (consistent with the Classification of the financial instruments in the SNA 93 and ESA 95). Reporting units distinguish resident from nonresident counterparts, using the definition on residency prescribed in the Law on Foreign Exchange Operations (Official Gazette of the Republic of Macedonia No. 34/01, 49/01, 103/01, 51/03, 81/08, 24/11, 135/11, 188/13, 97/15, 153/15 and 23/16). Residency of institutional units is defined under the Law on Foreign Exchange Operations, where natural persons staying temporarily in the Republic of North Macedonia based on a valid residence permit or work permit for a period of at least six months are treated as residents of the country. (Such definition of residency of natural persons and its application in the process of creating statistical data is an exception to the recommendation given in the international statistical standards, according to which a criterion for determining residency is a stay or intention to stay in a country for a period of one year or more). The resident sector is divided into five sub sectors:(i) Non-financial corporations, covering public and other (private) nonfinancial corporations; (ii) Government, which is disaggregated to central government , social security's funds, local government, (iii) Depository corporations (NBRNM, banks and savings houses);(iv) Other financial corporations (Insurance companies, Pension funds and Other financial corporations) (v) Other resident sector including households(individuals and self-employed individuals) and Nonprofit institutions serving households. Scope of the data The current Central Bank Aggregates compiled by the NBRNM covers the NBRNM data and are broadly consistent with internationally accepted standards. 2.4.1 Valuation The general recommendation of the MFSMCG is to evaluate financial assets and liabilities based on market prices or market price equivalents. The exceptions to this rule is the valuation of deposits and loans (and most other accounts payable/receivable categories) and for the liabilities in form of shares and other equity. These principles are broadly followed in North Macedonia's Central Bank Aggregates. Monetary gold is valued monthly using the market price quoted at the London Stock Exchange; traded financial instruments are valued monthly using market prices. Conversion of foreign-currency denominated assets and liabilities into local currency is carried out using official exchange rates in effect at the balance sheet date. For this purpose, the NBRNM use the end-period midpoint of buying and selling rates quoted by the NBRNM. The official exchange rate is a weighted average rate determined by the inter-bank foreign exchange market conditions. Likewise, SDR denominated accounts are also converted into local currency using the end-of-period market exchange rate. Valuation adjustments are presented in the valuation accounts in the balance sheets of the NBRNM, but not separately identified in their analytical accounts. 2.4.2 Recording basis In Macedonia's Central Bank Survey, financial transactions are recorded on an accrual basis. Interest on financial assets or liabilities is accrued at the end of each month, and accrued interest is recorded under the underlying financial instruments consistent with MFSMCG recommendations. The MFSMCG also recommends the interest arrears to be included with the underlying instruments. The current practice of the NBRNM, is consistent with the MFSMCG. Financial transactions are recorded simultaneously between transactors in conformity with MFSMCG guidelines. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures A general principle in macroeconomic statistics is that data should be collected and compiled on a gross basis. For data presented on a net basis, the underlying gross data should also be shown. In North Macedonia's Central Bank Survey, assets and liabilities are presented on a gross or net basis in conformity with the MFSMCG guidelines. In particular, claims on a particular transactor or group of transactors are not netted against liabilities to that transactor or group. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The source data for compiling the Central Bank Survey are the chart of accounts prepared by the NBRNM's Financial, Accounting and Control Department Financial, on a monthly basis. These data are generated by an electronic general ledger system based on complete accounting records. With the implementation of the New charts of accounts for the NBRNM (January 2009), consistent with the International Accounting Standards, the banks and saving houses' reporting format, provides sufficient detail to compile monetary statistics in conformity with the MFSMCG. The source data are kept under continuous review to ensure that the data collection system remains comprehensive, reflects recent developments, and meets new data demands. These reviews take into account (i) ongoing revisions to the charts of accounts, (ii) concerns arising from the NBRNM's weekly liquidity planning meeting, and (iii) issues raised by other government agencies and other data users. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The source data, reported in accordance with NBRNM requirements, reasonably approximate the definitions, scope, classification, valuation, and timing of reporting needed for Central Bank Survey data. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The NBRNM' chart of accounts are required to become available eight days after the end of the reference month. These source data are provided sufficiently timely to allow for timely compilation of Central Bank Survey. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The NBRNM Central Bank Survey data compilers, in collaboration with other NBRNM units involved in source data collection, review the reporting system for its adequacy for providing reliable source data. Automated data validation procedures are employed to check the internal consistency of reported data. In addition, source data are also reviewed against those of previous periods to identify out-of-trend items. The identified data inconsistencies are investigated with the reporting units. However, questions about the adequacy of the processes in place for assessing the source data arose from data inconsistency and misclassification identified by users on several occasions. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The forms for reporting source data are designed to encourage use of computer programs, which enable prompt data processing. The potential for processing errors is minimized by the use of electronic reporting and processing procedures 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The procedure for delayed reporting of data is to carry forward the chart of accounts of the last month of the late reporting bank into the current month's statistics. So far, no reporting delays have occurred. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The accuracy and reliability of the reported source data for the NBRNM are checked against other sources or information; for instance, data on NBRNM's government bond holdings are checked against records of the Financial Market Operations Department. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data The NBRNM Central Bank Survey data compilers identify statistical discrepancies and, in collaboration with the Financial, Accounting and Control Department and Division of Supervision, Banking Regulations and Financial Stability investigate their causes. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Procedures are in place to investigate large and unexplained movements in monetary and credit aggregates to detect possible classification and sectorization errors. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses The NBRNM studies and analyzes revisions as part of its data quality control process. The NBRNM data compilers cross-check preliminary data with final data and investigate any significant discrepancies. The results of investigations and adjustments made in the data compiling process are taken into account in subsequent periods. In general, revisions tend to be small in magnitude. Regular revision policy of final data is applied at the end of each year. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Studies and analysis of revisions are undertaken ad hoc to measure, assess, and explain the source of revisions made to the data. These studies are internally documented and not made available to the public. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Statistics not regularly published are made available to interested users upon their request and approval by the Governor's Office. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Information on specific contact persons is posted on the NBRNM`s website for users to send comments, queries, and requests concerning any category of Central Bank Survey published by the NBRNM. Users frequently request assistance in the form of clarification and explanation of data through email, telephone, or mail, and the assistance is in general promptly provided. The NBRNM reviews closely comments, queries, and requests from general data users as well as from the NBRNM Council to obtain users' feedback. In particular, all user queries sent to the Governor's Office are distributed to concerned NBRNM departments. Actions that are taken to address these issues are reported to the Governor's Office to ensure that users' requests are addressed. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The NBRNM publishes a list of publications on its website. NBRNM publications are also provided to users upon request free of charge.
