 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The unit closely follows UNSD guidelines on compilation of International Merchandise Trade Statistics (IMTS). As a result, the unit has been closely following 1998 IMTS Concepts & Definitions and is now gradually implementing IMTS 2010 Concepts & Definitions . The unit also follows guidelines in IMTS Compilers Manual 2010 (IMTS–CM 2010). The statistics produced follows internationally accepted standards or good practices. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Goods are classified using Harmonized Commodity Description Coding System (HS). Up to April 2002 goods were classified using HS 1996 (6 digits) while HS 2002 (8 digits) came into effect in May 2002. As from May 2007, the Division of Customs Excise started using HS 2007, which is at 8 digits like HS 2002. HS 2012 was introduced in January 2012. Data is also processed using the Standard International Trade Classification (SITC Revision 4) and the Broad Economic Categories (BEC Revision 4) which are based on HS codes. Categorization used for publication of external trade statistics in Botswana includes the following. Exports by major commodity group: · Diamonds · Meat & Meat Products · Copper Nickel & Mates · Textiles · Salt & Soda Ash · Gold Exports referred to above combines both domestic exports and re-exports. Imports by major commodity group: · Food Beverages & Tobacco · Fuel · Chemicals & Rubber Products · Diamonds · Wood & Paper Products · Textiles & Footwear · Metals & Metal Products · Machinery Electrical Equipment · Vehicles Transport Equipment Imports are compiled by country of origin while exports are compiled by country of last known destination. Scope of the data Currently the only source of data for compilation of IMTS is Customs & Excise. The scope closely follows that as described in IMTS 2010 Concepts & Definitions and goods are recorded as they cross the border. Under imports, inclusions are; imports for home consumption as well as imports for inward processing and re-imports of goods that were previously exported by the country. Electricity, gas and goods received through postal or courier services as well as migrants' effects are also included in compilation of IMTS. Exports include domestic exports, re-exports and goods exported for outward processing. Nonmonetary gold or unwrought or semi-manufactured gold is included in compilation of IMTS. Exceptions to coverage Excluded from compilation are goods in transit and those going out for repair and servicing as well as goods exported or imported temporarily for return in an unaltered state. Goods under operational lease are also excluded from compilation of IMTS. Unrecorded activity Bunker oil is currently not recorded and as a result this is not included in IMTS compilation. Imports of water from South Africa (through pipeline) is also not recorded and as a result not included in compilation of IMTS. The unit is aware of the significance of using surveys to cater for data not captured through customs border posts, however this is currently hindered by human constraints. 2.4.1 Valuation Imports are valued Cost, Insurance, Freight (CIF) and exports are valued Free on Board (FOB). With regard to imports, data can also be given in FOB in case of need and freight and insurance provided separately. 2.4.2 Recording basis Customs Excise has no threshold. As a result all trade is recorded as per the invoice value or item value. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures In case of goods that are packaged, both gross and net values are provided and net value is used in compilation of IMTS 3.1.1 Source data collection programs International Merchandise Trade Statistics (IMTS) are compiled by the IMTS Unit of Statistics Botswana. Trade Statistics are compiled from administrative documents of the Division of Customs & Excise, a division in the Botswana Unified Revenue Service (BURS). Customs & Excise Division does all data capture using ASYCUDA++ and sends data to IMTS Unit electronically. At IMTS unit, data is loaded into EUROTRACE for Windows for processing. Validation checks are incorporated into the EUROTRACE system for initial editing during data loading. As these validation checks are not exhaustive, further editing is done outside EUROTRACE using MS Access before further analysis leading to dissemination, can be carried out. The declarations are made by importers and exporters at the different Ports of Entry. Data is also captured by traders and Clearing Agents directly, through Direct Trader Input (DTI) where ASYCUDA++ is installed at Clearing Agents offices with a connection to the Division of Customs Excise Servers. At the end of each month, data from the Division of Customs & Excise's various Ports of Entry, as well as that captured directly by traders and clearing agents is merged into one file and sent to IMTS Unit for data processing and dissemination. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Definitions, scope, classifications and time of recording for source data is as per World Customs Organization (WCO) standards on Customs & Excise side and UNSD guidelines on statistics side. Compilation has been following IMTS 1998 Concepts Definitions and IMTS 2010 Concepts Definitions are gradually being implemented. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Source data is availed to Statistics Botswana within 15 days of the preceding month from computerized ports, which form about 99 percent of total expected monthly trade in value terms. The remaining trade from manual border posts, is availed within four weeks from the reference month. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Currently no census or sample surveys are carried out to check the correctness of coverage, even though Statistics Botswana is aware that some commodities are not recorded. Efforts are being made to fast tract the issue. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Trade Statistics Domain was set up after thorough consultation with Customs Excise with regard to explanations of Customs Procedure Codes (CPC) so as to be sure that the Domain will contain what is necessary for compilation of IMTS according to UNSD guide lines. Trade type was also determined after discussions with other statistical offices to fully understand the customs procedures involved in the different trade types. Date of registration of the transaction is used to determine the date on which the commodities crossed the border. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Currently no data adjustments or transformations are done. Data is taken as reported by traders. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Data validation is done through checking the data series for irregular figures, checking common commodities and checking for commodities that are not expected to be traded by Botswana. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data If noticed, discrepancies are discussed with Customs & Excise, invoices are requested for where there is need and traders are also involved where there is need. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Indicators are checked for irregular figures like unusually high or low figures and these are verified with customs officials and traders. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Currently there are no revision studies carried out. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses As updates are made on the data, where these are noticeable comments are included to guide users as to where updates have been made and the reasons why. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Data can be availed according to user specifications.
