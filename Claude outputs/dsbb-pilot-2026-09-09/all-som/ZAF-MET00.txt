 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The compilation of merchandise trade statistics broadly follows and is guided by the recommendations of UN IMTS 2010. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Trade statistics are reported on actual customs value declared and classified under Harmonized System (HS) and no estimates of any kind are used. Scope of the data South Africa currently compiles merchandise trade statistics from all customs declarations using the Strict Special Trade System. Exceptions to coverage N/A Unrecorded activity Trade statistics are reported on actual customs value declared and no estimates of any kind are used. 2.4.1 Valuation Exports and imports are valued free on board (FOB). 2.4.2 Recording basis Requirements for accrual accounting are met at declaration stage. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Yes 3.1.1 Source data collection programs All data used to compile the merchandise trade statistics is sourced from the customs declarations and refer to actual customs values. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The customs declaration process follows the WTO, WCO and UN guidelines. Goods are classified in accordance with the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS). All values are reported on Free on Board (FOB) basis in South African Rands. Time of recording is based on when the customs declarations are accepted by the customs system and passed all the system validations. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Time of recording is based on when the customs declarations are accepted by the customs system and passed all the system validations. Data is updated in real-time and the IMTS related rules are applied to the data daily. 3.2.1 Source data assessment All customs declarations are actual data and not sampled and are validated using various systematic risk rules. Flags are raised for investigation if risk rules are triggered. Investigators will then be provided with the information to conduct an audit on the declaration. Further checks are conducted by the data verification team for reasonableness over and above the automated risk rules validation. Queries are sent to traders should any anomalies be identified. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Currently statistical techniques involve using basic methods such as mean, median and mode tests to detect anomalies which are then investigated and verified. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures No adjustments are made to the data. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The current process of verifying the data is largely focused on identifying outliers and exceptions. Supporting documentations are sourced to verify the data. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Make use of unit values to identify discrepancies at commodity line-item level. Anomalies are queried and traders must submit VoC to correct the declaration. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Statistical discrepancies are highlighted using various statistical analyses tools. Asymmetry exercises are also conducted to determine any discrepancies. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revised trade statistics are published monthly due to changes made to declarations through VoCs. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Revised numbers from the previous month are published on the website at the same time as the preliminary number of the publishing month. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Requests for non-confidential trade statistics are considered and processed accordingly, request of special data by third are shared after complying with relevant criteria, e,g completion of Declaration of Authority (DOA). 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Queries on any trade statistics related matters can be sent to tradestatistics@sars.gov.za 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs All trade statistics related documents are available on the SARS website and any queries for additional information should be emailed to tradestatistics@sars.gov.za
