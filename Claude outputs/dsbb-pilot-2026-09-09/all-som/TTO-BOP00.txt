 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The Central Bank of Trinidad and Tobago (the Bank), in collaboration with the Central Statistical Office, is officially responsible for compiling Trinidad and Tobago’s balance of payments statistics. Data are collected from administrative sources including the Central Statistical Office, the Central Bank, the Central Government and deposit-taking corporations as well as via enterprise surveys of public and private corporations. Data are compiled on a quarterly basis with a 5-7 month lag, and published in the Bank’s serial publications and on the website. The statistics are reported in U.S. dollars. The classification and compilation of the balance of payments follow the guidelines recommended in the sixth edition of the IMF’s Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6). Annual balance of payments data have been published by the CSO covering data from 1951 to 1987 and by the Bank from 1988. From 1989 onwards, the data are compiled and disseminated on a quarterly basis and published quarterly in the Bank’s Economic Bulletin and Monetary Policy Report and annually in the Annual Economic Survey. Prior to 1989 the balance of payments data were compiled only on an annual basis in millions of Trinidad and Tobago dollars. The Balance of Payments Statistics of Trinidad and Tobago are published with a 5-7 month lag, but key elements such as the international reserves are published monthly. The analytical framework divides the transactions between the Current Account, the Capital Account and Financial Account. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Economic transactions are largely classified according to international standards and conventions described in BPM6. 2.2.1 Scope Economic transactions are largely classified according to international standards and conventions described in BPM6. Scope of the data Unit Coverage: The Balance of Payments summarizes economic transactions between residents of Trinidad and Tobago and nonresidents during a specific time period. The current account covers transactions in goods, services, primary income and secondary income (current transfers). The capital account covers capital transfers and transactions in non-produced, non-financial assets. The financial account covers transactions in financial assets and liabilities. Exceptions to coverage No attempts are made to include estimates for informal transactions or data on shuttle trade. Unrecorded activity No attempts are made to include estimates for informal transactions or data on shuttle trade. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: The valuation generally used is that agreed upon between two transactors and is, as such, recorded at market price. Conversion to unit of account: The BOP is compiled in U.S. dollars; transactions are converted from Trinidad and Tobago dollars to U.S. dollars using the exchange rate prevailing at the time of the transaction. When the actual date of the transaction is not available, the average exchange rate for the reporting period is used. 2.4.2 Recording basis The Balance of Payments is recorded as close to an accrual basis as possible. The accrual principle is mainly used with the exception of Central Government external debt data for which accrued interest is not included. Positions for Central Government external debt data are also valued at nominal values. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The Central Bank of Trinidad and Tobago (the Bank), in collaboration with the Central Statistical Office, is officially responsible for compiling Trinidad and Tobago’s balance of payments statistics. Data are collected from administrative sources including the Central Statistical Office, the Central Bank, the Central Government and deposit-taking corporations as well as via enterprise surveys of public and private corporations. Current Account Goods: Administrative data from the Central Statistical Office and energy companies. Services: Categories are captured via different methods as listed below: Transport: Surveys of national and foreign-owned airlines, shipping lines and port authorities. Travel: Administrative and survey data from the Central Statistical Office, commercial banks and surveys of tertiary institutions. Commercial services: Mostly collected through enterprise surveys and complemented by administrative data sources. Government services: Data from the Central Government and international agencies. Primary Income : Compensation of employees: Surveys of international organizations, national and foreign-owned carriers and enterprises. Investment Income: Administrative data from the Central Bank, the Central Government, the Trinidad and Tobago Stock Exchange, deposit-taking corporations and enterprise surveys. Secondary Income : Enterprise surveys and administrative data from deposit-taking corporations and the Central Government. Capital Account : Administrative data and enterprise surveys. Direct investment : Enterprise surveys and administrative data from deposit-taking corporations. Portfolio investment : Enterprise surveys as well as administrative data from deposit-taking corporations, the Trinidad and Tobago Stock Exchange and the Central Bank. Other investment : Enterprise surveys as well as administrative data from deposit-taking corporations and the Central Bank. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Quarterly surveys are requested to be returned no later than one month following the end of the reference quarter. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Ongoing efforts are being made to strengthen assessment procedures at all stages of data collection and compilation. Formal data verification checks are conducted routinely to ensure data consistency. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Data on energy exports and fuel imports are sourced directly from energy companies. Non-energy exports and non-fuel imports are sourced from the Central Statistical Office. Adjustments have been made to the trade data in accordance with IMF's definitions of merchandise namely: The fee received for processing services is included as manufacturing services on physical inputs owned by others. Imports are converted from a c.i.f. basis to a f.o.b. basis. The methodology to estimate the cif/fob margins employs a range of source data, including OECD data on transport costs of merchandise trade, IMTS, and some partner country data. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Survey submissions of larger respondents particularly from the energy sector are reviewed for inconsistencies between transactions and stocks. Data on surveys for the insurance companies are validated with the stocks reported in its regulatory submissions. There are procedures for assessing and checking the consistency of balance of payments data for consistency with other macroeconomic datasets as well as with external data such as the BIS mirror data. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs There are ongoing efforts to strengthen the assessment procedures at all stages of data collection and compilation. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Data for the current year are revised on an ongoing basis. Data for previous periods are usually revised on an ad-hoc basis. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished but non-confidential data are not available to users without approval from the Deputy Governor.
