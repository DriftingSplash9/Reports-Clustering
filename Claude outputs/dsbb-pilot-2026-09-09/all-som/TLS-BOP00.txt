 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The Central Bank of Timor-Leste (CBTL) is responsible for compiling and disseminating balance of payments data. These data, in principle, cover all transactions of the Timor-Leste’ resident sector with the nonresidents (see however 2.2.1.2). The published data are expressed in millions of US dollars. The BOP statistics are broadly consistent with the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual, Sixth Edition (BPM6). The item “other primary income” representing income of Timor-Leste for allowing nonresidents to use its natural resources in the Joint Petroleum Development Area (JPDA). For individuals, the following are in principle considered residents of Timor-Leste: (a) Timor-Leste nationals living here; (b) all those who have been residing in the country for at least one year, regardless of their nationality. Exceptions are diplomats and civilian staff of foreign embassies located in Timor-Leste and international military personnel; (c) Timorese nationals who are abroad for study or medical treatment regardless of their length of stay abroad; (d) Timorese diplomats and civilian staff of Timor-Leste embassies abroad; (e) Timorese working abroad for less than one year; and (f) civilian staff of international organizations located in Timor-Leste, if they have been living/working in Timor-Leste for at least one year. Companies operating or undertaking production activities within Timor-Leste are residents of the Timor-Leste economy, regardless of the nationality of their owners. All economic-producing units operating in the Timor Sea exclusive territory [1] are treated as residents of Timor-Leste, regardless of their owners. On the other hand, there is a Joint Petroleum Development Area or JPDA, which is a joint territory of Australia and Timor-Leste. In the BOP of Timor-Leste, all producing units operating in the JPDA are treated as nonresidents of Timor-Leste since they are effectively operated by the other economy.& It is noted that Timor-Leste’s role in the JPDA activities is as owner of the natural resources and not as a producer. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The Central Bank of Timor – Leste BOP statistics are classified according to the standard components of the BPM6 and with an analytical presentation showing the balance of payments position, current account balance, and capital and financial account balance. Major Classifications The standard components are comprised of two main groups of accounts: The current account pertains to goods and services, Primary income, and Secondary income. The capital and financial account, and financial assets and liabilities. Most items entered in the current account of the standard components should show gross debits and credits. Most entries in the capital and financial account should be made on a net basis; that is, each component should be shown only as a credit or a debit. 2.2.1 Scope The Central Bank of Timor – Leste BOP statistics are classified according to the standard components of the BPM6 and with an analytical presentation showing the balance of payments position, current account balance, and capital and financial account balance. Scope of the data The balance of payments data, in principle, cover all transactions of the Timor-Leste with the rest of the world (see, however, also 2.2.1.2.). Exceptions to coverage Due to lack of data sources, oil exports are currently not included in goods exports (see also 9.2.1 Short-term Plans). Adjustments are made for the coverage of goods imports and exports, amongst others based on the government budget execution. Estimates are made for travel and compensation of employees. Adjustments are also made in data on professional workers, based on data from Budget execution. 2.4.1 Valuation For the balance of payments compilation, the transactions are valued at market prices where they are available. The valuation of goods imports and exports are free on board (f.o.b.). 2.4.2 Recording basis The balance of payments data are, in principle, compiled on an accrual basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures In line with the recommendations of BPM6, current and capital account transactions are recorded on a gross basis while financial account transactions are recorded separately for the individual asset and liability components. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Several diverse source data sets are used for compiling the balance of payments data. These include (1) Customs data for estimating merchandise trade, which are provided by the Directorate of National Statistics (DNE); (2) Quarterly Donor and Enterprise Surveys conducted by the CBTL for estimating foreign donor grants and foreign direct investment (FDI); (3) Central bank and commercial bank data for estimating foreign exchange transactions; (4) Immigration data for estimating travel credit and debit (5) Government budget execution data compiled by the CBTL for compiling services and estimating under coverage of imports of goods and services; and (6) CBTL’s international reserves data. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The source data are generally provided at a timely basis for Quarterly BOP publication in line with the GDDS recommendation. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data are checked for plausibility. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Survey data are adjusted for non-responses and other factors. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Staff involved in producing balance of payments data monitor developments in the errors and omissions item and seek to understand them, e.g., by cross-checks between entries in the current, capital, and financial accounts. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses No revision studies are done.
