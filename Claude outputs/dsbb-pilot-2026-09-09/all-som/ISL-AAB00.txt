 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The concepts and definitions used by the CBI to compile monetary statistics broadly conform to the guidelines outlined in the Monetary and Financial Statistics Manual and Compilation Guide (MFSMCG) . The monthly monetary survey compiled by the CBI (Accounts of the Banking System) is equivalent to the MFSMCG's Depository Corporations Survey (DCS). It presents measures of liquidity and its counterparts. There are four measures of monetary aggregates—M1, M2, M3, and M3+. The CBI term M3+ as broad money. M1 is composed of currency in circulation (outside commercial banks and savings banks) plus current accounts (current accounts are equivalent to transferable deposits). Current accounts comprises accounts in ISK and foreign currency-linked accounts. M2 comprises M1 plus general savings accounts, of which free savings accounts and foreign currency accounts owned by residents. M3 is defined as M2 plus time deposits. Time deposits comprises indexed deposits (fixed-term deposits indexed to the CPI), holiday pay accounts, supplementary pension deposits (restricted for withdrawal until the holder reaches the age of 60) and other time deposits. M3+ is calculated as M3 plus domestic short term bonds (≤ 1 year) issued by DMB‘s plus money market fund shares net of money market rate accounts. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The residency criterion to distinguish between domestic and external accounts for the compilation of monetary statistics is in line with SNA 2008 and ESA 2010. The sectors are classified as follows: S.11 Non-financial companies S.12 Financial companies S.121 CBI S.122 Deposit money banks S.122X Deposit money banks in winding-up proceedings or composition of creditors negotiations S.123 Money market funds (MMF) S.124 Other mutual and investment funds (other than MMF) S.125 Other financial intermediaries S.125X Other financial intermediaries in winding-up proceedings or composition of creditors negotiations S.126 Financial auxiliaries S.127 Captive financial institutions and money lenders. S.128 Insurance companies S.129 Pension funds S.13 General government S.1311 Treasury and govt. institutions S.1313 Municipalities S.14 Households S.15 Non-profit institutions serving households S.2 Non-residents The sectorization of institutional units in Iceland is mostly in line with the SNA2008 and ESA 2010 . Scope of the data The scope of monetary statistics is broadly consistent with guidelines in the MFSMCG. The financial corporations sector in Iceland consists of the CBI, domestic deposit money banks, and savings banks. The data for collapsed banks is not included in the monetary statistics of DMB´s. Loans are assessed at claim value; Loans purchased at a discount in autumn 2008 are entered at claim value instead of book value, as was done previously. The change is made retroactively; therefore, loans from 2008 are republished according to the new methodology. Exceptions to coverage The data for collapsed banks is not included in the monetary statistics of DMB´s. 2.4.2 Recording basis Monetary statistics are compiled on an accrual basis, in line with the recommendations of the MFSMCG, SNA2008 and ESA 2010 . 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures In line with the general principles of the MFSMCG, SNA 2008 and ESA 2010 , assets and liabilities of the CBI and the DMBs are collected and subsequently compiled on a gross basis. Claims on particular transactors are not netted against liabilities to those transactors. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data are submitted directly to the Central Bank by all the DMBs. The report for each DMB is a complete balance sheet disaggregated by instrument, currencies and counterparty. The DMBs generally submit data in the form of excel tables. The report forms contain sufficient detail to classify sectors and instruments in line with the MFSMCG. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The source data broadly approximate the required definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording. No supplementary data sources are used in compiling monetary statistics. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness DMBs submit balance sheet data on the previous month to the CBI on the 11th day of each month. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The CBI undertakes several steps to validate the accuracy of data. Vertical checks are made within each report. Any unusual movements in any category are followed-up for further information and explanation. If any misreporting is noticed, the DMBs are required to correct the mistake and resubmit the data. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The compilation of monetary statistics is highly automated. Electronic data reporting, data processing, and validation procedures contribute to the production of accurate and timely monetary statistics. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The vertical check procedures compare the detailed data reported. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data The CBI investigates the nature and origin of any statistical discrepancies observed in the intermediate data. In the event any noticeable changes are observed in the data submitted by the DMBs, the compiler contacts the respective DMB immediately and seeks explanation for the sudden movements. If the movements are caused by misreporting, the DMBs are required to resubmit the revised data. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Any reporting errors and out-of-trend reporting by the DMBs are immediately investigated and addressed. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses No formal revision studies or documentation exist.
