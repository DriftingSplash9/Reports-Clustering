 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Changes in inventories : they are measured by the value of the entries into inventories less the value of withdrawals and the value of any recurrent losses of goods held in inventories. Compensation of employees : it is defined as the total remuneration, in cash or in kind, payable by an employer to an employee in return for work done by the latter during the reference period. It is broken down into: wages and salaries and employers' social contributions . Employee : it is defined as person who, by agreement, works for a resident institutional unit and receives a remuneration recorded as compensation of employees. «Employees» correspond to the International Labour Organization definition of «paid employment». The relationship of employer to employee exists when there is a contract, which may be formal or informal, between an enterprise and a person, entered into voluntarily by both parties, whereby the person works for the enterprise in return for remuneration in cash or in kind. Employment : covers all persons (employees or self-employed persons) engaged in productive activity that falls within the production boundary of the national accounts. Exports of goods and services : consist of transactions in goods and services (sales, barter, gifts or donations) from residents to non-residents. Final consumption expenditure : consists of the expenditure incurred by resident institutional units on goods and services that are used for the direct satisfaction of individual or collective needs. The institutional sectors that incur expenditure on final consumption are Households , Non-Profit Institutions Serving Households and General Government . Full-time equivalent employment : equals the number of full-time equivalent jobs, is defined as total hours worked divided by the average annual number of hours worked in full-time within the economic territory. Gross value added : it is the balancing item of the production account and it is the net result of the output at basic prices less the intermediate consumption at acquisition prices. Its components are: compensation of employees, other taxes on production less subsidies, net mixed income, net operating surplus and fixed capital consumption. Gross domestic product at market prices (GDPmp) ( expenditure side ): it is the sum of final consumption expenditure on the economic territory by households and private non-profit institutions and general government, gross fixed capital formation , change in inventories and the difference between exports and imports of goods and services. Gross Domestic Product at market prices (GDPmp) ( income side ): it is the sum of compensation of employees , gross operating surplus of the economy, and taxes linked to production and imports less subsidies . Gross Domestic Product at market prices (GDPmp) (output side): it is the difference between output of goods and services and intermediate consumption plus tax on products less subsidies. Gross Domestic Product (GDP) at market price : it is the final result of productive activity result of the resident producers. It can be defined in three ways: a) Production approach: GDP is equal to the sum of the gross added values by institutional sectors or by all activities, plus the taxes less the subsidies over products (that are not assigned to the sectors nor the industries). It is also the balancing item of the production account at the level of the total economy. b) Expenditure approach: GDP is the total of final expenditures made in either consuming the final output of the economy or in investment, plus exports less imports of goods and services. c) Income approach: GDP is the total of all incomes earned in the process of producing goods and services plus taxes on production and imports less subsidies. Gross fixed capital formation : consists of resident producers acquisitions less disposals of fixed assets, tangible fixed asset or intellectual property products, during a given period plus certain additions to the value of non-produced assets realized by the productive activity of producer or institutional units. Fixed assets are produced assets used in production for more than one year. Imports of goods and services : consist of transactions in goods and services (purchases, barter and gifts) from non-residents to residents. Job: it is defined as an explicit or implicit contract between a person (who can live in other economic territory) and a resident institutional unit to perform work in return for compensation for a defined period or until further notice. Job concept differs from employment since a person (employment) can carry out more than a job; one after another, during a given period (usually a week), or in parallel, as it happens when someone has a job during the day and another one at night. For measuring labour input to domestic economic activity, only the residence of the producer institutional unit is relevant, because resident producers alone contribute to gross domestic product. Operating Surplus : it is the balance item of the generation of income account. It corresponds to the income received by the rest of production units, not integrated in mixed income due to participation in the productive process. In the case of household unincorporated companies, the balancing item of the operating account implicitly has an element that corresponds to the payment of the work carried out by the owner or his/her family members and that cannot be distinguished from the benefits as entrepreneur. In this case it is Mixed Income. Self-employed persons : they are defined as persons who are the sole owners, or joint owners, of the unincorporated enterprises in which they work, excluding those unincorporated enterprises that are classified as quasi-corporations . Subsides : they are current unrequited payments which general government or the institutions of the European Union make to resident producers. Taxes on production and imports : they consist of compulsory, unrequited payments, in cash or in kind, which are levied by general government, or by the institutions of the European Union, in respect of the production and importation of goods and services, the employment of labour, the ownership or use of land, buildings or other assets used in production. Such taxes are payable irrespective of profits made. They are comprised of taxes on products and other taxes on production. Total hours worked : they represent the aggregate number of hours actually worked as employee or self-employee person during the accounting period, when their output is within the production boundary. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classifications used are those that appear in the ESA-2010 manual: In the case of the gross added value, employment as well as compensation of employees and its disaggregation between wages and salaries and employers' social contributions , branches of activity are grouped and coded in reference to the National Classification of National Activities 2009 (CNAE-2009). Scope of the data Quarterly Spanish National Accounts (CNTR) is a short-term synthesis statistical operation whose main objective is to provide a coherent quantitative behavioural description of the Spanish economy as a whole. CNTR constitutes a sub-system of the Spanish National Accounts that is fully integrated methodologically and quantitatively: From a conceptual point of view, it adopts the methodology established in the European System Accounts 2010 (ESA 2010), Regulation of the Council 549/2013. In this sense, the concepts, valuation principles and structures are the same as the ones used for the Annual Spanish National Accounts. There are, however, some special features due to the time interval they cover. From a numerical point of view, the aggregation of the quarterly estimates. For the quarters corresponding to any year, it coincides with the estimate of the Annual Spanish National Accounts for that year. Therefore, there is also a quantitative coherence between the annual and quarterly accounts. Results of the CNTR are the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) and its components from the perspectives of supply, demand and income as well as employment and some important aggregates regarding the current accounts of the national economy and rest of the world, as Gross National Income and Gross National Disposable Income. CNTR estimates on GDP and GDP components and aggregates of the current accounts of the national economy are carried out at current prices in terms of linked volume and average prices of the previous year (in case of aggregates of the income approach of the GDP and the current accounts of the national economy, only at current prices) and, in terms of gross and adjusted seasonality and calendar effects. Furthermore, auxiliary indicators are collected: contribution to GDP growth, productivity of the work factor, unit labour costs, pay per wage earner and wage-earner rate. The scope of the data is: - Transactions coverage: The production boundary is in accordance with ESA-2010. The data cover the entire economy. - Geographic coverage: Spanish economic territory. 2.4.1 Valuation The GDP is valued at market prices. In the supply side: all transactions are valued at basic prices and intermediate consumption at purchaser's prices. In the demand side: household final consumption expenditure, gross fixed capital formation and inventories are valued at purchaser's prices. Imports and exports of goods are valued free on board (fob) and imports and exports of services are valued at purchaser's and basic prices respectively. Employment is measured in number of persons, jobs, full-time equivalent Jobs and hours worked. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: all economic transactions are recorded on accrual basis. Recording period: calendar quarters. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Production approach: the basic sources of information are surveys on industrial production, services activities agricultural output and other administrative data. Net taxes and VAT are obtained from administrative records. Expenditure approach: information provided by the household's expenditure survey is used to estimate household final consumption expenditure, complemented by the supply-based data on turnover of companies such as retail trade index, service index with available information by types of goods and other information from administrative records. Available consumption of capital goods is used to estimate gross fixed capital formation of capital. Administrative data and other information on construction activity is used to compile the gross fixed capital formation of construction. Data on exports and imports of goods and services come from the ITGS and ITSS data and estimates of General Government activities integrating the results of the General Government Accounts provided by the Audit Office. Income approach and employment estimates: information provided by the labor force and wages survey as well as other administrative data and business surveys are used to compile the compensation of employees as well as the employment estimation in national accounts terms. Net taxes on production and imports are compiled integrating the General Government Accounts and using other administrative records. Aggregates of the current account of the national economy come from the rest of the world accounts/Balance of Payments data (where source of very different nature are used: financial statements of financial intermediaries and insurance companies, administrative data, etc.). 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Selection of indicators: as a general principle, the selection of indicators follows, as close as possible, the criteria, coverage and definitions used by the annual national accounts, as well as a high correlation with the magnitude that they try to distribute quarterly. Minimum temporal delay, enough length, future length, future availability, small forecast errors and statistical quality are also taken into account. 3.2.1 Source data assessment 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The basic approach used in the CNTR is the temporal disaggregation of the annual figures of the national accounts using the high-frequency indicators above mentioned, by means of the Chow-Lin method mainly (see Chow, G.C. and Lin, A.L. (1971) "Best linear unbiased distribution and extrapolation of economic time series by related series", The Review of Economics and Statistics, n. 53, p. 471-476). Multivariate methods are also used (e.g. multivariate Denton method). Regarding the following operations: Exports of goods and services. Imports of goods and services. Final consumption expenditure by government Taxes and subsidies Financial industries direct information is used. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Base year and update cycle: 2020 is the reference year and it is updated according to ESA-2010 rules. Seasonal adjustment: the seasonal adjustment is applied to the final series using an ARIMA model-based procedure (programs JDemetra+ 2.2.2 and TRAMO and SEATS). The CNTR compiles two types of estimations: raw data (or unadjusted), and adjusted from seasonal and calendar effects. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs There is no statistical discrepancy in CNTR. The initial balanced quarterly estimates of the macroeconomic aggregates are obtained from automatic methods (like multivariate Denton method). The final version is the result of a control and debugging process, in which the following analysis and tests are carried out: Comparison of the quarterly aggregates obtained from the available short-term information. Consistency analysis and interpretation of some indexes (deflators, productivity, unit costs etc). The study of the revisions of current data in comparison with the previous quarterly data.
