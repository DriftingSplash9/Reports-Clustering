 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical Framework: The Irish National Accounts are compiled in accordance with the rules of the European System of Accounts 2010 ( ESA 2010 ) https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/documents/3859598/5925693/KS-02-13-269-EN.PDF/44cd9d01-bc 64-40e5-bd40-d17df0c69334 and the Eurostat Handbook on prices and volume measures in national accounts 2016 Edition https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/documents/3859598/7152852/KS-GQ-14-005-EN-N.pdf/839297d1- 3456-487b-8788-24e47b7d98b2 Quarterly data are available for reference quarters since the beginning of 1995 (i.e. Q1 1995 onwards) and are compiled in accordance with the Eurostat Handbook on Quarterly National Accounts 2013 Edition https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/documents/3859598/5936013/KS-GQ-13-004-EN.PDF/3544793c-0bde- 4381-a7ad-a5cfe5d8c8d0 and taking into account the conceptual and practical guidance contained in the IMF Quarterly National Accounts Manual 2017 edition https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/qna/pdf/2017/QNAManual2017text.pdf Definition: GDP measures the market value of the goods and services produced by Irish resident units. The expenditure-based GDP measure is broken down into the following categories: Personal consumption of goods and services, Net expenditure by Central and Local Government on current goods and services, Gross domestic fixed capital formation, Value of physical changes in stocks Exports of goods and services Imports of goods and services. The categories Personal consumption and Government expenditure are defined slightly differently to their ESA2010 counterparts because of the way social benefits-in-kind, paid for by Government, are allocated. In the Irish accounts these benefits in kind are classified as Personal consumption or Government expenditure depending on the amount of freedom households have to source the particular good or service. However, the combined totals of Personal consumption and Government expenditure correspond to Final Consumption Expenditure, as defined in ESA2010. Changes in stocks of agricultural breeding animals that had been classified up to NIE 2020 (i.e. the Annual National Accounts in respect of year 2020) published in July 2021 as stock changes were reclassified to be included in Gross fixed capital formation for Annual National Accounts 2021 (ANA 2021) published July 2022, to accord with ESA 2010 accounting rules. The current and constant price output-based estimates of GDP are compiled at a detailed level using Nace Rev 2 industrial activity classification, at A10 and A38 sectoral breakdown at Basic Prices for the Annual National Accounts (ANA) (formerly known as the National Income & Expenditure [NIE] accounts) statistical publication and by A10 sectoral breakdown at Basic Prices for the Quarterly National Accounts releases (QNAs). Compensation of Employees, Wages & Salaries, Employers' Social Contributions, Net Operating Surplus & Net Mixed Income, Consumption of Fixed Capital and Gross Value Added (GVA) data at Current Basic Prices breakdowns at NACE A64 level are also routinely published as part of the ANA. However, some accounts of public sector entities are still compiled on a cash basis. These figures are time adjusted where such information is available to make this feasible, otherwise are used without adjustment. Imports and exports are recorded when change of ownership takes place. Recording Period: Calendar quarters. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures 3.1.1 Source data collection programs As far as possible, the data sources and methodology used for the quarterly estimates are aligned with those used for the annual accounts. In a small number of instances appropriate volume indicators are not available and the values are generated by interpolation/extrapolation of the annual series. In the case of agricultural output and rent of dwellings, constant price value added is calculated by subtracting intermediate consumption from estimated output, both of which are separately deflated. Agriculture, Forestry and Fishing: The volume value of agriculture is compiled by double deflation. For forestry, the quarterly indicator is interpolated from the annual data. For fishing, a volume index based on sea fish landings is applied to the previous year GVA. Industry: The gross value added of manufacturing industry is calculated by applying monthly volume of production indices to the previous year's quarterly gross value added, including royalties. Royalties are separately deflated using the export price index and then deducted. Construction: Changes in the volume of investment in construction projects are used to estimate the output of the building and construction branch. Distribution/Transport/Hotels & Restaurants: The methods used for distribution are similar to those used for the annual calculations. Volume indicators are calculated by deflating retail sales by appropriate price indices. These are applied to previous year value added. Calculations are undertaken at the most detailed level possible. Most of the output measures used for the annual calculations for Transport are also available on a quarterly basis. These include data on the quantum of passenger and freight movements. Information & Communications: For Information & Communications the main data source is a direct survey inquiry to the suppliers, use of EHECS (CSO Earnings, Hours & Employment Costs survey) data and with additional data and trend estimation for some residual components. Public Administration & Defence: The quarterly estimate of value added for public administration and defence is calculated based on the employment trends in the public sector. No adjustment is made for changes in labour productivity. The other services categories cover a variety of service activities, some of which are surveyed on a quarterly basis by the CSO. For some services, quarterly information on outputs is not directly available. In most of these cases, quarterly estimates are derived using trends in related/proxy indicators. Taxes and subsidies: Taxes and subsidies are deflated at a detailed level using appropriate price indices. Receipts from value added tax (VAT) and excise taxes are recorded on an estimated accruals (time adjusted cash) basis. Expenditure Approach (Constant prices) The various components of the expenditure-based measure of GDP are defined in the Annual National Accounts publication (ANA). The methods used for the quarterly series are similar to those used for the annual where possible but in some cases the same basic data are not available and alternative methods have to be applied. Household final consumption expenditure: This is first estimated, as described above, at current prices using CSO data, such as the Retail Sales Indices (RSI), together with some direct inquiries. Administrative data sources are used for some goods such as fuel and power products and motor vehicles. Constant price estimates are obtained using appropriate price indices such as components of the Consumer Price Index (CPI) and the quarterly earnings indices. This category also includes the final consumption expenditure of Non-profit institutions serving households (NPISH). Final consumption expenditure of general government: This is estimated using public accounting records. Constant price estimates of employee's remuneration are derived by extrapolating previous year estimates using an index of employment or using appropriate deflators for wages. Net expenditure on goods and services is deflated partly by consumer price indices and partly by wholesale price indices. Gross fixed capital formation: This includes expenditure on building and construction work and machinery and equipment. Current price estimates of investment in building and construction are calculated using the information available from the Department of Housing, Local Government & Heritage and quarterly returns on voted capital expenditure. Quarterly estimates of commercial vehicles used in the business sector are based on administrative data. Estimates of other machinery and equipment are made by reference to import statistics and the CSO Quarterly Capital Assets in Industry Inquiry. Current estimates are deflated to the previous year's prices for the purpose of chain linking using appropriate price indices such as components of the Wholesale Price Index (WPI), Producer Price Indices, Capital Goods Price Indices and indices for construction activity. Changes in inventories: This item measures the actual value change in stocks between the beginning and end of quarter adjusted for any changes in the prices of the underlying commodities. Constant price estimates are calculated by deflating the current estimates using relevant price indices (e.g. producer price indices) and price data on agricultural and EU intervention stocks. Some adjustments have been made to the stocks values to improve the coherence of the quarterly accounts. These are judgmental adjustments to allow for within-year timing differences between the output and expenditure measures; over a calendar year, these adjustments are statistically neutral. Exports and Imports of goods: Current price data for exports and imports of goods and services at current prices are taken from the Balance of Payments statistics of the International Accounts. The basic data measures the physical movement of goods across the Irish border and must be adjusted by the CSO's Balance of Payments Division to reflect change in ownership. Merchandise exports are deflated by an average of the WPI Manufacturing Export Index and the Export Unit Value index calculated from the detailed International Trade in Goods statistics. Merchandise imports are deflated by the Unit Value index calculated from import data from the International Trade in Goods statistics. Exports and Imports of services: Current price data are again obtained from the Balance of Payments statistics. Services, other than royalties, are deflated by sub-elements of the consumer price index (CPI), sub-elements of the Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices (HICP), and EHECS (Earnings, Hours & Employment Cost Survey) data. Royalty imports and exports are deflated by the implied price index calculated from the current and constant price exports of goods and non-royalty services. Volumes and Update cycle: At constant prices, two measures ( output and expenditure ) are used. These measures are produced using annual chain linked indices. On the output side, for each quarter, the volume growth measures at a detailed level are weighted together using value added weights of the previous year. Similarly, on the expenditure side, the growth estimates are weighted by expenditure weights of the previous year. The average of the two measures is the growth measure used. The change over a period of years is then calculated by linking together the annual changes. The latest estimates available are referenced to 2022 values (for constant price chain linked data contained in Annual National Accounts 2023 [ANA 2023] and QNA Q1-Q3 2024 publications). 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Reconciliation: GDP at previous year's prices is estimated independently using the expenditure and output-based methods. The official measure of GDP is taken to be the average of the two results. Seasonal adjustment: The accounts are adjusted using the X-13 ARIMA procedure. Each time series is independently adjusted with the Indirect approach then being used (please see further details under the bullet point directly below). The seasonally adjusted GDP estimates are derived by summing of the independently seasonally adjusted components (such as Personal Consumption, Exports etc). The seasonal factors are based on a relatively long series of quarterly data (for QNA Q3 2024 published December 2024, the time series length was 119 reference quarters, from Q1 1995 to Q3 2024). Seasonally adjusted aggregates can be computed either by aggregating the seasonally adjusted components (indirect adjustment) or adjusting the aggregate and the components independently (direct adjustment). In the CSO's QNA release, seasonal adjustment is conducted using the indirect seasonal adjustment approach. This approach is in line with CSO's Policy on Seasonal Adjustment and Eurostat's Recommendations on Seasonal Adjustment. The indirect approach can give the best results when the component series show very different seasonal patterns, which is a feature of some data series in the Irish National Accounts. Under this indirect approach, individual time series are independently adjusted at the component level. These individual series are then aggregated to compute seasonally adjusted results for GDP, GNP etc. This indirect approach is applied to GDP and GNP and the seasonally adjusted GDP and GNP are derived as the sum of the seasonally adjusted components (such as Personal Consumption, Exports etc.). Once the statistical discrepancy is taken into account, the sum of the seasonally adjusted components of GDP and GNP add up to the respective seasonally adjusted GDP and GNP series. As part of the seasonal adjustment process using the US Census Bureau's X-13-ARIMA framework, ARIMA models are identified for each series based on unadjusted data spanning Q1 1995 to Q1 2024 Final (following an annual Seasonal Adjustment methodological review conducted in conjunction with CSO Methodology Division for the Q1 2024 reference quarter). These models are then applied to the entire series (so for example Q1 1995 to Q3 2024 for the Q3 2024 QNA issued early-December 2024). Seasonal factors and the parameters of the ARIMA models are updated each quarter. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions: Previously published quarterly estimates for the latest year are updated in each new Quarterly National Accounts (QNA) release to incorporate any more final data that have become available. ANA 2024 shall be published in mid-2025).
