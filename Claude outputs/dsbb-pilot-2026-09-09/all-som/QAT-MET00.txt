 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions In compilation of the foreign trade statistics (compiled by the Statistics Department of the Planning Council (SD/PC) in coordination with the Customs and Ports General Authority) the special trade system is followed. There are no major free zones, although duty-free shops are located in the airport and some processing for inland use takes place in bonded warehouses. Export data distinguish between exports of national goods and re-exports. Re-exports are defined as those goods which have previously been imported, cleared through customs formalities, and then exported without undergoing any transformation leading to change in shape or value. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Trade is classified by the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS) as adapted by the Gulf Cooperation Council to reflect regional specifics (8 digits in total). In addition, data broken down by the Standard International Trade Classification (SITC) Rev.3 (6 digits) and the Classification by Broad Economic Categories are available. Imports are broken down by country of origin and exports by country of last known destination. Scope of the data In principle, all imports and exports of merchandise are covered with a few exceptions. Not included are weaponry and military equipment; illegal trade; foreign aid; temporary imports and exports; goods under financial leasing arrangements; fish sold abroad by Qatari vessels; and bunkers, stores, etc. acquired abroad by Qatari vessels and aircraft , as well as similar supplies to foreign operators in Qatar. Imports of postal items are included, but not exports. Improvement and repair trade is not included. Processing trade, on the other hand, is recorded on a gross basis. Foreign supplies to bonded warehouses for inland processing are included in imports. Barter trade, commercial samples, and live animals brought by land through customs frontiers also are included. Trade below a threshold of 100 Qatari Riyals (QR) is not reflected in the foreign trade statistics. Deliveries to off-shore installations may partially escape registration. The foreign trade statistics refer to the entire territory of Qatar. 2.4.1 Valuation Imports are recorded at their cost, insurance, and freight (C.I.F.) values and exports at their free on board (F.O.B.) values. Value data are expressed in QR. Data reported in foreign currencies are converted in QR using average monthly rates according to a statement received from the Ministry of Finance. Import data for which only the F.O.B. values are known (for instance from bills of lading) are converted to C.I.F. values by adding eleven percent for transportation and insurance costs. Quantity data generally refer to kilogram net weight, that is, excluding packing. The quantities of trade in crude oil are expressed in metric tons, however. Crude oil is also measured in thousands of barrels. The quantities of precious stones, jewelry, and gold are expressed in grams. In addition to weight, the number of units is given for goods like animals and vehicles. 2.4.2 Recording basis In accordance with merchandise trade statistics, goods are recorded when released in free circulation or taken out of free circulation through Customs. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The main source for foreign trade data is customs declarations. In addition, direct information is received from some major companies. Many customs centers are linked to an automated system, but this does not encompass the center receiving oil and natural gas-related declarations. The Customs and Ports General Authority processes the information received from the customs centers with the help of a self-developed Oracle based system. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The source data ensure classifying trade data by the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System as adapted by the GCC to reflect regional specifics (8 digits in total), by the SITC, Rev.3 (6 digits), and the Classification by Broad Economic Categories. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Although the SD/PC receives the customs data on a monthly basis, t he published data are exclusively annual and are available to the public with a timeliness of about six months. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Many customs centers are linked to an automated system, but this does not encompass the center receiving oil and natural gas-related declarations. The Customs and Ports General Authority processes the information received from the customs centers with the help of a self-developed Oracle based system. The SD/PC receives the customs data on a monthly basis and conducts a thorough verification. This process is not automated. Invoices and other documents accompanying customs declarations may be used for verification purposes. No estimates for unrecorded trade are made. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Import data for which only the F.O.B. values are known (for instance from bills of lading) are converted to C.I.F. values by adding eleven percent for transportation and insurance costs. As the Qatari foreign trade statistics are exclusively annual, no seasonal adjusted data are being compiled. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results There is no procedure to validate intermediate results against other data sources. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data There is no procedure in place for regularly assessing and investigating the behavior of statistical data series against related series. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request SD/PC usually makes available trade statistics not routinely disseminated upon request (e.g. data by mode of transportation), depending on the level of detail and type of requested data.
