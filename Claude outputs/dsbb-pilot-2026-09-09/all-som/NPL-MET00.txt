 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Data on international merchandise trade are compiled following internationally accepted standards and disseminated by Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB), Department of Customs (DC), and Trade and Export Promotion Centre (TEPC). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The DC and TEPC use six-digit Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System for commodity classification. The NRB uses one-digit Standard International Trade Classification. Scope of the data The data of the DC and NRB cover trade with all countries. Customs has introduced the Automated System of Customs Data (ASYCUDA) in ten customs points. Exceptions to coverage TEPC data exclude trade with India. 2.4.1 Valuation Imports are recorded on a c.i.f. basis and the exports are recorded on a f.o.b. basis. Export and import values reported in foreign currencies are converted into the national currency using daily selling exchange rates published by the NRB. 2.4.2 Recording basis Transactions in international trade statistics are recorded according to the day of clearance of the Customs declarations. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs All three agencies use the same source, the Customs declarations. Customs records merchandise exports and imports on the basis of goods crossing the customs border, which is the same as the country’s economic territory. Customs data do not cover cross-border informal trade. The NRB and the TEPC staff are stationed in major Customs points. Information from Customs declarations is used to prepare a summary report, which is sent to the head offices of the respective agencies. Customs has introduced the Automated System of Customs Data (ASYCUDA) in ten customs points. The ASYCUDA is now partially using the custom data for statistical purposes. Its division is established at the Department of Customs (DOC). IT Statistics Division has started to function and now DOC is able to obtain custom data for statistical purposes. None None
