 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Concepts: The Office for National Statistics (ONS) produces the UK National Accounts on an internationally comparable basis, in accordance with the System of National Accounts 2008: SNA 2008 and the European System of Accounts 2010: ESA 2010. The methodology used for the compilation of non-financial accounts of general government (revenue, expenditure and net lending/net borrowing) and of net acquisition of financial assets and net incurrence of liabilities is based on the ESA 2010 and the Eurostat Manual on Government Deficit and Debt (MGDD). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The general government sector and its sub-sectors are defined in accordance with the regulations of ESA 2010 and the rules and clarifications provided in the Eurostat's Manual on Government Deficit and Debt (MGDD), which are also consistent with the 2008 SNA and GFSM 2014. The UK general government sector consists of the central government and local government. The Office for National Statistics compiles and disseminates government finance statistics for each sub-sector as well as for the general government sector. Geographic coverage: The United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland, excluding the offshore islands (the Channel Islands and the Isle of Man). General government operations cover revenue, expenditure, and balance for general government. General government covers central government and local government. The UK does not have a separate social security fund sector. Extra budgetary funds are not distinguished from budgetary funds. Exceptions to coverage Not applicable. Unrecorded activity Not applicable. 2.4.1 Valuation All nonfinancial and financial transactions are valued in accordance with ESA 2010. Transactions denominated in foreign currency are converted to £ Sterling by using prevailing market rates. 2.4.2 Recording basis Data on non-financial and financial transactions of general government are recorded in line with ESA 2010, i.e. the principal of accruals recording is applied. The data for central government are based on actual accounting records. The data for local governments are based on the administrative accounting returns from regional government departments. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The grossing/netting procedures are in accordance with ESA 2010. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Monthly accounting data, administration records and surveys together provide comprehensive coverage. The data for central government are based on actual accounting records, though unit level provisional estimates are used until accounts are produced and audited. The data for local government are based on the administrative and accounting returns from local authorities, collected by the departments responsible for monitoring their expenditure. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Definitions For central government, the administrative system includes a standard chart of accounts, which maps to the statistical transaction category (ESA2010 framework). Extracts from this administrative system are supplied to the Office for National Statistics. In exceptional cases where an incorrect statistical transaction category has been used in the source data, ONS applies adjustments to move the item to the correct category. For local government, the mapping from accounting categories to statistical category is carried out by the Office for National Statistics. Classifications The classification of units to institutional sectors is the responsibility of the Office for National Statistics. The administrative system for central government uses the classification of units as prescribed by the ONS. Time of recording The expenditure data are based on official records of actual transactions reported on an accruals basis. The tax receipts data are collected on a cash basis and time-adjusted to represent accrued statistics. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Timeliness meets the standard required. Estimates may be used for some components for the most recent period. These estimates are replaced with actual reported data when available. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The Office for National Statistics has a best practice process for the Quality Assurance of Administrative Data (QAAD). The principles are applied in the case of government data. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Consolidation: The data are on a consolidated basis, that is, transactions between the various accounting units within central government are eliminated through consolidation. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Data validation takes place at different stages within the statistical production process, including: Unit level data entry validation checks within the central government administrative reporting systems and within the data collections from local authorities. Plausibility checks of aggregate data upon receipt Plausibility checks of transaction totals by comparison with previously reported data or correlated variables During the preparation phase, unusual data are questioned with suppliers, errors are rectified, and data are confirmed. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Intermediate data are assessed in consultation with data providers. For example, there are specific meetings between ONS, Her Majesty's Treasury, and Her Majesty's Revenue and Customs to verify and explain movements in taxes data, either over time or revisions between periods. Aggregate data are compared to official forecasts as a reference. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The statistical discrepancy between net borrowing according to the non-financial account and net borrowing according to the financial account is monitored carefully. ONS aims to ensure, through verification of data sources, that this discrepancy is relatively small. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions analysis is published on the ONS website alongside the Public Sector Finances publication. This shows the revisions 12 months after the end of the reference period, and provides comparisons for aggregate data. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Information about the effect of classification and methodological decisions on the fiscal metrics is provided within the Public Sector Finances bulletin when the changes are implemented. Where a classification or methodological change significantly impacts the key fiscal metrics (i.e. PSNB and/or PSND), the ONS publishes information post-implementation on the reconciliation between the metrics on an old and new basis. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request ONS does not usually provide additional data on request, and instead seeks to ensure that all relevant data are included within the publication, thereby ensuring access for all. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Contact names (for ONS statisticians) are included for each publication, alongside a contact email address. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The Release Calendar on the ONS website shows the schedule of all publications.
