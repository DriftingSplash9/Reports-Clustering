 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Malta’s external debt data closely follows internationally accepted standards and guidelines as specified in the IMF’s External Debt Statistics: Guide for compilers and users (2013) . Such data, which are compiled as a subset of the international investment position (IIP), comprises the outstanding amount of the liability side of the IIP excluding equities, reinvested earnings and financial derivatives. The CBM is always in close contact with international organizations such as the IMF, ECB and Eurostat to discuss the implementation of BPM6 rules and the IMF's External Debt Statistics guide. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classifications are in line with internationally accepted external debt standards. Scope of the data External debt data are compiled on the basis of outstanding debt liability positions between residents and non-residents. Coverage: End-of-period stocks of external liabilities are classified according to institutional sector (General Government, Central Bank, Deposit-taking Corporations, except the Central Bank and the Other sectors), type of instrument, and the original maturity of the instrument. Direct investment liabilities are separately identified. Positions in financial derivatives and equity securities including retained earnings are excluded, while debt securities issued in the domestic markets by residents and purchased by non-residents and inter-company lending between entities in a direct investment relationship are included. Financial leases with non-residents are treated as loans. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: In general, all positions are valued at market values. Financial instruments traded on markets are mostly valued by directly using market values. Non-tradable financial instruments are valued at nominal value. The conversion of foreign currency denominated liabilities into euro is based on the exchange rate prevailing at the close of business on the last working day of the reference period. 2.4.2 Recording basis Data are recorded on an accrual basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Grossing/netting procedures are broadly consistent with the 6th edition of the IMF’s Balance of Payments Manual . 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The CBM collects fully reconciled data on financial transactions and positions, and related income by means of direct reporting from the financial sectors; whilst the NSO collects data from non-financial corporations and also uses administrative sources.. This data is used for the compilation of the BOP, the IIP and external debt statistics, amongst others. The reconciliation model implies that all changes between the opening and closing positions of the reporting period need to be explained. The changes are broken down into gross transactions, changes in market prices, exchange rate and other changes. Portfolio investment data are generally reported on a security-by-security basis. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording External debt data in Malta are a subset of the IIP. This model closely follows internationally accepted standards and guidelines. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data are compiled and submitted every quarter 3 months after the reference period. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The sample chosen for NFCs is regularly updated to mirror the population as much as possible. Administrative sources are also used in the compilation of the NFCs and SPEs. For the Financial Sector, which is regulated by the MFSA, there is a census. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Data on both stocks and flows, as well as revaluations, are collected simultaneously from reporting agents. This allows for the checking of consistency between flows, stocks, market price changes, exchange rate changes and other changes. Estimates are made for non-respondents. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The checking and vetting of data focus mainly on identifying any irregularities, inconsistencies, reclassifications and any outliers. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Reporting agents may be asked for ad hoc information on non-plausible changes and are alerted for any discrepancies in the stock flow data. Necessary corrections are carried out by the reporting agent. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses In the case of reclassifications, data may be revised over a longer time period. If these reclassifications are substantial, they are explained in the notes. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses When major revisions to the compilation methodology are carried out, the CBM releases explanations, in its official publications or in its website. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request When available, further breakdowns of external debt data can be made available to users by the CBM upon request. It is the policy of the CBM to provide consolidated or aggregated statistics to the public or the media when it deems necessary . Statistics to be provided should not be confidential or sensitive in nature. Statistics to be provided are principally those for which the compilation of which the CBM is either fully responsible or has shared responsibility. Requests for statistics, or queries on statistics, are received in writing or by e-mail, through the Statistics Department's general email address: contact@centralbankmalta.org. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Adequate assistance is given to the general public or the media on request. Any requests for data are either received in the Statistics Department's general e-mail address contact@centralbankmalta.org , or in the BOP Section's mailbox bop@centralbankmalta.org . 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs Information can be found on the CBM’s website.
