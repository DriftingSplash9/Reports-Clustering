 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The Barbadian national accounts are based on the United Nations standard System of National Accounts of 1968 ( SNA 68 ). There is no periodic production of accounts for institutional sectors. In June 1996 the Barbados Statistical Service published non-financial and financial accounts, including balance sheets, for the public sector from 1983 to 1992. The classification of transactions and balancing items were those of the SNA 68 . The classification of public sector's units departed from the SNA 68 with respect to the National Insurance Service (NIS, social security scheme). The NIS was classified in the Public Financial Corporations. According to the SNA 68 it should have been classified in the Government. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification of economic activities refers to the International Standard Industrial Classification, revision 2 (ISIC rev.2), with two major modifications: 1/ Sugar includes the production of sugar cane as well as the industrial production of sugar, and 2/ Tourism includes the activities impacted on by the direct expenditure of visitors (stay-over and cruise) such as hotels, restaurants, duty free shops, etc. Scope of the data All activities performed on the national territory, including off-shore banking and business services, are covered. The framework of reference is the United Nations System of National Accounts, 1968 ( SNA 68 ). Annual GDP at current prices is produced and disseminated by the Barbados Statistical Service. Annual and quarterly GDP at 1974 constant prices are produced and disseminated by the Central Bank of Barbados. The data series began in 1974. All figures are expressed in millions of Barbados dollars. All economic activities performed in Barbados are covered, except the underground economy. Unrecorded activity 2.4.1 Valuation At current prices, the GDP by economic activity is estimated at basic prices, and at market prices after addition of all indirect taxes net of subsidies. The GDP by type of expenditure is estimated at market prices. The domestic final expenditure is estimated at purchasers prices, imports are valued c.i.f. (cost of insurance and freight), exports are valued f.o.b. (free on board). 2.4.2 Recording basis Transactions are primarily recorded on an accrual basis, except indirect taxes that are recorded on a cash basis. At 1974 constant prices, the GDP by economic activity is estimated at factor cost, i.e., excluding indirect taxes net of governmental subsidies. The recording periods are the calendar year (current and constant prices) and the calendar quarter (constant prices). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The Barbados Statistical Service produces annual estimates at current prices of the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) by economic activity (production approach), and by type of expenditure (expenditure approach). The Central Bank of Barbados produces quarterly estimates at 1974 constant prices of the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) by economic activity (production approach). Annual estimates are the sum of the four quarters. Production approach at current prices (Statistical Service) Sugar: Data on production value from the Barbados Agricultural Management Co. Exports and imports of goods and services: Foreign trade statistics for goods, balance of payments for services. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Production approach at current prices (Statistical Service) For sugar, non-sugar agriculture and fishing value added in the current year is extrapolated from the previous year using the change in value of the production. For mining and quarrying, manufacturing industry, electricity, gas and water, wholesale and retail trade, transport, storage and communication, banking, and government value added can be computed from the basic data. The value added in the current year is extrapolated from the previous year using the change in value added computed from the basic data. For construction, changes are monitored by inputs of building materials. Value added in the current year is extrapolated from the previous year using the change in value of domestic production and imports of building materials. For tourism, expenditure of visitors is obtained from surveys of accommodation establishments. A suitable percentage of this expenditure is taken as contribution to GDP of the tourism sector. This percentage is obtained by weighting value added ratios collected through ad hoc surveys. For insurance, business, and general services value added in the current year is extrapolated from the previous year using the change in value of earnings paid to employees. Indirect taxes net of subsidies are derived from the detailed revenue and expenditure of government. Production approach at constant prices (Central Bank) The general compilation practice is to extrapolate value added in the current quarter from the previous quarter using the change in quantity of the production of each economic activity. A first exception is made for construction. Value added in the current quarter is extrapolated from the previous quarter using the change in quantity of consumption of building materials. A second exception is made for wholesale and retail trade, transport, storage and communication, business and other services. Value added at constant prices is extrapolated using the trend over the medium term for the whole "non-traded sector" i.e., mining and quarrying, electricity, gas and water, construction, wholesale and retail trade, transport, storage and communication, business and other services, and government. This trend is derived through econometric regression. Expenditure approach at current prices (Statistical Service) Government consumption, gross capital formation, exports and imports of goods and services are estimated using the basic data listed in section IV. Personal consumption is derived as a residual, by subtracting government consumption, gross capital formation, and imports net of exports from GDP at market prices.
