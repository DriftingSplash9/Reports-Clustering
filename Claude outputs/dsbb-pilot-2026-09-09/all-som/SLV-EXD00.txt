 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The data are based on the methodology of the BOP6 and the document External Debt Statistics: Guidelines for Compilers and Users of the International Monetary Fund. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The external debt positions are classified as follows: By institutional sector: Central Bank, General Government, Deposit-TakingCorporations, except the Central Bank, and Other Sectors. The latter are classified in accordance with External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (hereafter referred to as the Guide) and in line with the System of National Accounts 2008 (SNA2008). The other sectors are subdivided into: Otherfinancial coporations, Nonfinancial corporations, Households, and nonprofit institutions serving households (NPISHs). We also present a fourth category, which belongs to: Direct investment intercompany lending; The positions are also presented by contract maturity: short term is debt of one year or less and long term is debt of over one year; The positions are presented by instrument: Currency and deposits, debt securities, loans, trade credits and advances, and other debt liabilities; The external debt positions are not presented in domestic currency, as the Monetary Integration Law stipulates that the financial system accounts be expressed in dollars. Scope of the data The external debt includes liabilities with nonresidents, in accordance with the residence criterion concept in the sixth edition of the Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) . And the document External Debt Statistics: Guidelines for Compilers and Users of the International Monetary Fund. 2.4.1 Valuation According to "The Monetary Integration Act" in force since January 1st, 2001, the US Dollar is legal tender and the exchange rate between the Colon and the US Dollar is fixed at 8.75 Colones per 1US Dollar and liabilities included in El Salvador's IIP are valued at this exchange rate. Uses the face value and in the case of foreign debt, denominated in a currency other than the US Dollar, this is adjusted to the type of exchange rate. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Figures are registered in their gross value. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Constant reviews are carried out to expand the coverage of information whenever necessary. A significant part of the data are based on results from the surveys, the rest comes from official sources. Besides, the information for the private sector is complemented with data reported by international Banks eligible for tax exemption by the Central Bank. The National Accounts Department, based on data from the Ministry of Finance and Banks, provides information on the government's foreign debt, monetary authority, and banks. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording In general, there is a good degree of application of the definitions, scope, classification and time of recording prescribed by the VI Balance of Payments Manual and the Guidelines for compilers and users. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The survey system and the agreements for data delivery match the dates to submit statistics. 3.2.1 Source data assessment External public debt is provided by the Ministry of Finance from their administrative records. Meanwhile, the External Private debt comes from different sources of information; the external debt of deposit-taking corporations is obtained from balance sheets. The debt of other sectors is obtained from quarterly surveys and administrative records; the consistency of the data reported in the previous quarter is verified. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Transactions for high amounts reported in the surveys or in the administrative records are confirmed by informants. When there is no report from the respondents, data imputation is made based on the recent behavior of the transactions of said company or institution. A comparison of balances and flows between quarterly reports is performed to maintain consistency. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Data are adjusted during two years following publication date of said statistics. Adjustments are made considering the information that is not reported timely, corrections to the information registered or improvements in data coverage. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Evolution of the debt reported through surveys is assessed on a regular basis, furthermore, coverage is also reviewed. In some cases, the data from surveys is comparable with other source of information, or validation may be requested by the person in charge to submit the data. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data In some cases the series of data are compared with other related variables. Reported financial flows are reconciled with variations from the corresponding balances compiled for the purpose of foreign debt and other components of the international investment position. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Comparisons are made every now and then with BIS foreign debt statistics. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses No studies have been carried out. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Bank’s publications disclose users the reasons giving way to review statistics, although the studies explain in detail such changes dare not available to the public. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Upon the public's request non-confidential information that is not disclosed any of the bank’s publications is provided. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Fernando Vásquez, fernando.vasquez@bcr.gob.sv César Villamariona, mateo.villamariona@bcr.gob.sv 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs There is no catalog of publications, documents and other services.
