 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Definition: The concepts and definitions follow the principles outlined in the (CPI Manual 2020) - Consumer Price Index Manual - Concepts and Methods and (SNA 2008) - System of National Accounts 2008. The main exception is the exclusion of illegal goods and services, though this follows HICP practice. Concepts which do not follow international standards are kept under review. For example, (CPI 2005=100), church taxes and labour union membership fees, previously included in consumption expenditure, were excluded from because they contained elements of income transfers, a point similarly taken up by the European Union's HICP legal framework. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: According to ECOICOP as defined in EU regulations on Harmonized Index of Consumer Prices to 5-digit level. Under this national version of COICOP is broken-down to 7-digit level. From beginning of year 2016 updated version of eCOICOP to 5-digit level was implemented into the production of CPI. 2.2.1 Scope Classification: According to ECOICOP as defined in EU regulations on Harmonized Index of Consumer Prices to 5-digit level. Under this national version of COICOP is broken-down to 7-digit level. From beginning of year 2016 updated version of eCOICOP to 5-digit level was implemented into the production of CPI. Scope of the data Population coverage: All private consumption in the territory of Finland, all population groups without regard to nationality or residential status. Geographic coverage: All of Finland, urban and rural areas. Item coverage: Broad coverage including among other things owner occupied housing and public goods and services (net of reimbursements from the government). Exceptions to coverage Regarding COICOP sub-indices, especially at 5-digit level, certain indices are not provided because • they are not included in the coverage of the CPI, OR • because their consumption expenditure of the private household sector in Finland is very low. 2.4.1 Valuation Types of prices: Consumption expenditure is valued at purchasers’ prices. Transaction prices are collected from outlets and centralized sources, with appropriate guidelines. In case the actual transaction price is not achievable (e.g. new cars) the list price is used as representative of transactions prices. Cash discounts, hire purchase or credit terms not taken into account. 2.4.2 Recording basis Timing of price observation: Prices are collected mainly between 10th and 20th of the month. Days are randomly chosen to avoid day of the week effects (seasonal clothing items are collected when in season). Scanner-data (a.k.a transaction data) is available for, for example, daily products, alcoholic beverages, pharmaceutical products. In these cases data covers all monthly sales; all products and their prices and quantities. For some services (e.g. package holidays, flight tickets) prices are collected several months in advance. These prices are included into the index on the month that consumption can commence. The exceptions are renovations, interests on housing loans and on consumer credit which are included into the next month's index due to data gathering procedure. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The weights for cars, lotteries and insurance are net weights. The actual prices for these categories are collected on gross bases. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Sources of weights: National Accounts quarterly data (Q1-Q3) together with estimated Q4 for period t-1 (e.g. 2022 the NA is from 2021) covering all households and population groups. Time period of current weights: t-1 price updated to December t-1. Frequency of weight updates: yearly, until 2012 every 5 year. Item selection: Cut off sampling, Representative item method. Outlet selection: For durable and semi-durable goods and most services the larger cities were selected taking care of the regional coverage. Within cities, local samples of outlets selected purposively taking into account the rough turnover of the outlets. For clothing, footwear and books, some local outlets have been replaced by online stores. Scanner-data is obtained regarding the daily products, alcoholic beverages, pharmaceutical products, second-hand cars, train journeys, wireless telephone services, internet subscription fees, alcohol in restaurant, semi-durable goods and long-distance passenger transport by coach. In most cases scannerdata covers all outlets and their monthly sales. In cases when it is not possible to obtain all outlets of a retail chain then specified number of outlets are selected based on turnover acknowledging the regional coverage. Large, medium size and small outlets are included to the sample. Web-scraping is used for flights (sample of most representative destinations and days), fuels and holiday cottage prices. Sample sizes: Approximately 14.000 price observations are collected from outlets per month. No sampling for scanner-data because the data covers all sold items. For durables the target size of sample is approximately 100-170. No sampling for rents as data on rents is based on the Social Insurance Institution's register of housing allowances and on the data on rental dwellings of rental housing companies. In addition, some administrative data with full coverage of transactions (e.g. health services). Price collection methods: Data collection methods are 1. price collection carried out by price collectors, 2. centralised data collection in the CPI-team, 3. obtain of scanner-data and web-scraping, 4. web form for restaurants regarding alcoholic beverages in the restaurant. The prices for certain commodities are collected centrally by email or by copying information from the enterprises web-pages. Scanner-data is used as primary price collection method when possible. Web-scraping is used for selected products where scanner-data is not available. Item/product specification: The CPI-team provides product specifications to price collector including specifications on quality details, size range, material, acceptable brands, etc. These product specifications are used also for product replacements. Detailed product descriptions are given by local price collection to identify same product in next months' price collection. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Weights are derived from the National Accounts, and they represent the total annual private expenditure in Finland. Prices are collected specifically for the CPI and they fully meet the concepts and requirements of CPI price collection. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Prices are collected mid-month in outlets web-sites, mainly. Only in duly justified cases prices are still collected by visiting outlets. By contrast to this, scanner-data covers all monthly sales; from 1st of month to end of month. Web-scraping is done mid-monthly Organizations providing centralized prices are informed of the required due-by dates for submission of data. The data provision is monitored by CPI staff. 3.2.1 Source data assessment There are different procedures to detect data entry errors. 1. Price collectors have a mobile app that performs plausability checks at the same time as data is recorded to the application. If the warning or error-signal is received, price collector need to check the recording and correct it according to the instructions given by CPI-team or received from the app 2. Locally collected data is transferred to the central office right after data collection period ends. As the data is updated to the database, several automatic checks are applied such as quantity and quality adjustment procedures, as well as treatment of missing prices. 3. All observations that are collected by price collectors are re-checked at the central office with pre-defined rules in the production system. Central staff executes these separate check several times a month; once before the Flash HICP calculation and second time before the final CPI calculation commences. This way critical data entries are checked, corrected when necessary and confirmed. The decisions about whether or not to include a price quote in the compilation of the CPI is mostly done by central staff. All centrally collected prices are validated at the moment of data collection. Scanner-datasets are checked for inconsistent values (e.g. missing values, quantities, product names or classifications or changes in package sizes) . Also extreme price changes are checked. After these, erroneous observations are excluded from the index compilation. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Reference period: From the beginning of 2016 reference period is 2015=100 Computation of lowest level indices: Unweighted Jevons and weighted geometric Laspeyres are mainly used to aggregate price changes to elementary aggregate level. Exceptions to this rule are the following scanner-datasets, where weighted Törnqvist formula is applied: pharmaceutical products, second-hand cars (includes hedonics) alcoholic beverages wireless telephone services and internet subscription fees food, non-alcoholic beverages, daily products semi-durable goods long-distance passenger transport by coach Formula for aggregation: The index is Laspeyres type chain index with annual links. Method used to update weights for price change: Weights are price updated to December t-1. Linking method: Most of the old series are chained forward and they are available on request. Old series are chained forward using the monthly-change (%) of the CPI 2015=100. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Missing prices: Temporarily missing are estimated based on the average price change of other products belonging to same sub-sub-class and same area; in case price is permanently unavailable then replacement product is selected. This rule is not applied to scanner-data where dynamic basket-approach is in use; only those observations having price and quantity for current month and base month are acknowledged in the compilation of microindex. Quality differences: Adjusted mostly by means of rather detailed classification and replacement rule to be applied within the narrow class. For housing and second-hand cars, hedonic regression methods, for tariffs consumer profiles or constant quality unit values. Replacement items: Following the instructions and product specification, a representative substitute is chosen. New items: Fundamentally new items (entirely new products) are added to the CPI-basket on a yearly basis. In the lowest level of the index, the sample is renewed more often depending on the nature of the market circumstances. Seasonal items: Prices for seasonal products and services, such as clothing and sports equipment, are collected for pre-specified months. During the out-of-season period, no prices are collected and the weights of seasonal items are allocated to the other sub-sub-classes belonging to the same sub-class. The class-confined seasonal weight method given in the European Commission Regulation is applied. Owner-occupied housing: Is measured based of four components: New flats and detached houses. (Currently approximated with house price index of old flats and detached houses) Costs of housing companies for capital repairs and costs of owners for capital repairs Mortgage rates Real estate broakers fee, asset transfer tax and premium for fire insurance on detached houses 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Critical data that have been entered by price collectors must be confirmed and/or be specified by specially trained staff of the Statistical Offices. The most peculiar results after the calculation of CPI are checked by statisticians of the CPI-team. This is done with the checking lists and manually observing the observations. Detected errors are corrected immediately after discovery. A number of factors are used to highlight potentially incorrect prices such as price change, price range, correct entry of metadata including coding information. Scanner-datasets do not have similar kind of errors as traditionally collected data therefore these are taken as such. Only observations having limited amount of information for identification of product OR monthly price change exceeds 200% or drops 50% are excluded . 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Intermediate data are carefully checked on a monthly basis. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Any discrepancies in statistical output are carefully checked on a monthly basis. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses When weights are updated, revision studies (comparison of old and new weights) are carried out and published on the Statistics Finland's website. Indexes are generally not revised. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses When weights are updated, revision studies (comparison of old and new weights) are carried out and published on the Statistics Finland's website. Indexes are generally not revised. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Special aggregate indexes are available on request from the published data. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Information on contact points is available on the web-site.
