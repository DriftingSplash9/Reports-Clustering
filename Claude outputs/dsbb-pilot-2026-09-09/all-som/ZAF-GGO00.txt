 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions All data have been converted and are presented in accordance with the GFSM 2014 framework as recommended by the IMF. The data are reported according to this framework. The Statement of Sources and Uses of Cash for each level of government is published quarterly in the Bank's Quarterly Bulletin . The Statement of Government Operations for each level of government is only submitted to the IMF annually for publication in the GFS Yearbook. Budgetary and State government statistics are not yet on full accrual accounting systems, but adjusted cash and certain adjustments are made to complete the Statement of Government Operations for these spheres of government. No Other economic flows statements are published. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The quarterly analysis of general government presents revenue, expense, cash deficit/surplus and financing broken down by debt instrument, broadly defined and classified according to the GFSM 2014 . Institutional sectors are defined in accordance with the System of National Accounts (SNA) 2008 and the GFSM 2014 . GFS data are provided separately for the consolidated central and general government. Data for individual levels of government are available. Data for the last two years are preliminary and subject to revision and replaced by final audited data when it becomes available. Scope of the data The operation of general government covers: · The national government: The budgetary accounts of national government (or main budget accounts) comprising 42 government departments, standing appropriations and stator payments. · Social security funds: The social security funds consist of 4 funds, namely, the Compensation Commissioner for Occupational Diseases in Mines and Works, Unemployment Insurance Fund, the Compensation Fund, and the Road Accident Fund. · Extrabudgetary units: The extra-budgetary units consist of 261 institutions comprising of 154 national extra-budgetary institutions, 10 constitutional units, 26 universities, 50 Technical and Vocational education and training (TVET) colleges as well as 21 Skills Education and Training Authorities (SETA's). The financial year of universities and TVETs runs between 1 January - 31 December. This is different from other levels of general government which runs between 1 April - 31 March of the following year. · Consolidated Provincial governments: The financial accounts of the 120 provincial government departments (provinces) and 72 provincial extra-budgetary institutions (PEBIs) are included in the analysis of the general government. The provincial data is sourced quarterly from the National Treasury and provincial extra-budgetary institutions data is sourced yearly from the annual financial statements. · Local governments: Prior to the December 2000 elections, there were 843 local authorities. There are currently 257 units (comprising of 8 metropolitan cities, 44 district municipalities and 205 local municipalities), with financial reporting based on the new structures, taking effect as of 31 August 2016. The MFMA was introduced by National Treasury to enhance financial management reforms across government; this became effective in July 2004. Generally Recognised Accounting Practices (GRAP)/Generally Accepted Municipal Accounting Practices (GAMAP) reporting standards are implemented. The financial year of local governments runs between 1 July - 30 June of the following year. Exceptions to coverage The Statement of Sources and Uses of Cash for each level of government is published quarterly in the Bank's Quarterly Bulletin . The Statement of Government Operations for each level of government is only submitted to the IMF annually for publication in the GFS Yearbook. Budgetary and State government statistics are not yet on full accrual accounting systems, but adjusted cash and certain adjustments are made to complete the Statement of Government Operations for these spheres of government. No Other economic flows statements are published. Unrecorded activity No data on outstanding debt is currently being recorded for consolidated provincial governments. 2.4.1 Valuation Both domestic and foreign bonds of national government are revaluated on a monthly basis. The outstanding value is adjusted accordingly. However, no Statement of other economic flows is currently compiled. National and Local governments as well as National Extra-budgetary institutions issue bonds and these bonds are valued at face value and market prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis National government: In accordance with GFS principles, the analysis of total revenue and expenditure are based on (adjusted cash) reporting system. Social security funds: Although the original accounts are compiled on an accrual basis, the analysis are adjusted to reflect cash flows. Extra-budgetary institutions: Although the original accounts are compiled on an accrual basis, the analysis are adjusted to reflect cash flows. Consolidated provincial governments: In accordance with GFS principles, the analysis of total revenue and expenditure are based on (adjusted cash) reporting system. Local governments: Although the original accounts are compiled on an accrual basis, the analysis are adjusted to reflect cash flows. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures All relevant data are recorded on a gross basis. This allows for data to be netted out where necessary. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs National government: Data from administrative records kept by the National Treasury and the government departments. No estimates are required, as the accounts are comprehensive. Social security funds: Data are taken from the annual financial records of the funds and from quarterly surveys submitted by the funds. In cases where the latest data are not available, quarterly estimates based upon the last available audited financial statements are made. The SARB receives quarterly surveys from three of the social security funds, whereas data for the fourth is received from the Public Entities Quarterly Report. Extra-budgetary institutions: Data are taken from National Treasury and the annual financial records of the entities and from quarterly surveys of the institutions. In cases where the latest data are not available, estimates based upon the last available audited financial statements are made. Annual data are taken from the annual audited financial records of the institutions. The financial year of universities and TVETs runs between 1 January - 31 December. This is different from other levels of general government which runs between 1 April - 31 March of the following year. Consolidated Provincial governments: Data are taken from the financial statements submitted by the provincial treasuries to the National Treasury. Since the April-June quarter of 2000, provincial governments data are obtained from the Provincial Revenue Fund Statements published by National Treasury, which reflect the cash book transactions of the provinces. Annual data for both provincial governments and PEBIs are obtained from the audited financial statements of the provinces. Local governments: Quarterly data are taken from the Quarterly Financial Statistics (QFS) of selected municipalities (P9110.1) and Financial Census of Municipalities statistical releases of Stats SA (P9114). The financial year of local governments runs between 1 July - 30 June of the following year. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording All the data have been converted and is presented according to the GFSM 2014 framework as recommended by the IMF. The data are being reported according to this framework with the exception of the balance sheet and the other economic flows. All relevant data are recorded on a gross basis. This allows for data to be netted out where necessary. The Budget Review has definitions for some of the categories. The metadata pages on the Standard Chart of Accounts also have information. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The PFMA requires that the data be made available within 30 days after the end of each month. An advance release calendar can be accessed through the internet on: https://www.resbank.co.za/en/home/calendar/2021-ADVANCE-RELEASE-CALENDAR It indicates the date and time of the release to the public and also to be posted on the website for the SDDS. 3.2.1 Source data assessment All the data on the operations of central government are subject to an annual audit by the Office of the Auditor-General of South Africa. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques General government operates on an adjusted cash-flow basis, therefore it is not possible to compile a Statement of Other economic flows , hence the 98% adherence to the GFSM 2014 . 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures An abridged table has been constructed to facilitate adjustments to the source data. No adjustments are made to the cash-flow revenue. The cash-flow expenditure is adjusted for the following items, namely, late departmental requests, outstanding transfers, revaluation of index linked bonds, amortised discount, capitalised interest on retail bonds and surrenders. The extraordinary receipts are also adjusted for premiums. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Data are assessed on a monthly and quarterly basis and statistical discrepancies are corrected in the following month or quarter. Over and above this, the data are balanced against the annual audit report. A general note is also published in the Quarterly Bulletin which states that data are preliminary for two years. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Data are assessed on a monthly and quarterly basis, and statistical discrepancies are corrected in the following month or quarter. Over and above this, the data are balanced against the annual audit report. A general note is also published in the Quarterly Bulletin which states that data are preliminary for the past two years and subject to revisions. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs A nil test has been built into the analysis. Should this test not equate to nil, an investigation is immediately embarked on to solve the problem before publication date. In other cases, rounding off numbers can result in the test not equating to nil. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions are done quarterly. Audited financial statements data are used to revise preliminary data on an annual basis. Data are passed on to the National Accounts Division (NAD) and Capital Markets and Flow of Funds Division (CMFFD) in our own department on a quarterly basis to be used in their analysis. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Revisions are done quarterly. Unallocated data are reclassified to the correct categories. Data flow internally to our NAD and CMFFD on a quarterly basis. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Requests are received telephonically or via email by institutions such as the BIS, IMF, National Treasury, private institutions and scholars. The requests are placed on record and disseminated accordingly as mentioned in 0.1.3. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Details of contact persons are disseminated on the metadata pages. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs None. However, a list of all publications of the Bank is sent to the outside agencies with the application forms for inclusion on the distribution list. The list also contains the prices charged for the respective publications.
