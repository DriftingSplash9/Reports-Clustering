Exceptions to coverage Debt data do not include other accounts payable, currency and deposits, and liabilities in respect of insurance, pensions and standardized guarantee schemes. Domestic debt are debt liabilities owed by residents to residents of same economy. Debt liabilities owed by residents to non-residents are external debt.. Unrecorded activity N/A 2.4.1 Valuation Central Government Debt is shown at face value, that is, at the undiscounted amount of principal to be repaid to the creditor. Transactions originally denominated in foreign currencies are converted into Mauritian rupees using the prevailing rates of exchange. The conversion is made using the exchange rate on the day of the transaction obtained from the BOM. Debt stocks in foreign currencies, which are maintained at face values, are converted into Mauritian rupees using the exchange rate on valuation date which is obtained from the BOM. 2.4.2 Recording basis The debts are recorded as per the valuations stated at 2.4.1. Government’s accounts are maintained on a cash basis. However, for computation of government budget deficit, interest on accrual basis is used. Appropriate adjustment is made for the difference between cash and accrual interest to come up with the government borrowing requirement. For recording of flows and debt stocks, the cash basis of accounting is used. Thus, any accrued interest at the end of the period that is not yet payable is not included in the debt position. The debt stock is shown at face value . 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The outstanding debts of budgetary central government and the extra budgetary units are compiled and disseminated on a gross basis. However, the computation of central government debt takes into account intersectoral and intrasectoral consolidations. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The sources for Government external debt data are loan agreements and disbursements and repayment notifications received from creditors and debt service reports from the BOM. For domestic debt of budgetary central government debt, the data source is the BOM. The DMU maintains debt data for extra budgetary units from information submitted on a template either by mails or electronically through emails. The data are obtained from the extra budgetary units on a monthly basis. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The definition as per GFS 2014 is adopted and shared by the Debt Unit, Statistics Mauritius and BOM to ensure common understanding of terms. The extra budgetary units are provided standard templates to fill in so that there is a common understanding among all organisations. Data are available to allow disaggregation into subsectors, i.e budgetary central government and extra budgetary units. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data collection and processing are adequate to meet timeliness and periodicity for disseminating central government debt statistics. The DMU maintains strict follow-up procedures, including direct communications with respondents, to ensure timely receipt of comprehensive debt data. 3.2.1 Source data assessment All the debt data, whether available internally or obtained from creditors, BOM, EBU and local authorities are assesd for their consistency and competencies. All the data are audited annually by the National Audit Office. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques None 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures NA 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Data are routinely compared with financial statements and press reports released by the extra budgetary units. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data All data submitted by extra budgetary units are subject to verification and validation. Where appropriate, an organisation may be requested to submit a copy of bank statement certifying the loan amount, interest and the outstanding debt stock. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Any discrepancies and problems are sorted out and verified prior to publication. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses On a quarterly basis.
