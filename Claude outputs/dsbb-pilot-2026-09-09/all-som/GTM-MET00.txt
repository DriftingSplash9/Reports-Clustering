 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Data are disseminated in millions of U.S. dollars on total exports (f.o.b.) and total imports (c.i.f.). Definitions: Merchandise exports include all the goods that leaves from national territory of Guatemala definitively or temporarily through a customs declaration, fulfilling with the law's provisions and the current regulations, for sale to nonresidents. Merchandise imports include all goods from abroad acquired by the economy's residents. The conceptual framework for foreign trade statistics follows the guidelines contained in the International Merchandise Trade Statistics: Concepts and Definitions 2010 (IMTS2010) of the United Nations (UN); Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual, Sixth Edition (BPM6) of the International Monetary Fund (IMF). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The Department of Macroeconomic Statistics (DEMAC) is responsible for the recording and control of statistics on foreign trade in goods. Foreign trade statistics are prepared on the basis of customs import and export forms and declarations, which are submitted at Guatemala’s various customs offices, and are forwarded to the BG on magnetic media by the Superintendency of Tax Administration (SAT), Bank of Guatemala classified as follows: 1. Customs territory trade; 2. Trade of enterprises under Decree 29-89 of the Congress of the Republic; and 3. Free trade areas under Decree 65-89 of the Congress of the Republic. Exports Exports are expressed in f.o.b. values, in thousands of U.S. dollars, and are compiled monthly according to the following groups: A. Product (a) Key products: value and volume exported (b) Products, Central America: value of exports classified by specific product (c) Other products: value of exports classified by specific product B. Total value of exports by purchasing country. Imports Imports are expressed c.i.f. values, in thousands of U.S. dollars, and are compiled monthly in the following groups: A. Economic group: (a) Consumer goods (b) Raw materials and intermediate goods (c) Fuel and lubricants (d) Construction materials (e) Capital goods (f) Other B. Total value of imports by selling country C. Total value of imports by country of origin D. Imports are expressed c.i.f., in thousands of U.S. dollars. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data provided by the Superintendency of Tax Administration (SAT). 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The data collected from the Superintendency of Tax Administration (SAT) was defined prior to the preparation of the Foreign Trade Statistics, evaluating one by one the fields to request from the Import and Export Customs Declarations such as: date, country ( seller and origin), tariff classification (Central American Tariff System), units, volume and value among others. The frequency of receiving information is 10 calendar days from the end of the previous month. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The data (administrative records) of Financial and Government sectors are generally obtained on a monthly basis, and according to the publication schedules established by the source institution. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The data from administrative records are evaluated monthly in accordance with the previously established “Control Figures” procedure, which includes monitoring mainly the following variables: number of records, amount in US dollars, customs regimes used, historical behavior of the registered values, both in number of registrations and in dollar amounts for each of the reported customs regimes, seller country and country of origin, among others. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The technique of monitoring the historical behavior of the main variables that are recorded, both in imports and exports, is used, such as: amounts recorded both in value in US dollars and in records by Regime, purchasing country, country of origin, product, section and chapter of the Central American Tariff System. Information verification is carried out for selected products, mainly with duly accredited exporters' unions. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The administrative records used to record the volume of import and export operations of Electric Power, Fuels and Lubricants and selected products such as sugar, banana, coffee, cardamom and nickel, among others, are individually reviewed. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The information registered on a monthly basis is validated with the information sent by the Superintendency of Tax Administration (SAT) and according to previously established procedures. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Monthly data is evaluated for consistency according to previously established procedures. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Reviews of analysis and consistency of information are carried out on a quarterly basis, mainly for the following variables: volume, value, product and country. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Specific requests for information are met, normally they are required by email.
