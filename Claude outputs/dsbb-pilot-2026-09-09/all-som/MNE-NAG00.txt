 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions National accounts statistics follow European System of Accounts 2010 (ESA10). First estimation by ESA95 concept is compiled and published in 2003. ESA 2010 has been implemented in September 2015 for the period 2010 onwards. The calculation of GDP at current and constant prices is performed by the production approach within the annual national accounts, as well as GDP by the expenditure approach which is calculated independently on the basis of aggregate consumption (for final consumption expenditure of households, final consumption expenditure on state expenses for final consumption of non-profit institutions serving households, gross investments in fixed assets, changes in inventories and exports and imports of goods and services) at current and constant prices. In order to develop NA data, compilation of GDP by institutional sectors by NACE Rev 2. is done for the first time in 2011. Analytical Framework: Estimation of GDP generally follows the concepts and definitions set out in the System of National Accounts 2008 (SNA 2008), and European System of Accounts (ESA 2010). Concepts and Definitions: GDP is a measure of the total value of final goods and services produced by residential institutional units during the reference period. Basically, GDP is the concept of value added. GDP also measures the amount of goods and services which, added to imports from abroad, provides the total resources which may be used for the final uses including the export. Gross value added is the difference between output and intermediate consumption. The main categories of GDP compilation by production approach are: Gross output is defined as a market value of produced goods and services. It is calculated by activities at approximate basic prices, since all subsidies are treated as subsidies on products and included into calculation on the level of national economy. Intermediate consumption at purchase prices is the value of goods and services, which are transformed, used and consumed in the production process. Gross domestic product at market prices is the value of all goods and services produced by resident units, i.e., the sum of gross value added by activities and taxes on products less subsidies on products. In compilation of intermediate consumption by activities is included the value of financial intermediation services indirectly measured (FISIM) which increase level of harmonization of GDP compilation in line with European standards. FISIM is broken down by activities. Investments into fixed capital are purchases made by enterprises and other organizations and associations in order to obtain new assets, increase the value of existing assets or replace them. Acquired investment goods can be new or already used, purchased or received without compensation from third person in country or abroad, produced in own arrangement or obtained through financial leasing. All investments into new facilities are included, as well as investments for capacity extensions, rebuilding and modernization of existing facilities or replacement of obsolete, worn out or accidentally destroyed assets. Payments for investments are investments in cash during a reference year in which payments were actually made regardless the time of construction or purchase. Realized investment during the reference year is the value of actual construction, building or procurement of facilities, equipment etc., regardless of whether they are completed or not and payment made (without revaluation of realized investment). Investments in buildings include also the value of the integral equipment mounted or built in (elevators, heating systems and similar, as well as associated project). Investments in equipments include also the costs of their mounting, transport, customs duties, and insurance. Other investments include investments in the principle herd, forestation, mining explorations, software, licenses, etc. General government final consumption expenditures on goods and services provided as social transfers to households have the following characteristics: a). It must be possible to observe and record the acquisition of the good or services by an individual household or member thereof, and also the time at which it took place; b) The household or member must agree to the provision of the good or service; c) the good or service must be such that its acquisition by one household or person precludes its acquisition by other households or persons. According to the Classification of the Functions of the Government (COFOG), these expenditures refer to, above all, the fields of education, health, social welfare, a part of cultural and sports activities, and to a less degree, certain social welfare appropriation at the communal (municipal) level. The second part of General government final consumption expenditure has the character of collective consumption and refers to expenditures on public administration, security and defense, the maintenance of law and order, passing and enforcement of laws, research and development at the General government level, etc. These expenditures are especially expressed in the System. Household Final consumption expenditure consists of expenditure incurred by resident institutional units on goods or services that are used for the direct satisfaction of individual needs or wants or the collective needs of members of the community. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization GDP data by production approach are classified by NACE Rev.2. Estimation of GDP at current prices is done on the 2 digit level of NACE Rev.1, and published on the level of sections (1 alphabetical level: A-Q) Residential institutional units are not allocated in institutional sector as it is recommended by SNA2008/ESA2010. Government final consumption expenditure are classified by COFOG into: · Individual and · Collective Import and export of goods are classified by the Nomenclature of the External Trade statistics based on the Standard International Trade Classification of OUN. 2.2.1 Scope GDP data by production approach are classified by NACE Rev.2. Scope of the data Geographic Coverage National accounts data cover the whole territory of Montenegro. Transaction Coverage All non-financial transactions are covered by annual accounts data. Unit Coverage The national accounts cover the economic activity of all resident units in conformity with the SNA 2008. Unrecorded activity Illegal activities are not included in the national accounts of Montenegro. 2.4.1 Valuation Market prices are used to evaluate flows and stocks in national accounts. Output is valued at basic prices and intermediate consumption is valued at purchase prices excluding the deductible part of the value added taxes. Total exports are valued on an F.O.B. basis and import at C.I.F. basis. 2.4.2 Recording basis With the exception of general government income and expenditure, recording of all transactions is done on an accrual basis. Source data for government are on a cash basis, and no adjustments are made at present for their conversion to an accrual basis for national accounts compilation purposes. Nevertheless, national accounts make adjustment for taxes on products on VAT and excise data. Adjustment is done because enterprises have one month deadline for duties payment. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The main data source for the compilation of GDP from production side is the financial statements of enterprises, and because of that transactions between establishments within the same enterprise are recorded net. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Production approach: Main data sources for estimation of GDP by production approach are following: · Annual Financial Reports for enterprises and entrepreneurs (Central Bank of Montenegro and Tax Authority); · Annual financial statement reports for financial institution (Central Bank of Montenegro, insurance houses and privatized funds); · Monetary statistics (Central Bank of Montenegro); · Data from regular statistical survey (MONSTAT). Annual Financial Reports (balance sheets, profit and loss accounts, cash flow data and statistical annex) are submitted to the Tax Administration in paper and electronic format. Tax administration is responsible institution for data entry of reports. According to the Memorandum of Understanding between MONSTAT and Tax Administration, the electronic database of annual financial reports is submitted by end of June to the MONSTAT. Central Bank of Montenegro also makes a database of financial reports but only the balance sheets and profit and loss accounts. From Annual Financial Reports national accountants use the data from income statement in order to estimate output and intermediate consumption for non-financial institutional units and financial institutional units at current prices. Advantage of using financial reports data is that it provides information for all corporation sectors. In accordance with amendments to the Law on Accounting in 09.08.2016, enterprises have to submit financial report to the Commercial Court by end of March, instead by end of February as it is defined in the previous version of Law from 2009. Data from monetary statistics on loans and deposit by sectors on monthly basis are used for estimation of Financial Intermediate Services Indirectly Measured (FISIM). Statistical surveys data provide information in volume and monetary terms for a wide range of economic activities. Surveys data are disseminate in the form of indices and/or absolute values. Estimation of gross value added for agricultural industry is based on commodity flow framework. Main data sources are data from agricultural statistics about quantity of crop and animal production, as well as prices for agricultural products. Data source for estimation of output and intermediate consumption of government sector is administrative data from Ministry of Finance (central and local government data), and for Funds data are collected directly from social security funds. Expenditure approach: The main data sources for estimation of GDP by expenditure approach are following: · Household Budget Survey (MONSTAT); · Foreign trade statistics (MONSTAT); · Annual survey on realized investment (MONSTAT); · Balance of payment data (Central Bank) and · Government expenditure data (Ministry of Finance). Household final consumption expenditure (HFCE) is calculated on the basis of data from regular statistical surveys, as well as on the data from various administrative sources. Household final consumption expenditure is classified at 3-digit level of COICOP. Data according to the detailed classification were not published. For the group: Housing, water, electricity, gas and other fuels, the compilation of imputed rent on the basis of international user-cost approach was included, which improved the compilation of household consumption. Concerning Gross Fixed Capital Formation (GFCF) estimation is based on commodity flow approach. The main data sources for estimation of GFCF for construction are foreign trade statistics, financial reports of construction enterprises, and annual survey on realized investment (questionnaire code: INV-01). Reporting units for annual survey INV-01 of all companies and enterprises which have more than 5 employees and which act as direct investors. Data are available approximately 10 months after the end of the year. For enterprises, the following items are identified: new tangible fixed assets; used tangible fixed assets (purchases and disposals) and new intangible fixed assets (purchases and disposals). Data from current account on credit and debit services are data source for estimation of import and export of services. According to the Classification of the Functions of the Government (COFOG), we get final consumption expenditure, like sum of individual and collective consumption. Health, education, social work, cultural and sporting services are treated as an individual consumption. Consumer price index (CPI), compiled by MONSTAT, and is use for calculation of final household consumption expenditure at constant prices. Unit value index (UVI) for export and import of goods is calculated by Foreign trade statistics department and used by national accountants for deflating of total export and import of goods. Deflation of the total of imports services is based on the CPI of all EU countries as available in the Eurostat database. Export of services is deflated by implicit deflator for travel services, because in Montenegro export of services relates mostly to the tourism services. For estimation of GFCF at constant prices the deflation by UVI from imports is applied. UVIs for import on the level of total machinery and equipment and total construction are used. Volume estimation for government consumption is based on the extrapolation of the preceding year with the change in the number of employees. Changes in inventories at constant prices are a balancing item as a result of difference between GDP compilation in constant prices according to the output method, and the expenditure method. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording In general, the source data are consistent with the definitions, scope, and classifications of National accounts statistics. Value added by economic activity is done by the main activity of the institutional units, and not by the establishments. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Timeliness of all source data meets the timeliness and periodicity requirements of the annual national accounts estimates. Programme of statistical surveys clearly defines deadlines for the provision of source data. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Production approach: For compilation of GDP from production approach, MONSTAT uses a wide range of data so Estimation of gross value added for agricultural industry is based on the commodity flow framework. Quantities of produced agricultural products are multiply with appropriate prices. Financial statement report as a main data source for estimation of GVA is checked on individual level. Tax Authority data on turnover and costs are used for estimation of small unincorporated units. Imputed rental is estimated following user-cost approach. FISIM is allocated to intermediate consumption at section level and final consumption of households. Expenditure approach: The method used for the calculation HFCE is the commodity flow. Commodity flow approach is used for estimation of final consumption expenditure of households which represent balance of all goods and services flows that are produced and imported from one side and used and exported on the other side. GFCF estimation is based on commodity flow approach. The main data sources for estimation of GFCF for construction are foreign trade statistics, financial reports of construction enterprises and annual survey on realized investment (INV-01). Estimation of CFCF for machinery and equipment are based on import and export data, and data from domestic production. During calculation of investment in machinery and equipment by market prices, for each product duty tax is calculated (in accordance with custom tariffs) and non-deductible VAT, while the calculation of the trade margin is based on estimation. Montenegro has a small domestic production of machinery and equipment and because of that applied commodity flow approach for estimation GFCF in machinery and equipment provide exhaustive data. Data for export and import of goods are distributed by Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System. Import of machinery and equipment at purchasing prices is estimated from foreign trade data. Import at purchasing prices is sum of value of import, custom duties and VAT. Afterwards, purchasing prices of imported machinery and equipment is increased for trade margin and transport cost (TTM). Splitting of VAT value on deductible and non-deductible is done in proportion of 95% for deductible and 5% for non-deductible. Most of imported machinery and equipment is used for investment purpose, and because of that, we estimated that 95% is deductible VAT. Reminder value is non-deductible and reflects the cases of directly imported machinery and equipment by households. Import and export data on construction materials are used as a base for estimation of GFCF in construction. Data sources for estimation of domestic production of construction work are financial statement reports. For estimating the central government, we use a cross table for expenditure by economic and functional codes collected from the Ministry of Finance (administrative sources). Data for local government and extra-budgetary funds included in the consolidated account of the budget were provided by economic categories. Quarterly data for extra-budgetary funds are taken directly from funds. Supplementary detailed revenue data were collected from the Ministry of Finance for annual and quarterly estimation of revenue. An agreement was signed between MONSTAT and the Ministry of Finance for regular data supply for quarterly and annually government revenue and expenditure data. In June 2010, the Ministry of Finance gave access to MONSTAT to their database of revenue and expenditure. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures In the category of Housing (04) the imputed rental were calculated, according to the "User Cost" method, which the European Community set as the standard for candidate countries. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results There is no procedure to validate intermediate results against other data sources. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revision of GDP data is done in 2015 for the period 2010-2014 when ESA 2010 methodology was implemented. National accounts data are subject to revision as soon as new data sources become available. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request MONSTAT meets requests for additional information free of charge.
