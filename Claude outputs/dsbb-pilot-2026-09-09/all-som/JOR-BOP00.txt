 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Jordan's balance of payments is a summary of transactions between the Kingdom and the rest of the world, it is compiled and disseminated quarterly and annually in millions of Jordanian dinar. It also includes transactions between resident sectors in foreign financial assets. The disseminated data conform in most respects to the 6th edition of the IMF’s Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6). The presentation of the data provide the balances for the current account composed of goods, services, Primary income, and Secondary income, and capital account composed of capital transfers and financial accounts composed of, foreign direct investment, portfolio investment, other investment and reserve assets. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The Jordanian BOP transactions are classified according to the IMF's BPM6. 2.2.1 Scope The Jordanian BOP transactions are classified according to the IMF's BPM6. Scope of the data The BOP data cover all transactions between residents of Jordan and non-residents. No transactions or geographical areas are excluded (except for the Zarqa Free Zone). Necessary adjustments are made for any unrecorded transactions. An institutional unit is said to be resident within the economic territory of a country when it maintains a center of economic interest in that territory. The center of economic interest for households and individuals is considered to be in an economic territory where they stay for one year or more (with some exceptions). Economic territory includes free zones (except for the Zarqa Free Zone) and bonded warehouses or factories operated by offshore enterprises under customs control. There are no offshore banks operating in Jordan. Exceptions to coverage Transactions of the Zarqa Free Zone are not included in the BOP. Unrecorded activity Some transactions (e.g. gifts, private imports) of households may not be captured by existing sources and methods. 2.4.1 Valuation Jordan’s BOP statistics are compiled in millions of Jordanian dinars. Goods are valued at market prices by the Customs Department. While exports data are recorded on an F.O.B. basis, imports are recorded on a C.I.F. basis. Transactions in foreign currencies are converted into Jordanian dinars -at the mid-point of buying and selling exchange rates announced by the CBJ- at the time of the transaction. In the case of reserves, the effect of changes in prices and valuation are removed when deriving transactions from positions data. For stocks data, end of period exchange rates are applied and for flows data; period average rates are applied based on the daily exchange rates announced by the CBJ. Transactions are valued at market prices. For portfolio investment, debt and equity instruments are valued at their market prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis BOP accounting, where possible, is done on an accrual basis. Income transactions and financial account items (debt and equity instruments) transactions are recorded on accrual basis. Income related to government operations are recorded on an accrual basis, while, the remaining transactions on interest, dividend, and profits are recorded on a cash and accrual basis. Also, services are recorded when paid not when provided. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Goods, services, Primary income and Secondary income are recorded on a gross basis. Most financial account transactions are recorded on a net basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The BOP Division uses a diverse range of data sources in compiling BOP statistics. The primary data sources being employed for compiling BOP data are (i) customs declaration forms; (ii) the International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) forms; (iii) administrative records of the government; (iv) information provided by the various departments within the CBJ. These primary sources data are supplemented with information from several secondary data sources, namely, transport services obtained directly from the major transport enterprises, travel services based on travel surveys undertaken by the Department of Statistics and departure/arrival data obtained from the Ministry of Interior through Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities, workers' remittances based on information provided by banks and exchange companies, and data on nonresident sales and purchases of domestic securities compiled by Amman Stock Exchange (ASE) and Companies Control Department (CCD). 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The BOP Division uses a diverse range of data sources in compiling BOP statistics. The primary data sources being employed for compiling BOP data are (i) customs declaration forms; (ii) the International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) forms; (iii) administrative records of the government; (iv) information provided by the various departments within the CBJ. These primary sources data are supplemented with information from several secondary data sources, namely, transport services obtained directly from the major transport enterprises, travel services based on travel surveys undertaken by the Department of Statistics and departure/arrival data obtained from the Ministry of Interior through Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities, workers' remittances based on information provided by the banks and the exchange companies (A new methodology since 2024), and data on nonresident sales and purchases of domestic securities compiled by Amman Stock Exchange (ASE). 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Goods (trade Balance): Exports less imports in the BOP. Represents the net of international transactions in goods, and consists of the following: General merchandise net: covers goods which economic ownership changes between residents and non-residents. it represents Exports (credit) minus imports (debit). The Department of Statistics is the source of data based on data received from the Customs department. Exports: Domestic exports (f.o.b) plus re-exports. Imports: Imports (f.o.b) less imports of non-resident agencies. Net exports of goods under merchanting (credit): The difference between the value of goods purchased by residents in the Kingdom from non-resident for the purpose of reselling the same goods later to other non-residents without the entry of goods into the Kingdom. They are recorded as negative exports when purchased by a resident from nonresident and as positive exports when sold by a resident to nonresident. Non - Monetary Gold : The gold which is held as a store of value and gold held for other purposes (industrial) , except the gold that is held as a reserve asset by the authorities (monetary gold). Services Account: Represents receipts (credit) less payments (debit) of the following services: Manufacturing services on physical inputs owned by others: the net fees received and paid for the processing, compilation, and labelling, packaging without change of ownership of the goods. Maintenance and repair services: the net fees received and paid for the maintenance and repair of goods without change of ownership. Travel : credit represent expenditures of non-resident tourists visiting Jordan. These receipts are estimated on the basis of the results of the Arrivals and Departures Survey carried out by DOS, which provides estimates for expenditure averages according to the nationality of tourists. These averages are usually adjusted by the prevailing inflation rates. The number of arrivals and departures is provided every month by the Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities in coordination with the Ministry of Interior. On the other hand, debit represent expenditures abroad by Jordanian residents who travel abroad for the purpose of tourism, education or health treatment. These payments are estimated on the basis of the same survey used for the purposes of travel receipts. the basic elements of travel : Business : the net expenditure of borders workers, seasonal and other workers employed for the short term. personal: the net expenditure for purposes relating to health, education, and others. Transportation: includes International transport of passenger, freight and other transport services provided by residents of an economy to residents of another economy. Transport also includes postal and message delivery services (these include the receipt and delivery of newspapers, periodicals, bulletins and other printed materials and parcels, in addition of the sale of stamps and services of renting post boxes). The credit of this item represent the revenues received by the resident transport companies for providing transport services to non-residents. While debit of this item represent the amounts paid by residents to non-resident companies and transport organizations for the provision of transport services to residents. Financial services represent net fees received and paid for brokerage services such as lending, finance leasing, bank acceptance certificates and credit lines, commissions and fees associated with securities brokerage, new issues of shares, underwriters, redemption, exchanges, options and futures contracts for commodities, portfolio management fees and other financial charges , In addition to explicit financial fees. Fees for the use of intellectual property rights: represent the net fees received and paid related to the use of patents, copyrights, trademarks, franchises, etc., computer software, cinematography, development and licensing of reproduction and / or distribution such as copyright of books, sound recordings and related rights. Telecommunications, Computer and Information Technology: These include: Telecommunications services: Net receipts and payments for broadcasting or broadcasting of audio, video, data or other information via telephone, telex, telegraph, cable and satellite television and radio programs, e-mail, networks, telephone conferences, etc. Computer Services: represents net receipts and payments for database creation, saving and time series software on the Internet, data processing, hardware consulting, application design and development, customized implementation and programming, maintenance and repair of related sub-equipment, and upload computer-related materials through Internet. Information technology services: Net receipts and payments for news agency services, database services and Internet search outlets. This category also includes direct subscriptions to newspapers and periodicals. Personal, cultural and recreational services: Net receipts and payments for the services and fees of actors, directors and producers involved in the production of films and television, and the download of audiovisual works, films and music, including recordings of live performances, heritage services and other cultural services, sports and other recreational services . Insurance services: represent net receipts and payments for life insurance, savings insurance and non-life insurance, freight insurance, common guarantees, insurance-related services, pension schemes and standardized guarantees. There business services: Net receipts and payments for R & D services, Professional and management services and Technical, trade related other business services as well as operational leasing services. Government Services: The credit entries cover mostly income from services rendered by Jordanian embassies and Jordanian diplomatic & political missions abroad as well as services provided by public institutions to non-residents. The debit entries cover expenditures of Jordanian military, the government and public institutions to non-residents for settlement of commercial payments and for the purchase of services. Related data are obtained from ITRS forms. Primary income : represent net receipts and payments for the provision of factor of production (labor, capital, land and natural resources) This account includes the following items: Worker's Compensations (credit): Represent income and benefits obtained by resident workers, including seasonal and cross border workers, as a compensation for being employed abroad by non-residents. Workers Compensations (debit): Represent income and benefits obtained by non-resident workers , including seasonal and cross border workers, as a compensation for working in the kingdom. Investment income (credit) :represents the profits and interest earned on resident's holdings of equity or financial assets such as bank deposits, loan bonds, and securities issued by non-resident entities. It covers the interests of central bank investments, commercial banks, public government and the private sector. Investment income (debit) :represent interest and profits payable to non-residents on their acquisition of equity or financial assets issued by resident entities. Secondary income : shows current transfers between residents and non-residents and consists of personal transfers and other current transfers. Personal transfers: All current transfers in cash or in kind made or received by resident households to or from nonresident households as workers' remittances. Other current transfers include taxes on income, wealth and social contributions paid by households to social insurance . Current international cooperation: current transfers in cash or in kind between governments of different countries or between governments and international organizations. Capital and Financial Account : Consists of the following: Capital Account : This account consists of both capital transfers and acquisition or disposal of non-financial non-produced assets, It also includes the value of grants and assistance of a capitalist nature. Financial Account : Consists of the following : Direct Investment (Assets): Net residents investment in equity outside the Kingdom (10% or more of total voting power / right of ownership), reinvested earnings, and net other claims on non-resident subsidiaries. Direct Investment (Liabilities):Net non-resident investments in the Kingdom (10% or more of total voting power right of ownership), reinvested earnings, and net other claims for non-resident enterprises in the Kingdom's resident institutions. Portfolio Investment (Assets): Covers residents ownership of foreign equity (less than 10% of total voting power/ right of ownership) , foreign debt securities and foreign financial instruments, held by residents and externally issued, other than those included in foreign direct investment and reserve assets Portfolio Investment (Liabilities): Covers non-residents ownership of domestic equity less than 10% of total voting power/ right of ownership), debt securities and financial instruments held by non-residents issued by resident institutions, other than those included in foreign direct investment and reserve assets. Financial derivatives (Assets): represent the resident claims arising from transactions in securities of futures contracts and option contracts. Financial Derivatives (Liabilities): Non-resident claims arising from transactions in securities of futures contracts and option contracts. Other Investment (Assets): Includes all financial transactions related to residents and not included in direct investment, portfolio investment or reserve assets. Classified according to the tool to Other equity, trade credit and loans and cash and deposits Insurance, pension, and standardized guarantee schemes and other accounts receivable/payable. Other Investment (Liabilities): Includes all financial transactions related to non-residents and not included in direct investment, portfolio investment or reserve assets. Classified according to the tool to other equity, trade credit and loans and cash and deposits Insurance, pension, and standardized guarantee schemes and other accounts receivable/payable. Reserve Assets: External assets available and controlled by the CBJ and could be used for financing BOP imbalances. It includes monetary gold, SDRs, reserve position with the IMF, foreign exchange assets (currency & deposits, and securities), and other financial assets. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The data collection system provides for the timely receipt of data by the CBJ. Thus, monthly data from the ITRS and data from the MOF are received three weeks after the end of the month. Data from the CCD and ASE are received two weeks after the end of the reference month. Survey data from transportation and other companies are received one month after the end of the reference month. Merchandise trade data are received electronically from DOS around six weeks after the end of the reference month. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The BOP Division continuously verifies the reported statistics where possible. The verification process involves the following: (i) trend analysis to isolate data outliers; (ii) verification of relations between components and totals; (iii) price/volume analysis of merchandise trade data; (iv) data checks on ITRS forms; and through surveys implemented by DOS or related agencies in a necessary basis. High- value or other unusual transactions identified in primary or secondary data sources are queried with transactors or with the source data providers. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Adjustments in line with the BOP guidelines are applied in the compilation of the BOP data when necessary as following: Goods : Exports (f.o.b.) and Imports ( c.i.f.) data are compiled according to the special system of trade by DOS from customs declaration forms. Adjustments are made on imports data to f.o.b. basis by deducting freight and insurance costs which are estimated based on data officially declared by the Jordan Customs Department since 2019. Services: Travel: data estimations are based on the results of travel survey conducted by DOS and the data on arrivals and departures provided by the Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities via the Ministry of Interior on a monthly basis, besides to the information provided by Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities with regard to performance of the hotels and travel operating tours. Transportation: data are broken down to additional categories on passengers and freight and by type of transport in light of information from main transportation companies. Primary Income : Compensation of Employees : data are estimated based on data of inward and outward employees' wages and salaries reported by banks Secondary Income : Workers remittances: are estimated based on data reported by banks and exchange companies about workers remittances since 2024, with a back casting since 2019, along with estimates of informal remittances inflows and outflows Reserve assets : Flows computed from end-month outstanding positions reported in the CBJ balance sheet are adjusted to exclude changes in positions due to valuation and other changes in volume 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures There are no other statistical procedures. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The BOP Division validate its data where possible, unless availability of new data from other sources than it is used. The media is closely monitored for information on international transactions, especially for financial account transactions such as grants, loans, direct investment, and portfolio investment. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The BOP Division Staff routinely monitoring the developments in the size and sign of the errors and omissions item and seeks to understand them. For this purpose, crosschecks between entries in the current, capital, and financial accounts are analyzed. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Studies on fluctuations in the data, including the source of errors and omissions, are carried out occasionally by the BOP Division. Numerical analyses of revision performance, estimates of the impact of revisions on movements between periods, and detailed assessment of BOP items are undertaken to inform statistical processes. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Revision analyses are used primarily for internal use. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Non published but non confidential sub aggregated data are made available upon request. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points A contact telephone, fax, e-mail and address are identified in the CBJ’s Monthly Statistical Bulletin (MSB) and website, as well as on the IMF’s Dissemination Standard Bulletin Board, where in addition contact names are provided for each subject field. Assistance to users of statistics is available upon request. The survey of users are conducted periodically. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs No catalogue of publications is available, but users have access to information provided on the CBJ website. Also, the Monthly Statistical Bulletin (MSB) and Annual Report (AR) of the CBJ are free of charge.
