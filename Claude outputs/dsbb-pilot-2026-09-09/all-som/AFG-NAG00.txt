 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Statistical Framework: Currently, the national accounts estimates broadly follow the concepts, definitions, and classifications of the System of National Accounts 1993 (SNA 1993), as the basic data availability permit. However, based on current state of basic data, analytical and policy needs, and resources available for implementation, 1968 SNA concepts are applied in most cases. Concepts: Value added by industrial activity is calculated as gross production less intermediate consumption for production activities (agriculture and industry sectors) and for services the income approach is used (wages and salaries and other benefits + mixed income + consumption of fixed capital + net indirect taxes. GDP by expenditure category is calculated as the sum of the final use of goods and services through final consumption, gross capital formation, and exports less imports. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification/sectorization systems used are broadly consistent with internationally accepted standards, guidelines, or good practices. Agriculture covers all establishments included in major division 1 of the ISIC, and the following sections: agricultural and livestock production (ISIC major group 112), hunting, trapping and game propagation (ISIC major 113), forestry and logging (ISIC division 12), and fishing (ISIC division 13). Industry comprises value added in mining and quarrying; manufacturing; electricity, gas and water; and construction. Manufacturing is also reported in the subgroup breakdown as in the ISIC. Because of the growing importance of manufacturing, more details of the sub-sectors are shown separately in the Industry Sector. Manufacturing is the sum of gross output less the value of intermediate goods consumed in production for industries classified in ISIC major division 3. It consists of a) food, beverage, and tobacco comprising ISIC division 31; b) textiles and clothing, division 32; c) wood and related products, division 33; d) paper and paper related products, division 34; e) chemical and petroleum product, groups 351—356; and f) basic metals and mineral products, division 36 and 37. Services correspond to ISIC division 50-99. They include value added in all other branches of economic activity, i.e. wholesale and retail trade, hotels, restaurants, transportation and communication, banking insurance and real estate, ownership of dwellings, public administration and defense, NGOs, professional, and personal services including domestic help, education, health care, garage, repair, and other services. GDP by expenditure categories includes 1) private consumption (or final consumption expenditure of household) is imputed as residual item, 2) consumption of government and non-profit institutions serving households (NPISH), 3) gross domestic investment including gross fixed capital formation and increase in stock, and exports and imports of goods and non-factor services. Scope of the data Unit Coverage National accounts cover, in principle, economic activities of all resident units in conformity with the SNA 1993 . All resident units are not yet classified into five institutional sectors as recommended by the SNA 1993 (plan to prepare in three years; see plans for improvement) . In addition, there is the external sector (rest of the world sector) covering the external transactions with nonresident units. Transaction Coverage In principle, a ll recorded market transactions, including barter transactions, are covered, as is production for own use. Geographic Coverage National accounts cover the entire territory of Afghanistan. Unrecorded activity Adjustments are made to cover the unrecorded imports, the undercoverage in food industry and in building construction industry, and informal activities. 2.4.1 Valuation Market output is valued at producers ’ prices. Output for own use is valued at equivalent market prices. Sales and excise taxes are included in the valuation of intermediate consumption. Both imports and exports are valued f.o.b.. Information on insurance and freight for merchandise imports is available, and c.i.f./f.o.b. adjustment is made by the CSO. Transactions in foreign currency are converted using the mid-point exchange rate prevailing in the market at the moment they take place. 2.4.2 Recording basis Transactions and flows are recorded on a cash basis, including government-related transactions. Work-in-progress is not yet recorded in the period it is produced. Recording period: Solar year ending March 20, e.g. for the year 1383 the covered period is from 21 March 2004-20 March 2005 . 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Presently, data are collected during March to June on the basis of administrative reporting system for government sector, including public enterprises and large private enterprise activities. For the private non-registered small and informal sector activities, ad-hoc surveys are conducted throughout the year. However, the data sources are not quite kept under continuous review to ensure that the data collection program is comprehensive. Annual enterprise/establishment statistics to be collected through a regular survey program not yet established . Currently the Ministry of Finance (MOF) is in the process of computerizing the taxpayer enterprises/establishments records (about 20,000), and in the due course the MOF will provide the up-to-date comprehensive registered records to the CSO. Further, the Asian Development Bank is in process of financing the Integrated Business Enterprise Survey which also will provide information on enterprises/establishments. Household surveys are not conducted on a regular basis. The last household income and expenditure survey was conducted in 1987. The Asian Development Bank is in process of financing the Integrated Living Standard Survey which includes household income and expenditure surveys. Comprehensive government finance statistics are not available regularl y. The data do not include, in particular: operations of extra-budgetary funds, all defense related expenditures, local government operations, data on capital stocks. Data collections based on administrative data sources are not adequate , and there are significant gaps in terms of level of details. Price statistics used to derive constant price estimates are not adequate. Price statistics are not available at a sufficient level of detail. Price statistics are not consistent with the variables being deflated in terms of price concept and coverage. Ad hoc surveys are conducted when needed to fill data gaps. Ad-hoc surveys are conducted, when necessary, to supplement regular surveys. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Most source data in Afghanistan, including surveys and administrative records, are based on Afghan definitions, scope, and classifications which are not consistent with the SNA 1993 recommendations. Modification and adjustment are needed to ensure the consistency with international guidelines. However, source data are consistent with the time of recording, reference periods, and valuation of national accounts statistics. Administrative records used to compile the national accounts statistics are not quite adequate for the methodological requirements of the national accounts statistics. Pilot tests have been conducted to ensure that data collected will allow compilation of estimates according to international guidelines. The CSO does not have a comprehensive economic survey program that serves national accounts. The current surveys conducted by the CSO are limited to manufacturing, construction, and transport. The data collected covers only output (quantity and value) and wages and salaries. The umber of establishments surveyed are 220 for manufacturing, 400 for construction, and 150 for transport. The definitions, scope, and classifications are not consistent with national accounts requirements. To resolve all these issues substantial surveys are needed. As part of the Strategic Master Plan, the CSO is planning to conduct an Integrated Business Enterprise Survey that will cover main economic activities. The definitions, scope, valuation, and classification will be consistent with the SNA 1993 requirements. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data collection and processing timetables are adequate to meet timeliness and periodicity for disseminating the national accounts statistics. Respondents are made aware of the deadlines set for reporting. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The survey results undergo comprehensive adjustments as the scope, definitions, and the information compiled are not in line with the national accounts needs. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Compilation is done using direct and indirect methods as directed by the availability of basic data. Adjustments are made for undercoverage in several industries. Some indicators are used also to extrapolate existing estimates or to update estimates of ratios and coefficients. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Production method Agriculture Agriculture is the most important sector in the economy with more than 80% of population actively involved in it. Due to the high level of subsistence farming in Afghanistan, much of agricultural production is for own consumption or not exchanged for money. Especially, the current situation with more than 3 million returning refugees settling as subsistence farmers increases the difficulty of measuring the contribution of agriculture to GDP. Therefore, adjustments are made for unaccounted and auto-consumption in the agriculture sector. For most of agricultural crop, production figures are based on the volume of harvest multiplied by prices and value added ratio. The production data are provided by the Ministry of A griculture, the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), and the World Food Programme (WFP). The Ministry of Agriculture and FAO prepare only annual production and area data for major crops, vegetables and fruits, and livestock. But the ministry had just begun to rebuild its Research and Planning Department, including data collection. The CSO and Ministry of Agriculture work closely on the survey preparation including the questionnaires. The following surveys have been conducted in Kabul and nearby provinces (more provinces will be covered as security and financing permit): Production and intermediate consumption on other crops such as pulse and nuts, roots and tubers, vegetable, and fruits; Production and intermediate consumption of livestock; Producer prices for each crop and livestock to replace the current method (CPI-profit margin-transportation-taxes); Intermediate consumption of each crop including major crops; Stock of major crops and livestock; Consumption of fixed assets; and Forestry and fishing for both production and intermediate consumption. Mining and quarrying The Ministry of Mining and Industry reports only data on production quantity of public enterprises and the Economy Department of CSO (ED) provides private sector mining. However, the following surveys are needed to ensure the completeness of mining sectors. Complete set of data on mineral production and intermediate consumption, including informal sector activities; and Stocks for all mineral items. Manufacturing The Ministry of Industry, Ministry of Light Industry, and Ministry of Commerce provide manufacturing production and producer prices data for public and private enterprises. However, the statistical agencies of these ministries lack capacity and equipment to function properly. Small ad-hoc surveys are mainly conducted in Kabul as follows: Survey on informal sector activities on food and beverage; Survey on informal sector activities on textile, wearing apparel and leather including carpets and rug; Survey on informal sector activities on wood and wood products, including furniture; Stock and inventory of each manufacturing. Adjustm ents are made for the food and handicraft manufacturing. This is because food and handicraft manufacturing are small, un-registered, and informal sector activities. Electricity, gas and water The Ministry of Power and Water provides information related to public sector only. The Ministry of Commerce provides data for the private sector, but these data are not very reliable. Survey on private sector and informal sector activities, particularly the household generators and water supply (well). Stock and inventory of electricity, gas, and water. Construction Data sources from the Ministries’ of Construction, Reconstruction, Planning, Commerce, Industry, and Finance (donor financed construction, schools, clinics, etc) are used for estimating the growth in formal sector of construction, and the price of cement is used as a deflator. Informal sector activities are based purely on assumptions and ad-hoc surveys on private sector and informal sector activities, particularly the household construction and own construction and rural construction using mud. Wholesale and retail trade, hotels, and restaurants Wholesale and retail trade is estimated on the basis of assumption on domestic goods production and imports. Hotels and restaurants are based on public enterprises' annual balance sheets obtained by the CSO and some sample surveys for private sector activities. In Afghanistan, it is in general difficult to distinguish between wholesale and retail trade and between subgroups of hotels, restaurants, etc., because small units performing a combination of functions are relatively more important. The data on which the estimates have to be based are also generally insufficiently detailed. In the future, gross output and intermediate consumption may be estimated on the basis of employment data, coupled with output per person engaged, derived from a survey of a small number of establishments. A better approach would be to estimate gross margins in the case of the wholesale and retail trades by multiplying estimates of the producer’s value of supply of commodities according to kind by distribution percentage mark-ups collected from a small-scale sample of these establishments. Use of available data from the administration of sales, turnover or similar taxes and/or income taxes should also result in improved estimates. In Afghanistan, it is also difficult to achieve full coverage of retail trade and eating and drinking places because of the prevalence of smaller units, such as market and street stalls, street selling, coffee/tea houses, etc. The most satisfactory way of solving these problems is to undertake relatively large-scale, comprehensive sample surveys at infrequent intervals and to extrapolate the results of these inquiries by means of small-scale abbreviated annual sample surveys. Transport, storage and communication In Afghanistan the transport, storage, and communications industries are divided into land transport, air transport, and services allied to transport, including storage and warehousing and communication. This approach corresponds to the more detailed classification according to major groups of the ISIC recommended in the SNA 1993 . As a land-locked country, Afghanistan faces a difficult situation, being more than 2,000 km away from the nearest seaport. Country’s transport system is organized in three complementary modalities, namely, by land, by water, and by air. Note that transport by water is almost negligible. Currently basic data to derive value added in this sector are from the Ministry of Transportation and Communication, public and private enterprises for land and air transportation, and in-house surveys mainly for land transport (taxi, truck, and pickup) in Kabul city. Finance, insurance, and real estate Basic data are provided by DAB in aggregated form for central bank and commercial banks operations. In-house surveys are conducted for money service providers including money changers and lenders. No private insurance companies exist yet in Afghanistan. Producers of government services Data are provided by the MOF, but only the Central Government is covered. The CSO has requested the MOF to provide the local government budget as well, presently not available. Social, personal, and community services This sector covers all private services carried out within the country’s production boundaries. Limited sample surveys had been conducted in Kabul and assumptions made for the rest of the country. It is necessary to have more surveys in Kabul and surveys should be carried out in the other provinces. Assumptions are made for ownership of dwellings, domestic help, education, health care, garage, repair, and other services. The population census data (including rural, urban, provincial, and weights), small ad-hoc survey, and staff assumptions are mainly used to incorporate the informal sector activities. GDP by expenditure categories Presently, only the current price GDP by expenditure is estimated using indirect method of carrying over the GDP from the production side. Private consumption (or final consumption expenditure of household) is a residual item, including changes in stock and data discrepancies in the use of resources. Government consumption includes all current expenditure for purchases of goods and services by all levels of government. These estimates are drawn directly from the Government budget. Gross domestic fixed investment consists of additions to the fixed assets of the economy. Fixed assets cover land improvements (fences, ditches, drains, etc.) plant, machinery and equipment (durable goods) purchases; the construction of roads and the like, including commercial and industrial buildings, offices, schools, hospitals, and private residential dwellings. Fixed capital assets are: à durable goods that last more than one year ; à for the purpose of production or generating income ; and à cost more than US$300. The construction estimates are the gross output of construction in the GDP by type of economic activities and capital goods are from the imports of durable goods of Customs offices and Central Statistics Office plus 20% for installation mark-up. Exports and imports of goods and non-factor services are drawn directly from the BOP, which includes adjustments for unrecorded trade. Exports and imports of goods and non-factor services represent the value of all goods and other market services provided to and from the rest of the world. These cover merchandise, transportation, travel, insurance, and other non-factor services such as government transactions and various fees but exclude dividends, interest and other investment income receipts/payments as well as labour income. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results There is no procedure to validate intermediate results against other data sources, as there are no other independent data sources that the CSO national accounts estimates can be checked against. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data No systemic procedure exists to routinely assess the potential discrepancies in intermediate data. However, in the case by case the raw data are assessed and adjusted appropriately. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The GDP by economic activity is first estimated. The same GDP is used for expenditure components, leaving the private consumption expenditure as a residual, including data discrepancies. Because no recent household income expenditure survey has been conducted, there is no supply and use framework currently in place for investigating discrepancies and making the statistical outputs consistent. Supply and use tables, which are considered as a significant tool to check data discrepancies, are not compiled in Afghanistan. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revision studies are not undertaken on a regular basis. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Not applicable, as no revision studies on a regular basis are undertaken.
