 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions All data have been converted and are presented in accordance with the GFSM 2014 framework as recommended by the IMF. The data are reported according to this framework. The Statement of Sources and Uses of Cash for each level of government is published quarterly in the Bank's Quarterly Bulletin . The Statement of Government Operations for each level of general government is only submitted to the IMF annually for publication in the Government Finance Statistics (GFS) Yearbook. Budgetary and provincial government statistics are not yet on full accrual accounting systems, but adjusted cash and certain adjustments are made to complete the Statement of Government Operations for these spheres of government. No Other economic flows statements are published. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The monthly statement of Central Government Operations presents revenue, expenditure (including separate data on interest payments), the balance (deficit/surplus) and financing by financial instrument of the national government (i.e. the national government departments excluding SSFs and other national EBIs. The central government expenditure is available per vote and according to the organizational structure of government. Information on the indebtedness to different sub-sectors of the economy is not yet available. Revenue and expenditure are defined and classified broadly according to the IMF's " Government Finance Statistics Manual 1986 ( GFSM 1986 ) " before September 2003. From September 2003 data are compiled according to the IMF's GFSM 2014 , with the following exceptions: An insignificantly small number of indistinguishable grants are included in revenue. Central government operations, channelled through the consolidated Exchequer and Paymaster General Accounts, do not identify lending minus repayment operations separately. This implies that revenue data include repayments of loans extended and expenditure data include lending transactions that are part of the normal day to day operations of the spending agencies. Extraordinary receipts and payments are included in the calculations of the deficit. Premiums that form part of extraordinary receipts are excluded from revenue as this is included under financing. Discount on government stock issues is considered to be interest subtracted in advance and should be reported as expenditure at the time that the payment is made for the redemption of the debt. The discount is not included in expenditure but is treated as part of financing transactions. Total expenditure of national government included the cost of revaluation on maturing foreign loans until March 1999. Since April 1999, data was revised to treat this cost as part of financing transactions. National Treasury amended the way in which certain expenditures are recorded in the National Revenue Fund (NRF). Debt portfolio restructuring costs were previously presented as part of the cost of state debt. Since April 2000, debt portfolio restructuring costs are recorded as extraordinary payments. Scope of the data The operations of central government cover: 42 national government departments, standing appropriations, and statutory payments. Exceptions to coverage The data for national extra-budgetary institutions (EBIs) and social security funds (SSFs) are reported quarterly and not monthly. Therefore, this data is not included in central government statistics as only national government data are reported on a monthly basis. As such, central government only refers to national government (budgetary). National government operates on a cash-flow basis, therefore only the Statement of Sources and Uses of Cash is available. Unrecorded activity Data are comprehensive and no unrecorded activities are observed. 2.4.1 Valuation Both domestic and foreign bonds of national government are revaluated on a monthly basis. The outstanding value is adjusted accordingly. However, no Statement of other economic flows is currently compiled. National government debt is valued at face value and market prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis In accordance with GFS principles, the reporting of total revenue and expenditure are based on transactions when payment takes place, thus leading to cash-flow records. Transactions are recorded in the period in which payment takes place rather than in the budget period for which they were authorized. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures All relevant data are recorded on a gross basis. This allows for data to be netted out where necessary. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs All data on the operations of central government are compiled from administrative records kept by the National Treasury and the government departments. No estimates are required, as the accounts are comprehensive. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording All the data have been converted and is presented according to the GFSM 2014 framework as recommended by the IMF. The data are being reported according to this framework with the exception of the balance sheet and the other economic flows. All relevant data are recorded on a gross basis. This allows for data to be netted out where necessary. The "Budget Review" has definitions for some of the categories. The metadata pages on the Standard Chart of Accounts (SCOA) also have information. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The PFMA requires that the data be made available within 30 days after the end of each month. An advance release calendar can be accessed through the internet on: https://www.resbank.co.za/en/home/calendar/2021-ADVANCE-RELEASE-CALENDAR It indicates the date and time of the release to the public and also to be posted on the website for the SDDS. 3.2.1 Source data assessment All the data on the operations of central government are subject to an annual audit by the Office of the Auditor-General of South Africa (AGSA). 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Central government operates on an adjusted cash-flow basis, therefore it is not possible to compile a Statement of Other economic flows , hence the 98% adherence to the GFSM 2014 . At the same time, the national government data are aligned with the GFSM 2014 concepts and the data compilation procedures are sound. Annually the Auditor-General audits the accounts of national government. Once approved, preliminary data are replaced with final audited data. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures An abridged table has been constructed to facilitate adjustments to the source data. No adjustments are made to the cash-flow revenue. The cash-flow expenditure is adjusted for the following items, namely, late departmental requests, outstanding transfers, revaluation of index-linked bonds, amortised discount, capitalised interest on retail bonds and surrenders. The extraordinary receipts are also adjusted for premiums. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Data are assessed on a monthly and quarterly basis and statistical discrepancies are corrected in the following month or quarter. Over and above this, the data are balanced against the annual audit report. A general note is also published in the Quarterly Bulletin which states that data are preliminary for two years. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Data are assessed on a monthly and quarterly basis and statistical discrepancies are corrected in the following month or quarter. Over and above this, the data are balanced against the annual audit report. A general note is also published in the Quarterly Bulletin which states that data for the past two years are preliminary and subject to revisions. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs A nil test has been built into the analysis. Should this test not equate to nil, an investigation is immediately embarked on to solve the problem before publication date. In other cases rounding off numbers can result in the test not equating to nil. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions are done monthly and quarterly. Audited financial statements data are used to revise preliminary data on an annual basis. Unallocated data are reclassified to the correct categories if need be, monthly or quarterly. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Revisions are done monthly, quarterly, and annually. Unallocated data is reclassified to the correct categories. Data flow internally to National Accounts Division (NAD) and Capital Market and Flow of Funds Division (CMFFD) of ESD on a quarterly basis. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Requests are received telephonically or via email by institutions such as the BIS, IMF, National Treasury, Stats SA, SARS, private institutions and scholars. The requests are placed on record and disseminated accordingly as mentioned in 0.1.3. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Details of contact persons are disseminated on the metadata pages. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs None. However, a list of all publications of the Bank is sent to the outside agencies with the application forms for inclusion on the distribution list. The list also contains the prices charged for the respective publications.
