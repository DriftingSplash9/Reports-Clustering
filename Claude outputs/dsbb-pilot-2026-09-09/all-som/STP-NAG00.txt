 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The National Accounts Department of the National Statistics Institute (INE) is responsible for compiling the National Accounts. The accounts were prepared in detail for the year 1996, including supply and use tables by product, the aim being to apply a simplified compilation methodology in line with the recommendations of the 1993 United Nations System of National Accounts – SNA93, at current and constant prices. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization For economic activities, the classification used is the CEA-S.T.P. based on the ISIC Rev. 3. The Harmonized System is used for foreign trade and for the consumer price index and the classification of consumption according to purpose (COICOP). With respect to institutional sectors, an “ad-hoc” classification was applied that was meant to be based on the SNA93 classification, to be reviewed in the construction of the new basis for the national accounts. Scope of the data Geographic Coverage: The National Accounts cover the entire country. Transaction Coverage: All formal market and some informal market operations are covered in principle, including home consumption. Unit Coverage: In calculating the national accounts aggregates, all units with activities in the economic territory of São Tome and Principe are covered. The reference year and base year for the national accounts of São Tome and Principe is 1996. These accounts, compiled according to a simplified methodology, were intended to reflect the recommendations of the 1993 System of National Accounts (SNA93), at current and constant prices. The estimates for 1996 and 1997 were prepared for the principal institutional sectors (production account, distribution operations, and capital account) and by branches of activity and product, using the production and expenditure approaches. For the following years, from 1998 to 2001, rapid estimates of GDP are produced at current prices and constant 1996 prices, broken down by the GVA to the first level of the CEA, using the production and expenditure approaches. For the years 1996 and 1997, the national accounts were prepared based on the detailed supply and use table of products in terms of volume and value, for the principal institutional sectors and by branch of activity. Based on these tables, the production and use accounts were prepared by branch of activity and for the sectors of government, companies, households, non-profit financial institutions serving households, as well as a goods and services account for the entire economy (production and expenditure). For the year 1998, indicators resulting from the harmonized survey of companies and administrative sources were taken into consideration. In compiling the estimates for the following years (1999-2001), the national accounts felt the effects of the break in production of the base statistics that were used as their source. Thus, starting in 1999, the principal sources provide only administrative data. Unrecorded activity In the base year (1996), the informal sector is taken into account through household surveys and other specific surveys. Due to lack of reliable statistical information, activities related to the importing of goods not recorded at the customs border, such as tobacco, are not covered. No estimates are produced for illegal activities. 2.4.1 Valuation For the base year and up to the estimates for 1998, production is valued at producer prices; intermediate consumption and final consumption are estimated at purchase prices; estimates are done on an accrual basis, except for government agencies that use the cash basis. Exports are recorded on an FOB basis and imports on a CIF basis. The valuation period is the calendar year. 2.4.2 Recording basis National accounts aggregates are estimated at market prices. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The principal sources are: For general government: budget records and individual accounts of local government agencies. For nonfinancial corporations: harmonized annual survey of public and private companies. For financial corporations: special survey of Central Bank, depository corporations, exchange houses, and informal activities. For household transactions: information from other institutional sectors. For non-profit institutions: survey of non-governmental organizations (NGOs) For operations with the rest of the world: detailed data from the balance of payments. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording (a) The following sources were used to compile the estimates for the base year 1996 and for 1997: Using the production approach: Primary Sector: (agriculture, fishing, and forestry) the data are compiled administratively at the Ministry of Agriculture; harmonized annual survey of agricultural, fishing and forestry companies (1996-1997) to define total production, intermediate consumption, production for own use, and gross fixed capital formation; and specific household surveys. Processing Industry: information comes from the harmonized annual survey of large companies and small manufacturing units (1996-1997) to determine production, intermediate consumption, production for own use, and gross fixed capital formation. The sample for this survey was based on a survey of companies relating to the year 1996. Administrative records from the Ministry of Commerce and Industry, calculations of volume and of deflation based on measures of physical volume and prices, and specific household surveys are also used. Energy Sector: the information is obtained based on the harmonized annual survey of large companies (1996-1997) to determine production, intermediate consumption, production for own use, and gross fixed capital formation. Mining Industry: harmonized annual survey of companies (1996-1997) to determine production, intermediate consumption, production for own use, and gross fixed capital formation. Construction: the data are compiled based on the annual survey of construction companies (1996-1997) to determine the value of production; records on construction permits from the Public Works and City Planning Directorate; imported construction materials; calculation of volume and of deflation based on measures of physical volume and prices. Trade: the data are compiled based on the harmonized annual survey of companies (1996-1997) to determine business volumes and margins. Specific studies were conducted on the trade margins (wholesale and retail) in 1996 for the major food products and automobiles. Transport and Communications: the data are compiled based on the harmonized annual survey of companies (1996-1997) to determine the value of shipments, gross weight, and other indicators of volume; extrapolation based on measures of physical volume and specific surveys on transport of goods and people. The sample for this survey was based on administrative data from the Finance Directorate. Government: the data are compiled through the General State Budget; the table of central government operations compiled by the Treasury Directorate; management accounts of the District Chambers, Social Security, and autonomous agencies. Financial Institutions: the data are compiled through the specific survey of the Central Bank and depository corporations (1996-1997) to determine production, intermediate consumption, indirectly measured financial intermediation services (FISIM). There is also a survey of exchange agencies and informal exchange activity (1996-1997). Other Services: specific surveys of private sector units for activities in education and health, real estate, vehicle leasing, services to companies, and various services in order to determine sales, intermediate consumption, production for own use, fixed capital formation; administrative records; estimates based on the variation in residential units and variations in rental values and calculations of volume and of deflation based on measurements of volume and of prices. Using the expenditure approach : Government Consumption: the data are compiled based on the General State Budget to determine data on central government purchases. Gross fixed capital formation (GFCF): the source for the government component of GFCF is the Public Investment Program (PIP) and harmonized annual surveys of agricultural companies (new planting) (1996-1997); the private component is compiled based on data from construction activity taken from surveys of institutional units (companies and households – 1996); survey on livestock production. Changes in Stocks: the data are compiled essentially based on information from companies producing cacao and companies producing fuel and oils (1996-1997). Imports and Exports of Goods: the data are compiled based on the Balance of Payments, records from the Customs Directorate processed at the INE, and data from the offices for sanitary inspection of export products. Imports and Exports of Services: data are compiled based on the Balance of Payments. Private consumption: calculated as residual, the balance between estimated GDP using the production approach and the sum of other expenditures. Institutional sectors accounts (estimates for 1996 and 1997): General Government: budgetary records and individual accounts of local government agencies. Nonfinancial Corporations: harmonized annual survey of public and private companies. Financial Corporations: specific survey of Central Bank, depository corporations, exchange houses, and informal activity. Households: based on information from the other institutional sectors. Nonprofit institutions: survey of nongovernmental organizations (NGOs). Rest of world: detailed data from the balance of payments. (b) For the year 1998, indicators from the harmonized survey of companies and administrative sources were taken into account. In compiling the estimates for the following years (1999-2001), the National Accounts were affected by the break in production of the basic statistics that were used as their source. Thus, starting in 1999, the main sources come solely from administrative data. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques For the years before1996, the national accounts were prepared based on the detailed supply and use tables by products, in volume and in value, for the principal institutional sectors and by branch of activity. Based on these tables, the production and use accounts were prepared by branch of activity and for the sectors of government agencies, companies, households, nonprofit financial institutions serving households, as well as a goods and services account for the entire economy (production and expenditure). GDP using the production approach at current prices and at constant prices is the result of adding the GVA in 31 branches of activity and subtracting FISIM and import duties. Production Approach For years after 1996, the estimate was based on applying to the GVA for each branch of activity an index calculated for the increase in production and an estimated index reflecting the evolution of prices compared to the previous year. Expenditure Approach The data for public consumption, gross capital formation, and imports and exports of goods and services are estimated based on sources. The estimate for private consumption is derived from the reconciliation of GDP using the expenditure approach with GDP using the production approach. Reconciliation Reconciliation of differences between measures of GDP: a simplified supply and use table is used to determine the differences between the production and expenditure approaches. None
