 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: SNA2008 and ESA2010 are used as the basic concepts to the estimation of national accounts. Definition: GDP measures the total value of final goods and services produced by domestic institutional units during the period. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification systems: ESA2010 is used for classifying institutional sectors. NACE Rev.2 is used as a national classification for industries by kind of activities. United Nations Statistics Division - Trade Statistics and HCN (Hungarian Combined Nomenclature) are used for international transactions. International transactions in services are based on the Balance of Payments Manual, 6th edition (BMP6). The COICOP classification is used for household final comsumption and from 2010 the COFOG classification is used for the distinction of individual and collective government consumption (until 2010 a Hungarian national classification by purpose was used). 2.2.1 Scope Classification systems: ESA2010 is used for classifying institutional sectors. NACE Rev.2 is used as a national classification for industries by kind of activities. United Nations Statistics Division - Trade Statistics and HCN (Hungarian Combined Nomenclature) are used for international transactions. International transactions in services are based on the Balance of Payments Manual, 6th edition (BMP6). The COICOP classification is used for household final comsumption and from 2010 the COFOG classification is used for the distinction of individual and collective government consumption (until 2010 a Hungarian national classification by purpose was used). Scope of the data Transactions coverage: GDP includes all activities within the SNA production boundary, including illegal transactions such as prostitution and trade of narcotics, smuggling of tobacco and some hidden activities, for years 1995 and onwards. Geographic coverage: the estimates include the whole economic territory of the country, including the free trade zones. Exceptions to coverage Unrecorded activity Estimates are made for underreported and/or not surveyed production, for gross fixed capital formation (GFCF) of small-size units and for household production and consumption for own use. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: basic prices are used for measuring output and purchasers' prices for intermediate consumption. In government and NPI sectors cost basis is used. Final use of GDP is measured at market (purchaser's) prices. Owner-occupied dwellings are valued at cost basis. Exports of goods are valued at f.o.b.(free on board) and imports of goods are valued also at f.o.b. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: accrual basis is used for most transactions except production in government and part of nonprofit institutions (NPI) sectors where time-adjusted cash basis is used. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Sources used for national accounts estimations are own data collections of HCSO and administrative data takeover from other government offices and institutions. National accounts department and data collector departments of HCSO regularly discuss the yearly National Statistical Survey Programme (NSSP) and the contents and specifications of surveys to have the necessary detailed data for the national accounts estimations. HCSO has bilateral agreements with institutions which collect statistics in their own fields of work and with holders of administrative data collections (records, registers, databases etc.) on statistical cooperation are signed. Nearly 50 signed agreements exist between HCSO and other public data providers, of which data are used for national accounts purposes. Production approach: calculation of GDP is based to a great extent on the Business Register (BR). The BR contains every unit with a tax number. There are no criteria (for activity, revenue or work force) to get into the Register. A harmonised and consistent database, the „common database" (KAB) is used as an input data source of the non-financial corporations sector. Data from the survey of annual performances and expenses of enterprises are supplemented with corporate income tax return data at individual (enterprise) level. The common database is the basis of Structural Business Statistics (SBS) and the non-financial corporations sector data of national accounts. The main data sources of the financial corporations sector are balance sheets and profit and loss accounts collected by the National Bank of Hungary and corporate tax returns. These tax returns also contain supplementary information not included in annual reports. Among HCSO data collections labour statistics, labour cost surveys and surveys on performances and expenses and on investments are the most important ones. In addition, we use data from reports of the central government and local governments. Every general government unit in legal terms is a non-market producer and classified into the general government sector in NA. However, in the central or local government the budgetary institutions can be market producers. As they are not independent producers, they are considered as the local KAUs of the central and local government organs and the SS funds. They remain classified into the sector but their output is calculated on the basis of the sales revenue. Public corporations and public non-profit institutions are classified into GG on the basis of criteria as defined in ESA2010. For corporations classified into this sector, the data is calculated from the same sources, which are used in case of corporations classified into the non-Financial corporations sector: the corporate profit tax return and the SBS. The households sector, in line with ESA2010, covers the households both as consumers and producers. The Households sector accounts for the production of sole proprietors with a licence and other permission. The sources to estimate the figures of sole proprietors are the Business Register; the database of tax returns of personal income tax, value added tax (VAT) and fixed-rate tax of small-scale taxpayers (KATA); and the final, anonymous, individual results of value added tax audits of sole proprietors. The data sources of Non-profit institutions serving households sector are the following: statistical survey on non-profit organisations; statistical survey on churches; financial statements by political parties published in the Hungarian Official Journal. The output of the NPISHs is compiled on a cost basis. Quarterly data sources of production approach: Non-financial corporations sector: Infra-annual business survey, quarterly (STS) Deposit and loan data Retail sales, monthly VAT statement First releases published in different statistical domains Financial corporations sector: Quarterly reports of financial institutions via the Central Bank of Hungary Data transmission from the Central Bank of Hungary (MNB) for the allocation of FISIM across user sectors Government sector: Budget reports of the Hungarian State Treasury Households sector: Number of sole proprietors with a licence FISIM data on the Households sector Non-profit institutions sector: estimations based on benchmark year or previous year. Expenditure approach: Estimation of household final consumption expenditure is based on a model-based approach using the previous result of household survey and administrative data (online invoice and online cash register and VAT statement) and the retail trade survey. Total government consumption expenditure data are based on direct administrative data sources: annual calculations are based on annual reports of government institutions and the final budget report; quarterly calculations are based on monthly or quarterly reports of government institutions. The main data source is the annual survey on NPISHs conducted by HCSO. Estimates of gross fixed capital formation (GFCF) are based on quarterly statistical surveys which include imports of capital goods, use of domestic capital goods and information from housing statistics. The quarterly surveys refer not only to the new assets but for the used ones, as well. The main sources to estimate the annual GFCF are the Structural Investment Survey, within the Structural Business Statistics, the balance sheets of corporations with fewer than five employees, and enterprise data on stocks of tangible fixed assets. Data on new dwelling constructions in physical volume terms (number and square meter) from permits of put into operation are available. Changes in inventories are fully based on the quarterly survey. Acquisitions less disposals of valuables: Precious stones and metals, antiques and other objects, such as paintings, sculptures etc. and other valuables (such as jewellery fashioned out of precious stones, metals and collectors' items) are accounted as valuables. Data for estimation of the value of these items come from the external trade statistics and from the common database (KAB). Output of valuables were already annually accounted at the production side, but the P.53 was missing from the expenditure side till September 2017. Acquisition less disposals of valuables (P.53) is estimated back to 1995 at present. Exports and imports of goods had been based mainly on the monthly customs declarations for goods until May 1, 2004. Since then, Intrastat and Extrastat statistical systems have been used. For the foreign trade in services, the data had been derived entirely from the balance of payments statistics until 2004. Since then survey data have been used (collected by HCSO) for the foreign trade of business services and tourism surveys (conducted by HCSO) data are used for estimation of tourism expenditure. The other foreign trade services had been derived from the balance of payments statistics until 2004. From 2005 the data have been based on surveys of HCSO. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures GDP is estimated by production, expenditure and income approaches. The production and expenditure approaches to GDP are produced independently. The income approach to GDP is not an independent estimation because the gross operating/mixed income is estimated as a residual item. The main balancing procedure is made within the annual SUT framework . The three approaches for estimating GDP are combined in one system. In this system data are confronted and compared with each other on a detailed level in order to find possible causes of inconsistencies. The supply and use tables as an integral part of GDP balancing process were started to be used in 2008. Final SUT is compiled for t+21 months therefore at an earlier date a way of macro balancing is applied to eliminate the differences between the production side and expenditure side GDP. The macro balance comes from the balancing process between the production and expenditure sides. In the production side, the macro balance is divided into S11 and S14 sectors. Within the two sectors it is distributed into industries at NACE 4 level according to their gross value added share. Finally, using the output per gross value added ratio of a given industry, the macro balance is split between the output and the intermediate consumption. In the expenditure approach the balance is split among household final consumption expenditure, gross fixed capital formation and changes in inventories. The household final consumption expenditure and gross fixed capital formation get balance according to the share of GDP (without balance), the rest of it goes to the changes in inventories. In cases when the amount of the balance is insignificant, the whole amount is accounted as changes in inventories. Production approach: the quarterly GDP compilation is made at current and constant prices. The calculation of current price data is based on statistical and administrative data sources. During the calculations quarterly time series, consistent with available annual data, are extrapolated. Constant price data are calculated according to the annual deflation, taking into consideration the characteristics of chain-linking quarterly data. In quarterly estimations the chain-linking method has been applied for constant price calculations. HCSO decided to use the annual overlap method. In case of quarterly time series, first, constant price data are calculated at average prices of the previous year from current price data, and then the whole time series is chain-linked back to 1995 with the help of indices. The time series thus produced is built on year 2021 prices, which are only reference year prices, and the base year determining the structure is the previous year for all data of the time series, i.e. the base year differs annually. As a result, data of the time series at average prices of 2021 are not additive within the given quarter, i.e. the sum of sub-aggregates is not necessarily equal to an aggregate, therefore chain-linking has to be carried out in case of every time series (separately for sub-aggregates and aggregates). The seasonal adjustment of GDP data was made, in accordance with the principles uniformly applied in the HCSO, by the TRAMO-SEATS method with the help of JDemetra+ software. The programme options are fixed annually (the applied ARIMA model, its parameters, the regression variables quantifying the effects of working days and holidays), which change only if it is justified by the revision of basic data, or if the character of time series behaviour is strongly modified. Resulting from the character of calculation, the seasonally adjusted data in all periods can be overwritten by the last run. In case of GDP total data series, seasonal adjustment is made for the data previously adjusted for calendar effects, in compliance with Eurostat recommendations. Expenditure approach: Household final consumption expenditure is obtained by model estimation based on retail trade, administrative and soft data. For the quarterly constant price estimates, the expenditures at current prices are deflated with the CPI, at COICOP four-digit level. Government final consumption expenditures at current prices are based on administrative data sources from government institutions. Constant prices are calculated using data of labour force statistics, producer-price indices and consumer-price indices. Gross fixed capital formation (GFCF) is estimated from quarterly GFCF surveys of public and private corporations and budgetary institutions. Estimates on value of own-account construction by households are based on information on the number of dwellings built. GFCF at constant prices is calculated by deflating the current values using a price index for gross fixed capital formation, derived from construction, foreign trade and industrial price surveys. For exports and imports of goods the Intrastat and Extrastat statistical systems are used with no adjustments for shuttle trade. Constant price estimates are derived by deflation using quarterly exports and imports price indices based mainly on actual exports and imports prices. An exchange rate index is used to deflate exports and imports of services and expenditures of resident households abroad. Non-residents' consumption expenditure in Hungary is deflated by the CPI. FISIM: Calculation with two-type reference rate is used. Data are recalculated back until 1995. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Data validation refers to any activity aimed at verifying that the value of a data item comes from a given set of acceptable values. It is a key task performed in all statistical domains and particularly important for national accounts, which is a key dataset for economic analysis and policy decisions. Data source validation is conducted through multiple approaches: • completeness – detecting any missing data for specific variables or time periods, • accuracy – identifying anomalies such as unusually high, low, or unexpected values, • consistency – accounting consistency, frequency consistency between quarterly and annual accounts, consistency over time and consistency of prices, • additivity test – ensuring that the totals correspond to the sum of their components. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The project called "Integration of SUT/IOT framework into the National Accounts compilation" was concluded in 2011. Current price data for 2008 from production and expenditure sides of GDP were balanced using the SUT framework. The SUT framework brought a new dimension into data processing: products/services. The reconciliation process between the annual totals and sum of the quarterly figures is done and published at the same time, at the end of September and the end of March. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished and non-confidential data are disseminated on request, before the dissemination the confidentiality of data is checked.
