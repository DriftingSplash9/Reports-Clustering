 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The concepts, definitions, and classifications used are not in line with international standards or the UN guidelines. The data are compiled manually. The Central Statistics Office (CSO) does not make estimates for re-exports, smuggling, and undervalued imports/exports. The CSO records only duty free imports at the Kabul Airport. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The external trade statistics follow Afghan classifications. They are not consistent with Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS) and Standard International Trade Classification (SITC). The CSO is not using country codes to UN 3-digit standard codes. The external trade are presently classified as follows: Exports I. Fresh fruits II. Dried fruits III. Medicine IV. Spices V. Seeds VI. Skins VII. Wool VIII. Sausages IX. Carpets X. Others Imports I. Machinery and equipment II. Petroleum, oil, etc. III. Metals IV. Chemical materials V. Construction materials VI. Paper VII. Clothing materials VIII. Food IX. Cigarettes and drinks X. Fabrics and footwear XI. Household needs and medicine XII. Others Scope of the data External trade statistics include only customs records and not all merchandise entering and leaving the territory of Afghanistan. The territorial coverage is the customs points at the boundary. Afghanistan’s external trade linkages with all its neighbors, namely Pakistan, Iran, Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, Tajikistan, and China, the magnitude and composition of the re-export and transit trade with these countries is large, and the CSO does not capture these data in the trade statistics. The donor assistance recorded in 2002-2005 was more than USD10 billions (about two times of the GDP) part of which are imported goods. Only small amounts of these imports of donor duty free goods are recorded in the CSO trade data. On the Afghanistan side of the border, customs check points are usually not at the border but at the first major town inland, across from a range of border crossing in the area. The unofficial trade (excluding contrabands, arms, etc.) is not recorded officially by customs of Afghanistan and neighboring countries. Unofficial trade takes place to avoid customs duty (smugglers). Unofficial trade also takes place with those neighboring countries, whose domestic prices are lower than the international market prices due to subsidies or restrictions on exports (as in the case of wheat and food items in Pakistan). Some unofficial trade takes place across the borders of Afghanistan simply because of convenience of the route taken where a customs house may not exist. In brief, the CSO external trade statistics do not include: Unofficial trade including contrabands, smuggling, and re-exports; Nonmonetary gold; Government trade and duty free imports (only at Kabul Airport); Military goods (such as ammunition, vehicles, and aircraft); Sales and purchases of electricity, gas, and water, and Postal items. 2.4.1 Valuation The official imports are valued at the c.i.f. (cost, insurance, freight) value at the frontier of Afghanistan. Exports officially are valued at the f.o.b. (free on board) transaction value at the frontier of Afghanistan. The transaction is the value in the market prices existing between partner countries and Afghanistan. 2.4.2 Recording basis Data are compiled according to the Afghan s olar year ending March 20, e.g. for the year 1383 the period covered is from 21 March 2004-20 March 2005. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The CSO uses only customs documents, required from importers and exporters when clearing merchandise, as the principal source of external trade statistics for Afghanistan. Presently, the trade data are collected directly from customs documents. The CSO has two staff to record the transactions at each customs post and send to the CSO headquarters for processing and compilation. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data are based on Afghan definitions and concepts which are not consistent with the UN guidelines. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data collection and processing timetables are not adequate to meet timeliness and periodicity for disseminating the external trade statistics. There is about three month lag from the reference month. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The CSO does not make any adjustment to the customs documents. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The CSO trade data are not checked against other independent data sources such as the data sources from the UN, IMF, or partner countries. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request External trade data are made available upon request.
