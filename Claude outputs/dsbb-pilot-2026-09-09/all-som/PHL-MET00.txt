 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The concepts and definitions used for the compilation and generation of IMTS in the Philippines are adopted from the United Nations Statistics Division's IMTS Concepts and Definitions 2010 manual. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization IMTS uses the Philippine Standard Commodity Classification (PSCC) version 2022. The 2022 PSCC is used to classify the commodities at the 11-digit code level for statistical purposes. It is a detailed classification of all commodities on imports and exports being used for statistical purposes. The 2022 PSCC is an integration of the 2022 ASEAN Harmonized Tariff Nomenclature (AHTN) based on the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS) issued by the World Customs Organizations. Scope of the data The Philippines adopts the "General" trade system of recording IMTS. The customs frontier (not the national boundary) is used as the statistical frontier. Under this system, all goods entering any of the seaports or airports of the Philippines, which are properly cleared through customs and are remaining or under customs control, are considered imports, whether the goods are for direct consumption, merchandising, warehousing, or further processing. On the other hand, all goods leaving the country, which are properly cleared through customs, are considered exports. Exceptions to coverage The IMTS relate to the movements of goods between the Philippines and other countries and areas by sea or air, whether the goods are for private or government use or commercial purposes, gifts, or donations. However, the following classes of goods are excluded from the compilation of IMTS: a. Goods simply being transported through a country, includes goods under "in transit" or "in transshipment" customs procedures; b. Goods temporarily admitted or dispatched; c. Monetary gold; d. Issued banknotes and securities and coins in circulation; e. Goods consigned to and from the territorial enclaves; f. Non-financial assets, ownership of which has been transferred from residents to non-residents without crossing borders; g. Goods under merchanting; h. Goods under operating lease; i. Goods lost or destroyed after ownership has been acquired by the importer; j. Satellites (and their launchers as applicable) moved to and launched k. Goods from another country without change of ownership; l. Goods functioning as means of transport; m. Content delivered electronically; n. Goods for repair or maintenance; o. Waste and scrap with no commercial value; p. Goods entering or leaving the economic territory of a country illegally; q. Goods treated as part of the trade in services; o Goods carried by travelers, including non-resident workers, within the duty-free limits set by national law o Newspapers and periodicals sent under direct subscription o Media used for carrying software customized or written for a specific client or originals of any nature, where identified r. Fish and other marine products landed by Philippine vessels direct from the sea; s. Stores and fuels purchased abroad by ships and aircraft of the Philippine registry; t. Goods sent through parcel post; u. Goods with Free on Board (FOB) value less than USD 25; v. Abandoned or unclaimed goods by the consignee; and Goods admitted to the Freeport zones that did not pass through the Customs zones. Unrecorded activity Unrecorded activities related to international trade may exist due to illegal transactions or other activities not captured in customs records. These activities are not included in IMTS due to the absence of reliable data sources. 2.4.1 Valuation The Free On Board (FOB) is the value of the goods carried at the frontier of the exporting country. It includes inland freight, export duty, and other expenses. Ocean freight, insurance, and consular fees, however, are excluded. In addition, valuation of goods in the Philippine foreign trade statistics is FOB. Meanwhile, Cost, Insurance, Freight or CIF valuation could also be determined for import as data on freight and insurance are available. 2.4.2 Recording basis As a general guideline stipulated in the 2010 IMTS Compilers Manual, it is recommended that goods be recorded at the time when they enter or leave the economic territory of a country. The Date of Registry of the exported or imported goods in the BOC is used as the proxy date in recording the time when the goods enter or leave the economic territory of the Philippines. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures As a general guideline stipulated in the 2010 IMTS Compilers Manual, it is recommended that goods be recorded at the time when they enter or leave the economic territory of a country. The Date of Registry of the exported or imported goods in the BOC is used as the proxy date in recording the time when the goods enter or leave the economic territory of the Philippines. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The data used in the compilation and generation of IMTS are taken from the administrative customs data. IMTS is compiled by PSA from the copies of export and import documents submitted to the BOC by exporters and importers or their authorized representatives as required by law. All import documents provided to PSA were cleared or assessed by BOC. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The definitions, scope, classification, valuation, and time of recording applied to source data for IMTS in the Philippines are aligned with the concepts and recommendations of the United Nations Statistics Division's IMTS Concepts and Definitions 2010 manual. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The PSA regularly collects from BOC the following documents which are the sources of trade data: a. Export Declaration (ED) and Single Administrative Documents (SAD) for ED; and b. Informal Import Declaration and Entry. The electronic copies of the SAD-ED and SAD-IEIRD that pass through the Automated Export Documentation System and Electronic to Mobile System are provided by BOC and its authorized Value Added Service Providers, as well as, from Clark Development Corporation to PSA every month through email and via shared Google Drive. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data are routinely assessed for completeness, consistency, and plausibility. The results of these assessments are monitored and used to guide follow-up with source agencies and to improve data compilation procedures. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Electronic export and import declarations received from the BOC and the CDC are subjected to statistical processing procedures, including data editing, completeness and consistency checks, validation against reference files, and aggregation in accordance with IMTS 2010 concepts and definitions. These procedures ensure that administrative customs records are aligned with statistical requirements for the compilation of IMTS. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Statistical procedures include data editing, consistency checks across variables, aggregation of detailed records into statistical tables, and review of unusual movements prior to dissemination. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Validations and assessments are made through the following: (a) division assessment of data and coordination with source agency, (b) completeness and consistency checks, (c) review of consolidated data, and (d) validation of results with other reports and news clippings. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Assessment of intermediate data is conducted through division-level review and coordination with the source agency, application of completeness and internal consistency checks, and examination of consolidated datasets to identify statistical discrepancies prior to final aggregation. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Statistical outputs are reviewed for discrepancies and other potential problems through division-level assessments, higher-level technical discussions and presentations within the service and office, coordination with source agencies, consistency checks, and comparison of final results with related reports, historical trends, and relevant information from official sources and news releases. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions to IMTS are analyzed internally as part of routine compilation and review processes. Information from these analyses is used to improve data collection, processing, and validation practices. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Information on revisions is communicated to users through technical notes and explanatory footnotes accompanying IMTS releases. Analyses of revisions are conducted as part of internal review processes and are used to improve compilation and validation procedures. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Data request on IMTS are available through email at tsd.staff@psa.gov.ph or info@psa.gov.ph . 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The information of contact points are included in the releases and at the PSA website: https://psa.gov.ph/statistics/export-import/monthly 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The IMTS Publication can be accessed at the PSA website (Digital Library).
