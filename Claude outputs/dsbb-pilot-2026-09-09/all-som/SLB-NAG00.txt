 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The concepts and definitions used are broadly consistent with the United Nations, System of National Accounts 1993 . At the same time, Financial Intermediation Services Indirectly Measured (FISIM) is not estimated due to data constraints. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization For the purpose of sectorization, the following sectors are identified: (1) Agriculture and Hunting; (2) Forestry and Logging; (3) Fishing; (4) Mining and Quarrying; (5) Manufacturing; (6) Electricity and Water; (7) Construction; (8) Trade; (9) Hotel and Restaurant; (10) Transport and Storage; (11) Communication; (12) Financial Intermediation; (13) Insurance services; (14) Real Estate and Renting; (15) Owned Occupied Dwellings; (16) Business services; (17) Public Administration and Defense; (18) Education; (19) Health; and (20) Other Services. While ISIC Rev3 is used, the SNA 1993 institutional sectors are applied as closely as possible within the source data limitations. Scope of the data National Accounts cover the whole territory of the Solomon Islands. All resident entities engaged in economic activity or transactions, including subsistence production, are included. Exceptions to coverage Unrecorded activity Illegal activities (especially household production of kwaso, a local liquor) are not explicitly included. 2.4.1 Valuation Market values are used, based on business accounting data where available. Subsistence production is valued at producers’ prices, with adjustments where necessary to survey valuations based on local market prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis Private formal sector data follows standard, accrual- based, accounting practices. Government data are cash based, and consumption of fixed capital is not included. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures 3.1.1 Source data collection programs An annual, full enumeration, business survey is conducted, based on a business register. Updating of the register is based on administrative records but is subject to time lags. Administrative data from tax records supplements the business survey. Adjustments are made for transaction detail where required. Price statistics are limited to the CPI, although some development of import price indexes has begun. Government accounts are comprehensive only for the budget sector with gaps involving extra-budgetary units, local government and the “development” budget (i.e., non-recurrent expenditures funded by development partners). Household Income and Expenditure Surveys (HIESs) are used. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Since the survey data are collected at enterprise level only, an enterprise engaged in the production of multiple activities gets classified by the dominant activity. This, to some extent, limits the quality of industrial analysis, although few enterprises are involved in multiple activities. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness 3.2.1 Source data assessment Business survey data are analyzed for consistency across time. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The business survey uses a ratio adjustment to estimate for non-response. The business survey questionnaire is sufficiently detailed to not require adjustment to national accounts concepts. Valuation of output of owner-occupied dwellings is difficult with a high proportion of dwellings of local construction and a small rental market which is of little relevance to the majority of the households. No adjustment is made for inventory valuation. Book value depreciation is used to estimate consumption of fixed capital. Constant price estimates are base weighted—the present base year being 2004. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses 5.1.5 Dissemination on request
