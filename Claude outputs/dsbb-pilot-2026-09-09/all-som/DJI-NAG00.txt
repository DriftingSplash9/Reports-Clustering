 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The national accounts of Djibouti were compiled in accordance with the general principles of the UN’s SNA68 (NOTE: the compilation of national accounts data has been suspended since 2001). Gross domestic product (GDP) was estimated as the sum of value added of economic activities (production approach) divided into 23 branches. The expenditure and income approaches were not used. Estimates were prepared at current prices and at constant prices for 1986 (the base year) using chained estimates of annual changes. The volume and price indices were calculated according to the Laspeyres method. A double deflation was usually performed on output and intermediate consumption to yield the volumes and the value added deflator. In some cases where production quantities were more substantial, the volume indices were first calculated before extracting the implicit price indices. The data were gross and expressed in millions of Djibouti francs. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification of the national accounts described below refers to known practice until the compilation of the accounts was suspended in 2001 Only production and generation of income accounts were compiled. The industrial classification used was a variant of the International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC rev.2) adapted to the realities of the country. The industrial aggregates were: Agriculture, Livestock, Forestry, Fishing, Extractive Industries, Manufacturing Industries, Energy, Construction and Public Works, Business, Other market services, Government nonmarket services, Private administrations, and Households. More comprehensive accounts were compiled in 1999. The results of the estimates are presented in several tables in the consolidated national accounts, production accounts by branch of activity, sources and uses of GDP at current prices and at constant prices, along with the deflators. Scope of the data The coverage of the national accounts described below refers to known practice until the compilation of the accounts was suspended in 2001 Geographic Coverage: The national accounts were produced for the entire national territory of the Republic of Djibouti, excluding foreign territorial enclaves. The national territory also included the Djibouti’s territorial enclaves abroad. Transaction Coverage: All transactions in goods and services. The transactions of Djibouti’s embassies abroad were included in the government’s production accounts. Unit Coverage: The national accounts covered all the economic units resident in Djibouti, including those that operate in the free zone. Unrecorded activity The coverage of unrecorded activities described below refers to known practice until the compilation of the accounts was suspended in 2001 Unrecorded activities were those of the informal or unstructured sector, smuggling, and prohibited activities such as drug trafficking and prostitution. Nonetheless, a share of informal sector production (commerce) was estimated based on the imports declared by economic operators who do not have business licenses. 2.4.1 Valuation The basis for recoding of the national accounts described below refers to known practice until the compilation of the accounts was suspended in 2001 The valuation methods used producer prices for the production of enterprises with known income generation accounts, and purchase prices for other enterprises and for uses, applying the price index as a deflator. Imports were valued c.i.f. (cost, insurance, freight) and exports were valued f.o.b. (free on board). 2.4.2 Recording basis The basis for recoding of the national accounts described below refers to known practice until the compilation of the accounts was suspended in 2001 The time of recording was the same as for accrual-based accounting. Central government revenue and expenditure, derived from overall Treasury operations, were recorded on a cash basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The source data of the national accounts described below refers to known practice until the compilation of the accounts was suspended in 2001 Production Approach: Agriculture: the data come from the Agriculture Directorate, reports of agricultural and forestry missions, and the DISED statistical yearbook. Livestock: statistics on livestock are compiled from the data supplied by the Livestock Directorate on the number of heads, data on supervised slaughters taken from the DISED’s statistical yearbook, and foreign trade statistics. To estimate the number of heads from one year to the next, a growth rate and an offtake rate are applied to each animal species. Fishing: data are estimated on the basis of statistics in the reports of cooperatives, mission reports from the Ministry of Agriculture and statistical yearbooks. Lately, there are been difficulties with these estimates because the cooperatives are having problems and fishermen tend to sell their catch outside these cooperatives. Energy: data on electricity and water come from the companies in charge of production and distribution of electricity and water, namely Electricité de Djibouti (EED) and the Office National des Eaux de Djibouti (ONED). Oil and gas data are provided by the Société Internationale des Hydrocarbures de Djibouti (SIHD). Secondary sector:the data are estimated based on the structure of production in 1986, the results of the 1997 survey of businesses in the modern sector, and foreign trade statistics. The industrial production indices and market surveys were not undertaken. Construction and public works:construction and public works are valued using different sources depending on the sector (executed capital budget, Treasury special accounts and construction permits from the Housing Directorate). Transport: for road transport, the data are updated as the urban population grows and more passengers arrive to take taxis, based on a study conducted by CERD (formerly ISERST) in 1990, which provides the demand for public transportation by day and by type of vehicle, the average distance traveled, and the fares charged in 1990. For merchandise transport, the number of licensed cargo carriers and a standard production account for 1991 are used to update production by volume of merchandise imports and the price of fuel, without changing the structure. For rail transport, if the income generation accounts of the Société des Chemins de fer are unavailable, the volume of goods transported (DISED statistical yearbooks) and a 1988 production account from DISED are used. For air transport, production is updated on the basis of a 1988 production account by changes in passenger traffic and fuel prices. For marine transport, the 1988 production account is updated with changes in goods traffic and fuel prices. For transport intermediaries, income generation accounts are used along with the directory of enterprises in the sector from the social welfare office (OPS) when information is available. If not, it is assumed that production changes at the same rate as for marine transport. Hotels – bars – restaurants:the income generation accounts of large hotels, which account for almost 60 percent of hotel capacity, are used. Failing that, the production of large hotels is estimated based on the number of nightly stays sold and the average price per night. Otherwise, production is obtained by extrapolating an average small unit income generation account to the numbers obtained. Services to businesses and households:use of income generation accounts and average accounts extracted from records or by mining data from year-end documents from direct tax-paying services. Real estate services:assessment of rents and imputed rents based on the household trash collection tax, which is assessed on the rental value of the housing considered. Nonmarket services:information from overall Treasury operations. Rates and taxes:culled from overall Treasury operations. Expenditure approach: Foreign trade:imports and exports are taken from the foreign trade statistics of the customs departments and DISED. Gross fixed capital formation (GFCF): GFCF comprises the output of construction and public works, part of the output of livestock rearing and imports of capital goods. Changes in stocks are assumed to be zero in households. Final consumption: overall final consumption is obtained as a balance. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The compilation practices of the national accounts described below refers to known practice until the compilation of the accounts was suspended in 2001. To estimate the value added of each industry, it is assumed that the average change in value added is the same as the average change in output. The share of volume and prices of imports and exports is determined directly with the quantities and prices provided by the foreign trade statistics. Private and public investments are deflated by the price indices for capital goods and construction materials. Public consumption is deflated by the indices for the paper and energy industries. Private consumption (at current and constant prices) is obtained as a balance. None
