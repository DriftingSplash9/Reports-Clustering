 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The balance of payments statistics are compiled broadly following the guidelines recommended in the IMF’s Balance of Payments and Investment Position Manual , sixth edition ( BPM6 ). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization For the compilation of balance of payments, the classification system of the BPM6 is followed. The classification of accounts used in the national presentation contains both aggregated and detailed items. 2.2.1 Scope For the compilation of balance of payments, the classification system of the BPM6 is followed. The classification of accounts used in the national presentation contains both aggregated and detailed items. Scope of the data The balance of payments statistics cover, in principle, resident units having transactions with nonresident units The BOP covers in principle all transactions of all resident institutional sectors in Seychelles with nonresidents. However, within the current account, there are difficulties in: (a) covering certain items under goods that are not captured by the Customs, especially informal unrecorded imports. To adjust for (a) an estimate is made. The whole of Seychelles is covered. This includes a reasonable number of offshore companies registered in Seychelles, but for which the centre of economic activity is not in Seychelles. Exceptions to coverage See 2.2.1.1 Unrecorded activity See 2.2.1.1 2.4.1 Valuation Transactions are valued at market prices where they are available. Imports and exports are valued f.o.b. Transactions in currencies other than the US dollar are converted to US dollar using the official exchange rate applicable at the time the transaction is recorded. 2.4.2 Recording basis In principle, data are compiled on an accrual basis. Merchandise trade statistics are recorded when they enter the country. Investment income is recorded on an accrual basis and due for payment basis for interest on government loans. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures To the extent possible, all reporting are on a gross basis 3.1.1 Source data collection programs 1. Current Account Goods Data on merchandise exports and imports are provided by the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) based on information provided by the Customs Department. Monthly data on import and re-export of refined petroleum products are directly provided to CBS by the state-owned company, SEYPEC. Quarterly data on maintenance of foreign aircraft in Seychelles is provided by the Seychelles Civil Aviation Authority (SCAA) and repairs and maintenance of Air Seychelles aircrafts abroad are provided by Air Seychelles. Data on repair of foreign ships in Seychelles are provided by domestic shipping agents. Data on repairs of Seychelles ships abroad are provided by SEYPEC, which manages tankers for oil transportation. Services Transportation: Data on foreign passengers travelling to Seychelles are provided quarterly by Air Seychelles. Data on residents travel are provided by foreign airlines operating in Seychelles. Freight : Data on receipts of freight services rendered to nonresidents is provided by Air Seychelles and by SEYPEC. This information is also provided by the domestic shipping agents. Other transportation : The CBS obtains data on marine and port charges to foreign vessels from shipping agents. Air Seychelles provides data on unloading and handling fees to foreign air companies importing goods in Seychelles through the domestic airport, as well as data on landing fees abroad. Travel: Credit data are provided by commercial banks and exchange bureaus based on the sale of domestic currency against foreign currency to nonresidents. In addition, the NSB conducts a sample survey (of departing passengers) on tourists’ expenditures in the country’s various facilities (hotels, restaurants, other). Debit data are provided by commercial banks, based on sales of foreign currency to residents. MOF provides detailed information on the expenditures of residents undertaking studies or training abroad, which is fully financed by the budget. Other services: This category covers credits and debits relating to communication, insurance, financial, and government services. Data on (re)insurance service payments are provided by the four domestic insurance companies. Where applicable all surveyed companies provide to CBS data on insurance services payments on an annual basis. Data on royalty payments are submitted by the domestic companies in the frame of the quarterly balance of payments survey. Business services debit data are submitted by domestic companies through the quarterly balance of payments survey. For the communication item, credit entries cover international telephone and wireless service charges and other fees related to communication and postal transactions. Data are obtained from the relevant institutions and companies. For financial services, data collected are from financial intermediaries. Government’s construction service debit data are obtained from the MOF. This includes the part relating to payments to foreign companies undertaking government projects, as well as directly from domestic companies. For government services, estimates are based on information collected by the MOF inclusive of foreign diplomatic missions. Other information services account transactions are obtained from the quarterly surveys. Income Compensation of employees: The credit data is provided by the domestic banks, embassies and foreign airlines. The debit data is provided by domestic banks and checked against the quarterly balance of payments return from companies. Direct investment income: Debit data entries cover dividends payable to investors collected from relevant companies. Re-invested earnings are also calculated. Portfolio investment income: Data on external claims in the form of portfolio investment of the government is obtained from MOF. Statistics for the other sectors are obtained from the quarterly surveys and the monthly returns submitted by financial institutions. Other investment income : Credit entries relate to interest on reserve assets, as well as deposits abroad of all companies, which mainly include commercial banks, the domestic telecommunication companies, Air Seychelles, SEYPEC and the hotels. All these entities report this information directly to the CBS. Debit entries include interest due on external debt. These data are obtained from banks’ reports, from the MOF (based on a detailed public sector debt database), and from private companies (based on the quarterly balance of payments survey). Transfers For general government receipts, data on fishing license fees and grants are obtained from MOF. Other overseas donations such as educational grants are submitted by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MFA). Credit and debit entries for workers’ remittances are obtained from banks’ records and surveyed companies. 2. Capital Account Data on capital account transfers in kind from foreign government are provided by the MOF. 3. Financial Account Direct investment : Domestic direct investment abroad is estimated based on information obtained in the media, which is verified with the direct investor company. Foreign direct investment inflow data are collected through media, Seychelles Investment Bureau (SIB), MOF and quarterly surveys. Re-invested earnings are also calculated. Portfolio investment : Data on transaction in foreign securities are collected on a quarterly basis from surveyed companies. Since the fourth quarter of 2006, the statistics also include the Government of Seychelles Bond raised in the international capital market provided by MOF. Other investment : Assets and liabilities data are regularly reported to the CBS by commercial banks for supervisory purposes. The liabilities of the public sector, as well as the liabilities of the parastatal companies under a guarantee from the government, are included in the External Database Management System, which is used by the CBS to compile this item. The nonbank sector provides data in the frame of the balance of payments surveys. The household sector, however, is not included. Reserve assets : Seychelles’ reserve assets consist of SDR holdings, reserve position in the Fund, and foreign exchange assets held by the CBS. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness External Sector data are compiled and disseminated on a quarterly basis consistent with the SDDS requirement 3.2.1 Source data assessment Survey results are assessed on an annual basis with the objective to improve response rate and/or find alternative means of collecting the required statistics when appropriate 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Current Account Goods The CBS introduces adjustments for converting c.i.f. values to f.o.b. values for imports based on a 15 percent factor. Services Transportation: In the event of unavailability, freight debit data is partially estimated based on the c.i.ff.o.b. adjustments on imports, according to a 12.5 percent factor. Travel: The estimation for credit is obtained from tourism related companies and financial institutions. In addition, sample surveys of departing tourists are conducted at the international airport as well as through direct discussion with local tourist facilities. Debit estimates are obtained from survey of financial institutions and estimates of average travel allowance, and statistics on the number of temporary departure of residents. Insurance : Debit data is partially estimated based on the c.i.ff.o.b. adjustments on imports, according to a 2.5 percent factor. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Where applicable, consistency of source data is validated against data from other sources. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Non-confidential unpublished data are made available on request
