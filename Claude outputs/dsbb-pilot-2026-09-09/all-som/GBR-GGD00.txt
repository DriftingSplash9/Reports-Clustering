 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions General government gross debt is compiled based on the definitions and concepts of ESA 2010 which is consistent with the System of National Accounts 2008 (SNA 2008). General government gross debt definition is consistent with the concept of Maastricht debt and is equal to the sum of liabilities at the end of the period in currency and deposits, debt securities and loans; it excludes financial derivatives, shares and other equities, insurance, pension and standardized guarantee schemes, and other accounts payable. According to the definition of Maastricht debt, data is reported at face value and are consolidated between and within subsectors. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The general government sector and its sub-sectors are defined in accordance with the regulations of ESA 2010 and the rules and clarifications provided in the Eurostat’s Manual on Government Deficit and Debt (MGDD), which are also consistent with the 2008 SNA and GFSM 2014. The UK general government sector consists of the central government and local government. The Office for National Statistics compiles and disseminates government finance statistics for each sub-sector as well as for the general government sector. The UK does not have a separate social security fund sector. Extra budgetary funds are not distinguished from budgetary funds. General Government Gross Debt comprises the instruments currency and deposits, debt securities and loans. The national accounts sectoral balance sheet hold data on the financial instruments excluded from general government gross debt. Exceptions to coverage General Government Gross Debt is compiled based on the definitions of ESA 2010 instead of GFSM 2014. It excludes special drawing rights (SDR), insurance, pension and standardized guarantee schemes, and other accounts payable, and so differs from the coverage of General Government Total Gross Debt as defined under the SDDS Plus which consists of all liabilities that are debt instruments. Data on these instruments are provided for informational purposes. Furthermore, general government gross debt is valued at face value and not at nominal value. Pension entitlements under unfunded government defined benefit employer pension schemes are not recorded in the government accounts. Information on residual maturity of currency and deposits liabilities is not available. Unrecorded activity Not applicable. 2.4.1 Valuation In line with the definition of Maastricht debt, data are reported at face value. Foreign currency debt is converted at the exchange rate prevailing at the end of the reference period. Index-linked gilts are adjusted for the index-related capital uplift. 2.4.2 Recording basis Data are recorded in line with ESA 2010, i.e. the principal of accrual recording is applied. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Grossing/netting procedures are applied consistently with ESA2010. General government gross debt is consolidated between and within subsectors. The Public Sector Finances bulletin publishes a separate measure of public sector net debt. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources: Gilts data are provided by HM Treasury and the Debt Management Office. National Savings data are provided by National Savings and Investments. Data on sterling Treasury bills, certificates of tax deposits, court funds and other sterling debt (except for NI government debt, which is provided by the Northern Ireland Department of Finance and Personnel) are also provided by HM Treasury. Coin in circulation data are provided by the Royal Mint. Local Government loans data are provided by the Department for Levelling Up, Housing, and Communities. Monthly/quarterly accounting data, administration records and surveys. The data for central government are based on actual accounting records. The data for local governments are based on the administrative and accounting returns from regional government departments. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Definitions In many cases, the definitions used in the source data are already consistent with statistical definitions. If necessary, a mapping is applied on receipt by staff at the Office for National Statistics. Classifications The classification of units to institutional sectors is the responsibility of the Office for National Statistics. The administrative system for central government uses the classification of units as prescribed by the ONS. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Timeliness meets the standard required. Estimates may be used for some components for the most recent period. These estimates are replaced with actual reported data when available. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The Office for National Statistics has a best practice process for the Quality Assurance of Administrative Data (QAAD). The principles are applied in the case of government data. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Statistical techniques are applied when necessary. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Data validation takes place at different stages within the statistical production process, including: Unit level data entry validation checks within the central government administrative reporting systems and within the data collections from local authorities. Plausibility checks of aggregate data upon receipt Plausibility checks of transaction totals by comparison with previously reported data or correlated variables During the preparation phase, unusual data are questioned with suppliers, errors are rectified, and data are confirmed. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Intermediate data are assessed in consultation with data providers. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Discrepancies are investigated and resolved where possible. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions analysis is published on the ONS website alongside the Public Sector Finances publication. This shows the revisions 12 months after the end of the reference period, and provides comparisons for aggregate data. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Information about the effect of classification and methodological decisions on the fiscal metrics is provided within the Public Sector Finances bulletin when the changes are implemented. Where a classification or methodological change significantly impacts the key fiscal metrics such as public sector net borrowing and debt, the ONS publishes information post-implementation on the reconciliation between the metrics on an old and new basis. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request ONS does not usually provide additional data on request, and instead seeks to ensure that all relevant data are included within the publication, thereby ensuring access for all. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Contact names (for ONS statisticians) are included for each publication, alongside a contact email address. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The Release Calendar on the ONS website shows the schedule of all publications.
