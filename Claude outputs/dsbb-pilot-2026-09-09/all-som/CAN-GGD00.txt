 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The overall structure of the quarterly general government gross debt is generally in accordance with that described in the Government Finance Statistics Manual 2014 and the 2008 System of National Accounts. Occasional departures from this standard are primarily prompted by pragmatic considerations. Information on the concepts, structure, definitions can be found in "Methodological Guide: Canadian System of Macroeconomic Accounts " (Catalogue 13-607-X). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Institutional sectoring follows GFSM 2014 with a few differences. The general government sector is broken down into the following sub-sectors: federal general government, provincial general government, local general government and Indigenous general governments. In addition a number of sub-sectors have been created in the area of health, education, school boards, and pension funds. 2.2.1 Scope Institutional sectoring follows GFSM 2014 with a few differences. Scope of the data The general government gross debt are compiled according to the production and asset boundary defined by the GFSM 2014. This includes the economic activities of all levels of government and government business enterprises – together which make up the Canadian public sector universe. Transactions coverage: Includes all activities within the GFSM 2014 boundary for public sector revenues, expenses, financial transactions and stocks. Non-financial assets are presented on a net basis (i.e. net of the consumption of fixed capital). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data are extracted from administrative files and derived from other Statistics Canada surveys and/or macroeconomic accounts. Data for the federal general government sector are taken from the federal government's general ledger system. Data for the provincial and territorial general government sector are taken from the provincial and general government general ledger systems. Data for local governments are taken from aggregate local government reports compiled by provincial and territorial governments for local governments within their jurisdiction. Data forIndigenousgeneral governments are taken from federal general government reports on the activities of aboriginal general governments along with publicly available financial statements. Data for government business enterprises are taken from publicly available financial statements. Public Account information and budgetary information are also used as an additional data source and for confrontation purposes. Statistics Canada also has access to a number of commercial databases which are used to establish stocks of instruments such as mutual funds or establish the market value of equity. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The general government gross debt program meets regularly with data providers and are able to influence the receipt of information in terms of format (paper vs electronic) content and timeliness. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The majority of the source data used to compile the general government gross debt are available 45 days after the reference period. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data are assessed on a continuous basis. Issues with source data are communicated with the data providers, both internal and external, for follow-up. All source data programs are subject to the Statistics Canada quality guidelines and as such, response rates, coverage rates, sampling errors, non-sampling errors are reviewed on a continuous basis and appropriate action is taken when required. In cases where the data provider are external to Statistics Canada – agreements are put in place to ensure the agency can influence the quality and receipt of the information. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques A number of statistical techniques are used in the data compilation processing including: Outlier Detection Seasonal Adjustment Benchmarking Derivation of Market Valuation Linking Conversion ratios Deflation Perpetual Inventory Methods 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures See 3.3.1 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Reports are generated multiple times during a production cycle to validate results, ensure accounting identities are adhered to, revisions are examined, and outliers, discrepancies and trends are analyzed. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Data are analyzed in the context of domestic economic trends and events, stock market indices and exchange rates movements, price movements, changes in savings, financial flows, revaluations and international economic trends and events 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Statistical discrepancies, large movements, changes in positions relative to transactions and revaluation factors are monitored throughout the production cycle. Unexplained changes serve as an indicator of the quality of revenue, expenditures, positions and flow data sources. Further investigations are performed by staff to understand the nature of these changes and make recommendations to improve quality. Data are reconciled with public account information. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revision analyses are performed on a regular basis, mainly at the time the annual benchmark is introduced. The direction and the magnitude of the revisions are investigated by type of instrument, revenue or expense. Sources of revisions are identified and, if possible, actions are taken to minimize revisions in subsequent periods. Mechanisms are in place to integrate findings of revision studies to improve estimates. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Major changes to the estimates resulting from the implementation of new concepts and methodologies are explained to users through special studies. Users are informed of reference periods that have been revised at the time the data are disseminated. Information on the overall revision policy in place in the macroeconomic accounts is available on the Statistics Canada website , including the possible nature of these revisions. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Special tabulations can be produced upon requests and are subject to the same confidentiality provisions as standard tabulations. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points At the time the data are disseminated, information on contact points (general enquiry line or contact a subject-matter expert) are made available to users. Enquiries can be sent by mail or through phone calls. Subject-matter experts listed as contact points need to have completed training to deal with media enquiries. Client enquiries are monitored and subject to corporate services standards. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs All information (analytical write-up, charts, data tables, metadata, research paper and revision studies, users' guide and methodological guide, etc.) related to the statistical program are available on the Statistics Canada website free of charge.
