 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The Mauritanian balance of payments is a statistical statement prepared by the BCM, in which transactions between residents and nonresidents over a given period are summarized. It includes the goods and services account, the primary income account, the secondary income account, the capital account and the financial account. In the international accounts, the capital account presents: a) capital transfers (receivable and payable) between residents and nonresidents; and b) the acquisition and disposal of nonfinancial assets not produced between residents and nonresidents. Capital transfers are transfers for which the ownership of an asset (other than cash or inventory) is transferred from one party to another; or that require one or both parties to acquire or dispose of an asset (other than cash or inventory); or for which the creditor cancels the liability. The sum of the balances of the current and capital accounts represents the capacity (surplus) or the need for financing (deficit) for the economy as regards the rest of the world. It is presented as recommended in the sixth edition of the Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6). The data are published in millions of ouguiyas. Therefore, they are prepared for the International Monetary Fund in millions of US dollars and in special drawing rights. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification systems used are those recommended in the IMF sixth edition of the Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) . Scope of the data Transaction Coverage: All transactions are covered. Geographic Coverage: The balance of payments covers the entire national territory and Mauritanian diplomatic missions accredited abroad. Unit Coverage: The balance of payments records all transactions between residents and nonresidents. The data are published under the following headings: Balance of Current accounts, Capital account; Financing. Trade statistics, compiled from Customs data, are completed and adjusted based on surveys of enterprises, exporters, and other public institutions. Unrecorded activity Informal sector activities are not estimated, except for exports of cattle in the countries of the region. 2.4.2 Recording basis The time of recording of accounting entries is determined on the basis of the transfer of ownership, from the time when the service is rendered, from the time of the creation or extinction of the assets and liabilities in question. Imports/exports of goods for which data are obtained from Customs are recorded on an accrual basis. Transactions recorded on the basis of payment statements completed by the commercial banks are posted on a cash basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Balance of payments statistics are compiled on the basis of a mixed system consisting of surveys of bank settlements with the rest of the world, collected by the BCM Directorate of Studies, and surveys of enterprises and government departments. These sources are complemented by customs data on merchandise trade. Settlements statement : This document (monthly and annual) records all transactions settled by the BCM and/or by licensed intermediaries during the reference period. This document contains all the balance of payments headings as enumerated under “Analytical Framework, Concepts, and Definitions.” It will be adopted for services with settlements. Services consumed by the SNIM are shown in SNIM Table 43 (a document summarizing all SNIM transactions with the rest of the world). Monthly government data: Customs data: DGD import data are used instead of the settlements statement. Each month, the DGD forwards to the BCM a statement summarizing all shipments of merchandise for the reference month; A report to the Ministry of Economic and Development Affairs covers implementation of the external portion of the PIP. This document makes a distinction between loan and grant financing, broken down by sector. Monthly data from economic agents: Data from the export companies SMCP and SNIM on the situation of their exports (fish and iron); Data from the Regional Department of the Central Bank in Nouadhibou on other fishing exports (pelagic and artisanal). Annual questionnaires: Annual surveys are carried out at embassies accredited in Mauritania, ministerial departments, transportation companies, banks, hotels and inns, international agencies, and international NGOs operating in Mauritania. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques For the recording of imports at their f.o.b. value, the freight and insurance portion is estimated at 8 percent (7.5 percent for freight and 0.5 percent for insurance). For a revaluation of the data in ouguiyas, the following exchange rates are used: For settlement statements, the official rate prevailing at the time of the transaction; For the data forwarded monthly by government departments, companies, and other bodies, the average monthly exchange rate; For annual surveys, the average annual rate.
