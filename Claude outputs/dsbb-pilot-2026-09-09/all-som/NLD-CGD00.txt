 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions There are two different frameworks in which data on central government debt is published. Firstly, the methodology used for the compilation of non-financial and financial data of the sector central government is based on ESA2010 (EU Council Regulation 549/2013) and the Manual on Government Deficit and Debt. The ESA2010 framework is based on market values. Secondly, the data on deficit and debt are produced according to the Protocol on the Excessive Debt Procedure of the Maastricht Treaty (Council Regulation 479/2009). This framework is based on face values. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Central government is defined in the ESA2010 and the Manual on Government Deficit and Debt, which are also consistent with the 2008 SNA and GFSM 2014. Scope of the data The central government subsectors are defined in the ESA2010. Institutional coverage: Central government data covers: the "State", i.e. ministries, including some budget funds (e.g. the Municipal Budget Fund and the Infrastructure Fund) and state agencies (e.g. the prison system, state building system) Public corporate organisations Universities Non-profit institutions (NPI’s), which are controlled and financed by (other) government units and have a national function Instrument coverage: the data include currency and deposits, securities other than shares (mainly Dutch State loans), loans and other accounts payable. Exceptions to coverage The data coverage and definitions for general government debt for the Netherlands sometimes deviates from the IMF SDDS+ guidelines. In particular: The debt is valued at face value instead of nominal value. The residence of the creditor is not published for all debt instruments. Information on other accounts payable is not split between domestic and external creditors for data quality reasons. Therefore the total debt is not split between domestic and external creditor. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation practices: Government debt and its underlying instruments are available at face value in correspondence with the Excessive Debt Procedure of the Maastricht Treaty. The central government issues foreign currency-denominated debt for which the exchange rate is hedged. In correspondence with the Excessive Debt Procedure of the Maastricht Treaty the foreign currency-denominated debt is valued using the hedged exchange rate agreed in the contractual agreements. Data are presented in millions of Euros. 2.4.2 Recording basis Stocks in the central government account are recorded on accrual basis in line with ESA2010 (paragraph 1.101) and the Manual on Government Deficit and Debt . 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Statistics Netherlands applies grossing/netting procedures consistently with ESA2010 guidelines. No financial assets are offset against liabilities, with the sole exception of financial derivatives, which are always recorded on the asset side of the balance. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources: data on the State are derived from accounting records from the ministry of Finance. For universities and other non-profit institutions annual reports are used. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The data are based on official records of actual stocks. For the State, the data follow from an administration that is cash-based. However, in order to ensure that the recording and presentation of the data are in line with ESA2010, corrections are made for accrual-cash differences (for e.g. tax receipts and interest). All other sources are accrual-based. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The source data are supplied on a quarterly and annual basis and are in accordance with obligatory publication dates for Eurostat and publication dates of Statistics Netherlands. Not all entities of Central Government have source data on financial flows and balance sheets. For those sectors, imputations are made using the non-financial source data and counterpart information. Most notably quarterly data on financial flows and balance sheets are missing for universities and non-profit institutions. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The administrative records from the source data are continually assessed by Statistics Netherlands. Most notably the records from the different ministries are closely monitored and if necessary inquiries are done. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Statistics Netherlands employs sound statistical techniques in dealing with the data sources. For example: Estimates of missing data are based on annual historical data or budget reports of the sources. All data are corrected when more accurate data become available, if there are material differences. Any discrepancies between the sum of the quarterly data and annual data are removed by using proportional smoothing. No discrepancies can exist between stocks and their corresponding flows. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Intermediate data are assessed during the production process at several moments. First the source data is compared with counterpart information. Then the transaction-identity per transaction is assured for the whole economy, including the Central Government transactions. Finally the outcome is assessed in a check-counter-check evaluation for general plausibility, before being published. For each assessment a report is made but not published. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Statistics Netherlands investigates the changes in Central Government Debt statistics. Differences can occur for several reasons. Most common are differences due to new source data, changes in counterpart information or changes in methodology used. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Data are disseminated on request. The public can ask for data by telephone or e-mail at a special desk. In general, only data that have been published are delivered. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The public can ask for data by telephone or e-mail at a special desk. In general, only data that have been published are delivered.
