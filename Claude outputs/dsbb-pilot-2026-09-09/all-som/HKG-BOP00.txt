 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: In Hong Kong, the compilation of balance of payments (BoP) statistics is largely in conformity with the guidelines set out in the Sixth Edition of the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6) released by the International Monetary Fund (IMF) in 2009. The broad categories of data are presented under three main headings, namely, (a) the current account: goods, services, primary income and secondary income; (b) the capital account; and (c) the financial account: direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives, other investment and reserve assets. Definition: The concepts and definitions adopted in the Hong Kong's BoP account are largely in conformity with the guidelines set out in the BPM6 . BoP is a statistical statement that systematically summarises, for a specific time period (typically a year or a quarter), the economic transactions of an economy with the rest of the world (i.e. between residents and non-residents). Transactions between Hong Kong residents and non-residents consist of those involving goods, services, and primary income; those involving financial claims on, and liabilities to, the rest of the world; and those classified as transfers (such as gifts), which involve offsetting entries for one-sided transactions. Sign convention: The BPM6 sign convention has been adopted for BoP statistics since June 2023. From then on: (i) debit entries in the current account and the capital account are switched from negative to positive figures; (ii) increases in assets and liabilities in the financial account are both indicated by positive figures; and (iii) the capital account and the financial account are no longer presented as a combined account. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification system: The classification/sectorization systems of the BPM6 are largely adopted. For institutional sector, only the breakdowns for “banks” and “other sectors” are published. Data in regard to the central bank and general government are subsumed in “other sectors”. For classification by type of financial instrument, the BPM6 ’s recommended convention of classifying all interbank positions other than securities and accounts receivable/payable under deposits is adopted. 2.2.1 Scope Classification system: The classification/sectorization systems of the BPM6 are largely adopted. For institutional sector, only the breakdowns for “banks” and “other sectors” are published. Data in regard to the central bank and general government are subsumed in “other sectors”. For classification by type of financial instrument, the BPM6 ’s recommended convention of classifying all interbank positions other than securities and accounts receivable/payable under deposits is adopted. Scope of the data Transactions coverage: Standards and guidelines recommended in the BPM6 on residence are closely followed and all external transactions of Hong Kong are broadly covered. Geographic coverage: All territories under the administration of the Government of the HKSAR are covered. Transactions vis-à-vis Chinese Mainland are treated as external transactions and are included in the BoP statistics of Hong Kong. Exceptions to coverage Primary income: Two components, “rent” and “taxes and subsidies on products and production”, are not measured due to the lack of information, but are expected to be insignificant. Capital account: No separate estimate is made for external transactions in non-produced and non-financial assets due to the lack of information and the relative insignificance of such transactions in Hong Kong. Financial derivatives: No separate estimate is made for employee stock options (ESOs) due to the lack of information and the relative insignificance of such transactions in Hong Kong, as signified by the relatively small share of compensation of employees in Hong Kong’s BoP over the years. Other investment: No estimate is made for pension entitlements due to the lack of information and the relative insignificance of such positions in Hong Kong. Unrecorded activity External investment flows in relation to local enterprise groups, as well as those invested through local intermediaries and agents, are comprehensively gauged in the "Survey of External Claims, Liabilities, and Income (SECLI)". Income and investment not going through the formal sector, if any, is not captured. Unrecorded transactions, if any, are estimated whenever applicable (e.g. individuals' direct investment in Chinese Mainland without setting up companies in Hong Kong, individuals' real estate investment in Hong Kong and overseas countries.) 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: In principle, data are valued at market prices. Unlisted equity in direct investment is valuated by net asset value. For nonnegotiable instruments such as loans, deposits, and other accounts receivable / payable, the valuation is based on nominal value. Conversion to unit of account: Source data reported in foreign currencies are converted to local currency using average market exchange rates for the reporting period. 2.4.2 Recording basis Accrual basis is adopted in recording all transactions in the BoP. If accrual accounting is not in place, approximates are derived by using accounting and statistical models. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Current and capital account transactions are recorded on a gross basis while financial account transactions are recorded on a net basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The data sources for compiling the goods, services, secondary income and capital accounts of BoP include merchandise trade statistics based on trade declarations submitted by importers and exporters, various statistical surveys on business enterprises / establishments and households, administrative data from government departments and other relevant organisations, supplemented with some data models and media reports as and when appropriate. Data required for compiling the financial account and the primary income account are mainly collected from the Survey of External Claims, Liabilities and Income (SECLI), a mandatory quarterly survey conducted by the C&SD. Goods: Goods mainly cover general merchandise, net exports of goods under merchanting and non-monetary gold. Data on general merchandise and non-monetary gold are obtained from merchandise trade statistics, which are compiled on the basis of information contained in trade declarations submitted by importers and exporters, and supplemented with administrative records. Exports and imports of goods in the context of BoP are compiled based on the change of ownership principle in recording goods sent abroad for processing and merchanting. The value of imports on c.i.f. basis is converted to f.o.b. basis by using estimates on insurance and freight costs, which are based on data from establishment surveys, administrative records and relevant market information. Statistics on net exports of goods under merchanting are obtained from the Quarterly Survey of Merchanting and Other Trading Activities and Annual Survey of Imports and Exports of Services. As inward processing trade is relatively insignificant for Hong Kong, statistics on such processing trade are not estimated but included under general merchandise. Services: Statistics on trade in services are mainly obtained from the Annual Survey of Imports and Exports of Services, supplemented with data obtained from other annual / quarterly surveys on economic activities, General Household Survey and relevant administrative records. Statistics on manufacturing services on physical inputs owned by others are obtained from the Quarterly Survey of Merchanting and Other Trading Activities, Annual Survey of Imports and Exports of Services, and Annual Survey of Economic Activities. There are frame-updating operations to ensure that the coverage is comprehensive over time. Data from administrative records include those from the Hong Kong Tourism Board, the Immigration Department, the Treasury, and the Customs and Excise Department. Primary income: Statistics on primary income flows (including direct investment income, portfolio investment income, other investment income, and income on reserve assets) are mainly obtained from SECLI, supplemented with data from other sources. For direct investment income, enterprise groups are used as units for data reporting. Primary income data are collected with taxes being deducted at source. Secondary income: Administrative records, data models, and media reports are used for the compilation of statistics on secondary income. The input parameters for the data models are mainly derived from information collected from some prominent organisations and administrative records from the Government. Assumptions are made in formulating some of the input parameters to be used in the models. These assumptions include, for example, the number of working emigrants and percentage of remittances out of salaries of working emigrants. Capital account: Information collected from some prominent organisations, administrative records from the Government, and media reports are used for the compilation of capital transfers. Direct investment: A Hong Kong enterprise group (HKEG) is taken as the basic unit for data reporting on direct investment in SECLI. The Hong Kong parent company of an HKEG is designated as the target company for reporting data for the enterprise group as a whole since relevant data can normally be found in the consolidated accounts of the enterprise group. Direct investment outside Hong Kong made by individuals, but not through a registered company in Hong Kong, are not captured by SECLI. Given the close economic links between Hong Kong and Chinese Mainland, it is common that some residents have made direct investment in Chinese Mainland without setting up companies in Hong Kong. To account for such external transactions not covered by SECLI, other data sources are used to derive estimates of their position, flow and related income. Resident individuals' real estate investment in popular overseas countries and non-resident individuals' real estate investment in Hong Kong are estimated based on administrative records and data models. The input parameters for the data models are mainly derived from media reports and statistics released by overseas governments. Portfolio investment and financial derivatives: The major data suppliers for statistics on portfolio investment and financial derivatives are local financial intermediaries or agents such as banks, securities houses, share registrars, nominees, custodians, trusts and fund managers that are often engaged in external financial investment activities. They are required to report external claims and liabilities under their own account and those on behalf of their clients. For portfolio investment, data are collected on a security-by-security basis for shares listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange and on an aggregated basis for other securities. Other investment: The main data suppliers for statistics on other investment are banks. They are required to report external claims and liabilities under their own account and those on behalf of their clients. To make up for any omission of data on offshore deposits placed by and offshore loans owed by Hong Kong's nonbank residents (i.e. other sectors: other investment, assets, deposits; and other sectors: other investment, liabilities, loans), reconciliation with relevant statistics published by the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) is conducted regularly. Reserve assets: Changes in and the positions of reserve assets are compiled from data on foreign currency reserve assets of the Exchange Fund managed by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA). 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording A complete set of well-structured SECLI questionnaire, including a checklist, data reporting instructions and a separate reference guide, is used to collect the required data from the respondents for the primary income and financial account. The use of the checklist is to enable easy identification of specific reporting sections in the questionnaire that need to be completed by a respondent. Detailed reporting requirements are given in the data reporting instructions, while relevant explanatory notes as well as other useful information for assisting respondents to report relevant data are provided in the reference guide. Specially designed data entry forms and supplementary information sheets are provided for respondents to fill in the required data. Separate reporting instructions are designed for different economic sectors to facilitate survey respondents in easily focusing on the reporting requirements that are relevant to them. Through the carefully designed questions, respondents can be appropriately classified and they can provide data in accordance with the concepts and definitions adopted in the survey, which follow closely the guidelines set out in the BPM 6 . For those required data being collected through other existing statistical systems of the C&SD, such as statistics on trade in goods and services, appropriate adjustments are applied on the existing statistics to ensure compliance with the BPM 6 . In addition, new developments in the external environment are regularly monitored to ensure that the existing data collection programme remains as comprehensive as practicable. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The SECLI adopts an integrated survey approach with three reporting frequencies. Under this approach, all prominent data suppliers selected in the sample are required to report data on direct investment, portfolio investment and other investment on a quarterly basis, of which some are required to report portfolio investment data on a monthly basis. The less prominent data suppliers are required to fill in survey returns on either quarterly or annual basis. This concept of "One Survey, Three Frequencies" helps to minimise the reporting burden to some extent. A work schedule for the compilation and dissemination of BoP statistics is drawn up and each involved party is well informed of the schedule beforehand. Enumerators are required to complete data collection for a survey period before the end of that survey cycle. Deadline is stated clearly in the survey questionnaires of SECLI. 3.2.1 Source data assessment For compiling the BoP statistics of Hong Kong, comprehensive consistency checking and revision study are performed before the statistics are published. All survey data are carefully edited, and subject to a comprehensive checking and validation mechanism. Any dubious reported data are carefully verified with respondents. Validity checks are computerised to minimise human errors. Imputation techniques for missing data are in place under the compilation system. There are compilation manuals that document the detailed compilation procedures for each of the published components of the BoP account. The questionnaires are designed in a way that makes them easy to complete and appropriate for computer processing and the survey samples are selected scientifically using stratified rotational replicated sampling techniques. As a significant portion of survey data are collected through fieldwork, a designated team was set up in 2002 within the C&SD to perform an internal audit exercise on fieldwork control procedures. The focus of the audit is to ascertain whether reported control procedures have been effectively implemented in the survey systems. Non-sampling errors are carefully controlled with the strength of the C&SD field teams. The field teams would arrange face-to-face interviews with the data suppliers to avoid misclassification and measurement errors by giving clear explanation and assisting them in completing the returns, and non-response rate is significantly reduced by continuous persuasion. In addition, there are in-house operations and checking mechanisms to reduce non-sampling errors, such as frequent updating of the survey frame; checking for validity and high-valued/unusual transactions; and conducting research work on special topics on the BoP statistics to ensure conceptual consistency and comprehensiveness of the system. Another important step of consistency checking is to cross-check the BoP statistics with press reports and other information available in the public domain. For example, international banking statistics released by the BIS, and local banking statistics published by the HKMA are regularly used for validation of the BoP statistics at aggregate level. The underlying reasons accounting for any major changes in levels and trends will be analysed and documented. Revisions to the BoP statistics are regularly assessed and the magnitudes of such revisions for individual BoP components are closely monitored. The additional information leading to each revision will be examined and documented as source of reference. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Census enumeration is conducted on prominent data suppliers while for the less prominent data suppliers, data are enumerated on a sample basis. Sample is selected using stratified rotational replicated sampling method based on meaningful stratifying variables. The statistical technique of grossing up is applied on the sampled cases to obtain the population estimate. For the non-responding cases, imputation is applied with reference to (i) publicly available information as far as possible and (ii) stratum average based on reported cases by broad business group and investment scale. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Seasonal adjustment: At present, seasonally adjusted data series showing the quarter-to-quarter percentage changes in the credit and debit items of current account and its major components is compiled and published. Seasonal adjustment of current account and its major components is performed using the X12-ARIMA method. Geographic detail: Geographic breakdowns for direct investment are available. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Statistics and reports published by international and local organisations, such as banking statistics published by the HKMA and statistics published by the BIS, are used to check the validity of the BoP statistics. Annual financial reports and news on prominent external transactions / positions, such as mergers and acquisitions, are used to verify data suppliers' returns. Also, any unusual movements of the BoP components will be examined and cross-checked with press reports and other available relevant information. Extreme-value transactions / positions are confirmed with respondents wherever possible. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Statistics from external sources, such as banking statistics published by the HKMA and statistics published by the BIS, are used for consistency checking on banks' data collected under SECLI. Position data on foreign currency reserve assets published by the HKMA are used for checking transaction flows and positions data on reserve assets. IIP statistics are used for assessing investment income payments and receipts. Consistencies on flow-stock relationship and investment yield are ensured at company level with reference to the data on stocks, flows, incomes and valuation changes which are being collected under SECLI. Consistency checking is a general practice in the compilation of trade statistics and is implemented smoothly. In compiling trade in services statistics attributable to travel-related activities, a data model is used. The resulting statistics depend on the number of visitors, average length of stay and average amount of money spent. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Net errors and omissions are monitored and bilateral comparisons / reconciliations are conducted with data of other countries and international organisations. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions to the BoP statistics are periodically assessed and the percentage changes resulting from each revision are closely monitored. Trends in the revision pattern are periodically assessed to identify the existence of systematic bias if any. Results of the analysis of various key quality indicators in the revision studies were published in the first quarter 2013 and the first quarter 2025 issues of Balance of Payments, International Investment Position and External Debt Statistics of Hong Kong ( https://www.censtatd.gov.hk/en/EIndexbySubject.html?pcode=B1040001&scode=260 ). 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses The preliminary estimates are subsequently revised when more complete data become available. Revisions to the BoP statistics are regularly assessed and the magnitudes of such revisions for individual BoP components are closely monitored. The additional information leading to each revision will be examined and documented as source of reference. Results of the analysis of various key quality indicators in the revision studies were published in the first quarter 2013 and the first quarter 2025 issues of Balance of Payments, International Investment Position and External Debt Statistics of Hong Kong ( https://www.censtatd.gov.hk/en/EIndexbySubject.html?pcode=B1040001&scode=260 ). 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Requests for additional statistics which are not available from the existing tabulations will be considered on a case-by-case basis having regard to such factors as the nature of the request, data relevance and precision, and data confidentiality. Where applicable and in consultation with users, customised tabulations will be provided at a charge under the cost recovery principle. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Details of contact for enquiries, including phone number, fax number, email address and office address, are clearly given in the quarterly report Balance of Payments , International Investment Position and External Debt Statistics of Hong Kong, the Performance Pledge of the C&SD and on the C&SD website ( https://www.censtatd.gov.hk/en/page_105.html ). The information is readily available to the public. Prompt and knowledgeable support is provided in accordance with the performance pledge of the C&SD. The timeliness of response to enquiries from the public is regularly monitored. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The availability of the report Balance of Payments , International Investment Position and External Debt Statistics of Hong Kong is publicised in the quarterly press releases for disseminating the updated BoP statistics. Such information is also available on the C&SD website ( https://www.censtatd.gov.hk/en/ ). Lists of publications, feature articles and other services available are also obtainable from the C&SD website ( https://www.censtatd.gov.hk/en/page_1273.html ).
