 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The methodological basis for the statistics follows internationally accepted standards and good practices and broadly conforms to the guidelines outlined in the Monetary and Financial Statistics Manual. 1. Analytical framework: The conceptual framework for the analytical accounts of the banking sector is the monetary survey. 2. Definitions: South Africa's current definition of money supply includes negotiable certificates of deposits (NCDs) as well as negotiable promissory notes (NPNs) and similar instruments, issued by banks and held by the non-bank domestic private sector. 3. Monetary aggregates comprise: notes and coins in circulation outside the monetary sector, M1A, M1, M2, M3 and major classes of deposit liabilities defined as follows: (i) Notes and coin in circulation outside the monetary sector, i.e., in the hands of the public; (ii) M1A= notes and coin in circulation outside the monetary sector + current and transmission deposits of the domestic private sector with monetary institutions; (iii) M1= M1A + other demand deposits of the domestic private sector with monetary institutions; (iv) M2= M1 + short-term deposits (other than demand deposits) and medium-term deposits (including savings deposits) of the domestic private sector with monetary institutions, including savings deposits with and national savings certificates issued by the Postbank; (short-term: up to 31 days; medium-term: 32 days up to 6 months); and (v) M3= M2 plus long-term deposits of the domestic private sector with monetary institutions, including savings deposits and national savings certificates issued by the Postbank (long-term: 6 months and greater). 4. Domestic credit extended by monetary institutions is disaggregated into: (i) Credit extended to the domestic private sector which is further subdivided into investments, bills discounted, instalment sale credit, leasing finance, mortgage advances and other loans and advances; and (ii) Credit extended to the general government sector, with the following detailed breakdown: gross claims; government deposits; and net credit (the data covers the central government, provincial governments and social security funds). 5. Net foreign assets (foreign assets minus foreign liabilities) are shown as a counterpart item to the change in M3. (Deposits of non-residents are not included in the broad monetary aggregate.) 6. Net other assets: Represent the residual to the counterparts of a change in M3. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: The monetary survey presents as stock data the following: (i) Monetary aggregates; (ii) Domestic credit extended by monetary institutions; (iii) Net foreign assets; and (iv) Net other assets. Domestic and external accounts are distinguished on the basis of residency in accordance with the definition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual. Scope of the data The monetary survey is a consolidation of the accounts of the South African Reserve Bank, the Corporation for Public Deposits, the Land and Agricultural Development Bank of South Africa, the Postbank, registered banks and mutual banks. These institutions form the monetary sector of South Africa. The banks include all domestic private banks and local branches of foreign banks who are registered in terms of the Banks Act (Act No. 24 of 1990) with the Registrar of Banks to conduct banking business in the Republic of South Africa. The monetary team constantly monitors the landscape for developments related to new deposit taking institutions or changes to existing ones. Deposit taking institutions that are placed under curatorship/liquidation are treated as constant by keeping the return of the last submission periopd constant for computing purposes and calculation of monetary aggregates and credit extended by the monetary sector until the institution is officially deregistered upon which time their data is removed. Exceptions to coverage Financial companies such as the Industrial Development Corporation (IDC), the Development Bank of Southern Africa (DBSA), money-market unit trusts, as well as rural credit co-operatives fall outside the monetary sector. Unrecorded activity N/A 2.4.1 Valuation Accounting practices: The consolidated financial statements of monetary institutions covered by the monetary survey are prepared in accordance with International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) and so as to reflect rand amounts. The equity method of accounting is applied in the presentation of the consolidated financial statements. Maturity of an asset or liability relates to the remaining period to maturity and not to the date on which the liability was incurred or the asset acquired. Valuation methods: All foreign currency denominated positions are valued at end of period exchange rates. Local currency denominated securities are generally valued at market value. Banks were requested to report deposits and loans at nominal value as prescribed in the Monetary and Financial Statistics Manual and Compilation Guide as from 1 January 2016. 2.4.2 Recording basis Accounting practices: All liabilities and assets include accrued interest up to the end of the period to which the statement relates. If this is not possible, accrued interest is included under "other assets and liabilities". 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Balances are generally reported on a gross basis. Credit impairments are deducted on the asset side from the loans and advances and investments categories. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data included in the monetary survey are based on (i) the balance sheet of the South African Reserve Bank (SARB) and Corporation for Public Deposits (CPD) and (ii) data from other institutions compiled from analytical returns designed by the Economic Statistics Department of the central bank. The Prudential Authority of the central bank, in turn, co-ordinates the completion of the banking returns in accordance with the Banks Act. Banks (or in the case of a group of banks, the controlling company in that group) are required to submit consolidated returns to represent the state of affairs and the results of operations in respect of banking business and all other business activities conducted by the reporting bank and its subsidiaries or the controlling company and its subsidiaries. The returns submitted by monetary institutions and used in the SARB's monetary analysis, with their respective due dates, are as follows: BA310: Minimum reserve balance and liquid assets, monthly, within 15 business days after the end of the reference month. BA900: Institutional and maturity breakdown of assets and liabilities, monthly, within 15 business days after the end of the reference month. This return is a detailed balance sheet and the main source of information for compiling the monetary and credit aggregates. The BA900 must be reconcilable with the balance sheet which banks and mutual banks submit for bank supervision purposes (BA100). Mutual banks submit the DI900 return which gets converted or mapped into the BA900 format. All returns used in the monetary survey are completed in rand. These returns are in the process of being revised to reflect developments in financial instruments and markets. Items in the report forms of the institutions are linked to the reporting institutions' accounting procedures. Efforts to harmonise the statistical and accounting systems have so far largely focused on explaining adjustments that statisticians need to make to the accounting data that is used as a major source to produce macroeconomic datasets. The need for such adjustments stems from accounting conventions and valuations differing from those required for statistical outputs. This is continuously addressed as a long-term strategic objective. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The frequency of the monetary survey is adequate to support the publication of monetary and credit aggregates. Source data are consistent with the definitions, scope and classification of monetary and credit aggregates. Source data are consistent with the time of recording and the valuation of assets. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Reporting banks are made aware of the deadline set for reporting and data is disseminated to the users and general public within the time limits laid down by SDDS requirements. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The survey is not a sample but covers all registered banks. Quality control is conducted through manually checking the consistency of data against previous values and following up inconsistencies with the respondents. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The monetary survey is based, in principle, on complete reporting by the reporting institutions. Reporting institutions submit their returns electronically to the South African Reserve Bank (SARB). Data are reported directly into the Economic Statistics Department's data collection systems, where validation rules are applied to ensure correct reporting by respondents. Reports are used to check for consistency and excessive deviations in item lines and columns. If a bank is not in a position to submit its balance sheet (BA900) returns to the South African Reserve Bank (SARB) by the 15th business day, the Money and Banking Division must be approached for an extension. If no return is received, the return of the previous month is kept constant for computing purposes and carried forward to calculate preliminary monetary aggregates and credit extended by the monetary sector until such time as the return has officially been received and processed. In the event of an erroneous return, the specific institution must re-submit a return to the Money and Banking Division, after which a final calculation will be made. Directives are issued to the reporting institutions on a regular basis to assist them in the correct and uniform completion of the returns. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures All key monetary aggregates are presented as both actual and seasonally adjusted data. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Infrastructure is in place to ensure that information collected from the reporting banks conforms to a certain set standard. Editing rules are in place to ensure the validity of electronically submitted data and that the content adds up correctly to totals. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Quality control is conducted through manually checking the consistency of data against previous values and following up inconsistencies with the respondents. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs In cases of new additions or revisions to information, changes are made to ensure accurate representation of data before dissemination of statistics. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Data are usually revised when there are structural changes to the data. For example, in March 2004 the monetary aggregates were revised historically to January 2001 to include negotiable promissory notes (see Note on the revision of monetary aggregates, March 2004 Quarterly Bulletin, which included an addendum on the original and revised data). In another example the Public Investment Commissioners corporatised in April 2005 and subsequently its deposits were re-classified as part of the broadly defined money supply (M3). At the same time, the government deposits with the monetary sector were reduced by a similar magnitude while net claims on the government sector of the monetary sector were raised. In this case the monetary aggregates were revised backward to 1996 (see Note on the corporatisation of the Public Investment Commissioners, June 2005 Quarterly Bulletin, which included an addendum on the original and revised data). During the first half of 2016, the financial instruments which banks include in deposits were reviewed. Subsequently, reporting banks were requested to move certain debt instruments, which fall outside the traditional definition of deposits, such as structured and credit-linked notes, from deposits into debt securities. This impacted the monetary aggregates reported by the SARB from January 2016. A note explaining the revisions were published in the September 2016 Quarterly Bulletin. The level of private sector credit extension (PSCE) decreased in January 2018, impacted by a technical increase in impairments. Loan loss provision increased when the International Financial Reporting Standard (IFRS) 9 for financial instruments became effective for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2018. The measurement of credit impairments according to the expected credit loss model replaced the incurred loss model. A note explaining the impact was published in the June 2018 Quarterly Bulletin. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses The public is advised of major changes in methodology, source data, and statistical techniques in a special "Notes to tables" at the time of release of the Quarterly Bulletin. Major changes affecting data is also indicated as footnotes to the tables in the Bulletin and on notes to Monthly Release of Selected Data on the Bank's website: www.resbank.co.za. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Data are available via e-mail from the contact person above, on request. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Presentations on the latest economic developments are given at the time of the release of the Quarterly Bulletin. Representatives from the press are invited to ask questions which are clarified by the respective divisional heads. A query facility is also available on the website, where questions are sorted by category and allocated to responsible persons in the various divisions. In general queries are received on an ad hoc basis, mostly by telephone or e-mail and referred to and handled by the various experts. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs Catalogs of publications, documents, and other services, including information on any changes is available on request.
