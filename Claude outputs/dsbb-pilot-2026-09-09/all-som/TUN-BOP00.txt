 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Definition: Tunisia's balance of payments is a statistical statement that summarizes economic transactions between residents of the Tunisian economy and nonresidents during a specific time period (exp: quarter...) Analytical Framework : Tunisia's balance of payments is compiled in accordance with the methodology of the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6). The presentation of the balance of payments includes the current account, the capital account, and the financial account. The current account shows flows of goods, services, primary income and secondary income between residents and nonresidents. The capital account shows credit and debit entries for non-produced nonfinancial assets and capital transfers between residents and nonresidents. The financial account shows net acquisition and disposal of financial assets and liabilities. It is classified according to five functional categories: direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives, other investments and reserve assets. Finally, a line for net errors and omissions is added as a balancing item. Concepts : The balance of payments presentation incorporates the standard components specified in BPM6. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification of balance of payments statistics of Tunisia is, essentially, based on the one stated in the sixth edition of the Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6): Goods: This heading covers all merchandise transactions whose economic ownership is changed between a resident and a nonresident. It is divided into three components: general merchandise, net exports of goods under merchanting and nonmonetary gold. Services : Twelve items are distinguished for services: Manufacturing services on physical inputs owned by others, Maintenance and repair services n.i.e., Transport, Travel, Construction, Insurance and pension services, Financial services, Charges for the use of intellectual property n.i.e, Telecommunications, computer, and information services, Other business services, Personal, cultural, and recreational services, Government goods and services n.i.e. Primary income . The primary income item covers compensation of employees and investment income including reinvested earnings and rent of natural resources to another non-resident institutional unit. The investment income is broken down by functional categories. Secondary income is broken-down as public and private transfers including workers' remittances. Net nonlife insurance premiums and claim payments are introduced as secondary income. Capital account . In the balance of payments of Tunisia, this account covers capital transfers as well as the acquisition or disposal of non-produced, non-financial assets. Financial account : operations related to this account are not recorded in terms of "Credit" and "Debit" but are henceforth entered on a net base "Net acquisition of financial assets" and "Net incurrence of liabilities". The financial account indicates functional categories, sectors, instruments and maturities used for international financial operations (investment and financial transactions). The functional categories are direct investments including reinvested earnings, portfolio investments, financial derivatives, other investments and reserve assets. 2.2.1 Scope The classification of balance of payments statistics of Tunisia is, essentially, based on the one stated in the sixth edition of the Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6): Goods: This heading covers all merchandise transactions whose economic ownership is changed between a resident and a nonresident. Scope of the data Transactions coverage: In principle, the balance of payments system is designed to include all transactions, regions, or activities that are meant to be covered. 2.4.1 Valuation The data are recorded at market price. Conversion rates: balance of payments data are converted according to the average monthly exchange rate 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Unlike the "Current account" and the "Capital account" that are recorded in gross value (Credits/Debits), operations related to financial account are not recorded in terms of "Credit" and "Debit", but are henceforth entered on a net base "Net acquisition of financial assets" and "Net incurrence of liabilities". 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The main sources for quarterly balances are: Goods: customs declarations, administrative sources and ITRS. Services: services are calculated mainly using the ITRS, exchange office reporting, administrative sources and targeted surveys of certain operators. Primary income: ITRS, customs, administrative sources and targeted surveys of economic operators. Secondary income: ITRS, exchange office reporting, customs and targeted surveys of certain operators. Capital account: ITRS, customs and targeted surveys of certain economic operators. Direct investment: the main sources are the ITRS, surveys of various government departments and administrative sources as part of an ad hoc committee responsible for monitoring foreign investments. Portfolio investment: the main sources are the ITRS, the stock market, monetary statistics and external debt statistics. Other investment: ITRS, administrative sources, monetary statistics, external debt statistics and surveys of enterprises. Reserve assets: Reserve assets data are drawn from the CBT's monetary statistics. The statement of settlements is prepared on the basis of the financial operations carried out by the Central Bank of Tunisia and financial institutions classified as authorized intermediaries, in the form of transfers of foreign exchange, activity in foreign accounts in convertible dinars, and purchases and sales of foreign banknotes. The main sources are ITRS, exchange office reporting, monetary statistics and external debt statistics. Transactions with no financial counterpart are recorded by Customs and the National Statistics Institute or are obtained in periodic surveys of economic agents, specialized agencies, ministries, and embassies and other diplomatic missions installed in Tunisia. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques For quarterly balances, the various items are determined as follows: Goods: Exports and imports are expressed f.o.b.; imports are adjusted from the c.i.f. value to the f.o.b. value. Services: Services are calculated mainly from the ITRS, exchange office reporting and targeted surveys of certain operators. Adjustments in coverage and classification are made for freight and insurance and are established by the conversion of imports from the c.i.f. value to the f.o.b. value. Primary income: data on cash flows are drawn from the ITRS, while transactions with no financial counterpart are drawn from enterprises and customs. Secondary income: data on cash flows are drawn from the ITRS, exchange office reporting, while data on in-kind flows are drawn from enterprises and customs. Capital account: data on flows are drawn from the ITRS, customs and targeted surveys. Direct investment: the data issued by the ITRS are checked against the results of the monthly survey of the various government departments and administrative sources as part of an ad hoc committee responsible for monitoring foreign investments. This comparison allows the amount of direct investment to be determined. Portfolio investment: the data issued by the ITRS and the stock market, monetary statistics and external debt statistics. Other investment: ITRS, administrative sources, monetary statistics, external debt statistics and surveys. Reserve assets: Reserve assets data are drawn from the BCT's monetary statistics. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Compilation method: the balance of payments is produced by combining the statement of financial settlements (International Transactions Reporting System—ITRS and exchange office reporting) with transactions with no financial counterpart (targeted surveys). 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Data from ITRS are verified by crosschecking with informations reported by other departments, exp: debt. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Geographic detail: Geographic allocation by country, continent, groups of countries, and international organizations is available in the annual balance of payments statistics.
