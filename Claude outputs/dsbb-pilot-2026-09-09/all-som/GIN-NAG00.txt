 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The national accounts are currently compiled according to SNA68, even though the new national accounts system of the United Nations (SNA93) was introduced in the country some time ago and is in the process of implementation. The economy is broken down into about 40 branches and sub-branches of economic activity. Gross domestic product (GDP) is calculated using three approaches: production, income, and expenditure. Revenue/expenditure accounts and capital/financing accounts are compiled for the various institutional sectors, and rest of the world accounts are compiled for the entire economy. The classification used is ISIC (International Standard Industrial Classification), Version 2. At the aggregate level are agriculture; forestry; fishing; livestock; manufacturing industry; electricity, water and gas; construction and public works; trade; transportation; banking and insurance; non-market services of government agencies. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The accounts are compiled according to SNA68. Classification follows the ISIC (International Standard Industrial Classification), Version 2. Scope of the data The accounts cover the entire national territory of Guinea. The framework for the Guinean national accounts is SNA68. However, it should be noted that AFRISTAT has launched the implementation of SNA93. Gross domestic product (GDP) covers both formal and informal sector activities but does not take into account illegal activities, much less the underground economy. It has been calculated at current prices and constant prices since 1986 with some basic changes over the years. All sectors and all resident units are covered by the surveys. To date, the informal sector is estimated on the basis of the results from the single large survey that was conducted on this sector in 1987. 2.4.1 Valuation Production is valued at factor costs and at market prices. Imports are valued at c.i.f. prices, and exports at f.o.b. prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis Most operations are recorded on an accrual basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The data used for the national accounts are obtained on the basis of surveys of resident economic units, primarily non-financial corporations and quasi-corporations, and financial institutions. For the government and the primary sector, the data are obtained from the various ministerial departments concerned, covering economy and finance, agriculture, fishing, water and forestry, and livestock. The data collected are in the form of company accounting statements, national economic indicators, and statistical data from the primary sector. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Given the existence of several inconsistent data sources, the Guinean national accountant performs a reconciliation of the various data to arrive at an estimate for each macroeconomic aggregate that is sufficiently close to economic reality. The accounts are compiled annually. However, there are often lags in compiling the accounts due to delays in financing for field surveys. GDP is calculated at constant prices by branch of economic activity and according to the uses of GDP (private final consumption + government final consumption + gross fixed capital formation + changes in stocks + exports).
