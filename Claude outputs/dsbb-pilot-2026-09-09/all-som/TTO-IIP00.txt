 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The Central Bank of Trinidad and Tobago (the Bank) is officially responsible for compiling Trinidad and Tobago’s international investment position (IIP). Data are collected from administrative sources including the Central Bank, the Central Government and deposit-taking corporations as well as via enterprise surveys of public and private corporations. Data are compiled on a quarterly basis with a 5-7 month lag, and published in the Bank’s serial publications and on the website. The statistics are reported in U.S. dollars. The classification and compilation of the international investment position follow the guidelines recommended in the sixth edition of the IMF’s Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6). The IIP of Trinidad and Tobago is published with a 5-7 month lag, but key elements such as the international reserves are published monthly. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Trinidad and Tobago’s IIP is largely classified according to international standards and conventions described in BPM6. 2.2.1 Scope Trinidad and Tobago’s IIP is largely classified according to international standards and conventions described in BPM6. Scope of the data Unit Coverage: The IIP of Trinidad and Tobago is a statistical statement that shows at a point in time the value of: financial assets of residents of an economy that are claims on nonresidents or are gold bullion held as reserve assets; and the liabilities of residents of an economy to nonresidents. Exceptions to coverage No attempts are made to include estimates for informal transactions. Unrecorded activity No attempts are made to include estimates for informal transactions. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: The valuation generally used is that agreed upon between two transactors and is, as such, recorded at market price. Conversion to unit of account: The IIP is compiled in U.S. dollars; transactions are converted from Trinidad and Tobago dollars to U.S. dollars using the exchange rate prevailing at the end of the reference period. 2.4.2 Recording basis The IIP is recorded as close to an accrual basis as possible. The accrual principle is mainly used with the exception of Central Government external debt data for which accrued interest is not included. Positions for Central Government external debt data are also valued at nominal values. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The Central Bank of Trinidad and Tobago (the Bank) is officially responsible for compiling Trinidad and Tobago’s IIP. Data are collected from administrative sources including the Central Bank, the Central Government and deposit-taking corporations as well as via enterprise surveys of public and private corporations. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Direct investment: Enterprise surveys and administrative data from deposit-taking corporations. Portfolio investment: Enterprise surveys as well as administrative data from deposit-taking corporations, the Trinidad and Tobago Stock Exchange, the Central Bank and the Central Government. Other investment: Enterprise surveys as well as administrative data from deposit-taking corporations, the Central Bank and the Central Government . Reserve assets: The Central Bank . 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Quarterly surveys are requested to be returned no later than one month following the end of the reference quarter. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Ongoing efforts are being made to strengthen assessment procedures at all stages of data collection and compilation. Formal data verification checks are conducted routinely to ensure data consistency. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures It is customary that stock data from the most recent reference period available are repeated for non-response. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Survey submissions of larger respondents particularly from the energy sector are reviewed for inconsistencies between transactions and stocks. . Data on surveys for the insurance companies are validated with the stocks reported in its regulatory submissions. There are procedures for assessing and checking the consistency of IIP data for consistency with other macroeconomic datasets as well as with external data such as the BIS mirror data. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs There are ongoing efforts to strengthen the assessment procedures at all stages of data collection and compilation. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Data for the current year are revised on an ongoing basis. Data for previous periods are usually revised on an ad-hoc basis. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished but non-confidential data are not available to users without approval from the Deputy Governor.
