 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Definition: The central government debt is the outstanding direct liabilities of the Federal (central) government, inclusive of on-budget and off-budget units. The concept is consistent with the International Monetary Fund's Government Finance Statistics Manual 2014. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification system: The central government debt data are classified into marketable and nonmarketable debt by type of debt instrument. The debt is further broken down based on original maturity into short-term (1 year and less) and long-term (greater than 1 year). Scope of the data Item coverage: The Central government debt includes the total Treasury securities, broken down into Debt Held by the Public and Intragovernmental Holdings and Agency securities. Data are showing the gross outstanding debt liabilities of the Federal (central) government. The data cover the outstanding debt of the Federal (central) government, both on-budget and off-budget units. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation practices: The central government marketable debt is valued at face value. Marketable securities sold at a discount are valued at face value. Foreign series securities are valued monthly at face value. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Gross vs. net: The central government debt data are disseminated on a gross and net basis. The total treasury securities outstanding are on a gross basis and the statutory debt limit is on a net basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources: The central government data are collected under the Summary Debt Accounting System (SDAS) based on daily reporting from Federal Reserve Banks and the Bureau of the Fiscal Service internal offices.
