 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The methodology used for the basic statistics (at the customs units) complies with United Nations recommendations and the Common Statistical Rules of the CAEMC (Act 8/1977-UDEAC-226). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The goods are identified by the six-digit number of the statistical classification of the customs tariff (Harmonized System) with a two-digit extension for national descriptors. Scope of the data These statistics cover imports and exports according to the General System. However, they do include imported military goods (imports of military equipment by Gabon-when such information is available) and imports for the French military base). Exceptions to coverage It should be noted that these data do not cover estimates of contraband, nor do they include financial instruments or monetary gold. Unrecorded activity 2.4.1 Valuation The statistics are valued in CFA francs; imports and exports in the external trade statistics are valued free at national border. The values used are: for imports: the c.i.f. (cost-insurance-freight) value, namely the value of the merchandise at the point of entry; for exports: the f.o.b. (free on board) value, namely the value of the merchandise at its point of exit. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The main source of external trade data is the Directorate General of Customs and Indirect Taxes (DGDDI) which, as a by-product of its administrative management of the country's external trade relations, produces customs statistics on external trade. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data The export value of some primary commodities (manganese, uranium, palm oil, coffee, cocoa, etc.) are the market values for customs services (on the basis of which export duties and taxes are calculated); these market values are often different from the real or statistical value. Generally, the external trade statistics compiled for the supply-use balance by national accounts compilers are taken from the customs units.
