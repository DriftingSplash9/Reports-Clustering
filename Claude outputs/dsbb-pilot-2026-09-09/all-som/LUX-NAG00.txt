 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: National accounts are compiled according to the methodological framework of the European System of Accounts 2010 (ESA 2010) and methodological guidelines (handbooks, guides, ..) published by Eurostat/OECD. Soundness of sources and methods is monitored by the European Commission (Eurostat) (GNI verification cycle, Eurostat working groups, quality reports). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification systems: The main reference classifications used are NACE Rev. 2 and, CPA2015. COICOP is used for private consumption. COFOG is used for public consumption. 2.2.1 Scope Classification systems: The main reference classifications used are NACE Rev. 2 and, CPA2015. COICOP is used for private consumption. COFOG is used for public consumption. Scope of the data Transaction coverage: The production boundary is in accordance with the European System of Accounts 2010. An estimate for consumption for own account agricultural production is also included. Informal and illegal economy (drugs, prostitution) is also included. Geographic coverage: Nation-wide. Exceptions to coverage Unrecorded activity The Luxembourg national accounts cover all transactions on the economic territory and between residents and non-residents. Estimates are made for certain types of under-reporting, such as activities not declared for tax evasion reasons, own-account production of dwellings and other informal activities. Illegal activities relating to drug trafficking and prostitution are also included in the estimate of GDP. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: Resources are recorded at basic prices (production) and CIF prices (imports). Uses are recorded at purchaser’s prices and FOB prices. Value added is recorded at basic prices. Transition to GDP at market prices is done by adding an aggregate estimate of net taxes on products. Output of government and NPISHs are recorded as sum of cost. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: In principle all transactions are recorded on an accrual basis (ESA 2010). Government tax income and social security contributions are recorded on a time adjusted cash basis. Imports and export are recorded at the time goods are released by customs (for flows with countries outside the European Union) or on the basis of change of ownership (for flows with countries inside the European Union). Recording period: Calendar quarters and years. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Grossing procedures have been monitored by Eurostat in the context of the inventory of sources and methods of compilation of GNI. Methods used are state of the art and consistent with European national accounts compilation quality standards. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Main data sources for annual accounts are : Production approach: The production side of the annual accounts is based on administrative data, business accounts data and business survey data. Expenditure approach: For the expenditure approach of the yearly accounts, the sources are: Household final consumption expenditure: A yearly households budget survey and VAT turnover for retail trade, hotels and restaurants and other services. Final consumption expenditure of NPISHs: Estimate on basis of employment, wages and salaries from Social Security data. Final consumption expenditure of general government: The budget statements. Gross fixed capital formation: Investment survey data, yearly accounts of enterprises and budget statements (for general government), foreign trade data for certain kind of investment goods. Changes in inventories: Business surveys and yearly accounts of enterprises. Exports and imports of goods and services: Customs statistics, Intrastat data and balance of payments data, adjusted to the national accounts concepts if necessary. The exhaustiveness and the coherence of the estimations are ensured by means of the balancing of the supply and use table. Income approach (only for annual accounts) : The sources are: Compensation of employees: Administrative source from social security Taxes on production an subsidies: The budget statements of general government Gross operating surplus is calculated as a residual item. Main data sources for quarterly accounts are: Quarterly accounts are also compiled and balanced in a supply and use framework. Quarterly trends using indirect indicators are used for estimating quarterly time series. Quarterly accounts are benchmarked to annual accounts, when annual figures have been published. Production approach Value added is estimated for 250 NaceRev2 branches of activity, but these are aggregated into ten main branches for publication. The indicators used are as follows: Industrial production indices for industry (manufacturing, construction and energy); VAT records for most market services and some industries; Quarterly business accounts for financial services; Monthly budget statements for general government; Quarterly statements for social security funds. E xpenditure Approach Household final consumption expenditure: VAT turnover for retail trade, hotels and restaurant services; Final consumption expenditure of NPISHs: Derived from annual accounts of NPISH units. For units without annual accounts, estimate on basis of employment, wages and salaries from Social Security data. Final consumption expenditure of general government: The estimates for central government are based on the monthly budget statement and the estimates for social security on quarterly statistics. The quarterly final consumption expenditure of local government is interpolated with employment and salary data starting from a yearly extrapolation. Gross fixed capital formation: Fixed gross capital formation of companies is based on imports and production of investment goods. Fixed gross capital formation on housing is based on VAT turnover (real estate construction). Fixed gross capital formation for public administration comes from the monthly budget statement. Change in inventories: Derived as a residual by subtracting the sum of the other expenditure components from total GDP as measured from the production approach. Exports and imports of goods and services: The customs statistics and Intrastat statistics for goods (adjusted to the national accounts concepts) Balance of payments data for services. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The national account department can influence the design of surveys collecting source data, like structural business statistics (SBS). In such a setting, the source data fits quite well the requirements for national accounts (ESA2010). For other source data, adaptations are performed or replaced by estimations to meet the definitions and scope of national account concepts. As national accounts use their own classifications, an important mapping work from source data classifications to national accounts classifications is needed (ex: Combined Nomenclature for foreign trade data to CPA). Sometimes source data are not classified by activities. NACE codification is then added to the source data. Valuation and time of recording of source data fits quite well the requirements of national accounts. If needed, adjustments are made to source data when they are integrated in national accounts: inventories are reevaluated based on average prices, source data are adjusted to fit a calendar year, insurance services needs to be calculated from insurance source data,… 3.1.3 Source data timeliness When data used for the quarterly benchmark indicators is missing, indicators are forecasted by econometric methods in order to compile quarterly accounts. For the yearly national accounts data from “structural business statistics”, which are the main data sources for non-financial corporations, are only available with delay (t-2 years). For the 1st preliminary annual estimate, they need to be estimated by appropriate benchmark indicators drawn mainly from administrative sources. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Assessment of the appropriate source data for national accounts is done by national accountants. Routine assessment of the quality of source data is mainly done by the departments in charge of collecting source data. Additional quality assessment of source data is done by national accountants. When different source data are available for a concept of national accounts, their respective quality is compared before one of the data source is integrated in the statistical process of calculating national accounts. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques National accounts are basically compiled through selection and aggregation of source data, while adjusting the data to the concepts of national when needed (bottom-up estimates). For years t-1 and t-2, some source data is not available. Missing data is estimated through benchmark indicators (related indicators) on micro level or intermediate level. In some cases estimations based on benchmark indicators may replace source data when the source data does not fit well with the concepts of the national accounts. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Quarterly compilation techniques: The statistical methods applied to draw up the quarterly accounts use mainly indirect methods as quarterly figures are estimated through a range of benchmark indicators for the estimation or extrapolation of the quarterly figures. When data used for the benchmark indicators are missing, indicators are forecasted by econometric methods in order to compile quarterly accounts. In order to guarantee coherence between annual and quarterly figures, Luxembourg’s quarterly national accounts use the Denton adjustment method. This econometric model is used to adjust the quarterly data so that the sum thereof corresponds to the annual data, whilst tallying as closely as possible with the related quarterly indicators. Current and constant prices: Volume measures are calculated by deflating value indicators by adequate price indices as follows: production approach – producer price, import price, export price and consumer price indices are used to derive volume estimates; expenditure approach – a) household final consumption expenditure: the consumer price index is used to deflate the VAT turnover for retail trade, hotels and restaurant services; b) gross fixed capital formation: the producer prices index and the price index of imports are used to deflate all the items of gross fixed capital formation; c) exports and imports of goods and services: indices of foreign trade prices for goods and services are used as deflators. For some cases, direct volume indicators are used to estimate constant prices values. Seasonal adjustment: Working days and seasonal adjustments are made by the X13-ARIMA model of JDemetra+ program. Base year and reference year: Volume measures are calculated at previous year prices. Chained linked series are published with respect to the reference period 2015. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The coherence of the compilation framework is guaranteed in a supply and use framework. Discrepancies are investigated on case by case base. Final estimates are fully balanced. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Reconciliation of the production and expenditure approach: The reconciliation of the production and expenditure side is obtained within supply and use tables. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses A benchmark revision of annual accounts is done every five years. The new benchmark revision is planned for 2027. In benchmark years the whole time series may be revised if new sources become available, methods have changed or other issues have to addressed like sector reclassification of some units. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses A study on revisions in the Luxembourg quarterly GDP estimates can be found in the publication Economie et statistiques N° 39/2010 Revisions to GDP estimates in Luxembourg https://statistiques.public.lu/en/publications/series/economie-statistiques/2010/39-2010.h tml 5.1.5 Dissemination on request 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points
