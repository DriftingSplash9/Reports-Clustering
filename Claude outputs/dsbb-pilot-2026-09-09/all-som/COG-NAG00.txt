 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The framework of reference for the Congolese national accounts is the 1968 System of National Accounts ( SNA68 ). Previously, the National Center for Statistics and Economic Studies ( CNSEE ) used to produce and disseminate the annual national accounts. Since November 2002, when the Directorate of Economic Studies and Digests was transferred to the Directorate General of Economy (DGE) at the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Budget (MEFB), this responsibility has been assigned to the DGE. The following data are compiled: GDP estimates broken down by branch of economic activity (total of 17, which are reduced to 10 for purposes of publication) and by type of expenditure (final consumption, gross fixed capital formation, changes in stocks, exports less imports of goods and services). The two estimates are calculated at current prices (CFAF billions) calculated at constant prices (base year: 1978 or 1990 = 100, as appropriate); Input-Output tables are provided for 17 branches of economic activity and 16 products at current prices and for prior-year prices; Comprehensive economic tables ( TEE ): institutional sector and rest-of-the-world accounts (production, operating expenses, income and expenditure, capital and financing) were compiled up to 1996. The tables for 1997 through 2000 are being prepared. GDP, which is valued at market prices, is defined as the final result of the productive activity of resident units during the reference period (usually one year). It is also defined as the sum of the end-uses of goods and services in the economy as a whole, less the value of imports of goods and services during the period. Productive units are grouped by their main economic activity. The end-uses of goods and services comprise final private consumption (of households and nonprofit institutions), final public consumption (of general government), gross fixed capital formation, changes in stocks, and exports. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization In general, the SNA68 is used as the basis for classifying institutional sectors (i.e., the local version of the classification is as follows: (i) non-financial corporate and (ii) quasi-corporate enterprises—subdivided into public, oil, and private non-oil; financial institutions; government agencies; private nonprofit institutions; households; and unincorporated enterprises). The classification of branches of activity is a local adaptation of the ISIC rev. 2. The classification of products is an adaptation of the Central Product Classification (CPC). Scope of the data Geographic Coverage The national accounts cover the entire economic territory, including territorial enclaves. Transaction Coverage The national accounts cover transactions in goods and services, broken down by intitutional sectors, and the consolidated accounts of the nation. Unit Coverage The national accounts data, except for a few exceptions, cover all resident units, including producers in the informal sector (i.e., those who do not maintain formal accounts). In the absence of survey data, the informal sector production is estimated by using assumptions and indirect methods. For example, for certain activities, it is calculated as a residual, namely, the difference between total production and formal sector production). Unrecorded activity Illegal activities (contraband, for example) are not explicitly excluded and may be recorded indirectly. The supply-use balances (by product) derived from data taken from different sources for calculating external trade are used as a basis for estimating contraband activity. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: production is recorded at ex-factory prices (all taxes included, i.e., turnover tax or VAT); imports are recorded c.i.f. (cost-insurance-freight), and exports are valued f.o.b. (free-on-board). Intermediate and final uses of goods and services are valued at purchase prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording : T ransactions are recorded on an accrual basis. The general government account registers expenditure when payment is authorized, but revenue is recorded when payments are received. Recording period : Calendar year. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs 1. Production Approach: Subsistence farming : Production is estimated on the basis of data from the 1982-86 Agricultural Census for a certain number of products. It is also based on certain assumptions/ hypotheses (formulated by the national accounts unit) for fruits and vegetables, in light of trends in household consumer prices for agricultural products observed in the country’s two main cities. Cash crops and livestock : basic data are furnished by the Ministry of Agriculture. Uncut and processed wood (lumber and veneers) : the data are taken from annual rapid ("flash") surveys, half-yearly market surveys, statistical and tax declarations ( DSFs ), from the Ministry of Forestry Economy, and estimates from the national accounts unit (for the informal sector) on the production of firewood. Oil : production is calculated on the basis of data furnished by the Oil and Gas Directorate General of the Oil and Gas Ministry, the valuation price committee, and oil companies' DSFs. Manufacturing industry : estimates of production from formal activities are based on the data from annual "flash" surveys of enterprises, half-yearly market surveys, statistical and tax declarations, and knowledge of trends in imports of raw materials. Production from informal sector activities is assessed on the basis of assumptions and indirect methods. Construction and Public Works (BTP) : production estimates are derived from DSFs, half-yearly market surveys, ass well as administrative data on public investment expenditure on construction of road infrastructure and buildings. Trade : trade output is composed of estimates of margins on goods by category—locally-produced or imported—taken from the goods supply-use balances. Transportation and Communications : in general, basic data come from annual "flash" surveys, statistical and tax declarations, and the annual and management reports of public enterprises to assess formal sector production. Regarding the informal sector, production is estimated on the basis of available information on the fleet of vehicles for goods and passenger transportation provided by the government, as well as trends in fuel consumption (gas and diesel). Real estate services : production is estimated on the basis of certain assumptions of the growth in the number of households in rural and urban areas and data from statistical and tax declarations. Insurance and financial institutions : production is determined on the basis of the annual and management reports of banks and insurance companies and data collected during annual "flash" surveys of financial institutions. General government: production is estimated using data from budget documents and administrative accounts, actual budget outcomes provided by the Government Flow of Funds Table ( TOFE ), and accounting data from autonomous agencies (social security fund, local governments, and other agencies). 2. Expenditure approach Household final consumption : the data are generally obtained as a residual by drawing up supply-use balances for each product (i.e., by comparing supply of both production and imports with demand, excluding final consumption. Government final consumption is estimated on the basis of data drawn from administrative documents. Gross fixed capital formation is compiled on the basis of statistical and tax declarations, actual public investment expenditure, the value of imported durables, and the results of annual "flash" surveys of businesses. Exports of goods and services are assessed on the basis of company declarations and estimates by the national accounts unit. This information is cross-checked with balance of payments data. Imports of goods and services : imports of goods and services are estimated on the basis of the statistics compiled by the Directorate General of Customs and the statistics published by the Economic Cooperation and Development Office ( OCDE ) to better measure trends in overall volume. Data on imported services are estimated on the basis of the projections in the intermediate consumption matrix of the input-output table. 3. Institutional sector accounts: General government: the various accounts are estimated on the basis of data drawn from the accounts and other administrative documents. Non-financial corporate and quasi-corporate enterprises: these are divided into three subsectors (public, oil, and non-oil). Data extracted from the statistical and tax declarations are processed and extrapolated by the national accounts unit. Financial institutions : These are derived from the management and other reports of insurance companies, banks and financial institutions, data from the annual rapid survey of these institutions and information on banks’ management indicators reported by the BEAC National Office. Households and unincorporated enterprises: the accounts are compiled using different sources (supply-use balances, administrative accounts, balance of payments, international aid, indirect estimates from the national accounts unit). Nonprofit institutions : estimates by the national accounts unit based on the trends observed in some services. Rest of the world : the various accounts are compiled on the basis of balance of payments items and estimates from the national accounts unit. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The compilation of data for year "n" is based mainly on several factors: Availability of data for previous year (t -1) at current prices; Processing of crude data drawn from statistical and tax declarations, the annual rapid ("flash") survey of businesses, as well as data from government agencies (government budgetary data, data on the output of certain activities reported by the planning and studies directorates of the ministries, TOFE data, foreign trade and balance of payments statistics). These crude data are processed in such a way that would allow the determination of the volume, value, price indices, and coefficients needed to extrapolate the data for compiling the intermediate consumption matrix of the input-output table, goods and services balances, the production accounts for branches of economic activity, and the institutional sector accounts. 1. Production Approach: The crude data from the DSFs, annual rapid surveys, annual and management reports of public enterprises, and the estimated DSF "no-response" rate are used to calculate the production accounts. Estimation of data at current and constant prices are calculated using the available information on value, volume, and prices for all products. The volume and price indices are the indicators used to extrapolate the data at current and constant prices. 2. Expenditure approach : The components of GDP by category of expenditure are not obtained independently for all accounts. Gross fixed capital formation is estimated on the basis of information derived from statistical and tax declarations, the national budget (for public investment expenditure), and the results of annual "flash" surveys of businesses. Imports and exports are derived from the statistics compiled by the Directorate General of Customs, declarations by enterprises, and estimates from the national accounts unit. This information is cross-checked with the balance of payments. Household final consumption is generally calculated as a balance by preparing supply-use balances for each product, if direct sources are not available. Government final consumption is calculated on the basis of administrative data. Changes in stocks are estimated according to the type of products comprising the stocks. For some products, the data are drawn from statistical and tax declarations; others are estimated as a balance (residual)after removing the share of household final consumption. T he extrapolation of data at current and constant prices is based on volume and price indices. Reconciliation: is attempted before closing the national accounts; statistical discrepancies are reduced by checking input-output table data against the institutional sector accounts. None
