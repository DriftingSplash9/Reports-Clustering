 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The overall structure of the Other Financial Corporations Survey program is generally in accordance with that described in the 2008 System of National Accounts and the Monetary and Financial Statistics Manual and Compilation Guide. Occasional departures from this standard are primarily prompted by pragmatic considerations. Information on the concepts, structure, definitions can be found in "Methodological Guide: Canadian System of Macroeconomic Accounts " (Catalogue 13-607-X). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Institutional sectoring follows 2008 SNA guidelines with a few key differences. The Other Financial Corporations Survey is sectored according to an institutional unit basis rather than a functional basis. This is a departure from the sectoring used in the National Balance Sheet Accounts which is a sectoring according to an institutional basis with a few exceptions. The main difference in sectoring is that the monetary authorities sector is split into its depository elements and its government elements. The Bank of Canada is classified as a depository corporation, and the exchange fund account and other monetary authorities sectors are classified as captive financial institutions under the government sector. The other financial corporations sector is comprised of insurance corporations and pension funds, other mutual funds, other financial intermediaries, except insurance corporations and pension funds, financial auxiliaries, captive financial institutions and money lenders, and federal financial government business enterprises. Other sectors comprise households and non-profit institutions serving households, and non-financial corporations. The depository corporations sector consists of total chartered banks and quasi-banks, the Bank of Canada, money market funds, and provincial financial government business enterprises. Note that this latter sector includes some provincial non-depository financial government business enterprises. The general government sector consists of the general government, the exchange fund account, and other monetary authorities. Claims on and from depository corporations include claims on and from provincial non-depository financial government business enterprises. The Other items (net) series represents assets and liabilities of the other financial corporations sector for which economic sectorization of the counterparty is unknown. It is a net liability position and is computed by subtracting liabilities from assets. In practice, a positive other items (net) implies that the unclassified liabilities are greater than unclassified assets and a negative other items (net) implies that the unclassified assets are greater than unclassified liabilities. Financial instruments that comprise other items (net) include: listed share assets, corporate bond assets and liabilities, corporate short-term paper assets and liabilities, corporate claims: loans and advances assets and liabilities, other accounts receivable and payable (which include financial derivatives). The compilation of complete financial accounts on a whom-to-whom basis for all financial instruments is in progress. As such, the other financial corporations survey will be revised on an ongoing basis as more whom-to-whom information is available. As the coverage of financial instruments in the financial accounts on a whom-to-whom basis expands, the other items (net) series will reduce in size, as more assets and liabilities are sectored to their appropriate counterparty. 2.2.1 Scope Institutional sectoring follows 2008 SNA guidelines with a few key differences. Scope of the data The Other Financial Corporations Survey is compiled according to the production and asset boundary defined by the 2008 SNA and comprises all assets and liabilities of the other financial corporations sector with one exception. The sector classification is not completely aligned with the 2016 Monetary and Financial Statistics Manual and Compilation Guide, which results in incomplete coverage of assets and liabilities by counterparty sector. The other financial corporations sector is comprised of insurance corporations and pension funds, other mutual funds, other financial intermediaries except insurance corporations and pension funds, financial auxiliaries, captive financial institutions and money lenders, and federal financial government business enterprises that primarily engage in extending credit or lending funds raised by credit-market borrowing and by borrowing from financial corporations other than themselves. Exceptions to coverage Non-depository provincial financial government business enterprises are excluded from the other financial corporations sector as they are included with deposit-taking provincial financial government business enterprises in the provincial financial government business enterprises sector which is classified as a whole to the depository corporations sector. Assets and liabilities of the other financial corporations sector for which economic sectorization is unknown are presented in the other items (net) series. 2.4.1 Valuation Market Valuation is used to record all stocks of financial assets and liabilities. 2.4.2 Recording basis To the extent possible, an accrual basis is used. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Stocks are presented on a gross basis with the exception of other items (net), which is presented on a net basis where liabilities are subtracted by assets. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data are extracted from administrative files and derived from other Statistics Canada surveys and/or macroeconomic accounts. These include the national balance sheet accounts program, the international investment position program and the financial accounts on a from-whom-to-whom basis program. Statistics Canada also has access to a number of several commercial databases, which are used to establish stocks of instruments such as mutual fund unitss or establish the market value of equity. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The Other Financial Corporations Survey program is an extension of existing macroeconomic programs, which deal directly with data providers and are able to influence the receipt of information in terms of format (paper vs electronic), content, and timeliness. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The majority of the source data used to compile the Other Financial Corporations Survey program are available 45 days after the reference period. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data are assessed on a continuous basis. Issues with source data are communicated with the data providers, both internal and external, for follow-up. All source data programs are subject to the Statistics Canada quality guidelines and as such, response rates, coverage rates, sampling errors, non-sampling errors are reviewed on a continuous basis and appropriate action is taken when required. In cases where the data provider is external to Statistics Canada – agreements are put in place to ensure the agency can influence the quality and receipt of the information. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques A number of statistical techniques are used in the data compilation processing including: Outlier Detection Seasonal Adjustment Benchmarking Derivation of Market Valuation Linking Conversion ratios Deflation Perpetual Inventory Methods 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures See 3.3.1 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Reports are generated multiple times during a production cycle to validate results, ensure accounting identities are adhered to, revisions are examined, and outliers, discrepancies and trends are analyzed. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Data are analyzed in the context of domestic economic trends and events, stock market indices and exchange rates movements, price movements, changes in savings, financial flows, revaluations and international economic trends and events 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Statistical discrepancies, large movements, changes in positions relative to transactions and revaluation factors are monitored throughout the production cycle. Unexplained changes serve as an indicator of the quality of position data sources. Further investigations are performed by staff to understand the nature of these changes and make recommendations to improve quality. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revision analyses are performed on a regular basis, mainly at the time the annual benchmark is introduced. The direction and the magnitude of the revisions are investigated by counterparty sector. Sources of revisions are identified and, if possible, actions are taken to minimize revisions in subsequent periods. Mechanisms are in place to integrate findings of revision studies to improve estimates. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Major changes to the estimates resulting from the implementation of new concepts and methodologies are explained to users through special studies. Users are informed of reference periods that have been revised at the time the data are disseminated. Information on the overall revision policy in place in the macroeconomic accounts is available on the Statistics Canada website, including the possible nature of these revisions. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Special tabulations can be produced upon requests and are subject to the same confidentiality provisions as standard tabulations. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points At the time the data are disseminated, information on contact points (general enquiry line or contact a subject-matter expert) are made available to users. Enquiries can be sent by mail or through phone calls. Subject-matter experts listed as contact points need to have completed training to deal with media enquiries. Client enquiries are monitored and subject to corporate services standards. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs All information (analytical write-up, charts, data tables, metadata, research paper and revision studies, users' guide and methodological guide, etc.) related to the statistical program are available on the Statistics Canada website free of charge.
