 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The IMAE is a synthetic index that captures the evolution of the real economic activity in the short term, and extrapolated to the quarterly national accounts. The estimate of IMAE is based on a set of monthly indicators related to production, sales, seasonality, and other representative variables of some economic activities. The main advantages of this index are: The IMAE results are more timely than the quarterly GDP. It is consistent with the CNT because it is based on an accounting framework that includes a set of monthly indicators from similar sources than CNT; also, IMAE uses the same level of economic activities and components for the estimates of the GDP of the national accounts, through a chaining process of mobile bases with which the total index is obtained. The construction of the index allows the analysis of the evolution of the economic activity by the production approach, assuming constant productivity in the short term, and productive structures remaining unchanged in time. The monthly estimation of economic activities is made through the use of production indicators, in which it is assumed that the monthly value added of a given economic activity evolves in the same way as the Gross Value of Production (VBP). These production indicators evolve according to a set of indicators related to economic activity, plus an estimation error derived from factors such as representativeness of the indicator, data coverage, registration time, and changes in productivity, registration errors, among others. It is expected that the accumulated error of the estimated indicator will be reduced when the aggregation of economic activities is carried out; however, in order to accomplish such reduction, it is necessary to have high correlation between the indicators and the VBP. Thus, the IMAE series are considered more robust than any estimate for a sector. For the IMAE to achieve its purpose, its sources of information must be timely, coherent, precise, and detailed, in such a way that they are homogeneous with the annual national accounts (CNA) and the quarterly national accounts. • Information sources Since the objective of the IMAE is to provide a close perspective of the behavior of the GDP from the production perspective, the estimation originates with the extrapolation of the value added at constant prices of all the economic activities that make up the GDP, in its most detailed level (the nomenclature of economic activities for Guatemala has 143 economic activities; of which 132 are market, three are for own final use and eight are non-market), by using various indicators and complementary estimations for those activities that lack reliable and timely monthly information. Given the heterogeneous conditions of the information, and the characteristics of each economic activity, the indicators used in the compilation of the IMAE are classified into three groups: The first group (A) comprises those indicators that come from data on gross production volume, nominal total income from a representative sample of companies, methodologies similar to those used in the CNT, and reliable administrative records. In the second group (B), are those indicators that originate from foreign trade figures, estimation of final demand or intermediate demand. Finally, in the third group (C) are those indicators that, on one hand, represent economic activities that do not have their own indicators, but behave similarly to the evolution of other activities; on the other hand, indicators of economic activities for which no information is available, but which evolve through stochastic processes. The IMAE components that support the estimates in group “A” indicators represent 62.4% of GDP, while components in “B” indicators represent 33.7% of GDP, and components in group “C” indicators represent 3.9% of GDP. • Harmonization of the IMAE with quarterly GDP The harmonization methodology is a technique that allows consistency in the evolution of the IMAE series with the quarterly GDP series. For this purpose, the Denton proportional method is used, as dictated by the IMF's Quarterly National Accounts Manual in its 2017 edition. This procedure combines high frequency data series (selected monthly basic indicators) with lower frequency data series (quarterly figures), so that the sum of the new monthly IMAE series is the same as their corresponding quarterly series. This procedure preserves to the maximum the movements of the IMAE indicators, respecting the restriction of the quarterly GDP figures. The temporal disaggregation technique used to harmonize the IMAE is the Denton proportional method, which minimizes the absolute differences in the relative adjustments of the monthly data, under the restriction that the sum of the monthly aggregates is equal to the corresponding quarterly data. .It should be noted that this method is the same as that used in the Quarterly National Accounts. The benchmarking procedure included conducting out-of-sample tests, to compare the predictive power of the basic indicators used between the non-harmonized series and the harmonized series, of the eighteen components that make up the IMAE. One of these tests was the calculation of the mean square error, which is understood as the root of the average of the sum of the squared differences, between the harmonized and non-harmonized values of the monthly series. This estimator allows knowing, for all the IMAE series, how accurate the estimations were for the non-harmonizing series, versus the harmonized series with the quarterly GDP series, taking into account the indicators that were selected for this purpose. When comparing all the information that could be incorporated in the compilation of the IMAE, it was decided to include those that minimize the mean square error in all the IMAE series. The series of the IMAE were evaluated through correlation coefficients, to estimate the degree of linear relationship between the series and the state of dispersion of the data with respect to the average. It was established that most of the indicators used to measure the different components of the IMAE maintain a positive linear relationship with the quarterly series of national accounts, which was evidenced by a high correlation coefficient (0.8929) between the total IMAE series and quarterly GDP. • Chaining the numbers Consistent with international best practices implemented in the compilation of annual and quarterly national accounts, the IMAE updates with the reference year 2013; also, it contemplates the valuation of the index as chained measure of volume, this procedure is contemplated to be used in order to not use traditional estimates at constant prices with a fixed base year, in order to keep the relative price structure updated. Hence, the 2013 base indexes were constructed using the harmonized basic indicators with their respective chained quarterly references, which is added by means of their respective weights in the immediately previous year, resulting in weighted mobile base indexes. The chained indexes of the IMAE series were obtained using the multiplication of the mobile base indexes and the average of the chained index of the previous year. • Seasonally adjusted series Considering that a time series consists in a sequence of measurements of one variable observed through time, it is relevant noting that any series has influence of trend-cycle, seasonality, calendar effect and irregular components. Therefore, the seasonally adjusted series consists of the trend-cycle plus the irregular component of a times series. Basically, it extracts the seasonal signal from the original series of the IMAE. Also, the effects of the seasonal movements that occur in a year, and the mobile holidays among with the composition of the days of the week of each period. Derived from this process, the underlying trends and short-term movements of the series are obtained, allowing a comparison between months. The seasonally adjusted series of the IMAE series were carried out through the X-12 ARIMA program, developed by the Census Directorate of the United States of America. • Trend-cycle estimation This process extracts the irregular and seasonal components of the series from the original series, using the Henderson moving average procedure (9-13 periods). This procedure gives the underlying path of the data; that is, the compound movement in the long-term and the evolution of the business cycle of the data. • Update and review of the series Due to the short-term nature of the IMAE, the statistical data reviews are an essential part of the good practices implemented in the compilation of the indicator, derived from the availability of new or better information by the sources. Continuous inclusion of up-to-date information provides users with more accurate data, while avoiding significant subsequent reviews. The IMAE should also be reviewed or updated when there are revisions in the national accounts for a particular quarter or year, since it entails the revision of the figures for the months that make up the period in question. Thus, each time new data is published from the national accounts (annual or quarterly), it will be necessary to reconcile the IMAE figures again, which will imply changes in the series. However, the ultimate goal of updating past IMAE values is to provide updated extrapolation bases to obtain correctly weighted current estimates. • Publication The IMAE results are published around 45 days after the end of the month in compilation. The aggregate data of the index and the consequent annual rate of change for the original series and the trend-cycle series are presented. When there is information available regarding the quarterly national accounts, the monthly data of the IMAE will be harmonized. This will allow the IMAE series to be compiled and published in greater detail. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The index contains one hundred forty-three variables within one hundred thirty-two are market activities, three are for own final use, and eight are non-market activities. A) Agriculture, livestock, forestry, and fishing: information comes from data provided by the Ministry of agriculture and livestock, statistical methods such as intermediate demand, and by the Tax administration department. B) Mining and quarrying: information comes from the energy and mining ministry, along with a private mining company settled d in the country. C) Manufacturing industries: information is provided by the Tax administration department, National Institute of Statistics along with some private companies that provide information. D-E) Electricity, water, and sanitation supply: information is provided by The National commission of electrical energy. F) Building activities: information mainly comes from the Central Bank of Guatemala’s own surveys, and the statistic records from the Public Finance Ministry. K) Financial and insurance activities: information mainly comes from the Bank Superintendence O) Public administration and defense: information comes from the Public Finance Ministry. R-S-T-U) Private Services: activities related with services areas collect the information from the Central Bank’s own surveys, Tax administration department, and statistical methods such as the intermediate demand. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The surveys made by the Central Bank are considered the primary source for the compilation and collection of the information. Also, administrative records that come from the different public departments of the Government and statistical methods used to infer information. This information is consistent with the time of recording, and is valued at the transaction prices required by the national accounts; the reference period is consistent with the quarterly and annual information. Records do not require adjustments since the fiscal period comprises from January to December. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The schedule data comes on monthly basis. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The information that comes from surveys follows some quality standards. This includes the minimization of the sample error and data collection errors, procedures that identify the explicit actions to be taken when monitoring the survey in field, such as atypical values to be confirmed, follows improvement programs of coverage and quality indicators. Administrative data records from the public offices are periodically evaluated. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Some protocols are followed to analyze the consistency of the information; identification of outliers and the further investigation of them are part of the essential tasks. Otherwise the recalculation is based on the justification or amended results. Imputation and adjustment procedures in cases of non-response have relatively rigorous bases. In addition data reported from administrative sources are often checked against similar reports from previous periods. In case of anomalies, data is adjusted after consultation with the respondent. Adjustments to survey data and administrative records are related to information on output levels, intermediate consumption, value added, number of employees, and wage levels for each economic activity. In order to carry out such task, the information has to be revised and tested for consistency through crosschecking with other sources or estimates. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Another statistical process used, is the one that estimates the production of those activities which are inferred by the intermediate demand. This methodology searches all economic activities, and detects those where there is a percentage destined to intermediate consumption for business activities. In other words, what it is done is to detect among all economic activities those that require the services of other business activities, through the use of technical coefficients. These coefficients come from the Input-Output Matrix (MIP), which were calculated in a square matrix of activity by activity. These coefficients are multiplied by production, for each month and for each year. This process is a constant iteration of calculation, that is to say that as production changes or moves, the calculation should be done again as many times as necessary. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The validation of the intermediate results is done by the staff of the National Accounts as they are in charge of constructing the quarterly and annual GDP. They assess the original source information, as well as the processed and calculated data carried out by the unit of Economic Indicators and Price Indexes. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Staff involved in collection, processing, and compile the information monitors the developments in the possible errors. Also, the staff always tries to seek appropriated estimation techniques when information is missing or delayed. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs It is expected that the accumulated error of the estimated indicators will be reduced when the aggregation of economic activities is completed. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Since the IMAE is adjusted to the methodology used in the quarterly national accounts, each quarter the information is updated; this includes reviewing the information, and collating the past data against the new information or their corrections. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Revisions are not measured, assessed, or explained in the publications and in the database accessible by users. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Statistics data not routinely disseminated can be made available to users upon request, to the extent that the statistical confidentiality is respected. Applications must be submitted to the Board for approval. No specific reference is made in the publications regarding the Central Bank of Guatemala, on the availability of sub-aggregates unpublished data, and the terms and conditions under which they are made available.
