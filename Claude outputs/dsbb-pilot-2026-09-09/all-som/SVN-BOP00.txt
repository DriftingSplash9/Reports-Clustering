 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytic framework: In Slovenia, balance of payments statistics are compiled in accordance with the recommendations of the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6) http://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/bop/2007/bopman6.htm . Ten broad categories of data are identified under three main headings, namely current account--exports and imports of goods, services, primary and secondary income; capital account--capital transfers and acquisition/disposal of non-produced, nonfinancial assets; and financial account--assets and liabilities for direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives, other investment, and international reserve assets (and net errors and omissions). Definition: The balance of payments is a statistical statement that systematically summarizes, for a specific time period, the economic transactions of the residents of Slovenia with the rest of the world. Transactions, for the most part between residents and nonresidents, consist of those involving goods, services, and income; those involving financial claims on, and liabilities to, the rest of the world; and those (such as gifts) classified as transfers, which involve offsetting entries to balance--in an accounting sense--one-sided transactions. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification system: Transactions are classified in accordance with BPM6. Scope of the data Transactions coverage: Balance of payments statistics systematically summarize, for a specific time period, all economic transactions between residents and nonresidents. It includes individuals who have a permit to work or live in Slovenia for more than one year. Geographic coverage: Before 1991, the year of Slovenia's independence, balance of payments data exclude transactions of Slovenia with other Yugoslav Republics. After 1991, all transactions between residents and nonresidents are included. Unrecorded activity Unrecorded activity: Illegal transactions are partially estimated. 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to unit of account: Data reported in foreign currencies are converted to local currency at daily exchange rates or average exchange rates of the period. Changes in stocks of reserve assets, expressed in foreign currencies, are converted to Euros and US Dollars (also to Tolars until end of 2006 - Slovenia joined the euro area on 1 January 2007) using average monthly exchange rates. Valuation: Data on stocks of external financial assets and liabilities are converted at market exchange rates prevailing at the end of the period, while transactions are valued at market prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis Recording of income: All income (income on other investment, income on reserve assets, income on bonds and notes and income on equity) is recorded on an accrual basis. Compensation of employees (receipts and expenditures) is recorded on a gross basis (including taxes and contributions). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs · Goods: The main source is the foreign trade statistics compiled by the Statistical Office of the Republic of Slovenia (SORS). Exports and imports of goods are supplemented with information for selected transactions not recorded in the customs data. · Services: The reports on trade in services, part of trade in goods and on current/capital transfers with nonresidents (BST reports) are the sources of data on services, excluding travel. The data source to estimate travel services are surveys conducted by the Statistical Office of the Republic of Slovenia (SORS). · Primary income: Data on compensation of employees - receipts are obtained from the Labor Force Survey (responsibility of SORS) and Eurostat's database. Data on compensation of employees – expenditures are provided by SORS. Data on income on direct investment are obtained from monthly and annual reports on capital investments (dividends, reinvested earnings) and from other reports on other investment. Data sources for income on portfolio investment (equity and debt) are reports on operations in securities (VRP reports) and data from the Central Securities Clearing Corporation (KDD). Income on other investment is available from reports on other investment, like reports on credits/loans, deposits and other debt instruments with non-residents (KRD reports) and reports of banks (PORFI reports). · Secondary income: The main sources of data are BST reports except for European Union transfers of government sector which are obtained directly from the Ministry of Finance (budget data). · Capital transfers and non-produced and nonfinancial assets: The sources for data are BST reports and budget data for EU transfers of government sector. Since 2002, the Bank of Slovenia also estimates the value of write-downs of debt from trade in goods and services abroad. · Direct investment: The main sources of data are monthly and annual reports on capital investment (SN reports) and reports on credits received/granted, deposits, short-term trade credits and advances and other debt instruments with non-residents (KRD reports – previously divided into KRD and SKV reports). From 2005 onwards loans of banking sector are not included. Aggregated data on Slovenian real estate owned by non-residents is reported by Surveying and Mapping Authority of the Republic of Slovenia (quarterly reporting on monthly transactions from 2008 onwards). Data on transactions with real estate in Croatia (purchases and sales) of Slovenian residents has been obtained from the National Bank of Croatia since 2008. · Portfolio investment: The main sources of data are reports on operations in securities provided by the authorized securities market participants (VRP reports) and data provided by the Slovenian Central Securities Clearing Corporation (KDD). Also, data on investments in mutual funds and investments of households in other equity securities are available since 2003. For central bank portfolio investment central bank's accounting department's data is used. SHSS statistics (data on securities holdings in the Eurosystem) have been used since 2015 to cover data gaps. · Financial derivatives: From 2004 until 2006 these types of transactions are included in VRP and KDD sources. Since 2007 quarterly data on financial account's statistics are source for financial derivatives for all sectors except the central bank and banks. The data source for financial derivatives of banks from 2011 onwards are the reports of banks (PORFI). From 2009 onwards financial derivatives of Bank of Slovenia are included in a separate financial derivatives item or international monetary reserves item (depending on the residency of the counterpart). . Other investment: Direct reports (KRD reports) are the sources of data for loans and trade credits and advances as well as deposits and other equity from nonbanking sector. Data for loans, currency and deposits for banking sector are derived from reports provided by banks (PORFI), including stock data in original currencies. Central bank's data are derived directly from accounting department's data. Data on deposits held by households are derived from Bank for International Settlements database and bilateral data within ECB database. Item central bank's currency and deposits contains Intra-Eurosystem technical claims and net liabilities in respect of the export of euro banknotes. Bilateral data from ECB database on loans of households borrowed from the banks abroad are included from 2012 onwards. Financial accounts statistics is the source for insurance, pension, and standardized guarantee schemes. The allocation of SDRs to IMF members is shown as a liability incurred by the recipient under SDRs. · Reserve assets: The source of the data on reserve assets, comprising monetary gold, SDRs, reserve position in the Fund, foreign exchange and financial derivatives are accounting records of Bank of Slovenia. This data is received monthly from Accounting department, which is stored in the Bank of Slovenia's data base and is used also for other instruments in BOP (deposits, loans, securities etc.). 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Goods: Coverage adjustments are made for goods exported and imported without customs declarations and for which data are available from BST reports. Since 1 May 2004, coverage adjustments also include estimated data on imports of motor vehicles from EU by natural persons not covered by Intrastat System. Imports are adjusted for valuation from a c.i.f. basis to an f.o.b. basis by means of coefficients that take account of the share of freight and insurance by type of commodity, means of transport, and country of origin of the goods. Services: Travel services are broken down by countries using data on mobile phone operators. Service charges, implicitly included in premiums on life and nonlife insurance, are estimated at 45 percent of premiums. The remainder is classified in current transfers or represents assets/liabilities of financial account (life insurance). Primary income: Data on compensation of employees receipts are estimated on the basis of Labor Force Survey and data on average gross wages from Eurostat's database. The receipts are geographically allocated to the four neighboring countries. Data on expenditures are obtained from the SORS on the basis of Labour Office register. A coefficient between net and gross wages is applied in order to obtain data on a gross basis (including taxes and contributions). Data on reinvested earnings in the current year are estimated (from a three year monthly average of actual data on total earnings, less extraordinary incomes [the source being annual reports on investments], less dividends and other incomes, paid in the current month [the source being monthly reports on capital investments]). The estimate is replaced by actual data when these data are available. Secondary income: Data from the BST reports are supplemented with data from the Ministry of Finance (for EU transfers of government sector). Secondary income of other sectors are subdivided into personal transfers and other current transfers. Transfers of government sector and other transfers of other sectors are subdivided into current taxes on income and wealth, social contributions, social benefits, net nonlife insurance premiums, nonlife insurance claims and other. European Union transfers of government sector are classified under current and capital transfers according to the goal for which the funds are used. The split was defined in cooperation with the SORS. Capital transfers and nonproduced and nonfinancial assets: Data are obtained from BST reports, but data on capital transfers between the Republic of Slovenia and the EU are obtained directly from the Ministry of Finance (budget data). Since 2002, the Bank of Slovenia also estimates the value of write-downs of debt from trade in goods and services abroad. Cross border transactions in emission allowances are included in the capital account (as purchases and sales of non-produced non-financial assets). Direct investment: Data on direct investment are prepared according to asset/liability principle. Direct investment according to directional principle can be derived from supplement items. From 2001 onwards inter-company debt transactions between affiliated enterprises (10 percent or more capital share) are not recorded as loans, but as direct investment – debt instrument transactions. Debt instruments between financial intermediaries have been excluded. Data between fellow enterprises have been included from 2009 onwards. Since 2005 debt instruments do not include banking sector's assets/liabilities with direct investment relationship (these data are included in other investment item). Portfolio investment: Transactions are divided into assets and liabilities with further breakdown into equity and debt portfolio investment. Equity portfolio investment is subdivided into equity securities (listed/unlisted) and investment fund shares. Financial derivatives: Slovenia collects data on financial derivatives since 2004. Financial derivatives are broken down by sector into Bank of Slovenia, government, banks and other. Central bank is trading currency swaps and data are included since 2009 in international reserves item or financial derivatives (depending on the residency of the counterpart). Other investment: Short-term and long-term trade credits and advances, loans and deposits between affiliated enterprises are included in direct investment while others are included in other investment item. For banking sector all other investment items regardless direct investment relationship are included in other investment item (the source of data does not allow the necessary split). Reserve assets: Changes in reserve assets are calculated in their original currencies as differences in the amounts outstanding. These changes are converted to EUR and USD (and SIT until end of 2006) using average monthly exchange rates. Reserve assets include also financial derivatives (since 2009). Seasonal adjustment: The data are not seasonally adjusted. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Verification and other practices: ITRS source was on 1 April 2014 replaced with monthly reporting of banks on readily available data on payments with the rest of the world. Data are used for quality controls of direct reporting on monthly basis. There are many other complementary data used for verifying data: companies' balance sheets data, profit and loss accounts of companies, press releases of events important for FDI, central clearing and depository institution data. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Data on detailed balance of payments items are available on request, from the above address, free of charge.
