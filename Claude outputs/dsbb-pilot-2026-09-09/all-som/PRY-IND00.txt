 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The IMAEP is a synthetic indicator of the monthly production of a set of economic activities valued in constant prices. The fundamental support for the establishment and elaboration of the IMAEP is provided by the System of Annual National Accounts of Paraguay base 2014, elaborated under the general guidelines of the System of National Accounts 2008 (SNA 2008), as well as the recommendations of the International Monetary Fund contained in the Manual of Quarterly National Accounts - Concepts, Data Sources and Compilation. It should be remembered, that the IMAEP cannot be interpreted as a measurement of the level of GDP, since in the short term there is no information on intermediate consumption (IC), in order to deduct the gross value added (GVA). However, the behavior of the synthetic indicator of production approximates the changes in GDP, if the technical relationship between gross added value and production in the short term is considered constant. The synthetic indicator should only be delivered in an aggregate way, globally, so that the indicator becomes representative. Well, the basic data with which the IMAEP is prepared only provide indirect information on the evolution of production. In this way, the biases in the measurements by economic activities are minimized in the aggregate sum, since it is to be expected that the sum of the biases, inevitable in the short-term series, in different senses will tend to reduce the aggregate bias of the indicator. The coverage and delimitation of the IMAEP for the Paraguayan economy includes 33 branches of economic activity, according to the National Classification of Economic Activities of Paraguay (CNAEP), established according to the International Standard Industrial Classification of all economic activities (ISIC 2009, Rev. 4) of the United Nations. In the selection of source data for estimating the production of the economic branches, aspects such as coverage, the relationship of the indicator with the target aggregate, the timeliness and availability of the data are considered. In this way, in the estimates of production by economic activity, monthly indicators are used, derived from data on gross production or income, which would be the best indirect method for estimating production, since it is strongly correlated with the value added given the assumption of a constant relationship in the short term between production and value added; indicators of apparent consumption of inputs, as is the case in the construction sector. Information sources and methodology of economic activities. For the monthly estimation of each economic activity, economic indicators of volume, value and prices were elaborated. There are three alternatives for obtaining constant values, which will depend on the type of data source to which one has access: direct valuation of quantities, deflation of current values (with an appropriate price index) and extrapolation of base year values, with representative volume indicators. Then the mention of some activities with their respective methodologies and data sources. Farming. The VBP by agricultural products was calculated from the production estimates of the main agricultural items provided by the Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock (MAG) and series of average prices of these same items obtained from the Producer Price Index (IPP). The VBP calculated in annual terms was distributed on a monthly basis using cost structures and considering the sowing and harvesting schedules of each product. Given the importance of Soy in the agricultural production structure and the fact that its production cycle begins and ends in different years, the calculation of the VBP of this product is carried out following the methodology of the work in progress. That is to say, production is not only recorded in the harvest period, but is recorded continuously from sowing as an accumulation of stocks; Once the production process is complete, all accumulated work-in-progress is effectively transformed into stock of finished products. The calculation of the VBP of soybeans is carried out by adding the costs (inputs, salaries paid and consumption of fixed capital) distributed throughout the agricultural season, the exploitation surplus is added to the months in which the harvest is carried out. Manufacturing industry. The estimation of manufacturing output uses two methods, depending on the nature of the data source. On the one hand, for the manufacturing sub-branches whose source of information comes from administrative records, with data on nominal values, the monthly indicator is calculated in nominal terms, to later deflate them by baskets of adequate price indices, obtained from the Producer Price Index. (PPI). On the other hand, the source data from companies, chambers, unions or associations, which provide data on production volume and value. Privileging the construction of monthly physical volume indices, used to extrapolate the constant values of the base year. Electricity and water. The monthly estimation of the generation and distribution of electric energy and drinking water is carried out by constructing physical volume indexes, used to extrapolate the base values in constant terms. The data on these economic activities come directly from the binational companies that generate electricity (ITAIPU and YACYRETA), in addition to the company that distributes electricity throughout the national territory (ANDE), and the state company in charge of supplying drinking water in the country (ESSAP). Publication. The IMAEP is published with a lag of 42 days after the end of the reference month. The data contained in this report are the original IMAEP index, the series without seasonal component, and the cycle-trend series. Harmonization. The desirable situation when compiling the base data for the elaboration of the macroeconomic indicator system is that they come from the same sources of information and are elaborated with the same technical-statistical criteria. However, since short-term indicators are required in a preparation period that describes the economic behavior of the situation, one must resort to more timely data sources, but usually less detailed and exhaustive, than those used in the preparation of CNAs and CNT. This means that the results derived from these sources present differences in their behavior, so that the results of the IMAEP and the CNA and CNT present discrepancies. Thus, in order to maintain coherence between these information systems, a process of harmonization of the figures of these schemes is required, so that the estimates for a given year are the same for these indicators. The harmonization benchmarking method (Denton) is used to harmonize the results. Review of the series. In the elaboration of the IMAEP, data sources are used, giving priority to the opportunity and speed in obtaining and disposing of the same, therefore, on many occasions they are preliminary data. In this way, IMAEP publications incorporate revisions of the published series, basically due to corrections or modifications of the short-term source data used in the preliminary versions of the IMAEP and the closure of the Quarterly and Annual National Accounts System and its revisions, determined by the availability of new data, which in turn requires a review of previous years. The revisions incorporated into the series is a very important practice in the compilation process, since it allows to increase the precision of the estimates. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The Scope of the data The coverage of the estimates is nationwide. The IMAEP comprises 33 branches of economic activity, according to the National Classification of Economic Activities of Paraguay (CNAEP), established according to the International Standard Industrial Classification of all economic activities (ISIC 2009, Rev.4) of the United Nations. Within each of them, the coverage is partial, considering that it aims exclusively at the indirect estimation of the dynamics of the Gross Value Added, if the technical relationship between gross value added and production in the short term is considered constant. In this sense, given the difficulties of the national statistical system, such as the lack of monthly or quarterly economic surveys (manufacturing and services sectors), administrative records are fundamental pillars for monitoring economic activity in the short term. For this reason, special attention has been paid to obtaining, organizing and systematizing the administrative records of sales, in order to create a structured directory that allows obtaining the greatest possible economic coverage per activity, thereby seeking to ensure the representativeness of the sample of companies, not only at the global level of activity, but also within the most relevant sub-branches, within each economic activity. For the creation of directories for each economic branch, the selection criterion of a stratum of companies is followed that represents at least 80% of the value of the economic units observed in those activities, which in some cases reaches 100%. Thus, the main sources of data according to activities are: 1) For agricultural and livestock activity, the main source of data is the agricultural survey carried out by the “Ministerio de Agricultura y Ganadería (MAG)”, and specifically the "Dirección de Censo y Estadísticas Agrícolas” (where you can observe the area cultivated and the yield of it for the zaphra of the different agricultural products), and the survey data from the same entity in conjunction with data received by the “Secretaría Nacional de Calidad y Salud Animal (SENACSA)”, where the work of slaughterhouses in general is calculated. Another major source of data for livestock comes from data provided by the same companies in the sector, where they report the production of other livestock products, such as milk, eggs and poultry and swine meat. 2) For the activities of forestry, fisheries, mining, most industries (including: meat, oils and flour production, dairy products, milling and bakery, sugar, beverages and tobacco, textiles and clothing, leathers and leather goods, wood, paper and paper products, chemicals, non-metallic minerals, common metals, metal products and machinery and equipment) and most services (trade , transport, telecommunications, real estate services, business services, home services and hotels and restaurants) administrative records are used. 3) For the production of dairy, flours and oils and telecommunications, data sent by the companies of those sectors are also used, companies which are among the most important in terms of weight of their respective activities. 4) For financial activities, the data used comes from the balance sheets of the country's banks and financial institutions, which are provided by the Superintendencia de Bancos (SIB), an entity belonging to the Central Bank of Paraguay. For financial activities, the data used comes from the balance sheets of the country's banks and financial institutions, which are provided by the Superintendency of Banks, an entity belonging to the Central Bank of Paraguay. Volume indicators are also used, such as the number of people employed, of given credits, and deposits made, also from the SIB. 5) For government services, data from the “Ministerio de Economía y Finanzas (MEF)” are used, specific to the financial situation table document. It details the egress and income of the central administration of the country. Finally, data from both the country's binationals (Itaipu and Yacyreta) and the energy management company (in this case, the ANDE) are used for electricity and water management activity. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The administrative records are considered main source of data for the compilation of activities. Also, the different information we receive form the companies themselves are used to cross-check all the administrative records, and double check the compilation with different sources. This data is consistent with the tie of recording and is valued at the transaction prices required by the national accounts; the reference period is consistent with the quarterly and annual information. Records do not require adjustments since the fiscal period comprises from January to December. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The schedule data comes on monthly basis. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The data that comes from administrative records follows some quality standards. This includes the minimization of the sample error and data collection errors, procedures that identify the explicit actions to be taken when monitoring the database, such as atypical values to be confirmed and missing values follows improvement techniques which smooth the data to adapt it to the tendency of each activity. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Because the data received is mainly administrative records, it can often be find missing data or irregular values, so first it proceeds to the imputation and smoothing of data based on the well-behaved companies of each sector. If these are exceptional cases of heavily weighted companies, they are consulted on the case at hand. After that, the information has to be revised again and tested for consistency through crosschecking with other sources or estimates. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Another of the statistical processes used is to calculate the gross value of production by the sum of the intermediate consumption. This process applies mainly to some agricultural products (spent on agricultural inputs) and in construction-related activity (where the consumption of inputs such as cement, ceramic materials, rods, among others) is considered. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The validation of the intermediate results is done by the staff of the Short-term Indicators Division, which are also in charge of constructing the quarterly GDP. They assess the original source information, as well as the processed and calculated data carried out by the unit of Economic Indicators and Price Indexes. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Staff involved in collection, processing, and compile the information monitors the developments in the possible errors. Also, the staff always tries to seek appropriated estimation techniques when information is missing or delayed. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs It is expected that the accumulated error of the estimated indicators will be reduced when the aggregation of economic activities is completed. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Since the data is mainly administrative record, the staff gather more information of the companies in later months, which are also included, so the update of the indicators are done monthly and can be made to previous publications. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Revisions are not measured, evaluated, or explained in posts but the database is accessible to users. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished information is not available.
