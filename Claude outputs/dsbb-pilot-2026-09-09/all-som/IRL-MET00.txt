 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Definition: Irish external trade statistics are compiled in accordance with the 'general' system of recording international trade. Under this system imports and exports are recorded at the time the goods are moved across the boundary of the State. In particular, this means that goods imported into a Custom bonded warehouse are recorded at the time of entry into the country and not at the time of their release from the warehouse. Also, agricultural products or other goods exported for storage abroad are included in the value of exports at the time of their departure from the country. International, regional guidelines: The methodology, definitions and classifications for the compilation of statistics on merchandise trade with EU and non-EU countries are laid down in EU legislation. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification - country: Countries are classified according to the EC country nomenclature (Geonomenclature) as set out annually in the Official Journal of the European Union . From January 1999, this follows the United Nations ISO alpha-2 coding system, in which each country is identified by a two letter code. Classification - commodity: Statistics are collected using the 8-digit Combined Nomenclature (CN) - the EU's tariff and statistical nomenclature. This nomenclature is managed and updated annually by the Commission in co-operation with the member states. The CN is aligned with the 6-digit Harmonised Commodity Description and Coding System (HS) - the nomenclature of the Customs Co-operation Council that is used throughout the world for applying customs tariffs and compiling external trade statistics. The CN contains about 9,500 8-digit subheadings, the first six digits of which correspond to the HS code. Scope of the data Geographic coverage: All merchandise trade between Ireland and the rest of the world is included. Exceptions to coverage The following classes of goods are excluded from the trade statistics. Means of payment, which are legal tender, and securities Monetary gold Emergency aid for disaster areas Goods benefiting from diplomatic, consular or similar immunity Goods for and following temporary use, provided all the following conditions are met: No processing is planned or made The expected duration of the temporary use is not longer than 24 months The dispatch/arrival has not to be declared as a delivery/acquisition for VAT purposes Goods used as carriers of information such as floppy disks, computer tapes, films, plans, audio and videotapes, CD-ROMs with stored computer software, where developed to order for a particular client or where they are not the subject of a commercial transaction, as well as complements for a previous delivery, e.g. updates for which the consignee is not invoiced Provided that they are not the subject of a commercial transaction: Advertising material Commercial samples Goods dispatched to national armed forces stationed outside the statistical territory and goods received from another Member State which had been conveyed outside the statistical territory by the national armed forces, as well as goods acquired or disposed of on the statistical territory of a Member State by the armed forces of another Member State which are stationed there Spacecraft launchers on dispatch and on arrival pending launching into space and at the time of launching into space Sales of new means of transport by natural or legal persons liable to VAT to private individuals from other Member States In addition, intra-EU trade statistics exclude: Trade by traders not registered for VAT Trade by private individuals who move goods within the EU Goods dispatched under distance selling arrangements that are below the special VAT distance selling threshold operating in the Member State of destination. 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to unit of account: Valuation is in EURO. For transactions in which the value is expressed in a foreign currency, conversion to EURO is done using the current exchange rate. Valuation: For imports, the CIF value (inclusive of cost, insurance and freight to the point of entry to the State) is used. For exports, the FOB value (free on board) is used. 2.4.2 Recording basis Timing of recording transactions: The calendar month is the basic period for compiling the data. Imports to and exports from non-EU countries are classified to the month in which the relevant customs entry is registered. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Sources: Since the completion of the EU single market at the end of 1992, external trade statistics are a combination of Customs-based non-EU trade statistics and estimates based on the Intrastat Survey (introduced at that time) of Irish traders involved in trade with other EU member states. Type of data collected: For each transaction, traders above the annual value threshold (see 'Estimation Methods' below) are obliged to give details of commodity code, country of origin/destination, country of consignment, mode of transport, nature of transaction, delivery terms, invoice value, statistical value, net mass and supplementary units. Basis of country data - imports: Imports are recorded by country of origin (as opposed to country of consignment or dispatch). Goods obtained or produced in one country are taken as originating in that country. If two or more countries are involved in the production of a commodity, the country of origin is considered to be the one where the last substantial manufacturing process was carried out. Packing, repacking, sorting and blending are not regarded as manufacturing processes. Basis of country data - exports: Exports are classified by country of final destination. The last country to which the goods are specifically directed on their outward movement from the State is regarded as their final destination. This is not necessarily the country in which the goods will be unshipped or that in which they will be finally consumed. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Estimation methods: Trade with EU member states is compiled from the monthly Intrastat Survey which covers traders whose annual imports exceed €750,000. Estimates are inserted for non-respondent traders. VAT returns are used to estimate for traders falling below the thresholds. Trade with non-EU countries is compiled primarily from customs declarations. Price indices: Trade price indices are compiled using a unit price methodology. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Monthly exports, imports, trade surplus and the volume series for exports and imports have been seasonally adjusted using the US Census Bureau X-13 framework as implemented in JDEMETRA+ 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Revisions are included in the latest monthly data, and details of such are included in the data on the CSO Statistical databank PX-Stat. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request A Trade Helpdesk (trade@cso.ie) operates to respond to individual customer data requests, or for data more detailed than that available in the monthly release or on the CSO Databank 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points All releases contain contact details, names and telephone number
