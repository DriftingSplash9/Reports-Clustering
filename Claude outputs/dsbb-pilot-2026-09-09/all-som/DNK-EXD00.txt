 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: The external debt is compiled by Danmarks Nationalbank. The methodology used for the compilation is the sixth edition of the IMF's "Balance of Payments Manual" . Definitions: The external debt is the outstanding liabilities vis-à-vis non-residents. Compared to the international investment position, of which the external debt is a part, the external debt does not include financial liabilities arising from participation rights, i.e. shares, investment fund certificates, equity capital, reinvested earnings and ownership of property. Also, financial derivatives are not included. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification system: Sixth edition of the Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) and the European Central Bank Guidelines as well as the European manual for national accounts (ESA2010) thus in line with SNA2008. Instrument (debt securities, trade credits, loans, other investment). Direct investments are shown separately Sector (monetary authorities, general government, the banking sector and other sectors) Maturity (short term, long term). Scope of the data Geographic coverage: The statistics comprise liabilities of residents denominated in DKK and foreign currency with non-residents. Residents are individuals who are permanently resident in Denmark, as well as companies and institutions domiciled in Denmark. As from July 2000 the Faroe Islands and Greenland are considered to be non-residents for statistical purposes. Institutional coverage: All institutional sectors are covered by the statistics. Data are compiled for the following sectors: Danmarks Nationalbank (reserve assets); General government (central government, local governments and social security funds); Banks; Private companies and households (broken down by Insurance companies and pension funds; other financial companies; non-financial companies; households including non-profit institutions serving households). The sectoral breakdown is performed according to the breakdown described in the European System of Accounts (ESA2010). Comments on special items: Direct investment: According to the definition of external debt, data on direct investments do not include financial claims relating to participation rights, i.e. equity capital and reinvested earnings. Portfolio investment: External debt data on portfolio investment cover debt securities, while equities are not included. Unrecorded activity We don't register unrecorded activity 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: The main valuation principle is market value as far as this information is available. Conversion to base currency: Outstanding positions in foreign currency are converted at the prevailing exchange rates. Reporting may be made either in Danish kroner or in Euro. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Baes on data from a commercial data provider Danmarks Nationalbank gross up to reach total. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs General remarks: Denmark's external debt is compiled as of the end of each quarter, as a part of the international investment position. Both annual and monthly sources are used. Interest costs that have accrued but are not yet payable are not included in the data. Comments on special items: Direct investment: Data are gathered via Danmarks Nationalbank's questionnaire survey of the external accounts of approximately 1200 private enterprises. Portfolio investment: Portfolio investment liabilities are obtained from the Danish securities statistics supplemented by data drawn from the balance sheets of the MFI sector and investment associations, respectively. Other investment: Trade credits and other investment are compiled from the questionnaire survey, whereas loans, currency and deposits are compiled partly from the balance sheets of the MFI sector and partly from the questionnaire survey. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording · The questionnaire forms used in the questionnaire survey are designed by Danmarks Nationalbank and are revised to reflect developments in international investment position standards and accounting standards. · The coverage of questionnaire survey participants is checked annually. The reporting population has been gradually reduced since 1991 using thresholds. There is a population reporting monthly with large transactions and/or stocks and a population reporting annually with small transactions and large stocks. Both populations jointly contribute to compilation of the international investment position. · Each data source is based on the following identity: stock, beginning of period + transactions during period + valuation changes, etc. during period = stock, end of period, where stock data corresponds to the international investment position and transaction data to the financial account of the Balance of Payments. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Monthly reporters must report at 10th working day after end of reference periode Annual reporting must be reported 4, 5 or 6 month after end of year depending of type of entity (follows Danish rules for publishing Annual Financial Report to the Danish Authorities) 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs We do regular perform assessment of the output None
