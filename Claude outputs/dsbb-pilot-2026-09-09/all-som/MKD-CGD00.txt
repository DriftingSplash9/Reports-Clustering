 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Central government debt is the sum of all financial liabilities established by the central government. Central government debt is comprised of external central government debt and domestic central government debt. External central government debt includes external central government debt to multilateral, bilateral and private creditors. Domestic central government debt includes domestically issued structural bonds (issued to address specific structural problems in the economy), regular issuances on the domestic market of Treasury bills and bonds. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization CG debt is broadly aligned with the definition of gross debt in the IMF Public Sector Debt Statistics Guide/GFSM 2014 and disseminated with the requirements of SDDS plus. 2.2.1 Scope CG debt is broadly aligned with the definition of gross debt in the IMF Public Sector Debt Statistics Guide/GFSM 2014 and disseminated with the requirements of SDDS plus. Scope of the data Institutional coverage Central Government debt includes the debt of all budgetary government units. This includes the core central government (ministries, agencies, government departments included in the Budget) as well as the three social securities funds (Employment Fund, Health Fund and Pension Fund) Central Government Debt includes all liabilities in the form of securities and loans. Exceptions to coverage Central government operations data currently excludes a number of entities that are substantially or largely financed from the budget, and which are recognized to be part of the central government sector when applying the definitions and guidance in GFSM 2014. Missing entities include Macedonia Radio Television, Agency for Electronic Communication, Macedonia Railway Infrastructure, Public Hospitals and others. Unrecorded activity Central government debt excludes liabilities in the form of other accounts payable. As government accounts are compiled on a cash rather than an accrual basis, other accounts payable are not available. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation of marketable securities: Central government debt data are disseminated at face value. - Data are presented in millions of national currencies (MKD). Foreign currency-denominated debt is converted into the national currency using the exchange rate prevailing at the end of the reference period. 2.4.2 Recording basis Central government debt data are recorded on a face value, and do not include accrued interest. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Central government debt data is disseminated on a gross basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data source: Data on external central government debt is provided by NBRNM which currently maintains external debt database. Data on domestic central government debt is provided from the Ministry of Finance database. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Source data timeliness is adequate for producing and disseminating Central Government debt within the timeliness required under the SDDS Plus. Data on external debt is provided by the Central Bank every 25th day of the month for the data for the previous month. MOF has near real time data for domestic central government debt, provided by the E-Debt system. A small portion of domestic debt data is provided by Central Securities Depositary. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data is regularly assessed for the purposes of data compilation. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Part of data on domestic central government debt related to structural bonds are cross-checked with the records of Central Security Depository. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses The revisions to the data are typically very small, typically as a result of debt transactions occurring later in the quarter and not appearing in the accounting systems. These are captured before the end of the subsequent quarter and data for the previous quarter is revised if required. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Contact persons for fiscal data are published on the IMF Dissemination Standards Bulletin Board
