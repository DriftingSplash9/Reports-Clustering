 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The Trade Division compiles the merchandise trade data broadly in accordance with the international methodology documented in the United Nations’ International Merchandise Trade Statistics . These data cover Guyana’s imports and exports. Imports are totals of all imports cleared through customs for the local economy. Exports are the exports of domestic producers plus the re-exports of imported goods which had been previously cleared through customs for the local economy. The merchandise trade data are compiled in terms of both the quantity and value of imports and exports. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization For merchandise trade data, classification schemes used are the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS) 2007 and the Standard International Trade Classification (SITC). Scope of the data Merchandise trade data covers exports and imports of all goods passing through the official customs channel. Exports include re-exports of imported goods. Exceptions to coverage Guyana uses the “Special System of Trade.” This means only the goods that cross the customs’ border are recorded. Goods in warehouses are recorded, but not counted as part of merchandise trade flows until cleared by the customs. Unrecorded activity A limited volume of goods can go unrecorded owing to underinvoicing or undeclared shopping across the border. 2.4.1 Valuation All quantities and values are based on the declaration of importers and exporters, and are subject to verification by customs officials. Values are shown in Guyana dollars (G$). Imports are valued at C.I.F. (Cost, Insurance and Freight); and exports of domestic and foreign goods (re-exports) are valued at F.O.B. (Free on Board). 2.4.2 Recording basis Trade flows are valued at the customs border for the actual quantities invoiced. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Merchandise trade data are compiled from the source data primarily obtained from the Customs Department of the Guyana Revenue Authority. For key export commodities—namely, sugar, rice, bauxite, gold, diamonds and forestry, and for a key import commodity—namely, fuel, a monthly administrative survey of the trading companies is done.
