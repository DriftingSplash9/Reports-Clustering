 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The concepts and definitions used by the BanRep for compiling monetary statistics follow the MFSM in terms of identifying institutional units and valuation financial assets. The departures in terms of concepts and definitions, coverage, classification/sectorization, concept of residency, accrual accounting, and presentation of gross assets and liabilities are explained below. The depository corporations survey (DCS) is built by consolidating the analytical accounts of the BanRep and other depository corporations (ODCs) in Colombia and generally follows the structure that is in accordance with the MFSM. In 2023 the IMF's Statistics Department (STA) conducted a TA mission for Colombia to improve the compilation of its monetary and financial statistics (MFS). The mission worked with officials of the BanRep to revise the compilation framework of monetary statistics for the Central Bank (CB), other depository corporations (ODCs), and other financial corporations (OFCs) in accordance with the methodology set out in the Monetary and Financial Statistics Manual and Compilation Guide 2016 (MFSMCG 2016). BanRep is implementing TA recommendations during 2024 and revising historical data (back to 2018). For that reason, there will be some changes compared to data released before November, 2024 and some transitory discrepancies might arise when comparing different statistical publications (i.e. SR submissions to IMF vs data on BanRep's website or monthly vs weekly non-sectorized data). As of November 2024 (information with data as of August 2024), the variables and definitions of the sectorization report of the other depositary corporations (ODC) were adjusted, following the guidelines of the IMF Monetary and Financial Statistics Manual of 2016. The most important change was the definition of broad money. M2 is the money aggregate that meets the 2016 MFSM guidelines for broad money definition. M2 definition was adjusted to exclude CDs with a maturity longer than 18 months and savings accounts earmarked for housing (AFC accounts, by its acronym in Spanish), and to include some non-transferable deposits issued by depository corporations, as well as some deposits (transferable and non-transferable) issued by the central bank. Previously, M3 was the money aggregate considered as broad money. However, M3 includes a variety of instruments that do not meet 2016 MFSM requirements. In order to reduce the impact of the methodological change for the users of monetary statistics, BanRep will continue producing and publishing M3 on its website, and it will be clear for the users than it does not meet the latest IMF guidelines. The definition of M1 was also adjusted to include savings accounts and other deposits commonly used to make payments as transferable deposits. The series were reprocessed since January 2018. In addition, for main deposits, CDs and loans, the sectorization of some entities was revised using alternate sources to the Form 338 (balance sheets and information from the financial accounts statistics). This was due to the fact that the Form 338 had a static list of public and financial entities that changed significantly with the implementation of the new Form 409 in May of 2022. The major changes occurred in the following sectors: other financial corporations, non-financial public corporations, other non-financial corporations and other residents. The sectorization of the balance sheet of trusts funds, which in the Form 338 were included in other non-financial corporations and other residents, has not yet been revised (as better information becomes available, these operations could be sectorized). The BanRep has posted on its website the metadata on the compilation procedures from the MFSM that applied until October 2024 release and will update the documentation with the new methodology. Temporarily, the data presented in the depository corporations survey may present discrepancies with those of other BanRep's statistical publications while the methodological changes are implemented. Based on the guidelines of the MFSM, sectorized monetary and credit aggregates in standardized forms meet the following definitions: M1, comprising currency held by the public and transferable deposits (saving deposits, checking accounts and deposits otherwise commonly used to make payments) held by state and local governments, nonfinancial corporations (both public and private), and other financial corporations and other resident sectors in the depository corporations (DCs). Broad money (M2), comprising M1, nontransferable deposits, certificates of deposit issued by DCs (with a maturity equal or less than 18 months at the date of issuance) and some deposits (transferable and non-transferable) issued by the central bank, held by the subsectors mentioned above. M2 is the money aggregate that meets the guidelines for broad money definition. Net assets vis-à-vis National Government. Gross assets vis-à-vis the rest of the public sector (local governments and public non-financial corporations). Gross assets vis-à-vis Other Financial Corporations (both public and private). Gross assets vis-à-vis private sector (nonfinancial corporations, households and non-profit institutions that serve households). Net Assets vis-à-vis non-residents sector. The BanRep also publishes weekly and monthly non-sectorized monetary and credit aggregates, with a shorter dissemination lag, calculated using a weekly report that summarizes the main accounts of the balance sheets. Since those aggregates are not sectorized, they depart from the recommendations of the MFSM. Those aggregates include the deposits of some entities of the central government (although national treasury holdings are excluded), non-residents and other depository corporations 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The principles underlying the classification of financial instruments in the monetary statistics are broadly consistent with the MFSM recommendations. The financial instruments in the balance sheets are classified according to degree of liquidity and the legal characteristics describing the underlying relationship between creditors and debtors. Assets and liabilities are classified according to the institutional sector of the counterparts. This classification of financial instruments is the same in the BanRep and ODCs since all financial corporations follow the same chart of accounts. Scope of the data The scope of the data (which refers to the Depositary Corporation Survey (DCS) for the purposes of this exercise) is BanRep broadly consistent with the guidelines outlined in the MFSM. The Depositary Corporations in Colombia, as of October of 2024, consists of: 1. Central bank: Banco de la República 2. Other Depository Corporations (ODCs), comprising commercial banks, financial corporations, commercial financing companies, financial cooperatives and 3 special financial institutions (Bancoldex, Findeter and Financiera de Desarrollo Nacional, the last one, since October 2020), which are under the supervision of Financial Superintendency of Colombia. Exceptions to coverage As of now, other financial intermediaries are not included in the monetary statistics, such as pension funds, trusts, investment funds, insurance corporations, capitalization societies, mutual funds. 2.4.1 Valuation The BanRep values monetary gold holdings monthly using the price of gold in the international market at the BanRep balance sheet closing date. The BanRep and the rest of the financial corporations value foreign-currency-denominated instruments at the midpoint market exchange rate prevailing on the balance sheet closing date. 2.4.2 Recording basis International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) constitute the base accounting model or reference applicable to all financial corporations. For the central bank, IFRS it is applicable in those aspects that are not contrary to its special regime, as described in Law 31/1992 and in its bylaws. Consequently, as per Resolution 037/2017 and its amendments, the Bank must apply the regulatory technical framework outlined in the attachment to National Decree 2420/2015 and its amendments, except in the following instances: If it is contrary to the Bank's special regime, as per Law 31/1992, and its bylaws issued through Decree 2520/1993 and amended by Decree 2386 of December 11, 2015, particularly with respect to: i) accounting treatment of the exchange adjustment for foreign reserves caused by fluctuations in the peso exchange rate for the currencies in which those reserves are represented (Article 62, Numeral 4 of the bylaws, as amended by Article 2 of Decree 2386/2015) and ii) the accounting treatment of issued metallic currency (Article 62, Literal b, Numeral 1 of the bylaws). Treatment of the loan portfolio and its detriment for which the Bank applies the special technical standards, interpretations and guidelines on accounting and financial information issued by the Office of the Financial Superintendent of Colombia. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures In line with the general principle of the MFSM, assets and liabilities of financial corporations are collected and compiled on a gross basis. In the case of ODCs, data are reported on a gross basis in the balance sheet. In the presentation of the Depositary Corporation Survey (DCS), the assets vis-à-vis the National Government and vis-à-vis the foreign sector are presented on a net basis. The assets vis-à-vis the rest of the public sector, vis-à-vis other financial corporations and vis-à-vis private sector are presented on a gross basis. The positions between the BanRep and ODCs and claims between ODCs are netted out. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The source for compiling the CBS is the general balance sheet of the BanRep prepared by the Accounting Department. The monthly monetary balance sheet produced by the BANREP is used to generate the sectoral balance sheet of the BanRep and the CBS on a monthly basis. The source for compiling the ODCS and the sectoral balance sheets of the ODCs is the balance sheets of the financial corporations that follow the same chart of accounts, as well as additional information on the sectorization of major accounts in Format 409 SFC (up to April 2022, format 338, SFC) and the detail of the composition of portfolio investments (format 351 SFC). Those sources from ODCS are provided by the financial corporations to the SFC, which in turns shares them to the BanRep, under the terms agreed on between the SFC and the BanRep. Once these sources are available in the system area, the BanRep produces the Depository Corporations survey on a monthly basis. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The BanRep makes ongoing efforts to use source data that reasonably approximate the definitions, scope, classification, valuation, and time of recording to compile sound monetary statistics. The sources data for the CBS provide sufficient information to derive the accounts according to the recommendations of the MFSM . Even though the chart of accounts does not contain the residency criterion or sectorization, the access to the accounting records of the BanRep, and the supplementary reports for ODCs allows the production of the monetary balance sheet with all the necessary information. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The data collection system allows compilation of monetary statistics, which are released in the website with a lag of approximately two months. The BanRep compiles its own sectoral balance sheet on a monthly basis based on daily data with a lag of approximately two weeks. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The source data for the ODCS are routinely analyzed for underreporting/misreporting. The SFC has automated procedures that facilitate the monitoring of the accuracy of data reported by individual financial corporations. The monitoring system checks the internal consistency of each corporation's data and identifies out-of-trend movements. Any problems with the data are confirmed with the reporting corporation, and records are kept with the returns. Once the data are sent to the BanRep, the BanRep also runs automated procedures to validate the information a second time. A more stringent check is done by the BanRep to the additional sectorized information. The results of the validation are analyzed by the BanRep, and measures are taken to make any needed corrections. The validations include searches for new accounts, use of inexistent accounts, trends, incorrect use of negative signs or currency, unusual values for data and consistency with other sources of information. In case of further questions on the returns, the BanRep routinely contacts the financial corporations directly and keeps the SFC informed. The ODCs are requested to provide explanations and, when needed, to resubmit the data with corrections to the SFC. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques As BanRep's statistics are mainly based on balance sheet data and the supplementary formats (Format 409 and Format 351 of the SFC's) prepared by ODCs, they do not, by and large, rely on the use of statistical techniques. However, for the period January 2018 to April 2022 (and by the November 2024 release) : · For main deposits, CDs and loans, the sectorization of some entities was revised using alternate sources to the Form 338 (balance sheets and information from the financial accounts000 statistics). The major changes occurred in the following sectors: other financial corporations, non-financial public corporations, other non-financial corporations and other residents. · The sectorization of the balance sheet of trusts funds, which in the Form 338 were included in other non-financial corporations and other residents, has not yet been revised (as better information becomes available, these operations could be sectorized). 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The BanRep does not use any other statistical procedure/technique in its monetary statistics. When the actual data do not approximate the guidelines of the MFSM, the BanRep does not use any specific procedure to adjust data. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The BanRep routinely uses other information to validate balance sheet data reported by DCs. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The BanRep frequently investigates statistical discrepancies and determines major factors that might contribute to them. It compares BanRep's data with the reported data for the ODCs to ensure that the interbank positions are insignificant. The interbank position for current periods shows an acceptable level of discrepancy, which can be explained by the different time of recording of the transactions. When a large discrepancy is noted, the BanRep contacts the financial corporation to seek an explanation and requests that corrected data be submitted to the SFC. The policy of BanRep is not to change any reported data until revised data are received. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The FSS offers, on request by users inside and outside the BanRep, timely and knowledgeable assistance on the information published. Contact points for monetary statistics by e-mail are specifically identified on the BanRep's website and the DSBB. Assistance to users is monitored regularly to ensure that prompt responses are sent to queries. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The BanRep has a catalog of publications listing all bulletins, reports, essays, and drafts. All BanRep publications available electronically are free of charge.
