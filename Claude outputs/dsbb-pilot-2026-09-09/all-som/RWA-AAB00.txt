 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Rwanda’s monetary survey is compiled monthly in accordance with the analytical frameworks defined by the IMF in “Monetary and Financial Statistics Compilation Guide,” (2008). The monetary survey is a consolidation of the surveys of the central bank (National Bank of Rwanda— BNR ) and all commercial banks and microfinance banks. The data are compiled in terms of stocks, in millions of Rwanda francs (RWF). The monetary survey is published the BNR’s Annual Report; it is also posted on the BNR website ( http://www.bnr.rw/ ). A separate statement is compiled for the Development Bank (BRD) which does not collect deposit but not published. The BNR’s Statistics Department gathers the source data for compiling the monetary statistics using (i) Monthly return from other depository corporations submitted to the BNR monthly, and (ii) BNR balance sheet. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification of financial instruments and the sectorization of financial and non-financial institutions is based on the methodology of the 1993 System of National Accounts (SNA 1993) and the 2000 Monetary and Financial Statistics Manual (MFSM) respectively which will be replaced by Monetary and financial statistics Manual and compilation guide 2016 (MFSMCG 2016). External positions are distinguished from internal positions using the residency criterion, as defined in the 6th edition of the Balance of Payments Manual . Scope of the data Transaction Coverage All operations recorded on the balance sheets of the depository institutions are covered by the monetary survey. Off-balance-sheet operations are not included. Geographic Coverage The monetary survey covers the entire national territory for the balance sheet operations of depository institutions. Unit Coverage Central bank: the BNR . Other depository Corporations: this subcategory includes the 15 deposit money banks currently doing business in Rwanda. The Treasury is not classified among depository corporations, but rather under central government. At present, demand deposits with the Postal Checking Center ( CCP ) are not included in the monetary statistics. The money and credit aggregates are derived from the Monetary Survey, which is a consolidation of the National Bank of Rwanda ( BNR ) Survey and the other depository institutions’ survey. The BNR compiles the monetary survey in accordance with the 1993 System of National Accounts (SNA 1993) , and the 2000 Monetary and Financial Statistics Manual (MFSM) . The monetary survey is published in the BNR’s Annual Report; it is also posted on the BNR website ( http://www.bnr.rw ). The aggregates are expressed gross in millions of Rwanda francs, unless otherwise indicated. The monetary survey includes 3 cumulative money aggregates: Narrow money (M1): the narrowest of the money aggregates. It includes currency in circulation (banknotes and coins outside banks) and the demand deposits in Rwanda francs of resident economic agents with the BNR or with other depository corporations. Broad money (M2): Includes, in addition to M1, term and saving deposits in Rwandan Francs of resident economic agents with BNR or other depository corporations. Restricted deposits are excluded from this definition. Broadly defined money supply (M3): This aggregate includes, in addition to narrow money (M2), foreign exchange deposits of resident economic agents with the BNR and other depository corporations. The counterparts for the money supply are: Net foreign assets: Net foreign assets represent the sum of the BNR’s gross claims on nonresidents and the gross claims of other depository corporations on nonresidents, minus the liabilities of these two categories of financial institutions vis-à-vis nonresidents. The residence criterion used to determine these foreign assets and liabilities is consistent with the definition used in the 6th edition of the Balance of Payments Manual . The data expressed gross (gross foreign assets and gross foreign liabilities) are also disseminated. Domestic credit includes: Net claims on government: These represent the difference between the monetary loans to the government by the banking system and the government’s deposits with that system. The data expressed gross (gross claims on government and gross liabilities to government) are also disseminated. Credit to the economy: This heading represents all credit extended by depository institutions to other sectors of the national economy with the exception of the central and local governments and other depository corporations. Other items (net): The difference between the other assets (net) and other liabilities (net) items for the BNR and the other depository institutions. Unrecorded activity The activities of non-bank financial institutions (insurance companies) are not yet recorded in the monetary survey. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation rules: The accounting systems of the BNR (except for operations relating to its special missions) and of the other depository institutions (from which the money and credit data are derived) are maintained in accordance with the standards of the International Accounting Standards Board (IASB), in particular as regards valuation methods. Valuation of financial instruments in foreign exchange: Financial instruments in foreign exchange (including assets and liabilities in SDRs) are converted into national currency terms using the exchange rate in effect on the date of compilation of the monetary statistics. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: Operations are recorded when ownership of financial assets is transferred (accrual basis accounting). Provisions: Provisions are established by applying the prudential principle whereby all risks and costs arising during the current fiscal year or those in respect of a previous fiscal year are recorded among costs for the respective fiscal years to which they accrue. Depository institutions establish provisions to at least 20 percent of doubtful assets, 50 percent for litigious assets, and 100 percent for disputed assets. Doubtful assets: Represent those assets whose collection or recovery in their entirety is uncertain and which are held against enterprises experiencing financial difficulties or other problems that may jeopardize their viability. This category also includes claims wherein interest and principal payments are overdue by 90 days to less than 180 days, as well as any other asset that is outstanding and is not settled for the same period. Litigious assets: Assets whose recovery or collection is in jeopardy and which are held in enterprises whose position is such that a high degree of losses may be foreseen or which are more serious than the characteristics of doubtful assets. This category must also include claims in respect of which interest and principal payments are overdue by 180 days to less than 360 days, as well as any other asset that is outstanding and is not settled for the same period. Disputed assets: These assets include all assets subject to legal action, as well as claims in respect of which interest and principal payments are overdue by 360 days or more, as well as any other asset outstanding and not settled for the same period. In the monetary survey, credits are valued gross (i.e., the value of credits is not adjusted to reflect anticipated losses). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources: Central bank: most the data in the BNR survey come from the accounting database of the central bank. With the introduction of a new core banking software in April 2014, BNR uses administrative data for currency in circulation and banks’ reserves. Other depository corporations: the data on the other depository corporations are gathered using a new form known as the “Monthly Accounting Statement, Model 3000” (Monthly Return), which is completed on the basis of their respective accounting systems. This new form is the result of the revision of the bank chart of accounts completed in 2004 and of the old form entitled “Monthly Accounting Statement, Model 2000 bis” already revised in 2000/2001 in order to take into account the requirements of the MFSM 2000 implementation. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Administrative data are used for currency out of BNR and Banks deposits at BNR since the introduction of a new core banking software ERP-T24 in 2014. The counterpart adjustments are done in the miscellaneous items. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The compilation of the other corporations’ survey is done using a mini application that automatically processes the monthly returns submitted by the ODCs. To this end, all depository institutions are required to submit their accounting statement monthly in soft copy in accordance with the provisions of Law No. 08/99 of June 18, 1999 regulating banks and other financial institutions (Articles 26, 27, and 28). The regulatory deadline for submitting the banks’ monthly statements is within 10 days following the reference date concerned. The statement of Rwanda’s central bank is automatically calculated on the basis of the detailed accounting statement of the BNR available on its computer network, but the compilation of the analytical survey and its consolidation with the other corporations’ survey are done manual. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The intermediate data are assessed against other records like the domestic public debt, the stock of outstanding interbank transactions and the foreign exchange cash flow plan of BNR to ensure accuracy 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The statistical outputs is assessed against each other (BNR against ODCs). Outstanding domestic credit is checked against the outstanding sectoral distribution of credit by sector of economic activity. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions: To date, whenever revisions or corrections have proven necessary, they have been made with effect from the date to which they pertain. In the monetary statistics published on the BNR website, a comment indicating the previous data entry is inserted in the cell which has been changed. In the quarterly bulletin, however, there is no mention yet of the modification made.
