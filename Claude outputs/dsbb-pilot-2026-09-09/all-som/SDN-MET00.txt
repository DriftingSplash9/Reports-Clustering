 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Trade statistics are also compiled by the CBS based on data furnished by the Customs Authorities. These data are compiled and classified according to the Harmonized System. Prior to 1996, the Standard International Trade Classification (SITC, Rev. 3) was the principal trade classification. The CBS trade data are published annually in the CBS publication Foreign Trade Statistics Summary . However, the data are disseminated with a time lag and, therefore, not immediately available for short-term statistical and policy analysis. Data are published in dinars. The Customs Authorities compile trade statistics according to the Automated System for Customs Data and Management (ASYCUDA) and classify goods following the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System, 2000 (HS) of the Customs Co-operation Council, with the eighth and tenth digit extensions for national purposes. Scope of the data The statistics cover all exports and imports registered by the customs offices at Port Sudan, Juba, Wadi Halfa, Kassala, Elgenina, Bassunda, Elkurmuk, Geesan, Maleet, Lokodi, and Elgalabat. They also include exports and imports channeled through Khartoum Airport and Khartoum Post office. The CBS publication also includes partner country data for imports and exports. Exceptions to coverage Excluded from the statistics are personal effects, diplomatic goods, goods in transit, and temporary imports. Unrecorded activity No estimate is made for smuggling. 2.4.1 Valuation Most imports are valued at cost and freight (C&F) basis because insurance on imports are paid locally. The Customs Authorities adjust the import value to C.I.F. basis by adding 2 percent of the C&F value on account of insurance. Exports are valued at free on board (FOB) basis. Values expressed in foreign currencies are converted into Sudanese Dinar (SD) using the official exchange rate as of the date the goods are assessed for clearance. 2.4.2 Recording basis Trade data are recorded on “special trade” basis. The data are recorded as of the dates the Customs Authorities clear the respective documents. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data on the merchandise trade of Sudan are compiled by the Bank of Sudan (BOS) and the Central Bureau of Statistics (CBS). Both data compilations are based on the same source, the records of the Customs Authorities. The Statistics Department (SD), which is one of two departments comprising the General Administration for Economic Research and Statistics at the BOS, compiles the trade data. The data are compiled based on customs records of physical import and export of goods across Sudan’s borders. The Customs Authorities send copies of the forms of cleared exports and floppy disks containing information on cleared imports to the BOS and the Central Bureau of Statistics (CBS). At the end of the month, all data are loaded into the database, validated, and processed again using Eurotrace software. The Customs Authorities also send to the BOS a separate report containing imports by concession companies, under personal effects, temporary imports, and imports under grants, donations, etc. Total exports and imports are disseminated as well as detailed components. The components are classified by magnitude and not according to international classification schemes. One of the main uses of the trade data by the BOS is input into the balance of payments.
