 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Concept: The methodology for compiling and classifying international reserves conforms with the IMF's Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual ( BPM6 ) and International Reserves and Foreign Currency Liquidity—Guidelines for a Data Template . Definition: The definition of international reserve assets follows the methodology of the BPM6. Gross international reserves comprise external assets that are readily available to and controlled by monetary authorities for meeting balance of payments financing needs, for intervention in exchange markets to affect the currency exchange rate, and for other related purposes. Net international reserves are defined as gross international reserves less gross foreign liabilities. Gross foreign liabilities include: use of IMF credit, SDR allocation, liabilities to non-resident banks, other liabilities to non-residents in convertible and national currencies. Relationship to international guidelines: Concept, definition, and classification of international reserves in Armenia fully agree with those outlined in the BPM6 and Guidelines for a Data Template . 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: Gross international reserves are broken down as follows: Gold SDR holdings in the IMF Reserve position in the IMF Foreign exchange Gross foreign liabilities include: Use of IMF credit SDR allocation Liabilities to non-resident banks Other liabilities to non-residents in convertible and national currencies. 2.2.1 Scope Classification: Gross international reserves are broken down as follows: Gold SDR holdings in the IMF Reserve position in the IMF Foreign exchange Gross foreign liabilities include: Use of IMF credit SDR allocation Liabilities to non-resident banks Other liabilities to non-residents in convertible and national currencies. Scope of the data Institutional coverage: Data on official international reserves are compiled based on the balance sheet of the Central Bank of Armenia. Instrument coverage: Detailed breakdown of instruments: Gold – consist of standard gold bullion (monetary gold) owned by the Central Bank (these must be at least 995/1000 pure), gold in metal accounts at foreign prime banks, as well as gold deposits at nonresident financial corporations. Gold is treated as a financial instrument because of its historical role in the international financial system and high liquidity in the international markets. SDR holdings – are reserve assets created by the IMF and allocated to member countries to supplement existing reserves. SDR holdings represent assured and unconditional rights to obtain freely usable currency. Reserve position in the IMF – is expressed in SDR and equals to the value by which the quota of the country exceeds the IMF holdings of national currency that is not related to purchase of reserve asset. Other foreign currency assets comprise several financial assets, which are included in international reserves depending on the possibility of their use by the Central Bank: Cash foreign currency – consist of foreign notes and coins held by the Central Bank that are in circulation in one or several foreign countries and are legally used for making payments. They may also be withdrawn or be under withdrawal but available for exchange by banknotes in circulation. Cash foreign currency owned is a claim on the issuer country or central bank. Correspondent accounts in nonresident banks – comprise the balances of Central Bank correspondent accounts in foreign exchange in foreign prime banks. Deposits – comprise all deposits of the Central Bank in foreign prime financial corporations (including banks). Securities – foreign reserves include only high liquid bonds and shares issued by nonresidents and consist of: (i) promissory notes and transferable promissory notes in foreign currency, (ii) bonds issued or warranted by governments of other countries, Central Banks or other prime banks or financial corporations. Trust accounts with non-resident financial organizations – only accounts with prime financial asset managers are included in international reserves. Repo agreements with nonresident financial corporations, as well as trust operations with these corporations are also included in foreign reserves, if Repo securities are liquid enough and assets handed over to trust management can be used by the Central Bank for making payments after a very short period of time upon request. Loans – only short-term loans to and used overdrafts of foreign prime banks and non-bank corporations in foreign exchange (due for payment upon request) are included in foreign reserves. Other external assets - this category covers short-term intermediate accounts and other short-term receivables of the CBA from nonresidents. Financial derivatives coverage: Only financial derivatives in convertible currencies are included in international reserves. Net marked-to-market value of financial derivatives is reflected in international reserves. Exceptions to coverage All other non-standard gold the Central Bank own is not monetary gold and should be classified as non-financial asset. Holdings of other precious metal standard and non-standard bullions are also treated as non-financial assets and are excluded from international reserves. Withdrawn notes and coins, as well as commemorative coins of all types of foreign countries are not included in international reserves. Restricted deposits and deposits with limitations of the Central Bank in foreign prime financial corporations (including banks) are not included in international reserves. Long-term loans to and receivables from nonresidents are not included in international reserves. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: International reserve assets are valued at prevailing market rates, except for deposits (including correspondent accounts) and loans, which are valued at nominal value of contracts. Conversion rates: International reserve assets are converted into Armenian dram daily at average market exchange rates announced by the CBA, which are based on market rates. 2.4.2 Recording basis Frequency of calculation and revaluation: International reserves are calculated daily on an accrual basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures International reserves are compiled on both gross and net basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources: Data on official international reserves are compiled based on the CBA balance sheet. Data on the reserves template are compiled based on the CBA balance sheet and selected information from the government authorities. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data generally meet the internationally accepted practices. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The source data are available timely to meet recommended standards of timeliness and periodicity for dissemination of the monetary statistics. The CBA balance sheet is available daily. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The source data are analyzed for accuracy by Statistics and Accounting Departments. The software program for generating monetary statistics offers the possibility of data cross-checking. The data are reviewed by the Internal Audit Department of the CBA on a regular basis. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Statistical adjustments: No statistical adjustments are made. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Accuracy of daily changes of each account is verified by individual transactions. In addition, the CBA balance sheet data are checked between the Statistics and Accounting Departments. Data are subject to both, internal and external audit on a regular basis. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Data are regularly monitored to investigate statistical discrepancies and to ensure data accuracy. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The CBA is committed to investigate any significant discrepancies that may be found. When relatively large fluctuations occur in the accounts due to random effects, they are thoroughly explained in statistical publications. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses When data are revised, current revisions are in the footnotes and comments to the tables. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Additional (non-confidential) information is available upon request. Users can request additional information either by contacting the contact point or the Public Relations Service of the CBA, which will forward the inquiries to the relevant department for response. The contact points are available on the CBA website and in the publications for inquiries. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Contact information (e-mail addresses and telephone numbers) are available on the CBA website. Assistance and support are provided to users, who can pose queries by contacting the contact point or the Public Relations Service of the CBA, which will forward the inquiries to the relevant department. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The CBA website provides users with considerable amount of information on publications and documents.
