 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: The compilation standard follows the 2008 System of National Accounts (SNA). The Bank of Korea (BOK) completed the transition from the fixed weighted method to the chained weighted method in March, 2009. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification systems: The industrial classification used for production data is the KSIC, a derivative of the ISIC, Rev. 4. Final consumption expenditures are classified by purpose at the two-digit level – household consumption by COICOP, NPISHs by COPNI and general government by COFOG. International transactions are classified in accordance with the SKTC, a derivative of the SITC, Rev. 4. Scope of the data Transaction coverage: The production boundary is mainly in accordance with the 2008 SNA. Geographical coverage: All regions associated with the Republic of Korea. Exceptions to coverage None Unrecorded activity Unrecorded activities: Illegal economic activities are not included due to a lack of data. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: GDP is basically recorded at market prices. Output and expenditure are valued at basic prices and purchasers' prices, respectively. Exports and imports are valued at f.o.b. prices. Imputed services are valued on an opportunity cost basis. Non-market services are valued at cost of production. GDP components are estimated at current prices and chained 2020 year prices. Income accounts are estimated at current prices only. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: GDP components are recorded on an accrual basis. Some taxes and government expenditures recorded on a cash basis are adjusted to an accrual basis. Imports and exports are recorded when change of ownership takes place. Recording period: GDP and GNI statistics are compiled for every calendar quarter. Integrated national economic accounts such as income accounts, capital accounts and the rest of world accounts are compiled annually. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Most of values are recorded according to 2008 SNA . 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The most comprehensive sources are the economic censuses conducted by the Ministry of Data and Statistics (MODS) every five years and the input-output tables compiled annually by the Bank of Korea. For the annual source data, annual surveys undertaken by the MODS are used. For more detailed data not provided by the MODS, administrative data from the National Tax Service (NTS) are used. Data from related associations are used at the detailed sub-level. The Financial Statement Analysis undertaken by the BOK is used for compilation of mining, quarrying, manufacturing, constructions, and the hotel, restaurant, wholesale and retail trade figures. The quarterly indicators are closely aligned to the data sources and methodology used for the annual accounts. The specific data sources for the different industries are as follows: Agriculture: Comprehensive quarterly data on output and intermediate consumption are available from the MODS, and the relevant agencies. Fishing: Output data on the fishing industry are available from the MODS. The survey covers all types of fishing activities by households, cooperatives and enterprises. Mining, quarrying and manufacturing: An index of industrial production is available on each of 490 representative products from the monthly survey by the MODS. The survey reports the quantities produced and the inventories for finished goods and these are converted to indices by appropriate methods. Production data for the mining and quarrying industry are also available from the relevant ministry. Electricity, gas and water supply: Quarterly data for electricity are obtained from the financial statements of the Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO) and reports from the Korea Power Exchange (KPX). For those firms that generate electricity for their own final use, quantitative data are gathered quarterly. For the gas and water supply, quarterly figures for sales and quantities are obtained directly from the companies. Construction: Monthly and quarterly data on building construction and civil engineering, such as the value of construction completed and building construction permits, are available from the MODS and the relevant ministry. Data on civil engineering are also obtained from the financial statements of the relevant government agencies and corporations. Wholesale and retail trade: Data on sales are available from the monthly Current Wholesale and Retail Trade Survey by the MODS. Finance and insurance: Quarterly financial statements are collected from companies in this industry. Transportation and storage: Quarterly data on sales values are collected either from the enterprises involved such as the Korea National Railway, or from trade organizations. Other services: Monthly data are available from the Service Industry Activity Index by the MODS. For some sub-industries, the BOK conducts quarterly surveys or uses the data from the related associations. General government: Government budget plans and reports are used for the estimation of government services. Taxes less subsidies on products: Annual data are available from the government administrative records. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Most source data are appropriate for the compilation of national accounts. If necessary, BOK makes some adjustments to inconsistent data. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Most source data are collected timely for the compilation and dissemination of national accounts statistics. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Annual chain-linking method (chained weighted method) for the compilation of real GDP is adopted. The chained Laspeyres and chained Paasche indexes are applied for volume and price measure, respectively. Annual overlap method is used for the compilation of quarterly real GDP. GDP in Korea is compiled primarily taking the production approach, because production data are more accurate and more readily available than those for expenditures and incomes. Production approach: Value added at current prices is calculated by subtracting intermediate input at current prices from output at current prices. When current-price intermediate input cannot be obtained, current-price value added is calculated using the ratio of value added derived from basic data such as those of the Financial Statement Analysis by the BOK. Value added at constant prices is mainly obtained using the double deflation method. In some industries, where current-price output data are not available for quarterly compilation, real value added is estimated using the increase rate of volume indices. Financial intermediation services indirectly measured (FISIM) are calculated in the financial intermediation industry and allocated to each industry and final expenditure sector. Agriculture, forestry & fishing: Quarterly output is estimated using the relevant indicators from the MODS and related institutions. Value added is estimated by deducting intermediate consumption from output. Mining, quarrying and manufacturing: Quarterly output is estimated using related indicators such as indices of industrial production. Current-price value added is estimated by multiplying the output by the derived value-added ratio. Electricity, gas and water supply, finance and insurance, Transportion and storage, Information & communication: Output and intermediate consumption are obtained from the financial statements of the relevant enterprises and government agencies. Value added is estimated by deducting intermediate consumption from output. Construction: Quarterly output of building construction is estimated using the data on the area of new buildings. Quarterly output of civil engineering is estimated from the financial statements of the relevant government agencies and corporations. Wholesale and retail trade: Quarterly output is derived by applying the mark-up ratio to the output of each agricultural, manufactured or imported good. Current mark-up ratio is derived using the data from the NTS. Current value added is estimated by applying the derived value added ratio to the output. Government services: Current price value added is estimated based on the production cost approach. Intermediate consumption, compensation of employees and indirect taxes on production are obtained by reclassifying the related items in the budget by kind of economic activity. Other services: Quarterly output is obtained using sales data surveyed by the BOK and the MODS. Quarterly value added is estimated using the value added ratio from the previous year. The final annual results are derived from the detailed data from the NTS and the MODS. Expenditure approach: Final consumption expenditures of households are derived from the consumption data and the survey of the MODS, the relevant ministries and related associations. Government final consumption expenditure is calculated as the sum of value added and intermediate consumption, less the sales of goods and services of the government. Annual amounts of final consumption and sales of goods and services of the government are estimated by reclassifying the annual government budget. Quarterly estimates of final consumption expenditure are derived by multiplying the annual estimated expenditure by the ratio of budget execution. GFCF expenditures on construction and intellectual property products are obtained directly from the outputs of related industries. GFCF expenditures on facilities are basically estimated through the commodity flow method (CFM), based on I-O tables. Consumption of fixed capital is derived from fixed capital stocks, measured by perpetual inventory method(PIM). The current estimates of external transactions are obtained from the balance of payments accounts. All figures expressed in dollars in the balance of payments are translated into domestic currency (Korean Won). The constant estimates of external transactions are estimated by deflating the current estimates by the appropriate price indexes. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Reference year and update cycle: The current reference year is 2020. The reference year will be revised every five years. Reconciliation: The figures for Gross Domestic Product estimated using the production approach and the expenditure approach differ due to the different resources and estimation methods adopted. To reconcile the two estimates, a statistical discrepancy term is included in the table of Expenditure on Gross Domestic Product. Seasonal adjustment: Quarterly seasonally adjusted statistics are produced through a Korean seasonal adjustment model, BOK-X-13ARIMA-SEATS, which was developed in 2016 by adjusting the X-13ARIMA-SEATS of the U.S. Bureau of the Census. Seasonally adjusted estimates of total GDP are derived by aggregating the seasonally adjusted estimates for GDP components. Geographic detail: There are no quarterly estimates of GDP by regions, only annual estimates are compiled by the KOSTAT. Other: Time series since 1953 are rebased using the reference year of 2020. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses When releasing the final annual figures and the preliminary quarterly figures, the BOK carries out revision analyses on the difference between previously-released data and current released data. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Primary factors for revisions are disseminated in the press releases. And the revision results of seasonal adjusted series are published annually in Quarterly National Accounts . 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished but non-confidential statistics are offered by internal dissemination standards. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Organizations and contact points are noticed on the Bank of Korea’s internet website. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs Catalogs of publications, documents and other services are noticed on the Bank of Korea’s internet website.
