 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The concepts and definitions of the revised national accounts are in broad conformity with guidelines outlined in the 1993 SNA . In practice, the compilation of the account may deviate from these guidelines in some respects—for example, compiling government accounts on a cash bases without making any adjustment for accrual basis, and using the depreciation data provided by the establishments as a proxy for consumption of fixed capital formation. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The ISIC Rev. 3 is used to classify economic activities. There is no classification system used to classify commodities except the harmonized system which is used by the customs department. Scope of the data The revised national accounts cover the following areas: GDP at current and constant prices by economic activity classified at the two-digit level of ISIC Rev.3: the estimates are derived by the production approach at basic prices. The previous estimates were compiled at factor cost. The estimates include large implicit subsidies on fuel, electricity and water. Expenditures on GDP: all items are compiled independently, with the sole exception of the change in the inventories, which is compiled as a residual element. The estimates are available at current prices only because there were no adequate deflators to be used for deriving the constant price estimates for the various components especially capital formation, imports and change in inventories. GDP by income components: The components are estimated. Gross fixed capital formation by type: these are derived at aggregated level as, owing to lack of data, it was not possible to derive the estimates by economic activity. The estimates were derived using the commodity flow method based on external trade statistics and the output estimates derived for construction, manufacturing and retail trade activities. Disposable income account is compiled. Exceptions to coverage Research and development on own account is not included in the estimation of the output. The defense-related assets that could be used for civilian purposes and the valuables and historical monuments are not considered as assets. Unrecorded activity The compiled estimates, which are national in scope, covering the 22 administrative districts of the country, cover all resident units except the producers engaged in illegal and unrecorded activities. Thus, these estimates cover all activities within the production boundary in the SNA 1993 except the unrecorded and illegal production. 2.4.1 Valuation The market output is valued at basic prices, and the exports and imports are valued f.o.b. 2.4.2 Recording basis Transactions and flows are recorded on an accrual basis except for government sector. The work-in-progress is recorded in the period it is produced. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Transactions between establishments within the same enterprise are recorded on a gross basis. This procedure is used for the enterprises that send their annual reports to the Ministry of Planning. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The available surveys cover only part of the large establishments in manufacturing, hotels and restaurants and construction. The results are only for the establishments that responded to the request for surveys. The surveys were conducted only for three years 2002, 2003 and 2004. In 2001, the General Authority for Information (GAI) conducted two censuses covering agriculture and manufacturing. The agriculture census provided quantitative data on crops and livestock while the manufacturing census provided data on output and intermediate consumption by economic activity. The GAI had conducted an establishment census in 2006 to obtain a statistical frame for the purpose of drawing the samples for surveys. The GAI conducted several surveys based on this frame in 2007 to collect data on the activities of the establishments during 2006. The surveys covered manufacturing, wholesale and retail trade, construction contractors, hotels and restaurants, personal, social and business services. The GAI put in place a program to update this frame annually via area sample surveys. The survey's questionnaires were designed after consultation with the national accounts division to ensure that the results would assist in the compilation of national accounts. The household income and expenditure surveys are conducted on a regular basis once every five years; the latest survey was conducted in 2007 covering all residents in the entire country. The coverage of resident foreigners is not complete. The data concerning government current and capital expenditure is not comprehensive; it was not possible to get the annual final expenditure by agency or to obtain detailed breakdown by item; data from administrative records are poor. The available price indicators are the monthly and quarterly CPIs and the annual external trade price indices (the latter are not compiled at the required level of detail). Beside these indices the national account compilers collected additional prices and quantities for several products and services in order to compile price and volume indices to be used for deflation and extrapolation. The national accounts division conducted surveys to collect data from the establishments operating in transport, telecommunication, hotels, and crude oil production, public and private education and financial sector. It was possible from these surveys to collect data that cover the period 2001-2006. The following summarizes the main sources by kind of activity distinguished in the national accounts: Agriculture, Fishing, Forestry: The Ministry of Agriculture is the main source of data on livestock, poultry, fish and field crops, while the households' expenditures on crops were used to estimate output using commodity flow method. Mining & Manufacturing: All necessary basic data on crude oil and natural gas are received from the National Oil Corporation (NOC). The NOC provided data on their share in the output of each company operating in the oil and natural gas sector. Data on the output of one foreign-owned company could not be obtained, and its output was estimated from the payments for its services made by the NOC-owned crude oil companies. Water and Electricity: The water and electricity companies, which are all public, produce final (annual) reports. Building & Construction: The data on the main construction materials, cement and iron bars, were used to calculate the output of construction. Although the surveys were not comprehensive, these provided technical coefficients for deriving the estimates for this activity. Transport & Communication: Many operators of air, sea transport and communication are public. While the national accounts division collected the necessary data from communication operators via a questionnaire, the data from operators in all other sectors was colected from their final (annual) accounts. The households' expenditure estimates on transportation were used to derive estimates for land passenger transport. Land freight transport estimates were obtained as a proportion of the domestically-produced or imported goods. Wholesale and Retail Trade, Hotels, and Restaurants: Fixed margins (commodity flow methods) are applied on the current/constant price values of traded goods from domestic production and imports. Hotels and restaurants: The national accounts division collected the necessary data from them through a questionnaire. Finance & Insurance: The Central Bank of Libya (CBL) receives data from commercial banks on their final balance sheets and through a special form according to the requirements of national accounts. The public and private insurance companies provide their annual reports to the national accounts division. Social & Personal Services: The household expenditure estimates on these services were taken as the output of these activities. Coefficients and percentages from other Arab countries where used to derive estimates for intermediate consumption and compensation of employees. The establishment census provided data on the number of establishments by economic activity and the dates when production was started. Based on this data, estimates were compiled. Government Services: All data needed for the calculation general government output originate from the Ministry of Planning and Finance. For constant price estimates the historical data on the government decisions were used to calculate a simple wage index to be used for deflating the value added. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The estimates were compiled for most of the activities at the third digit level of the ISIC Rev.3. The economic activities included in the surveys (of 2002-2004) were classified according to the ISIC Rev.3 in the 2006.surveys. The data from government is on cash basis, and there no adjustments was done to derive accrual basis. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data collection requires follow-ups from the staff of the national accounts division. Although the GAI is responsible for conducting surveys and censuses, it was not able to set a timetable for releasing the survey results. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The national accounts division checks the data for its reasonableness and consistency with other data sets and comparability with historical data series. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The commodity flow method is used to compile the output of the major activities to ensure coverage and accuracy. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The compilation techniques vary from activity to activity, depending on the availability of data. The statistical techniques used in the compilation of the national accounts can be summarized as follows. Agriculture, Fishing, Forestry: The estimates for livestock, field crops, fisheries and poultry were derived, based on the price and quantity statistics obtained from the Ministry of Agriculture. Owing to the absence of such data on fruits and vegetable production, the output was estimated as a residual using the commodity flow method. The data on the demand side was obtained from the external trade statistics and the households' expenditure on fruits and vegetables. The constant price estimates were derived by revaluing the quantities in each year by the 2003 prices every year. Mining & Manufacturing: The total output of crude oil and natural gas is estimated from the data obtained from NOC covering the values and quantities of production in addition to the operating expenses for each activity. Water and Electricity: the estimates are derived from the financial statements of the companies. Building & Construction: the estimates of output and intermediate consumption are derived by a commodity flow method based on the data on major construction materials. Transport & Communication: the estimates for air and sea transport and telecommunication are derived from the financial statements of the companies operating in these sectors. In addition, communication companies are covered by a questionnaire developed by the national accounts division. For land transport, variable margin ratios have been applied on the imported or domestically produced commodities. The margin ratios were obtained after consultation with experts in the land transportation. The output of passenger transportation was derived from the households' and tourists expenditure on transportation. Wholesale and Retail Trade, Hotels, and Restaurants: fixed margins are applied on the current/constant price values of traded goods from domestic production and imports. Hotels and restaurants: The national accounts division collected the necessary data through a questionnaire. The collected data were used to derive value added estimates. Finance & Insurance: the estimates are based on the data received from the commercial banks Social & Personal Services: The households' expenditure estimates on these services were taken as the output of these activities. Coefficients and percentages from other Arab countries where used to derive estimates for intermediate consumption and compensation of employees. The establishments' census provided data on the number of establishments by economic activity and on the dates when production started. Based on this data, estimates are compiled. Government Services: the estimates of value added are based on the data provided by the Ministry of Planning and Finance. For constant price estimates the historical data on the government decisions were used to calculate a wage index to be used for deflating the value added. The estimates for gross capital formation were derived by type of assets from the external trade statistics and the output estimates of the construction activity. It was not possible to derive them by economic activity owing to lack of data. The value added estimates may include some holding gains since no adjustment was done while deriving the estimates for output and intermediate consumption. Unobserved activities were not covered in the GDP estimates owing to the absence of data on such activities. The commercial depreciation was used as a proxy for the consumption of fixed capital; it was not possible to use the perpetual inventory method owing to the absence of the required data. For most of the activities single indicator methods were used to derive value added at constant prices. The net taxes were derived by extrapolating the base-year tax/subsidy using a volume extrapolator of transactions subject to a specific tax/subsidy. As for trade the base-year-trade margins were extrapolated using volume extrapolators of sales (tradable goods). All expenditure components of GDP are derived independently, except the changes in inventories, which was derived residually. The GDP estimated by expenditure components are derived at current prices only; there were no adequate price or volume indicators to derive constant price estimates. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses No revision studies are done.
