 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The Norwegian Balance of Payments (NBOP) is a statistical statement of Norway's economic transactions with the rest of the world, within a specific period of time (quarter and year). It is an integrated part of the National Accounts (NA) and is constructed as a mirror image of the institutional sector "Rest of the World" in NA. Analytical framework: NBOP is presented in accordance with the latest internationally approved guidelines. It consists of three main parts: (i) current account, (ii) capital account, and (iii) financial account. The current account comprises, first, exports and imports of goods and services with a balance of goods and services, secondly, primary incomes and current transfers from and to abroad with a balance of income and current transfers, and then adding both balances to become current account balance. The capital account shows net capital transfers to abroad and net acquisitions of patents, licenses etc. Lastly, the financial account shows how transactions recorded in the current account result in changes in foreign assets and liabilities, and in addition to purchases and sales of financial instruments. The financial account also includes transactions that do not have a counter entry in the current account. Total asset transactions less total liability transactions result in net lending. By adjusting net lending for known valuation changes, an estimate of changes in Norway's net foreign assets/liabilities is obtained. The international investment position publish Norway's net transactions, net other changes and foreign assets and liabilities. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The scope of the Balance of Payments is defined in international guidelines in the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6), System of National Accounts (2008 SNA, published by the UN, OECD, IMF, World Bank and the European Commission) and the European System of National and Regional Accounts (ESA 2010). Scope of the data Transactions coverage: The Balance of Payments shall in principle include all transactions between unit’s resident of the Norwegian economic territory and non-resident units. The main sources for the current- and capital account; External trade in goods Petroleum Statistics Sample survey, non-financial enterprises,non-financial corporations and financial institutions not under supervision Structural Business Statistics, Ocean Transport Annual accounting statistics for the general government. Quarterly accounting statistics for financial corporations under supervision Travel survey The main sources for the financial account; Quarterly accounting statistics for financial corporations under supervision. Positions are reported and some transactions (equity, other debt securities and loans) and some revaluations (exchange rate changes and other price changes) quarterly and annually. Quarterly accounting statistics for non-financial corporations and financial institutions not under supervision. Positions and revaluations are reported quarterly and annually. Annual accounting statistics for the general government. Positions and revaluations (exchange rate changes and other price changes) are reported. Quarterly data from the Norwegian Central Securities Depository (VPS) and data from a separate survey on mutual funds. Positions and transactions are reported. Data collected for non-financial corporations and mutual funds are based on sample surveys. See each survey for more information on sampling, for example sample survey and how to choose the sample. For areas with incomplete statistical coverage, it is necessary to do estimations or use supplementary sources such as tax returns. Geographic coverage: The Norwegian territory includes mainland Norway, the Norwegian part of the Continental Shelf, Svalbard and Jan Mayen with Bjørnøya. Exceptions to coverage Transactions coverage: No particular transactions are intentionally excluded. Unrecorded activity See above, on transactions coverage. 2.4.1 Valuation In general, transactions are valued at market prices. For crude oil, administratively set norm prices are in part used, but these are estimates made ex post to reflect observed market prices. Imports c.i.f. are adjusted to f.o.b. valuation on a global basis (based on data collected by the External Trade Statistics). Non-listed shares are recorded at book values, on the asset side often at historic cost while on the liability side own funds at book value is applied as the principle of valuation. As regards change of ownership in the case of exports and imports of goods, these transactions are in practice recorded when the goods cross the border as registered through customs declarations. 2.4.2 Recording basis Recording of income: Investment income as reported by the non-financial enterprises is based on business accounting (book-keeping) data which are considered to be a proxy for accrual data. Conversion to unit of account: The exchange rate on the transaction date or the average rate of the shortest period applicable shall be used for converting transactions in foreign currencies into the national currency. Stocks of assets and liabilities are to be valued at prices or rates in effect at the time to which the balance sheet relates. These principles are usually followed. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The current account shows data gross, e.g. gross interest income and expenses. In the capital account the data are presented as net expenses. In the financial account the data are shown as net of purchases and sales of each instrument. Income and expenditure are defined excluding holding gains and losses, irrespective of whether they are realised or unrealised. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Goods: The main source used is external trade statistics (ETS), i.e. monthly statistics on external trade in goods based on the administrative returns collected by the customs authorities. The ETS coverage is in accordance with the recommendations of the UN and relates to the General Trade principle. Transit trade is of minor importance to the Norwegian economy. Information on imports and exports of special goods (e.g. ships and electricity) are based on special reports. Also excluded from ETS are exports and imports of goods and services delivered directly to and from abroad to the petroleum installations on the Norwegian Continental Shelf. For these transactions, estimates are based on statistical surveys of petroleum companies, i.e.oil and gas activity statistics (quarterly and annual). Services: A new quarterly and annual sample survey on non-financial enterprises has been established from 2004 to serve mainly NBOP purposes. Services and income flows are grossed up using adequat methodology. For travel a quarterly survey on cross-border tourism cover the imports of this item. For exports estimates are based on tourist statistics (accommodation, passenger transport) published by Statisitcs Norway. An important complementary source used is Maritime Transport Statistics (MTS). An annual statistics compiled by Statistics Norway for operating income and expenditure covering all vessels in ocean transport operated by a Norwegian shipping company independent of the country of registration, including also information on foreign owned or registered ships operated by Norwegian companies. Government accounts supply information for some services. For insurance and financial services, banking statistics and government accounts are also utilized. The latter is also a relevant source for government services n.i.e. Adjustments made for conceptual differences from national accounts concepts: In general, the balance of payments data are based on principles and definitions from the BOP manual. The data of trade in services are required to be classified in accordance with the EU product standard “Classification of Products by Activity” (CPA). Adjustment 1: Some items in Merchandise trade are redefined as services based on a link between HS- and CPA-classification. Adjustment 2: Import transportation services are not collected , but estimated from Merchandise trade statistics. Income: The main sources on investment income are the sample survey on non-financial enterprises, the quarterly and annual survey of financial enterprises except. Compensation of employees is estimated for foreign seamen, crews, pilots and other short-term non-resident workers, while on the income side use is made of information from the tax authorities and the register of wages and salaries. Data for reinvested earnings on foreign investment in Norway are based on administrative profit information collected by the Directorate of Taxes, while data for reinvested earnings in the opposite direction are derived from data in a special survey on foreign direct investment abroad. Current transfers: Government accounts (for general government transfers) and the sample survey on non-financial enterprises (for workers' remittances and other transfers) are used as sources. Capital account: For both capital transfers and acquisitions of patents, licenses etc., the sample survey on non-financial enterprises and Government accounts are used as sources. Financial account (FA): All major Norwegian financial and non-financial enterprises are covered in the BoP-statistics. (FA) Direct investment: The sample survey on non-financial enterprises is the main source for direct investment transactions. (FA) Portfolio investment: The sample survey on non-financial enterprises, banking statistics and other financial institution statistics, and information from the Norwegian Central Securities Depository are the main sources. (FA) Other investment: The sample survey on non-financial enterprises and banking statistics and other financial institution statistics are the main sources. (FA) Reserve assets: Banking statistics for the central bank (Norges Bank) is the source for transactions in reserve assets. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Goods: In NBOP, no adjustment is presently made to the ETS to account for goods that cross the border without a change of ownership. For aircraft, a correction item is introduced for exports and imports of SAS aircraft, otherwise the register principle is employed in the ETS. The source used for determining the export value of crude oil and natural gas is the ETS, but also taking into account (due to valuation considerations and pipeline transportation involved) information on prices collected directly from oil companies, administrative norm prices, and monthly quantity data on oil and gas exported from the petroleum fields in the North Sea. Exports and imports of ships and oil platforms are extracted from the ETS, based on special reports from shipping and drilling companies. From 1988, the owner criterion was introduced to replace the register or flag criterion. In the Norwegian National Accounts (NNA), capital formation of platforms for petroleum production is recorded on a continuous basis, with a corresponding effect on imports related to investments in petroleum fields. Exports and imports of goods related to petroleum acitvities include items not recorded in the ETS, while instead based on the statistics on oil and gas activity published by Statistics Norway; exports representing an adjustment for the UK parts of petroleum fields crossing the borderline between Norway and the UK, while the imports in addition to this include unspecified imports of goods directly from abroad to the Continental Shelf for use in exploration and pipeline transport activities and similar deliveries of fuel, all calculated by use of estimated import shares based on information from the oil and gas activity statistics. Goods for processing are included in ETS on a gross basis. Services: Services are estimated on a quarterly basis by grossing up survey data. For maritime transport the maritime transport statistics (MTS) are the main source. MTS is an annual statistics compiled by Statistics Norway for operating income and expenditure covering all vessels in ocean transport operated by a Norwegian shipping company independent of the country of registration, including also information on foreign owned or registered ships operated by Norwegian companies. Adjustments are made for some transportation services. Exports of pipeline transport services are estimated from the oil and gas activity statistics based on reports from the Norwegian pipeline companies, employing the ownership criterion rather than the territorial criterion for the delimitation of domestic and non-domestic activity. The same source is used for oil drilling services and other services related to oil activities, but in this case supplemented with the sample survey on non-financial enterprises. Imports of services to oil and gas activities include imports of unspecified services both for intermediate consumption and capital formation in the various petroleum activities. Adjustment item for borderline fields is also made in this case. SAS is given a special treatment in the NNA and NBOP, as a part of the company's transactions are allocated to the three countries of ownership of SAS in proportion to their shares of the corporation equity. For travel, adjustments to the current price figures are partly based on volume indices considerations. No attempt is made to adjust the ETS which record goods imported for use in construction services projects, therefore using instead the sample survey on non-financial enterprises as source for exports and imports of construction services. Income: For compensation of employees, estimations are made based on several sources. For reinvested earnings, preliminary data are estimated based on the latest available profit accounts, but the estimates may be revised considerably when contemporary data are available. Some information on investment income to and from abroad is also found in the accounts for financial institutions and other sources used for institutional sector accounts in NNA, Current transfers: Main sources are Central Government Accounts, estimations based on insurance companies accounts and special estimations on household transfers to abroad. Capital account: Main sources are Central Government Accounts (capital transfers) and survey on non-financial enterprises. Acquisition of licenses and patents etc. are reported on net basis only, but recorded gross based on certain assumptions. Financial account in general: In NBOP the transactions of the financial account are published on a net basis, i.e. net figures for purchases and sales of the same instrument etc. Most of the enterprises provide stocks of assets and liabilities and revaluation data (price, exchange rate and other) by end of each quarter which allows calculation of transactions. For some sectors and instruments the transactions are reported directly. Direct investment: The sample survey on non-financial enterprises provides stocks of assets and liabilities by the end of each quarter. On the asset side equity capital (shares) is valued at book value, often historic cost. On the liability side the valuation principle for equity capital is own funds at book value. The few listed enterprises under direct investment are valued at market prices. Information on price revaluations is used to derive transactions at market or market equivalent prices. Since the survey of non-financial enterprises is a sample survey adjustments are made to gross up the total level of stocks by the end of each period but this does not effect the transactions. Only the stocks and revaluations are effected by the grossing up procedure. Portfolio investment: The data source for non-financial enterprises is the same as described under direct investment but most portfolio shares are listed and are thus valued at market prices. The market values on the liability side are provided by the Norwegian Central Securities Depository. The data for market price revaluations are calculated from these data and the collected face values and the transactions can then be derived. Exchange rate revaluations are mostly calculated by Statistics Norway. Most of the financial enterprises provide the same kind of data as the non-financial enterprises but on a full scale basis (cencus data). But banks, mortgage companies and insurance companies report portfolio transactions directly and the revaluations are then calculated as the residual of changes in stocks and transactions. However, Norges Bank (which also reports the data for the Government Pension Fund Global), reports stocks and a full set of revaluations which makes correct calculation of transactions possible. Most of the data for Norges Bank itself are classified as reserve assets. Other investment: The transactions for other investment are calculated in the same way for all sectors. Data for stocks and revaluations (exchange rate) are collected or calculated by Statistics Norway and the transactions are derived from this information. Included are transactions resulting from the recording of life insurance, the investment income element of which is estimated to be 85 per cent of the premiums. Reserve assets: In Norway all reserve assets are held by the central bank (Norges Bank). Norges Bank also provides data for the Government Pension Fund Global but this fund is owned by the government and is consequently not regarded as part of the reserve assets, but as part of the portfolio investment and other investment of Central Government. Transactions in reserves are calculated from changes in stocks and complete data for revaluations. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Seasonal adjustment: Seasonally-adjusted estimates are not compiled in NBOP. However, exports and imports are presented with seasonally adjusted figures as part of the Quarterly National Accounts. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Errors and omissions: The value of this item is normally well below the recommended 5 per cent of the sum of exports and imports. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Geographic detail: Detailed current and capital account data are published with geographical allocation covering 7 economic areas: Nordic countries, other EEA-countries, other European countries, North America, Central and South America, Africa, and Asia and Oceania. Aggregated items are published with more detailed geographical allocation.
