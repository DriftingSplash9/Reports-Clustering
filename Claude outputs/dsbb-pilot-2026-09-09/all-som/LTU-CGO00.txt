 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Central government operations are compiled following the methodology of the System of National Accounts 2008 (SNA 2008) and are consistent with the methodology set out in the IMF Government Finance Statistics Manual 2014 (GFSM 2014) . 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The current classification is almost consistent with the methodology set out in the IMF Government Finance Statistics Manual 2014 (GFSM 2014) . Classification System: According to the classification of revenue and expense of the Central government (including Social Security Funds) revenue consists of taxes, social contributions, grants and other revenue. Taxes: taxes on income and profits (individuals, corporations); taxes on payroll and workforce; taxes on property (tax on real estate, inheritance tax, tax on land); taxes on goods and services (value added tax, turnover tax, excises, sugar tax, taxes on lotteries and gambling, motor vehicle taxes, pollution tax, charges); taxes on international trade and transactions. Social contributions: compulsory and voluntary contributions of employers, employees and self-employed or non-employed. Grants: grants from foreign governments, grants from international organizations, European Union structural funds and grants from other general government units. Other revenue: property income (interest, dividends, residual profit of the LB, tax on the rent of state land and waters, tax on the state natural resources, tax on the oil and gas resources), sales of goods and services (rent of buildings, consular tax, tuition fees, other sales of goods and services), fines and penalties, voluntary transfers, other than grants, other revenue. Economic classification of expense: Compensation of employees: wages and salaries, social contributions. Use of goods and services: payments for the electricity, heating, transport, communication services, staff training, maintenance and repair of fixed assets, payments for the rental of buildings, transport equipment and other purchases of goods and services. Property expense: interest, rent. Subsidies: import subsidies, subsidies on products and subsidies on production, i.e. all subsidies from budgetary sources. Grants: current and capital grants to foreign governments, to international organizations and to other general government units. Payments to EU budget: traditional own resources, plastics, VAT, GNP-based own resources. Social benefits: social security benefits, social assistance benefits, employer social benefits. Other expense: current and capital (debt assumption, debt cancellation and capital transfers to market enterprises and non-profit institutions to finance their costs of acquiring non-financial assets). Expenditure from EU funds and co-financing: current and capital expenditure from EU funds, including EU subsidies to agriculture. Transactions in non-financial assets: acquisition and disposals of: land and subsoil assets, buildings and structures, machinery and equipment, valuables, intangible assets, stocks. Transactions in financial assets and liabilities (financing): currency and deposits, securities other than shares, financial derivatives, loans, shares and other equity, insurance technical reserves, other accounts receivable/payable. Also, financial assets and liabilities are classified according to the residence into domestic and foreign. The standard tables are used to disseminate information on central government operations. The data in these tables are compiled strictly in accordance with the methodological requirements set in the GFSM 2014 . Cash surplus/deficit is equal to the net cash inflow from operating activities minus the net cash outflow from investment in non-financial assets. Scope of the data Institutional coverage of central government in reported data: State budget (all budgetary entities of central government), the Social Security Funds and extra-budgetary funds. Exceptions to coverage Data on public corporations, classified in central government are not included. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation of transactions: The official exchange rates set by the LB on the day of transaction are used to convert foreign currency denominated transactions. 2.4.2 Recording basis Accounting basis: All operations are recorded on a cash basis except for flows from European Union which are recorded on accrual basis. Complementary period: No complementary period is used. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Adjustment for grossing and netting of transactions: In line with the international standards, the data are recorded on a gross basis. Revenue is not netted against expense. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources: The main data source is the accounting records of central budgetary units, social security funds and extra-budgetary funds. Data are received from the Ministry of Finance, from the social security funds. Data provided by the Central Bank is also used as an additional data source for calculation of assets and liabilities. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Classification system used for central budget revenues and expenditure in most cases matches GFSM 2014. Nevertheless, reclassifications are necessary to obtain GFS, it concerns consolidation, EU funds (according to the final beneficiary criteria), etc. Social security and extra-budgetary funds provide their data already in GFS format. Time of recording and valuation of source data are consistent with GFS methodology. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Source data are available on a timely basis for producing government finance statistics that meet SDDS requirements. 3.2.1 Source data assessment GFS compilation relies extensively on administrative data sources, there is no need for estimations. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Consolidation: consolidation is made in full compliance with the methodology set out in the GFSM 2014 . 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Because of the reliability of administrative data (main source data) there are no statistical discrepancies between financial and non-financial accounts. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses 5.1.5 Dissemination on request The SL provides statistical information on individual request (enquiry) if requested information is available and is not confidential. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The information on contact person is presented in each publication in the Official Statistics Portal. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs Catalogue of statistical publications of the SL is available on the Official Statistics Portal: https://osp.stat.gov.lt/statistikos-leidiniu-katalogas , terms and conditions for subscription are available on: https://osp.stat.gov.lt/e.-paslaugos .
