There are some 3000 different items on which we ask monthly production etc. Item selection: Most important items from selected establishments. Unit selection: Once a year new establishments are included, i.e., new establishments are inserted on those industries where the sample size on 5-digit Nace 2008 level is low (under 40 %). Sample sizes: Sample size in terms of number of establishments or enterprises is about 330. Data collection methods: email, internet, phone. Selection of industries, coverage and variables used: The establishments of the volume index of industrial output (about 330) are selected from the business register. The largest establishments or enterprises measured by the gross value of production by industry are selected for the sample of the volume index of industrial output. Measured in terms of the gross value of production, the coverage of the statistics is almost 80 per cent on the level of the whole industry. The coverage is calculated from the establishments included in the direct collection of the structural statistics. On the 2-digit level of industrial classification the coverage varies between 40 and 100 per cent. The direct inquiry for the volume index of industrial output is carried out with a questionnaire delivered to the establishments, requesting them to provide figures for output volumes in their major commodities. If data are not obtained on the quantities of goods produced, the establishments are asked to provide as dummy variables information on the amount of energy consumed or the raw materials used. For some industries the value of output is also used; the value data are deflated with the producer price index of the corresponding commodity heading or industry. Until now, industry-specific producer price indices have been used in deflating the Volume Index of Industrial Output. Starting from data concerning January 2019, price indices compiled by national accounts are used as deflators. Price indices compiled by national accounts are more detailed and they also take into account the secondary production of the industry.Thus far, the production of the statistics has been based on a sample of approximately 750 establishments or enterprises. The aim has been to reduce the data supply burden and the renewal has attained it. The sample of the Volume Index is now around 330 establishments or enterprises. However, coverage has improved as part of industries are obtained through turnover data.Previously, so-called mixed collection was used in the calculation of the Volume Index of Industrial Output, that is, both volume and value data were inquired inside industries. From 2019 onwards, use is made of industry-pure collection, that is, either volume or value data by industry are in use. These renewals help to improve the correspondence of the Volume Index of Industrial Output with the Trend Indicator of Output of national accounts. Sources of weights: Sources used for the aggregation weights (industry surveys). 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Nature of indicators used: Quantity measures of output (40 %) and deflated value of output (60 %). Timing of production observations: Frequency of data collection in the sampled establishments (monthly), including actual reporting dates 15th of the next month. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Calculation method of the volume index of industrial output: The monthly volume index is calculated as a weighted arithmetic average of output volumes (values). A volume index is calculated for each establishment where the weights used are value weights of establishment-specific commodity headings obtained from the direct inquiry. The monthly quantity and other data for the establishment are divided by the average for the previous year and multiplied by the value of the corresponding heading in the previous year. The weighting of all the indices for the establishments in the same industry will yield the index for the 3-digit industry level . After this the indices of the most detailed level are weighted with the newest available value added to higher industry levels. An original series by industry is calculated from the volume index. Compilation of monthly IPI: we have 3 stratums Stratum 1: Establishments of enterprises with 150 or more employees Stratum 2: Establishments of enterprises with 50 to 149 employees Stratum 3: Enterprises with under 50 employess from Tax Administration's payment control data. These are not available in the publication month, so we have to use estimation. The use of dummy variables may impair the reliability of the index. Other factors that have a bearing on reliability include quality changes in the goods manufactured, which cannot be taken into account in calculating the index. Procedures for non-response: The monthly non-response rate for the volume index of industrial output is under one per cent in the establishments included in the calculation, i.e., response rates of 95 %. Treatment of missing prices: Not applicable. Selection of replacement items: A replacement variety is selected when the current variety becomes permanently unavailable. Introducing new units and products: New establishments and also new products are inserted once a year. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Computation of lowest level indices: Methods used to combine the basic production observations to obtain the first level index (weighted of average quantities produced by establishment including to the same industry “Nace 2008” 5-digit level). Aggregation: Formula used to aggregate the basic item indices to obtain category, group, and the total indices (modified Laspeyres chain index). Nature of weights: Value added. Period of current index weights: Calendar year. Frequency of weight updates: Once a year. The most recent update took place in 2025. The industry value added weights are from 2023. Alignment of value of weights and base period: Value added weights (as new as possible). Chain index method. Base is linked to 2021=100. Linking of reweighted index to historical index: Process for linking of the re-based index, after a weight update, to the old series to produce a historical time-series. Finland uses a chain method to link historical time-series to new 2021=100 base year. Seasonally-adjusted indices: Working day adjustment and seasonal adjustment of the volume index are made on the basis of a model. Statistics Finland also produces index data seasonally adjusted and as a trend series. Seasonal adjustment is a calculation method for eliminating regular seasonal variation from working-day adjusted time series. Irregular variation is also removed from the trend series. Statistics Finland has changed the seasonal adjustment method. The new method is the Tramo/Seats method recommended by the Statistical Office of the European Communities (Eurostat). Aggregate series are derived from lower-level seasonally-adjusted series versus independently adjusted series. Indices calculated: original, working day adjusted, seasonally adjusted and trend series. Publication level: we have about 40 different industries or groups of industries. Other weight related issues: The fast structural change of the economy has resulted in that a fixed weight index can cause biased results. If the volume changes of industries and relative price changes correlate negatively, the variable weight index yields a smaller volume growth than the fixed weight index of the base year. In case of positive correlation, the variable weight index produces a larger volume growth than the fixed weight Laspeyres index. In the variable weight index the weights are changed yearly both within the industry and between industries. The weights within the industry are mainly derived by direct inquiries and the weights between industries are obtained from the structural statistics of industry. The problem with the variable weight index is that the time series is not additive, that is, the index series of a less detailed industry level cannot be calculated directly by weighting from more detailed industry levels. However, the variable weight index will increase the flexibility of the calculation by enabling inclusion of new industries and industry changes of establishments every year. Because of the above-mentioned reasons, Statistics Finland has decided to use the variable weight index in the calculation of the volume index of industrial output. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Comparability and reliability: Steps taken to improve reliability include careful inspection of the base data, regular monitoring of the weight structure of establishments. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Verification of data: Procedures to validate the accuracy and reliability of collected production information include careful inspection of the base data, regular monitoring of the weight structure of establishments.
