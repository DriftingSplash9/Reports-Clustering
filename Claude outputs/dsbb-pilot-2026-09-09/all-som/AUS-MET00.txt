 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Australia's international merchandise export and import statistics, are broadly compiled in line with the United Nations' (UN) recommendations in the International Merchandise Trade Statistics Concepts and Definitions. Merchandise exports are defined as goods which subtract from the stock of material resources in Australia as a result of their movement out of Australia. These include goods that have been produced or manufactured in Australia, and re-exports of imported goods (where the goods are exported in essentially the same condition as when they were imported). Merchandise exports exclude goods exported with the reasonable expectation of re-import within a limited time (e.g. goods exported for repair and return). Exports of goods which were imported with the reasonable expectation of re-export within a limited time are also excluded. Merchandise imports are defined as goods which add to the stock of material resources in Australia as a result of their movement into Australia. These include goods brought into Australia directly for home consumption plus goods imported into Customs (bonded) warehouses. Goods are cleared from a bonded warehouse once the duty applicable has been paid. Merchandise imports exclude goods imported with the reasonable expectation of re-export within a limited time (e.g. goods in transit or goods for temporary exhibition in Australia) and goods re-imported after temporary export. For more information on the concepts and definitions for international merchandise trade statistics please see the publication International Merchandise Trade, Australia: Concepts, Sources and Methods, 2018 (cat. no. 5489.0) 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classifications used to present international merchandise trade statistics include the World Customs Organisation's Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System, known as the Harmonized System (HS). Australia collects more detail than is provided for at the international level by extending the 6-digit HS to a: 10-digit classification for imports, the Combined Australian Customs Tariff Nomenclature and Statistical Classification (the Customs Tariff) 8-digit classification for exports, the Australian Harmonized Export Commodity Classification (AHECC) Australia also uses the following classifications: the UN Standard International Trade Classification, (SITC) Revision 4; the UN Classification by Broad Economic Categories (BEC); Industry of origin according to the Australian and New Zealand Standard Industrial Classification (ANZSIC); and country of destination/origin (Australian 4-digit alpha code which is closely linked to the UN country code). Further information about these classifications and those used by other Australian government departments and classification correspondences can be found in the ABS publication International Merchandise Tra de, Australia: Concepts, Sources and Methods, 2018 (cat. no. 5489.0) Scope of the data Australia's international merchandise trade statistics record goods which add to, or subtract from, the stock of material resources of Australia by entering (imports) or leaving (exports) its territory. Goods are 'physical, produced objects for which a demand exists, over which ownership rights can be established and whose ownership can be transferred from one institutional unit to another by engaging in transactions on markets' (2008 SNA, paragraph 6.15). Goods simply transported through Australia (goods in transit) and goods entering or leaving Australia on a temporary basis, e.g. for repair or for exhibition, do not add to or subtract from Australia's stock of material resources and are not included in Australia's international merchandise trade. Where such goods are entered on an import or export declaration they are included in Australia's international non-merchandise trade. Further information on the scope, including more detail on the definition of Australia's economic territory, can be located in the ABS publication International Merchandise Trade, Australia: Concepts, Sources and Methods . Exceptions to coverage Transactions coverage:The following transactions are excluded from recorded international merchandise trade statistics: Goods simply being transported through Australia, e.g. goods in transit (including transhipments). Goods temporarily admitted or dispatched. Goods for repairs or maintenance. Monetary gold. Issued banknotes and securities and coins in circulation. Goods consigned to and from territorial enclaves. Non-financial assets, ownership of which has been transferred from residents to non-residents, without crossing borders. Goods treated as part of trade in services. Goods under merchanting. Goods under operating lease. Goods lost or destroyed after leaving the economic territory of the exporting country, but before entering the economic territory of the intended importing country, whether or not ownership has been acquired by the importer. Satellites and their launchers. Goods functioning as a means of transport. Content delivered electronically. Waste and scrap. Goods entering or leaving Australia illegally. Mobile equipment (including ships and aircraft) that changes ownership while outside the country of residence of its original owner. Fish caught on the high seas by Australian registered vessels and landed in Australian ports. Fish catch, minerals from the seabed and salvage landed by Australian vessels in foreign ports or acquired by foreign vessels on the high seas from Australian vessels. Bunkers, stores, ballast and dunnage that are acquired by Australian vessels or aircraft, outside Australia. Geographical coverage :Australia uses export and import declarations collated by the Department of Home Affairs (Home Affairs) as the primary source for compiling international merchandise trade statistics. The jurisdiction of Home Affairs does not extend to goods exported and imported by Norfolk Island, Heard Island, McDonald Islands, Christmas Island, Cocos (Keeling) Islands, Ashmore and Cartier Islands, Coral Sea Islands, the Australian Antarctic Territory, the Joint Petroleum Development Area (JPDA) or Australia's territorial enclaves abroad. As a result these areas are excluded from the statistical territory covered by Australia's international merchandise trade statistics. Further detail on the coverage of Australia's international merchandise trade statistics, including further detail on the exclusions from Australia's statistical territory, can be found in the ABS publication International Merchandise Trade, Australia: Concepts, Sources and Methods . Unrecorded activity No estimates are made for smuggling activity or shuttle trade or goods below the low value threshold. 2.4.1 Valuation For Australia's international merchandise trade statistics the transaction value is used. Point of valuation: For imports, a cost, insurance and freight (CIF) type valuation is used (i.e. the value at the border of the importing country). For exports a free on board (FOB) type valuation is used (i.e. the value at the border of the exporting country). Australia's import statistics are published using the customs value, which is a FOB type value, but imports on a CIF basis are also available. Exports statistics are published on a FOB type basis, and the transaction values reported are assessed to be the most practical approximation of market price. Currency conversion: In Australia, data are presented in terms of Australian dollars, though for international comparisons it is customary to convert to US dollars. Import values are reported to the Home Affairs in the invoice currency of the transaction, with the Integrated Cargo System (ICS) automatically converting the values to Australian dollars, using exchange rates applicable on the date of export. For export transactions, the FOB value is reported to Home Affairs in the currency used on the invoice for the goods (provided that the currency was Australian dollars or one of the 28 allowable foreign currencies). Goods invoiced in all other currencies are converted to Australian dollars by the exporter of their agent. Exports data from Home Affairs that is in one of the accepted foreign currencies, is converted by ABS to Australian dollars. Further information about valuation of Australia's international merchandise trade statistics can be found in the ABS publication International Merchandise Trade, Australia: Concepts, Sources and Methods, 2018 (cat. no. 5489.0). 2.4.2 Recording basis International merchandise trade statistics are not compiled on an accrual basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures In accordance with general macroeconomic principles, ABS international merchandise trade statistics are compiled on a gross basis 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Merchandise trade statistics are sourced from the Department of Home Affairs. Data from Home Affairs has been the primary source of international merchandise trade statistics for over 100 years. Home Affairs is the federal authority responsible for the collection and management of export and import documentation. The documentation is used by Home Affairs to assess and collect duty and other revenue payable on imported goods, and to monitor and control the physical movement of goods into, and out of, Australia. For imports, a declaration must be lodged with, and cleared by Home Affairs before access to the goods can be obtained. Import declarations may be lodged well in advance of the expected arrival of the goods. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data : The system used by the Department of Home Affairs (Home Affairs) to collect and process export and import information is known as the Integrated Cargo System (ICS). When a declaration is entered into the ICS a range of checks are applied to ensure that only valid codes for the commodity, country, port etc. are used and that all mandatory information has been entered. Warning messages are sent to alert exporters and importers (or their agents) of possible errors. The Home Affair's checks are aimed at ensuring compliance with legislation in an environment of self-regulation and risk assessment. In practice, this means that while most declarations are processed without intervention, compliance is assured through the Home Affair's legislative power to monitor and intercept cargo and to audit company records and practices. Where evidence of non-compliance with customs legislation is identified, compliance can be enforced by imposing sanctions or fines, or by prosecution. Types of data collected : A range of data items are received by the ABS from Home Affairs in a daily file through the ICS which provide information on imports into and exports out of Australia. These data items include information on the importer/exporter, port of loading/destination, country of final destination/origin, timing of export/import, the type of good (full description including quantity and gross weight) and value. Further information about the source data, including individual data items and their detailed descriptions, can be found in the ‘Data Sources and Compilation Methods' section of the ABS publication International Merchandise Trade, Australia: Concepts, Sources and Methods, 2018 (cat. no. 5489.0) Basis of country data : Country data refer to country of origin (for imports) and country of final destination (for exports) where these are known by the importer or exporter. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Customs data is received daily (including weekends and public holidays). Every day, the ABS also receives a file from the ICS of import and clearance records which were finalised by Home Affairs in the previous 24 hours. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The source data from the ICS is routinely assessed to record, for example, the level of error in the data either through incorrect completion of custom declarations or through issues in the processing system (such as keying errors). 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The ABS strives to incorporate updates to international guidelines as soon as reasonable. This includes large scale reviews of, for example, the Harmonized System, and System of National Accounts. More regularly, the ABS Balance of Payments and International Trade in Goods Section incorporates classification updates for statistical codes as the result of changes made by Home Affairs or changes to free trade agreements. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Time series analysis : On a balance of payments basis several time series analysis techniques are employed (for example to create seasonal adjustments and trend series). However, on a merchandise trade basis only the original series is available. Other : The confidentiality of the data collected by ABS is protected by provisions in the Census and Statistics Act 1905 which prescribes the manner in which statistical information can be disclosed. For international merchandise trade statistics, where a business or organisation demonstrates that disclosure of their information would be likely to enable their identification, a variety of restrictions are available to protect the confidentiality of the business' or organisation's data. Each type of restriction is designed to protect a particular aspect of the data. Restrictions are placed at the commodity level, and the information is restricted for all trade in that commodity, not just that relating to the particular individual or organisation which requested the restriction. Processing system : Details of exports and imports are reported to Home Affairs and undergo some automated processing for customs' purposes before being transferred from the ICS to the ABS for statistical processing and compilation. Site of processing : The ABS processes Australia's international merchandise trade statistics at its Hobart capital city office location. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The ABS' international merchandise trade statistics are almost exclusively compiled from customs declarations. This together with a robust processing system and established processes for receiving, editing, aggregating, confidentialising, finalising and disseminating data ensures a high level of coherence. International merchandise exports and imports are one of the first releases of monthly economic statistics by the ABS so there are limited published sources available to confront against these estimates. Consequently, considerable attention is given to ensuring the consistency of the monthly aggregates at the Harmonized System (HS) 6 digit and Standard International Trade Classification (SITC) 2 digit level with previous monthly results. The adoption of the HS and SITC also enable comparison of import and export data at the international level. Unusual movements in the monthly data may be verified or corrected using publicly available information (e.g. press reports, company websites) or following contact with the exporter, importer or their agents. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Because the ABS publishes a large range of economic data it strives to ensure coherence across these estimates. To achieve this, processes have been implemented including regular meetings between representatives from the various economic collections. At these meetings there are discussions which lead to agreement on the statistical treatment of recent economic events such as major resource projects. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Reconciliation : The ABS has conducted bilateral reconciliations of international merchandise trade data with the United States, Japan, New Zealand and the European Union. These studies demonstrated that the main reasons for differences in the statistics of Australia and its major trading partners are due to the conceptual and methodological factors underlying the compilation of the data. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Periodically, revisions analyses are undertaken to assess the quality of the ABS' international merchandise trade statistics. The most recent analysis is available in the ‘Data Quality – Accuracy' section of the ABS publication International Merchandise Trade, Australia: Concepts, Sources and Methods, 2018 ( cat. no. 5489.0) 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Information regarding revisions to international merchandise trade statistics are published in the relevant publication(s) on the ABS website. Special articles on issues relating to international merchandise trade statistics, such as revisions analysis, are also published on the ABS website. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Users of ABS' international merchandise trade statistics requiring more detailed statistics than those available on the ABS website, can submit a data request, and if available, it will be provided as a ‘user pays' service. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Contact can be made with the ABS regarding help finding statistics on the website, requesting data and providing feedback, by using the contact information outlined on the ‘ Contact us ' page of the ABS website. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs Information about the ABS and the range of services it provides and statistics it produces (including information on international merchandise trade statistics) can be found on the ABS website.
