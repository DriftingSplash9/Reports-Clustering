 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The GFS manual 2014, Public Sector Debt Statistics Guide 2013, External Debt Statistics Guide 2014 as well as provisions as per the Public Debt Management Act 2008 are complied with. Concepts and definitions for private sector external debt are in line with IMF's Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6) and External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (the Guide). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification of external debt is made in accordance with the requirements of the External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users. Gross External Debt Stock is classified as follows: The external debt data are classified into four sectors (general government, monetary authorities, deposit taking institutions, and other sectors) and reported into the following sub-categories: Short Term Money market instruments Loans Currency and deposits Trade credits Other debt liabilities Long Term Bonds and notes Loans Currency and deposits Trade credits Other debt liabilities The short-term/long-term classification is on an original maturity basis. External debt data may be classified by type of debt instruments in line with the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6) and the External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (the Guide). Deposits of nonresidents with domestic banks are included. Trade credits (except for public sector) owed to nonresidents are included Intercompany lending between entities in a direct investment relationship are separately identified in the total external debt position. 2.2.1 Scope Classification of external debt is made in accordance with the requirements of the External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users. Gross External Debt Stock is classified as follows: The external debt data are classified into four sectors (general government, monetary authorities, deposit taking institutions, and other sectors) and reported into the following sub-categories: Short Term Money market instruments Loans Currency and deposits Trade credits Other debt liabilities Long Term Bonds and notes Loans Currency and deposits Trade credits Other debt liabilities The short-term/long-term classification is on an original maturity basis. External debt data may be classified by type of debt instruments in line with the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6) and the External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (the Guide). Deposits of nonresidents with domestic banks are included. Trade credits (except for public sector) owed to nonresidents are included Intercompany lending between entities in a direct investment relationship are separately identified in the total external debt position. Scope of the data External debt shows the liabilities of institutional sectors (central government, local government, extra budgetary units, public corporations, monetary authority, other deposit taking institutions and other private sector including Global business companies) to the rest of the world. The borrowings of households may not be fully captured by existing sources and methods. External debt data measure both domestic and foreign currency debt owed to nonresidents. Intercompany lending between entities in a direct investment relationship is fully included in the gross external debt position. Short-term debt included in the gross external debt position is all debt of original maturity of one year or less. Positions in financial derivatives, equity securities, and equity capital are excluded from the gross external debt position. External debt include securities issued in the domestic markets by residents and purchased by nonresidents. The shortcomings to the coverage of private sector external debt have to large extent been addressed through the conduct of the Foreign Assets and Liabilities Survey and ESSNAC Survey and Other Financial Corporations Survey. Exceptions to coverage NA Unrecorded activity Public sector trade credit is not recorded. 2.4.1 Valuation Transactions originally denominated in foreign currencies are converted into Mauritian rupees using the prevailing rates of exchange. The conversion is made using the exchange rate on the day of the transaction obtained from the BOM. Debt stocks in foreign currencies, which are maintained at face values, are converted into Mauritian rupees using the exchange rate on valuation date. 2.4.2 Recording basis The debts are recorded as per the valuations stated at 2.4.1. Government's accounts are maintained on a cash basis. However, for computation of government budget deficit, interest on accrual basis is used. Appropriate adjustment is made for the difference between cash and accrual interest to come up with the government borrowing requirement. For recording of flows and debt stocks, the cash basis of accounting is used. Thus, any accrued interest at the end of the period that is not yet payable is not included in the external debt position. The debt stock is shown at face value . 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The outstanding debts of budgetary central governemnt and the extra budgetary units are compiled and disseminated on a gross basis. However, the computation of central government debt takes into account intersectoral and intrasectoral consolidations. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The sources for Government external debt data are loan agreements, disbursements and repayments notifications received from creditors and debt service reports from the BOM. The current source for compiling private external debt data by BOM are the banking records, which provides monthly data that are complemented by the FALS, ESSNAC Survey and Other Financial Corporations Survey. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The definitions as per GFSM 2014 are adopted and shared by the DMU, Statistics Mauritius and the BOM to ensure common understanding of terms. Data are available to allow disaggregation into subsectors, i.e budgetary central government, extra budgetary units, local government and financial and non-financial public corporations. Data obtained through banking records are incomplete since only the transaction conducted through bank transfers are recorded. Other elements of the transaction that would imply potentially different time of recording and size of the actual transaction cannot be obtained through banking records. Part of these information is available through FALS, ESSNAC and Other Financial Corporations Surveys. The BOM conducts seminars, training sessions and provides additional guidance on request to ensure that the banking returns and survey respondents submit data in line with prescribed definitions, scope, classification, valuation and time of recording. Guidelines are constantly reviewed to make them as user-friendly as possible. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data collection and processing are adequate to meet timeliness and periodicity for disseminating the external debt statistics.Respondents are made aware of the deadlines set for data reporting. The DMU maintains strict follow-up procedures, including direct communication with respondents, to ensure timely and complete receipt of debt data. 3.2.1 Source data assessment All the debt data, whether available internally or obtained from creditors, BOM, EBU and local authorities are assesd for their consistency and competencies. All the data are audited annually by the National Audit Office. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques None 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Since the survey data are available after a time lag, estimates are provided based on observed trends, available prior survey results and other relevant information. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Data are routinely compared with financial statements and press reports released by the organisations. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data All data submitted by extra budgetary units, local authorities and public corporations are subject to verification and validation. Where appropriate, a public sector organisation may be requested to submit a copy of bank statement certifying the loan amount, interest and the outstanding debt stock. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Any discrepancies and problems are sorted out and verified again 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses On a quarterly basis.
