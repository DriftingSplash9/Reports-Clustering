 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The concepts, definitions and procedures used in the compilation of external debt statistics as far as possible, in line with the guidelines contained in the 2013 External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users and in the sixth edition of the IMF Balance of Payments Manual, 2009 (BPM6). Gross external debt is defined as the external debt liabilities of residents to nonresidents and it includes principal and interest arrears, and not yet matured principal. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization External debt is classified by residents and non-residents sector and maturity. Resident sectors cover: public (monetary authorities and government) and private (banks, enterprises, households and NPISHs) sector. Non-resident sectors are broken-down by international institutions, foreign governments, other financial institutions and other creditors. External debt is shown by original and remaining maturity. 2.2.1 Scope External debt is classified by residents and non-residents sector and maturity. Resident sectors cover: public (monetary authorities and government) and private (banks, enterprises, households and NPISHs) sector. Non-resident sectors are broken-down by international institutions, foreign governments, other financial institutions and other creditors. External debt is shown by original and remaining maturity. Scope of the data Residency is determined in accordance with BPM6. External Debt Statistics covers liabilities of residents vis-a-vis nonresidents of the following sectors: central bank, general government, deposit-taking corporations and other sectors and following instruments: financial loans, SPV liabilities of the government, debt securities issued on international market (excluding residents’ holdings), long-term trade loans, intercompany loans and short-term bank deposits. The outstanding debt amount also includes the debt of the Kosovo and Metohija province under loan agreements concluded before the arrival of the KFOR mission. 2.4.1 Valuation External debt is valued at nominal value. External debt stock is published in euros, and for conversion of stock in other currency official foreign exchange rate (last day of reference period) is used. Flows are converted in euros applying the NBS official foreign exchange rate on the transactions day. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording of external debt is the principle of ownership i.e. liabilities arise at the data of change of economic ownership. External debt is recorded on due-for-payment basis; principal and interest arrears are included. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The grossing/netting procedures are broadly consistent with international guidelines and data are published in gross terms (gross external debt and claims) and upon request in net terms (net external debt). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Source data are obtained on loan-by-loan basis through prescribed forms under the general obligation that all external loans and credits transactions should be reported to the National Bank of Serbia. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation and time of recording are broadly in line with international standards. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data collection is monitored (and cross-checked with other data sources) to ensure that non respondents can be identified. Borrowers are obliged to submit reporting forms 10 days after the date of every transaction. Cut out date is 20 days after the end of the reference month. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data are regularly assessed and cross-checked for coverage and response error. General government external debt is cross-checked with data published by Serbian Public Debt Administration and banking sector external debt is cross-checked with monetary statistics data. The results of the assessments are for internal use only. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques If some differences appear between data collected in loan-by-loan data base and by other statistics (or administrative data), further investigation is conducted and if needed external debt data are adjusted for coverage, classification, timing, or valuation. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures There are no additional data adjustments. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Cross-checking techniques are implemented for verifying the accuracy of the data. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Statistical discrepancies in intermediate data are assessed and investigated. The difference between the beginning and end-period value of external debt data is equal to the difference between the disbursements and repayments of principal, currency changes and other changes in the observed period. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Statistical discrepancies and other potential indicators of problems in statistical outputs are monitored and investigated. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Analyses of revisions are regularly conducted and are for internal use. During the year previous quarterly data are revised with publishing of new quarter. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Studies and analyzes of the revisions are conducted as part of data quality control process. Studies and analyses of revisions are used internally. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request It is possible to provide unpublished but non-confidential statistics on official request.
