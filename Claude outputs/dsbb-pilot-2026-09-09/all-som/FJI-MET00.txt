 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The compilation is based on the following manuals: · "United Nations International Merchandise Trade Statistics Concepts and definitions 2010". · "United Nations Classification by Broad Economic Categories Rev. 5". · "United Nations Standard International Trade Classification Revision 4". 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The commodity structure of external trade flows of goods was analysed using various internationally adopted commodity classifications, which have different levels of detail and are based on different classification criteria. The Fiji Bureau of Statistics (FBoS) from 2000 used the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS) for the monthly and annual collection, compilation and dissemination of International Merchandise Trade Statistics. HS is based on the nature of the commodity which for analytical purposes is not most appropriate. Commodity categories more suitable for economic analysis are provided by the Standard International Trade Classification, Revision 4 (SITC, Rev.4) which classifies commodities according to their stage of production. FBoS also releases annually International Merchandise Trade Statistics using the Classification by Broad Economic Categories (BEC) that groups large economic classes of goods with reference to their end use. The three basic classes of goods are: · Capital goods; · Intermediate goods; and · Consumption goods. 2.2.1 Scope The commodity structure of external trade flows of goods was analysed using various internationally adopted commodity classifications, which have different levels of detail and are based on different classification criteria. Scope of the data The International Merchandise Trade Statistics report and release includes all goods, which added to or subtracted from the stock of material resources of Fiji by entering (imports) or leaving (exports) its economic territory. Goods in transit, that is goods entering and leaving Fiji with the exclusive purpose of reaching a third country are excluded, since they do not add to or subtract from the stock of material resources of Fiji through which they pass. Other exclusions are: Under imports · Temporary imports in Fiji, such as tourists’ effects, including vehicles, travellers’ samples, returnable containers, ships and aircraft engaged in international trade or trans-shipment goods. · Fish and other sea products landed in Fiji, from either extra territorial waters or the high seas, by Fiji vessels. · Goods, other than motor vehicles, imported for official representatives of overseas countries. · Passengers’ baggage (except vehicles) imported permanently. · Currency transactions in gold, silver and current coin. · Goods on loan. · Replacements, short-shipped or short-packed goods previously recorded. · Goods imported for use by foreign armed forces. Under exports · Goods consigned to Fiji forces or diplomatic representatives overseas. · Goods consigned for modification or repair. · Currency transactions in gold, silver and current coin. · Goods for foreign aid projects. · Replacements, short-shipped or short-packed goods previously recorded. · Returnable containers e.g. used gas cylinders. · Returnable samples. · Aircraft parts being consigned overseas for use in Fiji aircraft and unserviceable parts which have been removed from foreign aircraft being returned overseas. · Temporary trade items and tourists or travellers effects leaving Fiji. 2.4.1 Valuation Provisional monthly data in Fijian dollars on total exports (fob) and total imports (includes re-exports) (cif) are compiled by the Fiji Bureau of Statistics. The World Trade Organizations Agreement on Valuation was used as the basis for valuation of the international merchandise trade for statistical purposes. This valuation method applied to all goods flows. Imports The statistical value of imported goods is a cost, insurance and freight (CIF) value. This includes the transaction value of the goods, the value of services performed to deliver goods to the border of exporting country and the value of services performed to deliver goods from the border of the exporting country to the border of the importing country. Exports The statistical value of exported goods is a free on board (FOB) value. This includes the transaction value of the goods and the value of services performed to deliver the goods to the border of the exporting country. 2.4.2 Recording basis International Merchandise Trade Statistics is a customs-based data collection system, therefore the time of recording is sourced from the customs declaration, known as the Single Administrative Document (SAD). The SAD provides an approximation of the time the goods crossed the border, that is, the date when the goods entered or left the economic territory of Fiji. Customs records goods at the time they enter or leave the country using the General Trade System. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Provisional monthly data are compiled by the Fiji Bureau of Statistics and disseminated in the news release, "Statistical News: International Merchandise Trade Statistics". The data is sourced from the Customs Department of the Fiji Revenue and Customs Services (FRCS), from their manifest known as the Single Administrative Document (SAD). Soft copy of the raw data is sent to the Fiji Bureau of Statistics where it is processed using SAS. Customs records goods at the time they enter or leave the country using the General Trade System. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording It is assumed that corresponding import and export data between partner countries should be consistent after adjusting for cost, insurance and freight. This is not so and the reasons for the difference are: · Classification concepts and detail: o Destination/origin classification concepts in the presence of transshipment e.g. when a good is consigned through another country; and o Lack of destination/source country detail in published statistics. · Time of recording: A lag occurs between the shipment of an export and the receipt of an import. · Valuation: Possible inconsistent valuation of a given shipment between source and destination due to currency conversion. · Coverage: Free trade zones and bonded warehouses shipments, exclusion of military and government goods, value threshold for Customs registration of shipments and other goods missed by Customs are examples of coverage difference. · Processing errors: Many types could occur e.g. provisional entries not corrected, wrong punching of information etc. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Processed data were edited for accuracy. Some of the checks made were: · Unit value checks; · Trend analysis; · Country of Origin; · Country of Destination; and · Verification of major commodities traded by making phone calls to those authorities/producers concerned. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Request for unpublished data is received and disseminated upon approval from the Government Statistician.
