 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The analytical framework used to compile the national accounts of Togo is the 1993 System of National Accounts (SNA2008). Gross domestic product (GDP) is analyzed under the three approaches (production, expenditure, and revenue) starting from the 2007 accounts. The working nomenclature for the national accounts includes 44 industries at level 2 and 91 products at level 3 (the most detailed), including FISIM, territorial adjustment, and revenue reported in a suspense account (produits d'attente). It is indispensable to use the appropriate nomenclatures for producing the national accounts. This facilitates the processing of the data and the analysis of the economic accounts. There are two main categories of international nomenclature: The nomenclature of concepts specific to the SNA: these are four standard nomenclatures described by the SNA and used as such by all countries. They are related to the institutional, operations, other flows, and stocks sectors; Nomenclatures external to the SCN: the ones most frequently used in the national accounts, either by adapting them to the economic realities of each economy or through bridge tables, are the International Standard Industrial Classification of All Economic Activities (ISIC), the Central Product Classification (CPC), and the Customs Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS). The nomenclatures for activities and products officially used by Togo are the nomenclature of the Activity Classification of AFRISTAT Member States (NAEMA) and the Product Classification of AFRISTAT Member States (NOPEMA). The NAEMA and NOPEMA are adaptations of International Standard Industrial Classification of All Economic Activities (ISIC), Rev. 3. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization In the context of updating the national accounts, the activity and product classifications in the AFRISTAT (NAEMA and NOPEMA), derived from ISIC, Rev. 3, were adapted to the Togolese economy. Classification of the institutional sectors has also been harmonized. Government includes central government, local governments, and the national social security agency as well as national public entities. Scope of the data The institutional units covered are: nonfinancial and financial enterprises; individual entrepreneurs/households; (modern or informal), central and local governments, the social security agency as well as nonprofit institutions serving households. Unrecorded activity Illegal activities are not recorded. N.B. Togo is preparing to migrate to SNA 2008 during 2018. 2.4.1 Valuation Domestic operations are valued at market prices for the secondary and tertiary sectors. For the primary sector, the producer price is used. Imports are valued at c.i.f. prices (including the cost of insurance and freight); exports are valued at f.o.b. prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis Transactions are recorded on an accrual basis. However, the data on government revenue are recorded on the basis of collections. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Production Approach: Agriculture: Most data come from the Ministry of Agriculture, Livestock and Fisheries. Data on export crops (coffee, cacao, cotton) come from the technical units of the Ministry of Agriculture, Livestock and Fisheries and from public agencies responsible for marketing. Forestry: The data are provided by the Ministry of the Environment and Forest Resources. Fishing: The data come from the General Fisheries Directorate. Those referring to traditional fishing are considered unreliable, given the absence of a recent statistical source. Mining: The accounting data from modern companies are used. The segment for informal companies is estimated using a coefficient applied to the modern sector data for construction and public works. Manufacturing: The principle source is all the accounting data from modern companies. The informal sector is estimated by applying a coefficient to the data on the modern sector. Electricity and water: The accounting data from modern companies are used. The informal segment changes with the growth rate of the rural population. Construction and public works: The accounting data from modern companies and purchases of construction materials are used. Transportation and telecommunications: The accounting data from modern companies are used. The informal sector is also covered. Wholesale and retail trade: The accounting data from modern companies are used. The estimate for the informal sector is provided by applying a fixed coefficient. Financial intermediation: The accounting data from financial institutions and the monetary statistics of the Central Bank of West African States (BCEAO) are used. Nonmarket government agency services: The statistics on government finances and the annual reports of autonomous agencies are used. Nonmarket services of private agencies: The accounting data from entities in the sector benefiting from a State subsidy are taken into account. Expenditure Approach: Household final consumption: There is no recent direct source used by the national accounts. Calculated by balance. Government agency final consumption: The accounting data from sectoral entities are taken into account. Gross fixed capital formation: The statements of changes in financial position of modern companies and the government investment budgets are taken into account. Changes in stocks: Agricultural and livestock statistics and accounting data from modern companies are used. Imports and exports of goods: Balance of payments. Imports and exports of services: Balance of payments. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Company aggregates are compiled on the basis of statistics collected from samples. The list of companies includes the main companies by industries. A representativeness rate is calculated for the units studied based on knowledge regarding previous years. The inverse rate makes it possible to extrapolate the result at the national level for each industry. Primary sector aggregates are valued on the basis of the quantities produced, prices, and technical data on the industries. There is no cross-checking of data sources, nor reconciliation with other estimates. The representativeness rates are old as they date back to 1985. In the context of the ongoing overhaul of the national accounts, a harmonized methodology is being implemented that plays an important role in data crosschecking and consistency (consistency with public finance data, balance of payments data, etc.). The emphasis is on the exhaustiveness of the data; the compilation procedure includes discretionary elements ( techniques d’arbitrage) that make it possible to highlight missing or underestimated data.
