 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The objective of merchandise trade statistics is to register the cross-border movement of commodities between Germany and other countries. In principle, all physically incoming and outgoing commodities are recorded, including transactions handled without remuneration or on foreign accounts (for exceptions see chapter 2.2.1). Since January 1, 1993 German merchandise trade statistics consists of "Extra-EU trade statistics" and "Intra-EU trade statistics". Extra-EU trade statistics apply to trade with countries outside the European Union (non-member countries), Intra-EU trade statistics to trade transactions with EU partner countries. The results of these two statistics are published as the total merchandise trade of Germany. Germany publishes merchandise trade according to the definitions of both general trade and special trade. The practical difference between both systems is marginal and regards trade within free zones and customs warehouses. Germany complies with international rules specified for general trade in the UN Concepts and Definitions and for special trade in the EU Compiler's Guide on European statistics on international trade in goods. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification of Commodities: The classification of commodities for reporting imports and exports is the European Customs tariff and statistical nomenclature (Combined Nomenclature / CN) based on the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS). The CN is published by the FSO in Germany as Commodity Classification for Merchandise Trade Statistics (Warenverzeichnis / WA). In Chapter 99 special codes for simplified declarations are added – besides European codes the WA contains also some national codes for some assortments of goods according to German legislation. In Chapter 98 the WA encompasses specific commodity codes for complete industrial plants which may be applied under specific conditions and only with the permission of the FSO. The most important classifications for the publication of merchandise trade are o the 2-, 4-, 6- and 8-digit levels of the CN; o the commodity groups and subgroups of the food-processing industry and the manufacturing industry (EGW) (provides a long time series back until 1913, with the only revision in 2002); o the divisions and classes of the German Classification of Products for Production Statistics (GP), based on the European classification CPA (Classification of products by activity) and the European Prodcom list; o The items of the Standard International Trade Classification (SITC Rev. 4). The categories and sub-categories of the Broad Economic Categories BEC. Classification of countries and regions: The classification of countries and regions used for Merchandise Trade Statistics purposes is the Country Nomenclature for the External Trade Statistics of the Community and Statistics of Trade between Member States (Geonomenclature). Scope of the data Transaction coverage: All movements of goods physically entering or leaving the economic territory of Germany that add or subtract from the stock of material resources of Germany are included. Goods comprise all movable objects, including electric current. Specific goods as vessels, aircraft and spacecraft are also covered. For these goods the change of economic ownership is the relevant reporting criterion. For sea products, goods to or from offshore installations, goods delivered to vessels and aircraft, specific collection rules are foreseen as well. Geographic coverage: The statistical territory of Germany corresponds to the German customs territory plus Heligoland. Data coverage: The quantities and values of imported and exported commodities are published by commodites and partner countries The most important variables are the following: Value (for more information see valuation); Quantity; type of commodity (CN code); country of origin for imports (and for Intra-EU exports); Country of destination for exports. Estimates: Estimates are made for transactions below threshold values and underreporting of intra-EU trade due to late or missing response. Exceptions to coverage In accordance with the EU regulations on merchandise trade statistics, movements of goods that do not lastingly change the stock of goods of an economy are exempted from declaration. Exempted are monetary gold, means of payment in circulation, temporary movements of goods (e.g. transit trade, goods for fairs or exhibitions, means of transport except when they are themselves bought or sold), trade with own territorial enclaves, goods for and after repair, advertising material and commercial samples.. Customized software and software downloaded from the Internet are not considered to be goods but services. Unrecorded activity Smuggling is only included if it was detected. 2.4.1 Valuation In accordance with international standards, the value of goods is based on their value at the German border (statistical value). Generally, it is derived from the invoice value with some adjustments for transport costs free the German border (border-crossing value). Tariffs, taxes or other charges which have been levied on the occasion of import or export are not included in the statistical value. The statistical value of transactions that are not invoiced, e.g. processing, transactions without compensation or transactions between affiliated companies, is their market value, i.e. the value of the good in the case of a sale/ purchase between independent companies. 2.4.2 Recording basis The reporting period is generally the calendar month when exports and imports of goods take place, i.e. the month when the goods physically enter or leave the statistical territory of Germany. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data on movements of commodities across borders are recorded either via the customs authorities (trade with non-EU member countries) or by direct company declaration (trade with EU member states). These different collection systems can be described as follows: (Extrastat system): For imports or exports of commodities from or to non-member countries of the EU and territories outside the EU customs area, the statistical data is collected by the customs authorities. Usually, the German customs authorities require an electronic declaration, so the statistical data can be transmitted without delay to the Federal Statistical Office. The statistical reports are an integral part of the customs forms. Commodity consignments are only reported if they are commercial in nature and exceed a value of 1,000 EUR or a total weight of the consignment of 1,000 kilograms. (Intrastat system): The Intrastat collection refers to imports and exports from and to other EU member states. This commodity trade has to be reported by the traders directly and electronically to the FSO on a monthly basis. Non- or underreporting traders are identified by comparing the IMTS data to the VAT data of the tax authorities. Companies with a value of their intra-EU trade below a certain threshold are exempted from reporting. The threshold is determined by the EU coverage rate (95% for intra-EU exports; since 2022 no coverage rate for intra EU-imports). In Germany, reporting thresholds of EUR 1.000,000 for exports and EUR 3.000,000 for imports are applied. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The source data equals the methodology prescribed above. The data source for imports and exports of goods from or to non-EU-countries is the customs declaration. The trade with EU member states is collected via a specific data collection system, the 'Intrastat' system. Merchandise trade statistics shall record imports and exports of goods. The classification on which the declaration is based is the Combined Nomenclature (CN). The valuation of the goods transactions is done by the statistical value which is the value calculated at the national border. Pro-rata costs for transportation and insurance up to the German border have to be included. For exports, these are the proportional costs for the transport within Germany up to the border, exports are valued on a fob basis (free on board). And for imports, the proportional costs for the transport abroad up to the German border have to be considered, the cif value (cost, insurance, freight). The merchandise trade statistics refers to a monthly reference period. The information has to be provided for the calendar month when the movement of the goods took place. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness For the Extrastat system (trade with non-EU member states) the data is transmitted by customs to the FSO. Timeliness is ensured by the customs authorities. For the Intrastat system, the timeliness has to be ensured by the FSO. The enterprises are expected to declare their trade within 10 working days after the end of the reference month. If they do not comply, they are reminded of their obligations. If a company totally or partially fails to declare its trade after being reminded, the company can be fined. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The data is checked for plausibility. These plausibility checks use amongst other things minimum and maximum values for statistical value and weight by commodity code, possible combinations of commodity codes and partner countries. These parameters are evaluated and – if necessary – adjusted. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques According to EU legislation, estimates for trade below the threshold and for non-response are mandatory for Intrastat. In addition to the import and export results in actual values, the volume, which enables a conclusion about the real development of merchandise trade is also calculated. Seasonal adjustment: For seasonal adjustment the method applied in the area of merchandise trade statistics is J-DEMETRA+, based on the X-13-ARIMA procedure developed by the U.S. Bureau of the Census. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The published results contain estimates for the trade below the Intrastat declaration thresholds and for non-response. The estimates for non-response are replaced in the revision process by real data, collected from delayed reports. The total results are also published as seasonally adjusted data. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Reported data are checked for plausibility, using different data sources and plausibility checks. To ensure an early data dissemination, estimation of missing data is needed. These data are revised several times and completed during the time by integrating real data form delayed reports. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data During the production of the monthly results, the reported data are checked for plausibility and for outliers. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Since 2009, output oriented plausibility checks are used. On a regular basis, mirror analyses with EU partners are carried out. Asymmetry studies with other partner countries are also carried out. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses The amount and direction of revisions are permanently observed. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses The amount and direction of revisions are permanently observed. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request The FSO also provides formerly unpublished but non-confidential statistics for researchers on request and tailors data tables according to customer needs. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points All publications containing merchandise trade data include information on methodology and contact points for providers of statistical information.
