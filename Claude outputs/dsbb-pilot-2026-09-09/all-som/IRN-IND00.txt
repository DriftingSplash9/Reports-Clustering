 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The production index measures the variation in the production of large manufacturing establishments by industrial groups with the Iranian year 1383 (2004) as a base year. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Sectorization used is as follows: (1) Food products and beverages; (2) Tobacco products; (3) Textiles; (4) Wearing apparel, dressing and dyeing of fur; (5) Tanning and dressing of leather, manufacture of luggage, handbags, saddlers, harness and footwear; (6) Wood and products of wood and cork, expect furniture, articles of straw and plaiting materials; (7) Paper and paper products; (8) Publishing, printing, and reproduction of recorded media; (9) Coke, refined petroleum products and nuclear fuel; (10) Chemicals and chemical products; (11) Rubber and plastic products; (12) Other non-metallic mineral products; (13) Basic metals; (14) Fabricated metal products expect machinery and equipment; (15) Machinery and equipment n.e.c.; (16) Office, accounting, and computing machinery; (17) Electrical machinery and apparatus n.e.c.; (18) Radio, television and communication equipment and apparatus; (19) Medical, precision and optical instruments, watches and clocks; (20) Motor vehicles, trailers and semi-trailers; (21) Other transport equipment; and, finally, (22) Furniture, manufacturing n.e.c. This sectorization is based on the International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC), Rev. 3.1. Scope of the data The production index is based on a survey of 2500 large manufacturing establishments drawn from different industrial groups. A large manufacturing establishment is defined as one which employs at least 100 employees on average in a year. Such establishments cover 75 per cent of total manufacturing value added in Iran. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Value added by the large manufacturing, not gross output, is captured by the Production Index. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs About 360 data collectors collect data, based on a questionnaire, from large manufacturing establishments. The list of such establishments is given to the CBI by the Ministry of Industries and Mines. These data are sent to the CBI, currently, as hard-copies as well through on-line. After due verification, the data are inputted into a software developed for this purpose. Then the software works out the production index. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Source data are collected within 6 weeks from the end of the reference quarter. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data are checked and verified by the CBI staff. Whenever questionable movements in data are identified, the data collector returns to the establishment for checking and verification. After that, the numbers are corrected, if necessary, and inputted into the software. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The production index is compiled using the standard Laspeyres formula. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses No revision studies are published.
