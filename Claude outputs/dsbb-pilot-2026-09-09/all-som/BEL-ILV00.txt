 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The concepts and definitions used are in line with international statistical standards: IMF's Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6) IMF's updated Guidelines for a data template on international reserves and foreign currency liquidity The data are compiled in accordance with the Statistical treatment of the Eurosystem's international reserves, October 2000 . The reserve assets must: (i) be under the effective control of the National Bank of Belgium and (ii) refer to highly liquid, marketable, and creditworthy non-euro area currency-denominated claims on nonresidents of the euro area as well as gold, Special Drawing Rights (SDRs) and the reserve position in the IMF of Belgium. Deposits with banks located in Belgium and/or in other euro area Member States are therefore not considered as part of the official reserves. Reserve assets are compiled on a gross basis without any netting of reserve-related liabilities (with the exception of those reserve assets included in the sub-item of financial derivatives, which are recorded on a net basis). All reversible gold transactions are treated as collateralized loans and recorded accordingly in the national reserves statistics. For the "International reserves and foreign currency liquidity" template, also the liquid assets in foreign currency of the government sector are taken into account. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification/sectorization is consistent with internationally accepted standards and guidelines. Scope of the data Institutional coverage :National Bank of Belgium. Instrument coverage :Monetary gold, SDRs, Reserve position in the Fund, Foreign exchange and liquid foreign currency assets. 2.4.1 Valuation The valuation is based on market prices by using: (i) the prevailing market prices at the time when the transaction takes place for transactions; and (ii) the closing mid-market prices at the end of the appropriate period for holdings, exception made for securities. Here the price of the second to last working day of the month is used, according to the best local practices. 2.4.2 Recording basis The conversion of the original currencies into euros is based on the monthly average exchange rate for transactions and other flows. The conversion of the original currencies into euros for outstanding amounts is based on the monthly end-of-month exchange rate. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures No grossing/netting procedures are applied. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs "Accounting division" and "Back-office division" of the National Bank of Belgium as well as the Government services. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data—including censuses, sample surveys, and administrative records—are routinely assessed, e.g., for coverage, sample error, response error, and non-sampling error; the results of the assessments are monitored and made available to guide statistical processes None 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Additional statistics can be obtained from BOP@nbb.be 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Contact information is published.
