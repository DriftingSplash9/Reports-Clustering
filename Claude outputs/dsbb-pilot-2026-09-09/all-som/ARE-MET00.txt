 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The key concepts and definitions of trade in goods are in line with the recommendations contained in the International Merchandise Trade Statistics: Concepts and Definitions (IMTS 2010). However, the strict definition of the concept of special trade system is used. Beginning this year 2018, we will move to the general trading system this month June 2018 and will publish the annual trade data 2017, on the "General Trading System" accordance with international recommendations. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Commodities Are Classified following the 2012 version of the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding Systems (H.S.) of the World Customs Council (six digits). The basic data show a further two digits that are specific to the countries belonging to the Gulf Cooperation Council. Countries are Classified according to the list drawn up by the United Nations Statistics Division for use in international merchandise trade statistics. 2.2.1 Scope Commodities Are Classified following the 2012 version of the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding Systems (H.S.) of the World Customs Council (six digits). The basic data show a further two digits that are specific to the countries belonging to the Gulf Cooperation Council. Countries are Classified according to the list drawn up by the United Nations Statistics Division for use in international merchandise trade statistics. Unrecorded activity The international foreign trade statistics do not contain adjustments to capture unrecorded trade. 2.4.1 Valuation Imports and exports are Reported at their CIF (cost, insurance, freight) and FOB. (free on board) valuations, respectively. Values expressed in foreign currencies are Converted into UAE Dirhams (AED) using official Exchange rates provided by the CBU. 2.4.2 Recording basis The time of recording is the moment that goods are brought into free circulation (Imports), or taken out of free circulation (Exports). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Exclusively customs records received from the various emirate services are being used. The emirate services aggregate the customs declarations into groups, so that the unit record transactions are not available to compilers. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The source data follow the definitions, scope, Classifications, Valuation, and Time of Recording required by the statistics, except for coverage issues mentioned earlier. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The timeliness of the source data differs depending on the emirate customs service. Some emirate customs services provide the source data within one month, while others have a timeliness of three to four months. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The FCSA conducts a routine assessment of the source data, focusing on incomplete information, incorrect coding, and improbable prices (values divided by weights). 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The data are aggregated from basic groupings of transactions supplied by the emirate customs services. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses The international merchandise trade statistics are revised, And any changing and auditing the data or missing we correct it together with the customs departments and other sources 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Estimates of the likely publication values are provided to all users at their request. At request, further breakdowns by type of commodities are also made available at no cost.
