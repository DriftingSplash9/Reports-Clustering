 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The external trade statistics are broken down by product following the international nomenclature of the Harmonized System, using the 10-character code, and in accordance with the partner country table (origin, destination). Flows of merchandise captured in the external trade statistics are subject to two classifications: special trade statistics and general trade statistics. The key reference for foreign trade data for Niger is the concept of special trade, as defined in the UN world standard on foreign trade statistics. Thus, special import trade records incoming merchandise from abroad, declared for domestic final and intermediate consumption. Special export trade records exports, goods re-exported after release from temporary admission, temporary exports, exports leaving bonded plants, and exports from licensed in-bond factories. It should be noted that goods transshipped or crossing the country in transit are not recorded as special trade or general trade. Scope of the data The external trade statistics record all merchandise trade declared at customs between Niger and the rest of the world. All merchandise entering or leaving the territory, whether or not it is taxable, is recorded in the statistics. The quantity and value of the traded merchandise is processed using in-house and EUROTRACE software. The value of merchandise traded is quoted in CFA francs. The data are regularly made available to the following units: Directorate of Economic Analysis and Forecasting, Directorate of Regional Integration, National Economic Policy Committee (CNPE), WAEMU, Niamey agency of BCEAO, economic operators, Chamber of Commerce, Agriculture, Industry, and Handicrafts of Niger (CCAIAN), Nigerien Commuters Council (CNUT), Ministry of Trade, Ministry of Finance, Nigerien External Trade Center. A National Customs Liaison Committee to monitor external trade statistics, including representatives of the DAEEFP, BCEAO, DSCN, Directorate General of Customs, CNCE, Chamber of Commerce, External Trade Directorate, etc., was established to make decisions regularly on external trade data, on the problems related to their production, and to find solutions to those problems. This national committee meets once a year, based on the availability of external trade data. Unrecorded activity However, owing to their peculiar nature, some transactions are not recorded in the foreign trade statistics, namely: used personal effects carried by travelers, effects for the personal use of members of the diplomatic corps and foreign nationals on mission in Niger, or personal belongings of Nigerien representatives overseas, monetary gold, securities, currency (coins and notes) issued, military equipment. 2.4.1 Valuation Imports are reported c.i.f. (cost-insurance-freight) and exports are reported f.o.b. (free on board). The data disseminated are the special trade statistics (exports f.o.b., imports c.i.f.), but general trade data can be consulted at the DSCN. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The Directorate of Statistics and National Accounts of the Ministry of Planning (DSCN) is responsible for the production and dissemination of external trade data in Niger. The source of the gross data is the Ministry of Finance's Directorate General of Customs. The data collected from this directorate are checked, processed, and disseminated by the DSCN. The information processed and disseminated includes data from the computerized customs offices as well as the others. There is some delay in the capture of data from the customs declarations of non-computerized offices. The DSCN proceeds monthly to collect customs data on electronic media from the Technical Units of the Directorate General of Customs. These data from customs declarations are processed to produce annual external trade statistics. These data are provided by product, country, or other possible cross-sections. None None
