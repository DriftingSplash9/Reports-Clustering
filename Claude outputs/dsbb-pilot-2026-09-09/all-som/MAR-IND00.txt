 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Concept: assessment of the change in the volume of production, based on a given level of outputs, and sometimes inputs or production times, reference technology, a sample of producing enterprises. The production index is a quantitative index (aggregate volume) - Base 100 = 2015. The Laspeyres formula with fixed based and fixed weights is used for the calculation. The industrial, energy, and mining production index (base 100: 2015) replaced the base 100: 2010 index. The calculation of this index is based on changes in both the population of producing enterprises (start-ups, closings, mergers etc.) and the structure of production and value added. This statistical indicator is calculated, like its predecessors, for 5 levels of the Moroccan Industrial Classification, namely: products, activities, sub-industries, industries, and the sector as a whole. Definition: assessment of the change in the volume of production of a fixed group of products (sample), broadly defined (similarity with cross-section series). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification : classification of enterprises and products according to the Moroccan classification (NMA), based on ISIC, Revision 4, in use since 2010. Scope of the data Sectoral coverage: reference population of production units consisting of enterprises in the manufacturing, mining, and energy sectors. The production index covers more than 1,500 enterprises operating in the industry, energy, and mining sectors. Of this total, 585 enterprises are surveyed directly. Each month such that each enterprise is visited once in two months. For energy and mining industries and other industrial units, including flour mills, sugar factories, and cement plants, missing production statistics are obtained from the supervisory agencies. Coverage of products: production covered for base year 2015: more than 70 percent of the value for industry and practically 100 percent for mines and energy. 2.4.1 Valuation Output is recorded by quantity. 2.4.2 Recording basis The weights for industries and sub-industries are calculated on the basis of value added at market prices, whereas the weights for products and activities are calculated using production values in the base year. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources: The source data are collected from certain supervisory agencies and from a representative sample of industrial enterprises in an ongoing monthly survey. Source of weights: national accounts for the weights of aggregates, the Ministry of Industry, Mines, and Energy for products and activities. Sample size: 585 enterprises + administrative statistics and 436 sets of products. Selection of products: by significance of the value within the activity. Selection of units: The enterprises included in the calculation of this volume index were selected on the basis of the significance of their production values within the activity. As a result, most industrial production and all mining and energy production are taken into consideration. Data collection methods: Data are collected from certain supervisory agencies and a representative sample of industrial enterprises in an ongoing monthly survey. The data are collected during personal visits to the enterprise's monthly and/or by mailing out questionnaires; the products are specified. Data on production come from the monthly survey of a sample of enterprises in these sectors. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Nature of indicators used: quantitative assessments of production, quantitative assessments of inputs, manufacturing time. Nature of weights: value added of sub-industries and industries and value of production for products and activities. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Procedures concerning non responses : The response forms filled out by the survey staff report on non responses and business closings. The response rate is close to 70 percent in numerical terms, and nearly 80 percent in value terms; the estimation procedures are based on the results of the survey of current economic conditions and observed trends. Treatment of missing prices: The imputation methods are used. Choice of replacement products: the procedure for selecting a replacement product is followed when a similar product is available. Adjustments for quality differences: this adjustment procedure is not used. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Calculation of lower-level indices: methods used to combine basic observations of production to obtain the first-level index: weighted ratio of current/base period quantities. Aggregation: the base indices aggregation formula used to calculate category indices is the Laspeyres formula. Alignment of the value of weights and the base period: The production weights are those of the base period. The weights for industries and sub-industries are calculated on the basis of value added at factor cost, whereas the weights for products and activities are calculated using production values in the base year. Link between the reweighted index and the historical index: procedure used to link the newly weighted index, after updating the weights, to the previous series in order to produce a historical series: selective linkage (connection to the last point: ponctuel linkage). Reference period: 2015 is the period for which the index equals 100. The current base 100: 2015 index includes an expanded field of observed enterprises and products. Period covered by current weights: fixed weights for base year 2015. Frequency of weight updates: 5 years between updates except for the most recent base. None
