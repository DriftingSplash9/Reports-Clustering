 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The framework of the national accounts is based on the "System of National Accounts 1968" ("1968 SNA"). The Department of Statistics produces and disseminates the following data on an annual basis: Gross Domestic Product (GDP) at current prices by: Industrial activity 13 major economic activities 11 sub-activities 2 detail activities Type of expenditure Final consumption Private [household] consumption General government consumption Gross capital formation 3 items of gross fixed capital formation Exports less imports of goods and services Gross Domestic Product (GDP) estimates at constant prices by: Source of production 13 major economic activities 11 sub-activities 2 detail activities Other accounts: Gross National Product (Gross National Income) and Saving Definitions The concepts used follow the "1968 SNA". Adjustments are made to approach the concepts of the “1993 SNA.” These include: Using basic prices to value output; Adopting the “1993 SNA” recommendations with respect to the treatments of mineral exploration and computer software and data bases as capital formation; and Including financial intermediation services indirectly measured (FISIM) in total estimates. (FISIM is not allocated to industry as recommended in “SNA 1993.”) GDP is compiled from the production approach as the difference between total output at basic prices and intermediate input at market (purchasers') prices. GDP by expenditure is the sum of final uses of goods and services in the total economy, less the value of imports of goods and services. Producer units are grouped according to principal economic activity. The components of GDP by expenditure follow the standard definitions for final uses provided in the "1968 SNA". 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Economic activities are classified using the International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC) of All Economic Activities, revision 3. The 13 activities covered are different from the ISIC presentation by combining some sectors and showing others separately. They comprise (with ISIC categories shown in parentheses) Agriculture (A-B), Mining and quarrying (C), Manufacturing (D), Electricity and water (E), Construction (F), Wholesale and retail trade (G), Hotels and restaurants (H), Transportation (I:60-62), Communication (I:64), Banks and insurance (J), Real estate and housing (K), Government services (L), and Other services (M:93). Scope of the data The national accounts cover the entire territory of St. Lucia. In principle, all resident institutional units plus the rest of the world are included in compiling GDP. However, own-account consumption of agricultural products and sales through informal channels are not covered by the available sources of data. The problem of capturing informal construction and distribution activity is addressed by taking a commodity flow approach. For construction, the value of output is equated with the value of inputs into construction. These inputs comprise imported and locally produced construction materials, trade and transport margins, and labor costs. Output for retail/wholesale trade, given by the gross margin, is computed as a fraction of the value of products entering the distribution system. Unrecorded activity An estimate of non-market output of Other crops (besides Bananas) is made to account for production for own consumption and sales through informal channels. No adjustments are made for illegal activities. 2.4.1 Valuation Output and value added (GDP) by industrial activity are recorded for St. Lucia at producers' prices, which are defined as market (purchasers') prices less invoiced trade and transportation margins. Data are published by industry for output at factor cost, which is derived from output at producers' prices by subtracting taxes on products and production and adding subsidies for products and on production. GDP by final use is valued at market prices, which is equivalent to the "SNA 1993" purchasers' price valuation concept. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: As the production account is based mostly on shipments and production values, most transactions are recorded in the source data, comprising financial statements, on an accrual basis. An exception is the government data in the expenditure account, which are recorded on a cash basis. Recording period: Data are compiled for the calendar year. However, a number of enterprises compile accounts on a fiscal accounting year. Their annual data are included in the national accounts for a given calendar year if their fiscal year begins before July 1 of that year. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Production approach: * Agriculture: Output of and intermediate input for bananas are obtained from the St. Lucia Banana Corporation (SLBC) and the Windward Islands Banana Development and Export Company (WIBDECO). Output is currently estimated on the basis of cost of production. Outputs of and intermediate inputs for other crops and livestock comprise estimates of marketed and non-marketed components obtained from the Ministry of Agriculture. Annual inter-census population data on charcoal consumption and Ministry of Agriculture sources on timber production supply the data on the output and intermediate input of forestry activities. Fishing output and intermediate consumption are derived as direct estimates from Ministry of Agriculture sources. Import data from the customs service provides a supplementary source of commodity flow information for inputs unique to or principally used in intermediate consumption for other (non-banana) crops, livestock, and fishing. * Mining and quarrying: There is no mining activity. Estimates of output intermediate consumption for quarrying are obtained from the annual National Establishment Survey. * Manufacturing: Output and intermediate consumption are obtained primarily from the annual National Establishment Survey, a monthly industrial production survey, and customs records on exports of tax-exempt items. * Electricity and water: Direct estimates of output, intermediate input, and value added for electricity and water are obtained from the financial statements of the St. Lucia Water and Sewage Authority and St. Lucia Electricity Services, Ltd, as part of the National Establishment Survey. * Construction: Output is measured as the total cost of inputs from customs data on imports, production survey data on locally produced construction materials, and the wages of construction labor. The value of trade and transport margins on inputs that is included to compute output prices is estimated from ad hoc surveys of building contractors and quantity surveyors. Value added is computed as a fraction of measured output. The ratio of value-added to output is estimated from a sample of construction companies. * Wholesale and retail trade: The gross margins (wholesale/retail output) for various classes of goods are estimated from a series of benchmark surveys of wholesale and retail establishments, and from administrative data on statutory price controls. The ratio of value added to output is derived from the annual National Establishment Survey for distributive activities. * Hotels and restaurants: Output of accommodation services in current and constant prices are estimated from data on number of tourists in paid accommodation, average length of stay, and average expenditure per day on paid accommodation from regular visitor expenditure surveys. The ratio of value added to output is estimated from the hotels and restaurants component of the annual National Establishment Survey. * Transport, storage, and communications: Output and value added of road transport is calculated using the number of vehicles registered obtained from the St. Lucia Transport Board, the CPI, and data from a benchmark survey conducted by the Statistical Department for the Transport Board on annual revenue and intermediate consumption for each of the following transportation activities: commuter and tour buses, taxis, self-hire vehicles, and freight trucks and vans. Output and value added for sea transport are directly estimated using data from the National Establishment Survey. Output and value added for air transport are obtained from the same source. Output and value added for post and communications derive from the accounts of the Cable and Wireless Company and the General Post Office. * Banks and insurance: Data for banking are obtained from the annual national accounts establishment survey and an Eastern Caribbean Central Bank commercial banking survey. Data for insurance come from Insurance Returns collected for regulatory purposes by the Registrar of Insurers. * Real estate and housing: Output and value added of real estate are directly estimated from the annual national accounts establishment survey. For owner occupied dwellings, output is measured by rent imputed as the stock of dwellings multiplied by the average rental value from the most recent (1991) census estimates. Intermediate consumption is estimated as one month's rent in the census year. In non-census years, the housing stock is revalued using data on rental value by type of dwelling from the real estate industry. * Government services: Value added is computed using the income approach, and comprises labor costs, National Insurance Scheme (NIS) payments, and other payments from government and NIS budget and administrative accounting records. * Other services: Estimates for professional and business services, private health, education, and entertainment activities are based on data received from a variety of sources including the National Insurance Scheme and the Inland Revenue Department. Expenditure approach: * Private final consumption: This component of expenditure is determined as the residual of GDP at market prices less Government final consumption, Gross capital formation, and net exports of goods and services. As increase in stocks (change in inventories) is not separately estimated, this method effectively includes the inventory change component of capital formation in private consumption. * Government final consumption: Consumption of general government services is defined as output less the value of commodity and non-commodity sales and of own-account capital formation. The source data are government budgetary and administrative records. * Gross fixed capital formation: Capital formation is compiled from customs sources for transport and other equipment using a commodity flow approach, as there is little domestic production of these items. Construction is compiled via a commodity flow approach, as in the treatment of the construction industry in the description of the production approach to compiling GDP above. * Increase in stocks: As noted under private consumption, there is no separately identified estimate for this item in expenditure on GDP. * Exports and imports of goods and non-factor services: These data are taken directly from the Balance of Payments compiled by the ECCB. See the comprehensive framework and categories and indicators for the External Sector for details on the compilation of net exports of goods and services. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Production approach: * Coverage adjustments: Various methods are used for the treatment of missing values of output and intermediate consumption for individual establishments in the National Establishment Survey and other sources. Among these are estimating current intermediate consumption on the basis of its past share in an establishment's output, estimating output on the basis of the past ratio of output to selected cost data, extrapolating the most recent past observed level of output, intermediate input, and value added on the basis of growth rates observed either in the past or currently in reported data for similar establishments. For non-banana crops and livestock, adjustments are made to market output to cover production for own consumption and informal sales on the basis of a factor provided by the Agriculture Department. These factors are designed to gross-up annual estimates of purchases of crop and livestock products by the large retail food outlets that are surveyed by that Department, which comprise about 40 percent of total food sales, to cover smaller outlets. Commodity flow approaches to output estimation are used for construction and wholesale/retail trade to limit potential coverage problems from incomplete survey coverage of informal and/or non-reporting establishments and vendors. * Estimation of current price data: Value added (GDP) by industry is the difference between output and intermediate consumption. For banana crops, quarrying, electricity and water, air and sea transport, post and communications, and banking and insurance, output and intermediate consumption are compiled from accounting records sampled in the National Establishment Survey or obtained from administrative sources. The output of banking is measured as net interest and other income, where net interest represents the imputed service charge for Financial Intermediation Services Implicitly Measured (FISIM) in the "SNA". There are insufficient data to allocate this imputation to intermediate and final consumption according to the use of financial intermediation services by enterprises and by final consumers. Consequently, aggregate intermediate consumption includes payment of net interest income to a notional industry. This approach is consistent with the "1968 SNA", although allocation to using sectors is recommended in the more recent "SNA 1993". For general government, value added is compiled from the income approach, rather than as the difference between output and intermediate consumption. For manufacturing and (commercial) real estate, the level of output and intermediate consumption are estimated from the annual National Establishment Survey. For construction, output is estimated using a commodity flow method, as the sum of production costs for materials and labor unique to the industry, plus trade and transport margins for those items. For wholesale and retail trade, output is also calculated via a commodity flow method as the product of a gross margin to output ratio estimated from a benchmark establishment survey, and the total value of goods entering distribution compiled as the sum of domestic goods output and imports. For non-banana crops, livestock, timber, fishing, hotels and restaurants, and road and sea transport, output is produced from estimates of quantity produced multiplied by average price. For non-banana crops, livestock, and timber, intermediate input is estimated via a commodity flow method as the value of imported inputs unique to agriculture and not used in banana production. For construction, wholesale and retail trade, hotels and restaurants, and road transport (in non-benchmark years), value added is estimated as the product of an estimated ratio of value added to output from a sample survey, either the annual National Establishment Survey or an ad hoc or special collection, multiplied by estimated total output for the industry. For residential housing, output is estimated for renters and imputed for owner occupants as average rent times the housing stock by type of dwelling. Intermediate consumption is estimated as one month's rent. The housing stock is only directly measured in census years. In non-census years the housing stock by type of dwelling is derived by extrapolating the census year stock for each type of dwelling by the rate of growth in the stock between the two most recent census years. Current year average rent is then applied to this estimate of the housing stock by dwelling type to obtain current year aggregate rent. Value added is estimated as 11/12 of aggregate rent. * Estimation of constant price data: One of two methods is used for compiling constant price data, depending on the industry. In the first method, constant price value added is computed by deflating current price value added by a price index of commodities produced for agriculture, livestock, fishing, portions of manufacturing, and telecommunications. For general government services, value added is deflated by an index of labor input costs for the wage and salary component of government expenditure combined with the CPI for the goods and services component to obtain constant price value added. Deflation of current price value added by a price index of inputs is also used for construction and wholesale and retail trade. In the second method, constant price value added is computed by extrapolating a reference year current price estimate of value added by an output volume index of physical indicators for forestry, mining and quarrying, portions of manufacturing, electricity and water, hotels and restaurants, road, sea, and air transport, post, banks, insurance, and real estate and owner occupied dwellings. Expenditure approach: * Coverage adjustments: There are no adjustments in the sources and estimates of expenditure on GDP not already mentioned under the production approach. * Estimation of current price data: Private consumption is determined as the residual of GDP less government consumption, gross capital formation, and exports less imports of goods and services. It thus effectively includes increase in stocks (change in inventories). Gross fixed capital formation comprises investment in equipment and structures. Equipment investment is estimated via a commodity flow method principally using data on imports, and structures investment is the gross output of the construction industry. There are no adjustments to equipment investment to include set-up costs. As noted in Nature of basic data sources above, exports and imports of goods and services derive from the Balance of Payments prepared by the ECCB. * Estimation of constant price data: GDP at constant prices is derived from the production approach as described above. Private consumption is determined residually after removing from GDP at constant prices the non-private consumption (and inventory change) expenditure components at constant prices. These components of expenditure at constant prices are estimated via deflation. The deflator for government consumption is a wage index for government employees. For gross fixed capital formation, the deflator for transportation equipment investment is the transportation and communications index from the CPI, and for other equipment investment is the all-items CPI. For the construction component of capital formation, the deflator is the same as that for the construction industry in the production approach to GDP compilation. The deflators for exports and imports are, respectively, the world export and import unit value indices published in the IMF's "International Financial Statistics." Reconciliation: As the sum of private consumption and increase in stocks (change in inventories) is determined as a residual both in the current and constant price accounts, the estimates from the production and expenditure approaches are not independently determined and reconcile by definition. Exports and imports of goods and services used in the calculation of GDP and net factor incomes from abroad used in the calculation of GNP (GNI) are taken from the balance of payments, and therefore reconcile with those data by definition. Frequency of major revisions: A comprehensive revision of methodology and changes in concepts and classifications took place in 1996-97. The principal areas of improvement were in the methodologies used for compiling GDP for Transportation, Agriculture, Hotels and restaurants, and General government services via the production approach. A revised time series is available for the period 1977-1997. * Base year and update cycle: The year of reference for constant price series is 1990; in principle, it is updated on a 5 -year cycle with a 5 to 8 year lag.
