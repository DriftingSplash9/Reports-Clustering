 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Data is compiled following methodology established in IMF´s Government Finance Statistics Manual (GFSM 2014). The release follows a statement of operations structure, which consists of a summary of government's transactions, detailing revenues, expenses and net investment in nonfinancial assets. The item “financing” is obtained using "below-the-line" criterion and corresponds to the nominal variation of the central government net debt balance, excluding some asset adjustments (such as privatization revenues from privatization and incorporation of previous debt). The methodology is described in Manual de Estatísticas Fiscais (the fiscal statistics handbook), available at BCB's website (www.bcb.gov.br). 2.2.1 Scope Data is compiled following methodology established in IMF´s Government Finance Statistics Manual (GFSM 2014). Scope of the data Data covers the following components: The budgetary central government subsector comprises all units included in the federal budget coverage (fiscal and social security budget). It includes: a) legislative, judiciary, and executive government federal bodies; and b) two social security funds: Regime Próprio de Previdência Social (RPPS), which covers public sector employees, and Regime Geral de Previdência Social (RGPS), that covers private sector workers. BCB is not included in the coverage, once it is included on the public corporations subsector. State Government subsector comprises budgetary units of 26 states and 1 federal district (with status of a state). Local Government subsector comprises 5570 municipalities. The data collection process has a response rate that exceeds 90%. Statistical methodology is applied to extrapolate the coverage to the whole of municipalities. BCB provides interest revenues and expenses. The data on borrowing requirements included in the net aquisition of financial assets and net incurrence of liabilities ("below-the-line") cover the operations of non-financial institutions of the direct and indirect administration, including social security. The Fundo Remanescente do PIS/Pasep and the Fundo de Garantia por Tempo de Serviço (FGTS) are not included in the borrowing requirements. Transactions are classified according to the instrument, but as the assets of other accounts receivable and liabilities of other accounts payable are not computed, their transactions affect deposits only when effectively received/paid. 2.4.1 Valuation The stages of budgetary and financial execution of expenditures are: preparation of budgetary proposal, enactment and publication of the LOA, bidding, commitments, production of goods or services by contractors, settlement, and payment. Expenditure commitments refer to the share of the budget to any given expenditure, with prior indication of creditor and amount. Settlement is the recognition that the expenditure was executed as agreed and acknowledges that the debt as net and accurate. The next and final step is payment, which is carried out by the issue of a bank order in favor of the creditor. Expenditures are recorded at the time of the settlement and there can be lags between settlement and effective disbursement. Considering that an expenditure settlement represents the acknowledgment that it has been properly executed, as well as the right of the creditor to receive the agreed amount, settlements are considered a good proxy for valuing expenditures and are used to create an accrual indicator, including those referring to commitments unsettled in previous years. Revenues are compiled in a cash basis in all statements, except for interest revenues. Nominal interest is appropriated according to the accrual basis. Nominal interest of the central government does not include the BCB. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data for central government comes mainly from Sistema Integrado de Administração Financeira do Governo Federal (SIAFI), the central government's integrated financial management system. Data for regional governments comes form accounting Balances Matrix, Siconfi (System of Financial and Accounting Information of the Brazilian Public Sector), RREO (Summarized Statement of Budgetary Execution) and Transparency Portals. The primary source for obtaining data from states, municipalities and the Federal District as of 2014 is the FINBRA annual accounts base, from the System of Financial and Accounting Information of the Brazilian Public Sector - Siconfi. This database compiles accounting and budgetary information for all Federation Units, and for the present work, in particular, information on budget revenues and expenditures was extracted, as well as the respective balance sheet accounts. Despite the legal requirement, less than 10% of municipalities do not comply with the data submission process. In this way, statistical techniques are applied to extrapolate the coverage to the complete set of municipalities. This methodology uses data from other sources, such as transparency portals and other government platforms. As of 2019, the data used for state governments were extracted from the Accounting Balances Matrix, which consists of a large mass of data extracted by the entities of the federation directly from their accounting-financial systems and forwarded to the System of Financial and Accounting Information of the Brazilian Public Sector - Siconfi. In specific cases, consultations were carried out with the fiscal transparency portals or the websites of the state finance departments to guarantee the consistency of the information with the data sources used. For municipalities, the Accounting Balances Matrix became the main data source in 2022. Banking system resources and liabilities are compiled by the BCB. Financing data is obtained from the balance of payments, from the financial system and from the central bank's accounts information, through a series of systems such as the Sistema Especial de Liquidação e Custódia (Selic), special settlement and custody system, and from the B3, a custody and liquidation chamber. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording See item 3.1.1 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques For the Central Government, the Conta Única do Tesouro Nacional, the Treasury's single account, centralizes all central government's financial transactions, which are registered in the Sistema Integrado de Administração Financeira do Governo Federal (SIAFI), the central government's integrated financial management system. For subnational governments, the source is official administrative data, which follows a legal auditing procedure. For municipalities, in order to enhamce the timeliness of data submission, there is a statistical imputation of missing data. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Brazilian official statistics exclude privatization revenues from total revenues and include interest expenditures in an accrual basis, starting from the nominal balance of the net public sector debt. STN started publishing general government statistics on an accrual basis for public expenses in 2015. Fiscal statistics are generated on a cash basis for revenues and on an accrual basis for expenses, leading to a modified accrual basis. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs See item 3.3.1 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Fiscal statistics from 2010 on follow GFSM 2014 orientations, and the methodology is harmonized with the system of national accounts. Harmonization was achieved through a STN and IBGE joint work. Changes in time series have been due to: (i) basis of recording: adoption of the accrual basis for expenses (although as already mentioned, in the uses and sources of cash statement, a cash basis system is adopted); (ii) classification: review and harmonization due to the STN/IBGE joint work (SNA 2008). In 2017 there was a methodological adjustment regarding Fundo Constitucional do Distrito Federal (FCDF), the federal district constitutional fund. The fund´s transactions, which were previously considered central government operations, were added to the states expenses, with a corresponding grant from the central government. In 2018, expenses related to the student loan government policy, Fundo de Financiamento Estudantil (FIES), were included in the central government as capital transfers, estimated as equivalent to the default rate (over 360 days) multiplied by the volume of uninsured loans. In 2019, in an effort to align with the international public finance best practices, there was a change in the registration of Income and Earnings Tax (IRRF) collected by states and municipalities for the Central Government. This adjustment seeks to comply with guidance expressed in GFSM 2014 §5.34, which indicates that the tax should be assigned to the unit of government that exercises the authority to impose the tax and set its rates. As it is a federal tax, as established in item III of Art. 153 of the Federal Constitution, compliance with the recommendation indicates registration at the federal level. Until 2019, this collection was kept in the subnational spheres, according to the amounts recorded in the Brazilian Public Sector Accounting and Tax Information System - SICONFI. In order to adjust the entries of this collection, the following adjustments were made: 1) at the federal level, the values of state and municipal collections were entered as income tax revenue and the same amount as intergovernmental grant expense; 2) in the states and municipalities, the amounts collected were reclassified from income taxes to intergovernmental grants. This methodological change does not impact the balances of the public finance tables. In 2020, Brazil implemented improvements to its fiscal statistics, including a new presentation format, enhanced revenue and expenditure details, improved consolidation processes, reclassification of IPI and ICMS as Value Added Taxes to align with international standards, creation of a glossary, and revision of operational processes, ensuring better adaptation to the national context and facilitating fiscal policy analysis. In 2024, the following methodological improvements were made: a) statistical treatment of the Service Time Guarantee Fund (FGTS) and the PIS-PASEP Remaining Fund: change of sectorization to the public financial corporations sector and consequently the exclusion of their flows from general government fiscal statistics; b) review of the treatment of imputed social contribution due to the current data unavailability on recording social security liabilities in GFS statistics. Therefore, based on this methodological review, it was decided not to record employers' imputed social contributions, considering that the current form of registration does not reflect the actuarial calculation of pension rights.; and c) New tables presenting government finance statistics in files separated by government levels: GG Statements (general government), GCO Statements (budgetary central government), GE Statements (state governments) and GM Statements (municipalities). 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses See item 4.3.1
