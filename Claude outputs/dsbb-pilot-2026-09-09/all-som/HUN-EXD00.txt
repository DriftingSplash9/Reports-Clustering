 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Definition: Statistical statement of the value and composition of the stock of Hungary’s gross external debt liabilities denominated in domestic and foreign currencies outstanding at the end of period. Gross external debt includes those debts (obligations) of a country’s residents vis-à-vis another country’s residents that involve a repayment obligation (with or without the payment of interest) or, conversely, an interest payment obligation (with or without principal payment). Under this definition, equity investments are not considered debt, irrespective of recording them under foreign direct investments, portfolio investments or other investments. In addition, financial derivatives are also not considered debt. Analytical framework: The external debt statistics follow the conceptual framework of the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) and External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (External Debt Guide). End-of-quarter stocks of gross external debt liabilities are presented under the following data categories: for each of four main sectors; central bank, general government, other MFIs, other sectors (subdivided to financial corporations other than MFIs, non-financial corporations, households, and non-profit institutions serving households); data are broken down by maturity (short- and long-term) on an original maturity basis and by debt instrument as specified in the BPM6 ; debt securities, trade credits, loans, deposits, other debt liabilities, and debt instruments under FDI; data on debt instruments under direct investment relationships are separately identified without sector attribution; the total external debt is broken down by national currency and other currencies. Hungary’s external debt statistics is a fraction of the International investment position and is based on the same methodology. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: Hungary’s external debt liabilities are a fraction of the International investment position and are based on the standard classification of the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) . 2.2.1 Scope Classification: Hungary’s external debt liabilities are a fraction of the International investment position and are based on the standard classification of the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) . Scope of the data Geographical coverage: No geographic regions are excluded from recording. Exceptions to coverage As defined equity investments are not considered debt, irrespective of recording them under foreign direct investments, portfolio investments or other investments. In addition, financial derivatives are also not considered debt. Stock of gross external debt of Hungary is published excluding SPEs. 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to unit of account: End-of-period data reported in foreign currencies are converted to domestic currency (Hungarian forint) at end-month exchange rates. Valuation: The main principle to use is the market value. If it is not available face value is used. Portfolio investment (debt securities): All stocks are valued at market price. Other investment (deposits, trade credits, loans and other debt liabilities): Since 2008 all stocks are valued at market price. 2.4.2 Recording basis Recording basis: As interest payments are recorded on an accrual basis in the BOP from 2004, entries are recorded on account interest costs accrued but not yet paid. Hence, since 2004 debt position data are recorded on an accrual basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Direct investment (debt instruments): The primary source of information is the direct reporting of the economic entities on their outstanding positions. Portfolio investment (debt securities): The primary source of information is the securities statistics which compiles monthly data on security-by security basis using the International Securities Identification Number (ISIN). As a supplementary source data on bills receivable/payable and participation not embodied in securities is collected within the questionnaires on other investments (monthly and quarterly). Other investment (deposits, trade credits, loans and other debt liabilities): The primary source of information is the direct reporting of the economic entities on their outstanding positions. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The timeliness of the source data is as follows: monthly direct reports: 10 working days after the end of the reference month; quarterly direct reports: 10 working days after the end of the reference quarter; annual direct reports on FDI: 30 June after the reference year; securities statistics: monthly, approximately 40 days after the end of the reference month. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Formal computerized checks of individual reports are built into the data reception process. All the consistency requirements within one report and between different reports of a single reporting entity are clearly identified in the instructions attached to each reporting form. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Direct investment (debt instruments) follow the conceptual framework of the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6). Prior to that, until 2013 we had identified the entities according to the resident status of not the final investor, rather that of the direct investor/investment: assets and liabilities relating to fellow enterprises had been classified by investment direction depending on whether the reporting entity has any non-resident direct investor (parent) or indirect investment abroad (subsidiary). Before 2008, debt instruments were recorded only based on (settlements) cash flows; stocks were calculated by the accumulation of flows and revaluations. Therefore, there was no stock data available in a breakdown by country and activity prior to 2008. Since the introduction of the new data collection system based on direct reports by the reporting entities in 2008, debt instrument type stock information has also been reported by data suppliers, and thus country and activity breakdowns can be generated. Other investment (deposits, trade credits, loans and other debt liabilities): Since 2008 (the launch of the direct reporting system) trade credits are monitored directly instead of use the difference between transactions in goods as recorded in foreign trade statistics and net settlements data. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Plausibility checks are carried out on both individual reports and aggregate levels. At both these levels, time series analysis is an essential tool for this purpose. Consistency checks with other statistics are also carried out at an aggregate level. Should there be any inconsistency at this level, there is a feedback to the individual report level and the reporting entity is asked to clarify the reason for the inconsistency. Staff closely monitor the scale of errors and omissions and seek to minimize them by carrying out cross-checks between current, capital and financial accounts. The behaviour of the BOP statistical time series is routinely assessed against related series, e.g. financial flow data are validated with the corresponding stock of external debt data, and reported data on investment income are assessed in relation to the corresponding stock in the IIP statistics. Information reported in the financial press is used to verify high-value FDI and other transactions. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished and non-confidential data can be obtained upon request.
