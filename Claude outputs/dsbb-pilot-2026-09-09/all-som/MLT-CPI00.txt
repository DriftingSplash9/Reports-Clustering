 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices (HICP) is used to measure inflation. The concepts and definitions used in the Maltese HICP are in line with the European Commission regulations and standards. The HICP is compiled according to the manual available at: https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/documents/3859598/18594110/KS-GQ-24-003-EN-N.pdf/8490b532-f9e2-5b63-16fa-d88cda14c7b4?version=4.0&t=1727422604655 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The items for the HICP are classified using the ECOICOP classification that is consistent with international guidelines. The HICP is not segmented in regions due to the size of the Maltese Islands. However, the most important 'areas' (including Gozo) are covered. On the other hand, the Household Budgetary Survey (HBS) 2015/16 was structured on the four and five-digit ECOICOP. The HBS is one of the main sources for expenditure when it comes to the HICP weighting structure. The HICP is also compiled at ECOICOP 5-digit level. In 2025, the HICP timeseries will additionally be published in line with the COICOP 2018 classification. In 2026, the current ECOICOP will be phased out and indices will solely be published using COICOP 2018. Within the European Union, COICOP 2018 is referred to as ECOICOP ver. 2, which is identical to COICOP 2018 barring some EU-specific case laws. Scope of the data Population Coverage: the HICP covers all monetary transactions involving goods and services consumed by residents and non-residents (e.g. tourists) in Malta and Gozo. Item Coverage: Representative goods and services consumed within the Maltese economic territory, and consistent with the Household Final Consumption Expenditure (HFCE). The HICP covers more than 500 different goods and services. Exceptions to coverage The HICP covers the expenditure of all households residing in the country, including non-residents and foreign visitors; consumer goods and services purchased by households (following the so-called ‘domestic concept’) Union fees are excluded from the HICP. Expenditure of residents abroad is excluded from the index. 2.4.1 Valuation Market prices are used to value flows and stocks. Specifically: All prices are inclusive of indirect taxes, such as value added taxes and excise taxes. Sale prices are included, provided that: the same item was available before the sale period and will continue to be available after the sale period; and the sale price is simultaneously available to all individuals residing (temporarily or permanently) in Malta. In the case of list prices, shop owners are asked to confirm that the prices are those actually being charged to consumers. All product specifications are carefully designed for the basket of items and their varieties, with guidance provided by the Price Statistics Unit staff. Price determining characteristics for transactions at market prices generally consider size, colour, brand, and quality, etc. 2.4.2 Recording basis Most prices are collected by our price collectors who collect the prices directly from the outlets. However, an increasing number of prices are collected from the internet nowadays as in the case of air fares, accommodation services, package tours, computers, cameras, fuels, electricity, water, and communication services. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The HICP covers purchases of existing goods such as used cars, which are predominantly imported, and so, in practice, netting of these transactions is not required. Used cars sold by households are not taken into account when compiling the index, since they are deemed unrepresentative. Insurance premiums are measured net of claims as required by international regulations. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources are kept under continuous review to ensure that the data collection program is comprehensive. To estimate the HICP, the NSO selected over 500 goods and services representing the structure of consumer expenditure of the population. Prices are collected from the most representative outlets across the Maltese islands. The great majority of prices are collected on a monthly basis. To supplement the core compilation, price information from other available sources is used, including State-Owned Utility Companies, the National Accounts Unit and the International Trade Unit within the National Statistics Office. Sources for HICP prices: The outlets for price-reading purposes are derived from the business register according to the market share of each outlet. This ensures that the outlets covered represent the entire population. For compiling the price index, there are around 450 outlets, for which over 190,000 price readings are collected. The sample information maintained by the NSO contains the name of the outlet, the address, the types of products sold and whether the outlet is public or private. Sources for HICP weights: The NSO conducts the HBS every five years or so. The last HBS, carried out between April 2015 and April 2016, covered a national representative sample of around 3,000 households throughout Malta, randomly selected. The initially designed sample was larger to account for any non-respondents. The NSO provided monetary payments as an incentive to improve the response rate. The backbone of the HICP weighting structure is the HBS. However, some weights are calculated using National Accounts Unit expenditure figures, as they tend to be under-represented in the HBS. Although the NSO conducts the HBS twice in any given decade, the HICP sub-index weights are reviewed and updated every year, taking into account preliminary National Accounts data on consumption patterns of year t-1 or HBS figures. Moreover, weights below sub-index level shall in no case be more than seven years old. These are provisions laid down in Commission Regulation (EU) No. 2010/1148. The amount of household expenditure allocated to different goods and services was worked out as a proportion of the total household expenditure. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Generally speaking, prices are collected over several working days towards the middle of the calendar month to which the index pertains. However, in the case of fresh vegetables, fresh fruit and fresh fish, two price readings are recorded over a period of more than one working week. Prices for accommodation services are monitored in the 4 months prior to the reference month. In other words, there are 4 readings every month. Airfares are monitored almost every working day during a month. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data are timely, enabling the Prices Unit to respect the commitment stipulated by the annual calendar of releases. Most prices are collected by price collectors. Utility prices are monitored every month from the price data available on websites. Other prices are supplemented with telephone interviewing or secondary source such as Residential Rents, Transportation Services and Medical services surveys. However, it should be noted that these surveys are followed up every month through an on-going survey conducted over the phone. Phone surveys are also carried out in order to obtain prices such as private tuition, garage rents etc. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Specialised personnel check all prices every month. Any abnormal price movement is identified and discussed with the price collector reporting that price change. If a plausible explanation for that price change is found, price is then accepted. Otherwise, the price collector is asked to check the price again. The weighting scheme is established using HBS data, National Accounts data and Trade data. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Treatment of missing prices: Missing prices are either estimated until the product returns or for a maximum of two months until a replacement is found. During the two months, the price collectors look for a replacement product similar to the original item. Whenever a replacement product is introduced, it's either of the same quality and specification of the old item or its quality adjusted to maintain comparability. Selection of replacement: Reliance on the expert judgment of price collectors in selecting a similar item. Whenever there's doubt in the selection, it's usually discussed with shop owner and approved by the administrative officer in charge of price collection. Adjustment for quality differences: If the price collector fails to price a particular product for two consecutive months, a new replacement (similar) product is introduced using the bridged overlap method. The method essentially boils down to estimating the base price of the replacement product. The new base price is calculated on the basis of the price movements exhibited by similar products pertaining to either the same outlet or the same locality. The direct comparison method is used in the case of clothing and footwear or whenever the products being compared are deemed to be homogenous. The hedonic method is used in the case of cars, motorcycles, computers, cameras and mobile phones, among others. Introducing new products: Newly significant goods and services are introduced in January every year, when a new good or service is deemed to be representative. The new weight is usually calculated using National Accounts figures or trade figures. This applies to the HICP only. Seasonal items: As from January 2010, vegetables, fruits and fish are considered as seasonal items. The methodology used for the compilation of the seasonal indices is in line with the requirements stipulated in Commission Regulation (EC) No 2020/1148. The regulation lays down detailed rules for the implementation of Council Regulation (EC) No 2016/792/95 as regards minimum standards for the treatment of seasonal products in the Harmonised Indices of Consumer Prices (HICP). 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Sources of weights: The sources of the weights are national accounts data and the Household Budgetary Survey. There are more than 400 weights at national level for the HICP; Frequency of weights update: Weights at ECOICOP four and five-digit level are updated on an annual basis whereas weights at a more detailed level are revised at least once every 7 years; Computation of lowest-level indices: The arithmetic means are used in the compilation of the elementary aggregates; Aggregation formula: Laspeyres formula (Chain-linked); Reference base period: 2015=100. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results All prices are then entered in working sheets and in the IT application which is tailor-made for HICP compilation. One of the reports available in the IT application pinpoints any major changes taking place at the most granular level. This facilitates validation which is carried out by qualified personnel. HICP data are not validated against other price indices such as the import/export price index and implicit deflator for household final consumption. Nevertheless, fruit and vegetables price indices are compared to the wholesale price indices produced by the Agriculture and Fisheries Unit within the National Statistics Office. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Any unusual movements, such as outliers in item indices, are verified and traced back to the reporting outlet if necessary. The processing software routinely identifies those sub-indices with a significant contribution to change in the total index. This allows them to be cross-checked for possible errors. It also quickly identifies large individual price changes, which are similarly cross-checked. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs A specific report is used to pinpoint price changes that are above/below the 5 per cent threshold. Any movement that exceeds this threshold is assessed and verified. Moreover, sensitive items such as accommodation, seasonal products and utilities are assessed individually every month. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses The HICP revision policy Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2020/1148 stipulates: The officially published HICP series may be revised on the grounds of mistakes, new or improved information, and provisional results in order to improve the comparability, reliability, or relevance of the HICP. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses HICP data are rarely revised once published. Whenever a series is revised, the public is informed of the changes taking place and an explanation is provided. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Detailed customized tabulation (detailed breakdowns) of price index data is available upon request, in addition to the tables routinely disseminated. For specific purposes, customized tabulation of data that satisfy the NSO’s disclosure requirements are also available at a cost recovery charge. Data users are aware of the availability of additional statistics and of the procedures for obtaining them. These procedures are explicitly provided on the NSO’s website. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The NSO’s Communication and Dissemination Unit is the point of contact for the HICP data users. Email and internet addresses can be found on all news releases. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs HICP news releases are available in PDF and Excel formats on the NSO website.
