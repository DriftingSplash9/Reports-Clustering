 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The concepts and definitions adopted in the compilation of Hong Kong's merchandise trade statistics are in conformity with the standards recommended by the United Nations in the International Merchandise Trade Statistics: Concepts and Definitions 2010 . Statistics are disseminated on Hong Kong's export quantum indices. Separate quantum indices for domestic exports, re-exports and total exports are also compiled. Quantum index measures changes in volume of external trade of a certain period with reference to a selected reference period. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Basic unit of compilation is commodity item classified according to the Standard International Trade Classification. Quantum indices analysed by selected trading partner and end-use categories are available. 2.2.1 Scope Basic unit of compilation is commodity item classified according to the Standard International Trade Classification. Quantum indices analysed by selected trading partner and end-use categories are available. Scope of the data Transaction coverage: Hong Kong's merchandise trade statistics refer to the movements of merchandise between Hong Kong and her trading partners, by land, air, water and by post. Geographic coverage: Hong Kong's trade with all countries/territories is included. Exceptions to coverage Movement of exempted articles, gold and specie are not included. Unrecorded activity No estimates are made for smuggling activity. 2.4.1 Valuation Domestic exports and re-exports are valued on f.o.b. basis. 2.4.2 Recording basis The time when the goods move into or out of Hong Kong is used as the time of recording. Calendar month is used as the standard reference month for monthly statistics. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The concepts and definitions adopted in the compilation of Hong Kong's merchandise trade statistics are in conformity with the standards recommended by the United Nations in the International Merchandise Trade Statistics : Concepts and Definitions 2010 . 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Hong Kong's merchandise trade statistics are compiled based on information in import/export declarations. All declarations are processed in compiling trade statistics. Major types of data collected include trade values, quantities, units of quantity, descriptions of goods (with Hong Kong Harmonized System commodity codes), mode of transport and countries/territories of origin/consignment for imports and re-exports, and countries/territories of consignment for domestic exports. Unit value index is compiled to deflate value index in the derivation of quantum index. In the compilation of unit value index, price indices of selected commodity items obtained from a specification price survey of imports and exports are used. For other commodity items, a simple unit value approach is used. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Data are supplied according to "GETS - Implementation Instructions". The information on data requirement is available on the website of the C&SD ( https://www.censtatd.gov.hk/en/page_94.html ). 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Importers/exporters are required to lodge import/export declarations within 14 days. Upon processing and verification as necessary, merchandise statistics are released within four weeks from end of the statistical month. Allowing some time for collating price data and compiling unit values, trade indices are released in another three weeks' time. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The declarations submitted by importers/exporters are matched against cargo manifests to ensure that there is no omission of declaration. It is a legal requirement for shipping companies, airline companies, transport companies or their agents to submit to the Government a manifest in respect of the cargoes carried on every ship, airplane or other transport vehicle that enters or leaves Hong Kong. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Reference period: 2024 = 100. Compilation of lowest level indices: Trade value at commodity-country level is first deflated using the corresponding unit value index to produce volume estimates revalued at preceding year prices. Aggregation: Volume estimate at a higher level of aggregation is obtained by aggregating the volume estimates of commodity-country level revalued at preceding year prices. The short-term volume index of trade aggregates for a year is then calculated by dividing the volume estimate of trade aggregates for the year by the corresponding trade values for the preceding year. With the effect of price changes eliminated by revaluing the trade values at preceding year prices, the short-term volume index measures the real growth of trade aggregates in a year over preceding year. The preceding year-weighted short-term volume indices for other years are then chain linked to a selected reference year in order to obtain a continuous time series. Compilation of the deflator: The unit value index is compiled for deflation purpose. The unit value index is a chained Laspeyres index. Instead of using fixed weights, the weights are updated each year based on trade values in the preceding year. Movement between adjacent years is chained by multiplication to give the complete time series. With more up-to-date weights, the chaining procedure has the advantage of facilitating comparison between adjacent years. Unit value of a commodity item is calculated by dividing its trade value by its corresponding quantity. Unit value is, however, an inadequate measure of genuine price change as there are changes which could not be accounted for, e.g. composition of products, quality of products, supply patterns of imports or demand patterns of exports, terms of payment and delivery as well as the trading practices and pricing policies of trading partners. To address such issues, a specification price survey of imports and exports has been conducted since 1982. Heterogeneous items with relatively large trade value and fluctuating unit value are selected for inclusion in the survey. Price indices of these commodity items are incorporated into the compilation of unit value index as far as practicable. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The seasonally adjusted series is compiled by the X-12 ARIMA method. The X-12 ARIMA method and its related versions are the standard method commonly employed in compiling seasonally adjusted data series. The method can be used to detect whether outlier(s) is present in the original series for estimation of seasonal factors. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Immediate results are reviewed against the historical data at detailed level. Where applicable, they are also checked against other related statistical indicators. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data For significant discrepancies, either from past trend or related indicators, the causes will be investigated and findings documented. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The discrepancies in statistical outputs are generally not significant. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Normally there is no revision to published export volume index other than due to re-referencing. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Normally there is no revision to published export volume index other than due to re-referencing. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Requests and enquiries related to trade statistics can be made to the Trade Analysis Section (13/F, South Tower, Tseung Kwan O Government Offices (TKOGO), 30 Tong Yin Street, Tseung Kwan O, New Territories, Hong Kong, China; Phone: 852 3863 2596; Fax: 852 2802 1101; E-mail: trade@censtatd.gov.hk ). 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Details of contact for enquiries, including phone number, fax number, email address and office address, are given in the trade statistics publications and on the Census and Statistics Department website ( https://www.censtatd.gov.hk/en/page_105.html ).
