 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The International Recommendations for the Index of Industrial Production (IRIIP) 2010, and International Recommendation for Industrial Statistics (IRIS) 2008 are used to guide the compilation of the Industrial Production Index (IPI). The ISU is currently compiling the Index of Mining Production and the Index of Electricity Generation on quarterly basis. Efforts are ongoing to collect data on section C and E of the ISIC Rev 4 to enable the compilation of the IPI which is lagging behind because of lack of data. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The International Standard Industrial Classification of all economic activities (ISIC) Revision 4 is used to classify establishments. This is used alongside the Botswana International Standard Industrial Classification (BSIC) Rev 4, which is the adaptation of ISIC. Scope of the data The Index of Industrial production covers Industrial production in Botswana, i.e. section B (Mining and Quarrying) , section C (Manufacturing), section D (Electricity and Gas) and section E (Water) , and its related services of CPC Ver.2 The current scope is defined in terms of ISIC Rev 4 2.4.1 Valuation Output is recorded using quantities and price value of industrial production 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures There are two general strategies to deal with non-response: the first one ignores all forms with missing values and confine analysis to the fully completed forms, the second strategy estimate missing data so that the data matrix is complete, this is known as imputation. The second strategy (imputation) is recommended because the first strategy leads to discarding the valid data contained in the partially complete forms. Imputation methods range from simple and intuitive to rather statistical procedures. Most common methods of imputation are as follows; i) mean/modal value imputation- this method impute the mean value of a variable for missing data; ii) post stratification- this method divide the sample into strata and then impute stratum mean, mode or median; iii) carry forward- the value for the reporting unit from the survey occasion in the previous period, adjusted to reflect the average increase (decrease) of the data item in the stratum. Under the Second strategy Botswana has adapted post stratification and carry forward imputation methods. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Surveys: Data required to compile IIP is collected by statistical office directly from the firms concerned or obtained from secondary sources. Collecting data from the Manufacturing sector is currently put on hold until the Statistical Business Register (SBR) is up to date. Efforts to collect data in the past three years has not borne any fruit owing to none response emanating from questionnaires being sent to wrong addresses and companies not being located during follow ups. The organization will be conducting the Economic Census during 2015/16 and this will help mitigate some of the challenges related to none response. Administrative data source: data is collected directly from Department of Mines and Botswana Power Corporation as a secondary data. Currently the organization is experiencing challenges with accessing water statistics. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The objective of compiling IPI is to measure short term changes in value added, measured in volume terms. First one has to identify variables and determine methods along with an agreed scope and classification system i.e. ISIC Rev 4 and CPC Ver. 2, to collect these data variables from the appropriate business population. Techniques are then required to obtain volume measure of the collected variables. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures IPI is compiled on monthly and quarterly using Laspeyres approach and sometimes seasonal variations are being used.
