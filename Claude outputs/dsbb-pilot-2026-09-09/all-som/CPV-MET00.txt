 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions 2.1.1 Concepts & Definitions 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Foreign Trade Statistics fall within the scope of the National Statistics Institute (INE), as the central executive body of the National Statistical System, allowing it to fulfill its mission of efficiently producing and disseminating quality official statistical information about the country, increasingly satisfying users' needs. In this sense, INE publishes the main statistics regarding trade between Cabo Verde and the Rest of the World, aiming to promote statistical culture, thus contributing to increasing understanding of the importance of statistics in the decision-making process and in forming an opinion of an increasingly responsible public sector. Foreign trade data is processed with reference to the UN Global Standard on foreign trade statistics. The International Merchandise Trade Statistics Manual (IMTS 2010) is also followed in compiling foreign trade data. The product nomenclature used in data processing is the Harmonized System (HS), which is used by many countries, thus allowing comparability of external trade statistics in Cabo Verde. 2.1.2 SPECIAL COMMERCE Special Commerce comprises: 1) When importing: a) Goods dispatched for internal consumption; b) Goods entered under a temporary admission regime in order to undergo transformation, repair or additional labor; c) Goods in storage, to later be subjected to transformation or additional labor; d) Goods intended for national navigation, although not dispatched for consumption. 2) When exporting: a) Goods produced in Cabo Verde; b) Nationalized goods, that is, imported goods, made freely available to importers after having been paid for any duties to which they are liable or having received transformation, repair or additional labor by virtue of which they had been admitted with temporary exemption; c) National and nationalized goods intended for foreign navigation. The following are excluded from these calculations: 1) When importing: a) Goods entered under normal temporary regime (temporary importation); b) Goods returning from another country to which they had been sent on a temporary basis (reimportation). 2) When exporting: a) Goods released temporarily; b) Goods returned as a result of temporary importation, that is, those re-exported; c) National and nationalized goods intended for national navigation. 2.1.3 REEXPORT (INDIRECT TRANSIT) Includes goods from foreign countries that, upon entering real or fictitious bonded warehouses and warehouses, are subsequently exported without having been made freely available to importers, or undergone any transformation, repair or additional labor, in addition to repackaging, rearrangement or mixing. 2.1.4 COUNTRY OF ORIGIN AND CONSUMPTION Imports are determined according to the country of origin, that is, if it is a natural product, the country where it was produced, if it is a construction product, the country where it received the form in which it was introduced into the importing country . Exports are determined according to the country of destination, that is, where the merchandise must have the application for which it was produced or manufactured or where it must be transformed or subject to additional labor. 2.1.5 QUANTITIES The net weight of the goods, expressed in tons, is considered. 2.1.6 VALUES When importing, the CIF value is considered and when exporting, the FOB value is considered, expressed in thousand escudos and million escudos. 2.1.7 ROUNDING The quantities and values ​​of goods are calculated in sub-multiples of the units in the publication, so, due to rounding, the totals may not correspond to the sum of the partials. 2.1.8 CLASSIFICATION The goods were classified according to the Harmonized System (HS) nomenclature. The figures included in the foreign trade publication are considered provisional data and subject to corrections either in the following issues of the Bulletin or in the annual "Foreign Trade" publication. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Foreign trade statistics are disaggregated by product, following the harmonized international classification system (10-digit code) and according to the partner country table (of origin or destination). Scope of the data 2.2.1 Scope Foreign trade statistics record all trade in goods between a given "statistical territory" and all other "statistical territories". "Statistical territory" means any territory under the supervision of the same customs service. In practice, statistical territories are identified by geographic codes. Any merchandise that enters or leaves, regardless of whether or not it results in tax collection, is recorded in statistics. The quantity of merchandise traded is always given in kilograms and expressed as net weight. The value of the traded merchandise is declared in Cabo Verde Escudos. However, certain exchanges, due to their private nature, are not recorded in commercial statistics, namely: 1. - personal effects used or items carried by travelers; 2. - effects and items for personal use by members of the diplomatic corps and foreign citizens who head missions in Cabo Verde; 3. - monetary gold, bonds, notes and coins issued as currency; 4. - fishing products unloaded from boats registered in Cabo Verde. Imports are recorded in statistics under the country of origin, while exports are recorded under the country of destination. Trade in goods in foreign trade statistics is classified into two categories: Special trade statistics; and General trade statistics . Special import trade : registers goods imported from abroad and declared for domestic consumption, which are temporarily admitted, housed in bonded industrial warehouses or in factories under customs control. Special export trade: is obtained from special import trade by adding goods entering storage warehouses and the free zone and deducting goods leaving storage warehouses and the free zone for use in the domestic market. General export trade: is obtained from special export trade, adding the goods that leave the warehouses and the free zone for re-export and subtracting the national or nationalized goods that enter the warehouses or the free zone. Published data on Cabo Verde's foreign trade are general trade statistics as defined in the UN global standard on foreign trade statistics. 2.4.1 Valuation The General Directorate of Customs provides f.o.b. data to the central bank, with merchandise freight values, and the respective insurance shown separately. The National Statistics Institute, also relying on the General Directorate of Customs as a source, publishes foreign trade statistics on a c.i.f. basis. Differences in coverage may also be observed because the BCV makes the above-mentioned adjustments. All goods that enter or leave, whether taxed or not, are recorded in statistics. The quantity of goods traded is always recorded in kilograms and expressed in their net weight. The value of traded goods is declared in Escudos. 2.4.2 Recording basis Published data on Cabo Verde's foreign trade are general trade statistics as defined in the UN global standard on foreign trade statistics. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data on merchandise imports and exports are compiled by the Statistics Economic and business Department of the, National Statistics Institute (INE) based on information from the foreign trade statistics compiled by the General Directorate of Customs. The General Directorate of Customs uses its own forms for this purpose, which it collects and processes monthly. Merchandise imports and exports are reported by product and by country of origin/destination. The National Statistics Institute also publishes volume and price indices, but these are calculated using the original foreign trade data provided by Customs. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness In the first 10 days of each month, INE collects customs data in electronic format and processes it using EUROTRACE software (the software installed in all ECOWAS countries) to produce external trade statistics. The data is processed on the basis of the International Trade in Goods Statistics Manual (IMTS 2010). 3.2.1 Source data assessment In the first 10 days of each month, INE collects customs data in electronic format and processes it using the EUROTRACE software (the software installed in all ECOWAS countries) to produce external trade statistics. Data is processed based on the International Goods Trade Statistics Manual (IMTS 2010). 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Imports are declared cif (cost, insurance, freight) and exports are declared fob . 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The periodicity of foreign trade statistics shall be quarterly and annual, published one month after the end of the reference period. Statistics are provisional in view of the overvaluation of export data. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The data is available on the INE website and users can make recommendations on the www.ine.cv website
