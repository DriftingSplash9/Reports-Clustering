 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: The national accounts reference framework for data with the new base 1989/90 is based on the System of National Accounts, 1993 (SNA93). Type and periodicity of the data produced: The Subdirectorate of National Accounts and Statistical Summaries of the INS produces and disseminates on an annual basis only the following data: Supply use tables (TRE) for 44 branches of activity and 44 products at current prices and chain-linked 2000 constant prices; estimates of gross domestic product (GDP) by branch (44 branches of economic activity), and by type of expense (final consumption, gross fixed capital formation, changes in stocks, exports minus imports of goods and services); both estimates are calculated at current prices and at prior-year prices; these data may also be obtained at a reference year price. Integrated economic accounts table for the five institutional sectors, from the production account to the capital account. Data with the new base year 1989/90 have already been published by the INS and cover the years 1989/90 and 1992/93-1997/98. To take into account Cameroon’s transition from fiscal-year to calendar-year accounting, a data series covering the period 1993-2003 was published in 2005. This series is supplemented every year by provisional accounts for that year. The provisional accounts for 2006 were published in June 2007. A TRE at current prices and at constant 2000 prices is published. Concepts: The concepts and methodology used are based on SNA 93 . GDP, assessed at current market prices, is the final outcome of the production activity of resident productive units over the period; it is also the sum of final uses of goods and services in the entire economy, less the value of imports of goods and services during the period. The productive units are grouped according to their main economic activity; the final uses of goods and services are private final consumption (households and nonprofit institutions), public final consumption (of all government agencies), gross fixed capital formation, changes in stocks, and exports. Production excludes services produced by households for their own a final consumption, but includes informal activities. The methodology is based on supply use balances for goods, sectoral activity accounts, and sectoral matrices for analyzing distribution operations. Supply use balances are used to analyze items by goods and services transactions and to realize volume-price distribution; Branch activity accounts can be used to prepare production and use accounts for sectors of activity and a bridge to the institutional sector accounts; Sectoral distribution analyses compare information from creditor sectors with information provided by debtor sectors. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization SNA93 for institutional sectors, transactions and other flows, account balances and assets, as well as uses; Local version of the third revision of the Cameroon SITC (CITI) for branches of activity; CPC for goods and services; Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS) for merchandise trade; Additional classifications for production, production output and process (formal, informal and government agencies). Scope of the data National accounts coverage includes the entire territory, including the “free zones,” and relations with the rest of the world. The concept of residence used is that of the SNA 93, namely having a center of economic interest in the country. All transactions are covered, whether market or nonmarket, legal or illegal, but always by mutual agreement. The national accounts cover all resident units, including informal sector producers. The informal sector is defined as comprising producers that do not keep regular accounts. The output of the informal sector is estimated using data from the 1993 and 2005 informal sector and employment survey (123 survey), annual data on the employment matrix, and indirect estimation methods. Merchandise trade data include an estimate of unrecorded trade. The 123 survey is so named because it takes place in three stages: the first stage consists of a household survey, the second is a survey of establishments and production units, which are determined after the first stage of the survey; and the third stage consists of a survey of jobs following the second stage of the survey. The initial data, taken from the Statistical and Tax Declarations (DSF), are adjusted to take account of the nonreporting and underreporting. Unrecorded activity Unrecorded activities are illegal activities for which there is no mutual agreement between the parties (theft, unilateral debt cancellation, etc.). Overall, no activity, even illegal (contraband, sales of drugs, etc.), is excluded from coverage. Estimates of contraband are calculated on the basis of the supply use balance by product, and estimates drawn from the Cameroon–Nigeria cross-border trade survey. 2.4.1 Valuation The production and stocks of finished goods, goods for resale, and goods in process are recorded at the base price; imports are valued c.i.f.; intermediate consumption, final consumption expenditure, gross fixed capital formation and stocks of raw materials are recorded at their acquisition prices (and also at the base price); and exports are valued f.o.b. The prices and exchange rates used are market prices. All uses are also valued at base prices by identifying margins and net taxes and tariffs associated with the item. The output of government agencies and production for home use are valued at production cost. The imputed production of family housing services is drawn from the 2001 household consumption survey, which uses a selection model. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: Most transactions are recorded on an accrual basis. Recording period: The calendar year (January 1 to December 31). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The Subdirectorate of National Accounts and Statistical Summaries of the INS produces and disseminates national accounts data on an annual basis: Production approach (core data sources for the main branches of activity) Foodcrop and livestock farming: production is estimated on the basis of the data in the 1984 Agricultural Census and is updated on the basis of INS and Ministry of Agriculture updates. The data source is AGRISTAT and a survey on the formation of food prices (FORPRIX). Cash crop farming: data on production of the main cash crops (cocoa, coffee, cotton, bananas for export, hevea rubber, tobacco, tea) are provided by the Ministry of Commerce and from the Statistical and Tax Declarations (DSF). Uncut wood : output is derived from the DSFs and INS estimates. Oil: production is calculated on the basis of data provided by the oil and gas company Société nationale des Hydrocarbures (SNH) and the DSFs of oil companies on the volumes of oil extracted and on the realized price. Modern sector (taxable enterprises): production is calculated on the basis of DSF data using the OHADA chart of accounts since 2002. The data for modern sector enterprises that do not complete the DSF are estimated on the basis of the annual industry survey (EAI) or the quarterly market survey and other ad hoc surveys of enterprises in the selected branches of activity. Informal sector : production is estimated on the basis of data from the 1993 informal sector survey (123 survey), annual data on the employment matrix, and indirect estimation methods. This 123 survey is used to obtain the initial structure of the activity production account. A new survey was conducted in 2005; it will be taken into account in the new national accounts series. Wholesale and retail trade: production is based on sectoral data, which are checked against the sum of commercial margins taken from the supply-use tables. Insurance and financial institutions: production is based on the accounting documents of insurance companies, banks and other financial institutions, as well as on the data provided by the Directorate of Economic Supervision of the Ministry of Economy and Finance. Government agencies: production is calculated on the basis of data from budgetary and administrative documents, such as the Treasury Account Balance, Credit Status Report (Budget Directorate), Trend Chart (Forecasting Directorate), accounting documents of the National Social Welfare Fund, administrative accounts for the decentralized agencies, and accounting documents of local governments and the sinking fund based on foreign aid. Expenditure approach: Final household consumption: the data are based on the series prepared on the basis of the budget-consumption surveys of 1984, 1996, and 2001 and on an analysis of the supply-use balances by product, in particular comparisons between supply (domestic production and imports) and demand other than final household consumption (exports, intermediate consumption, gross fixed capital formation, changes in stocks). A survey on commercial margins in 2003 supplements the estimation of transactions in foodcrop products. Final public consumption: the data are based on the budgetary and administrative documents of government agencies (see production approach ). Gross fixed capital formation: the data are prepared on the basis of the DSFs, international aid, the general government account and the value of imports of durable goods. Changes in stocks: the data are based on DSFs and the actual balances compiled for major products (oil, wood, cash crops). Exports and imports of goods: the data used are the customs statistics validated by the National Technical Committee on the Balance of Trade. Unrecorded trade is also estimated by monitoring cross-border trade with Nigeria. Exports and imports of services: the data used are taken from the balance of payments. Institutional sector accounts: General government sector: the data are based on the budgetary an administrative documents of government agencies (se production approach ). Nonfinancial corporations: statistical and tax declarations, and INS extrapolations. Individual entrepreneurs: DSFs and estimated data for the informal sector. Financial institutions: accounting documents of insurance companies, banks and other financial institutions, as well as data provided by the Directorate of Economic Supervision of the Ministry of Economy and Finance. Households: budget-consumption survey, data from other sectors. Nonprofit institutions: INS estimates Rest of the world: detailed balance of payments data. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Data are compiled mainly by using the ERE/TES computer module for assistance in national accounts compilation, which makes it possible to calculate simultaneously GDP using the production approach, and expenditure and revenue using the tools for compiling supply-use tables and sectoral accounts. A number of other tasks are also accomplished outside the ERE/TES module, in particular the compilation of institutional sector accounts (excluding production accounts) the preparation of digests, reconciliation and cross-checks, namely of taxes, gross fixed capital formation, and international assistance. Production approach: The gross data derived from the DSFs are enteres into the database and, in combination with the EAI data and per capita ratios from the employment matrix,; they are useful in estimating non-reporting by enterprises required to submit DSFs and underreporting. Estimation of data at current and constant prices: Data at current and constant prices are calculated simultaneously using any of three possible methods, depending on the data available on values, volumes, and prices for each product: (1) data at current prices are extrapolated from volume and price indexes; (2) data at constant prices are extrapolated from volume indexes; (3) data at constant prices are obtained by deflating data at current prices using price indexes. Expenditure approach: stimation of data at current and constant prices: Data at current and constant prices are calculated simultaneously using any of three methods, depending on the value, volume, and price data available for each product: (1) data at current prices are extrapolated from volume and price indexes; (2) data at constant prices are extrapolated from volume indexes; (3) data at constant prices are obtained by deflating data at current prices using price indexes. Reconciliation: The statistical deviation for balancing the Input-Output Table, as well as the differences between the goods and services accounts and the institutional sector accounts, are reduced by an iteration process based on the cross-checking of data,. This deviation reduction process is managed in ERE/TES, the computer tool that assists in the compilation of national accounts used by the INS, the control variable being the difference between intermediate consumption using the demand approach, on the one hand, and supply, on the other. Frequency of major revisions: The methodology was completely revised and the concepts and classifications changed in 1984 (base 1978/79) and 1998 (new base 1989/90). A homogenous time series, consistent with the 1993 revision, is available for 1989/90 and subsequent years. Reference year and updating cycle: The reference year for the series at constant prices is Financial Year 1989/90. In principle, it is updated every 10 years. None
