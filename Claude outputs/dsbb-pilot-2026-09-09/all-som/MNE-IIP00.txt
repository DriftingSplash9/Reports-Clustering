 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The methodological framework followed in the compilation of the IIP is defined in the IMF’s Balance of Payments and International Investment Positions Manual, Sixth Edition (BPM6) and EU regulations. Additional methodological references used is the OECD Benchmark Definition of Foreign Direct Investment (BD4). The concept of 'resident' in the BPM6 is identical to the one used in the BPM6 and the 2010 European System of National and Regional Accounts (ESA2010). The residence of each institutional unit is the economic territory with which it has the strongest connection, expressed as its center of predominant economic interest. Each institutional unit is a resident of one and only one economic territory determined by its center of predominant economic interest. The International Investment Position (IIP) is a statistical statement that provides an aggregate view of the net financial position (assets minus liabilities) of a country vis-à-vis the rest of the world. It shows at a point in time the value and composition of: financial assets of residents of an economy that are claims on non-residents and liabilities of residents of an economy to non-residents. The IIP is classified by functional categories (direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives, other investment and reserve assets), financial instruments (equity and investment fund shares, debt instruments, and other financial assets and liabilities) and institutional sectors of resident party (Central bank, General government, Deposit taking institutions excluding central banks and Other sectors) and maturity (short-term or long-term). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification of stock position is broadly consistent with guidelines set in the sixth edition of Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6). The IIP classification system follows the recommendations of BPM6. Assets Direct investment (Equity capital and reinvested earnings, Debt Instruments); Portfolio investment (Equity and investment fund shares, Debt Securities); Financial derivatives (other than reserves); Other investment (Currency and deposits, Loans, Trade credits and advances, Other accounts receivable); Reserve assets (Monetary gold, Special drawing rights, Reserve position in the IMF, Other reserve assets). Liabilities Direct investment (Equity capital and reinvested earnings, Debt Instruments); Portfolio investment (Equity and investment fund shares, Debt securities); Financial derivatives (other than reserves); Other investment (Currency and deposits, Loans, Trade credits and advances, Other accounts payable, SDRs). Also the data are classified as follows: by institutional sector (general government, monetary authorities, deposit taking institutions except central banks, other sector); by original (short term up to one year and long term) maturity; by instrument (currency and deposits, debt securities, loans, trade credits and advances and other accounts payable) 2.2.1 Scope The classification of stock position is broadly consistent with guidelines set in the sixth edition of Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6). The IIP classification system follows the recommendations of BPM6. Assets Direct investment (Equity capital and reinvested earnings, Debt Instruments); Portfolio investment (Equity and investment fund shares, Debt Securities); Financial derivatives (other than reserves); Other investment (Currency and deposits, Loans, Trade credits and advances, Other accounts receivable); Reserve assets (Monetary gold, Special drawing rights, Reserve position in the IMF, Other reserve assets). Liabilities Direct investment (Equity capital and reinvested earnings, Debt Instruments); Portfolio investment (Equity and investment fund shares, Debt securities); Financial derivatives (other than reserves); Other investment (Currency and deposits, Loans, Trade credits and advances, Other accounts payable, SDRs). Also the data are classified as follows: by institutional sector (general government, monetary authorities, deposit taking institutions except central banks, other sector); by original (short term up to one year and long term) maturity; by instrument (currency and deposits, debt securities, loans, trade credits and advances and other accounts payable) Scope of the data The scope is broadly consistent with internationally accepted standards, covering all types of financial assets and liabilities between residents and nonresidents at the end of the reporting period. Exceptions to coverage There are no exceptions to coverage. Unrecorded activity Portfolio investment abroad held by other sectors are not estimated, as well as trade credits and advances. 2.4.1 Valuation Main valuation method for listed equity is market value, while for unlisted equity main valuation method is own funds at book value. Nominal value as main valuation method is used for DI in the form of debt (debt securities and debt other than securities) and for the loans included in Other investments. For portfolio investment and reserve assets valuation method used for the compilation of IIP is market value. 2.4.2 Recording basis All the items of IIP are recorded on accrual basis, except for inputted data derived from Balance of Payments transactions and other evaluations made in the compilation of part of direct investment and portfolio investment (liabilities). The distinction between short-term and long-term debt is based on the original maturity. The long-term debt includes all liabilities with original maturity of more than a year, and the short-term those with original maturity of one year or less. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures IIP is compiled and published on a gross basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The methodological framework followed in the compilation of the Balance of Payments is defined in the IMF’s Balance of Payments and International Investment Positions Manual, Sixth Edition (BPM6) and the European system of national and regional accounts (ESA 2010), as defined in Regulation (EU) No 549/2013 of the European Parliament and of the Council. Main data sources for the Montenegro IIP statistics are: direct reporting by residents (direct reporting of residents on FDIs and loans); statistics compiled within the CBCG (balance of payments and monetary statistics); administrative sources of data (Ministry of Finance, IMF); and estimations. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The source data generally cover definitions, scope, classifications, valuation and time recording, almost fully in line with the BPM6 methodology. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data are collected within adequate time in line with set timeliness and periodicity for disseminating IIP (t+9 months). 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data are regularly assessed and cross-checked for coverage and response error. The results of the assessments are for internal use only. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The structure of the IIP is fully in line with the structure of the financial account. The main components are: foreign direct investments, portfolio investments, financial derivatives, and other investments, as well as reserve assets. For the compilation of IIP data, MS Excel spreadsheet applications are used. In order to compile the IIP, the CBCG uses: direct reporting by residents (direct reporting of residents on FDIs and loans); statistics compiled within the CBCG (balance of payments and monetary statistics); Administrative sources of data (Ministry of Finance, IMF), and estimates. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures There are no additional data adjustments. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Data obtained from enterprises (direct reporting) are validated using automated data validation procedures. The data entry procedures include checks to avoid miscoding and misreporting. Queries are made to reporter concerning unusual transactions and large values. Data sources are checked with data from annual financial statements of companies. Intermediate results are validated using other sources whenever is possible. Newspapers and information agencies are monitored for information about large transactions, and it is confirmed that the corresponding flows are covered in FDI flows and stock data. Survey data are checked against ITRS records and reconciled. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Intermediate data provided by reporters are analyzed and in the case of unusual data reporters are contacted to obtain additional information. Changes in the stock data are reconciled with changes in the corresponding financial assets/liabilities data collected for the financial account items of the balance of payments. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Statistical discrepancies and other potential indicators of problems in statistical outputs are monitored and investigated. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions are made for the current and previous year. Data are also revised ad hoc whenever addition information becomes available. In case of significant revisions, it is noted in publications with an explanation of the circumstance under which the revision is made. A footnote bellows the tables identifies the most recent data as preliminary data. Revisions are also noted in publications. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Analyses of revisions are regularly conducted (before publication of new IIP) and are for internal use. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request The IIP of Montenegro is published at the Central bank of Montenegro website. The Central bank of Montenegro meets requests for additional information without a fee.
