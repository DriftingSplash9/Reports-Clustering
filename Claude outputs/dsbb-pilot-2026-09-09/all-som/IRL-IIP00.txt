 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: The data collection system is designed in line with the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) recommendations and the presentation of the international investment position statistics conforms closely with international presentation formats. The external debt statistics exclude equity and financial derivative contracts which conform with the IMF’s External Debt manual. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The data collected follows as far as possible the recommendations of the IMF’s Balance of Payments Manual - 6th Edition (BPM6) concerning compilation of international investment position statistics. Four categories of functional investment are distinguished in the tables presented (i.e. direct investment, portfolio investment, other investment and reserve assets) and are based primarily on the relationship between the parties and secondly on the nature of the instrument involved. The category other investment includes the marked-to-market values of financial derivative positions. 2.2.1 Scope The data collected follows as far as possible the recommendations of the IMF’s Balance of Payments Manual - 6th Edition (BPM6) concerning compilation of international investment position statistics. Four categories of functional investment are distinguished in the tables presented (i.e. direct investment, portfolio investment, other investment and reserve assets) and are based primarily on the relationship between the parties and secondly on the nature of the instrument involved. The category other investment includes the marked-to-market values of financial derivative positions. Scope of the data Coverage of external financial assets and liabilities: The IIP results cover Ireland's total holdings (i.e. stocks) of financial assets and liabilities vis-a-vis non-residents at 31 December of each year. The information published nationally for reference periods commencing in 1998 incorporates a more comprehensive coverage of the activities of financial service enterprises operating from Ireland's International Financial Services Centre (IFSC) and elsewhere in Ireland. In addition, estimates of the purchases by households of residential property abroad are included. Geographical coverage: The ‘debtor/creditor’ principle is used as the basic guideline for geographical allocation of foreign assets and liabilities. Asset positions are allocated to the country of residence of the debtor counterpart. Liability positions should be allocated to the country of residence of the creditor counterpart. In the case of direct investment, transactions are geographically attributed on the basis of country of location of immediate ownership of the direct investment enterprise rather than that of the ultimate beneficial owner. Sectoral coverage: In terms of sectoral coverage, the results will reflect the activities of all sectors: government, monetary authority, monetary financial institutions excluding the monetary authority and other sectors – all other corporations and (implicitly) households. Included are: other financial intermediaries, i.e. investment funds, insurance companies and pension funds, asset finance companies, treasury companies, securities traders and other financial service companies, as well as non-financial service and manufacturing companies, other industrial enterprises, households and non-profit institutions serving household. Exceptions to coverage Sectoral coverage: There is no direct coverage of the household sector in the data collection system as, from the evidence available, it is understood that the bulk of the financial transactions of resident households with non-residents are implicitly captured in the survey of financial institutions. However, it would seem that there is increasing incidence of household acquisition/disposal of securities by means of the internet. Such activity is very difficult to detect directly from statistical inquiries. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation principles: In principle, IIP statistics (like BOP flows) should be recorded using current market values at the reference date and data providers are requested to report their data on this basis. In practice, this may not be possible in certain cases. Direct investment in equity capital is a particular case in point. Market valuation where not directly available is generally approximated using one of the following in order of preference: (a) a recent transaction price, (b) directors’ valuation, or (c) net asset value. Where stock data are converted from foreign currency to the Euro equivalent, data providers are required to use the spot rate for the relevant currency against the Euro on the reference date. Where positions are returned in non-Euro currency, the equivalent Euro valuation is obtained by applying the reference date spot exchange rate. Valuation of stocks should also reflect accruals recording principles particularly in relation to unpaid interest and trade credits. 2.4.2 Recording basis Conversion to unit of account: Where stock data are converted from foreign currency to the Euro equivalent, data providers are requested to use the reference day spot rate for the currency involved against the Euro. Recording basis: Data on stocks (and flows) are requested on an accruals basis in line with BPM6 requirements. Hence, acquisition of assets for which settlement does not take place until after the IIP reference date is recorded as part of stocks (assets) as is the corresponding liability in relation to payment. The reference date is the 31 December of each year. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The information used by CSO to compile the IIP results for Ireland is obtained primarily from CSO surveys and from administrative records. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording CSO surveys: Stocks data are collected by means of a number of surveys as follows: Foreign assets and liabilities of manufacturing and non-financial service enterprises: This survey covers direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives transactions and other investment flows as well as the related income flows. The reinvested earnings of foreign direct investment enterprises are distinguished in the survey form. Financial service enterprises: are, since 1998, subjected to quarterly surveys requesting all BOP-relevant data (i.e. BOP current, capital and financial account flows) as well as stock data on assets and liabilities with the required details by type of investment and by financial instrument. In order to encourage better response, these surveys are customised according to the type of financial service activity undertaken (e.g. insurance/reinsurance, treasury, asset financing and leasing etc.). Enterprises with lower levels of activity are surveyed annually. Since 2008 the data required from licensed banks (credit institutions) and from investment funds (including money market funds) to meet BOP, IIP and external debt requirements (as well as other statistical demands on both organisations) are being collected quarterly by the CBI under its legislation as well as European legislation. Complete coverage of the relevant financial enterprises is targeted over the course of a full year. Administrative records: Information on government foreign borrowings is obtained from the National Treasury Management Agency (which operates under the aegis of the Department of Finance). The Central Bank and Financial Services Authority of Ireland provides data from licensed banks (credit institutions) and from investment funds (including money market funds) to meet BOP, IIP and external debt requirements (as well as other statistical demands on both organisations) are being collected quarterly by the CBI under its legislation as well as European legislation. These data are supplied by the CBI to CSO for statistical compilation purposes The Central Bank commenced data collection form Financial Vehicle Corporations (FVC) in the last quarter of 2009. Preliminary data from this survey has been used to improve the existing estimates for FVCs in the current and financial accounts. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Statistical adjustments: The data required are collected quarterly on a statutory basis by means of surveys conducted by the CSO and by the Central Bank of Ireland (CBI). Other data obtained from administrative sources are also used. The data are entered into the BOPFACTS computer processing system, edited, and processed. Estimates are generated for partial or complete non-response on the basis of previous data available for the non-respondents. The results from this process along with the data collected from administrative sources are combined into aggregate spreadsheets designed to facilitate publication once the information is considered to be reliable and consistent with the BOP flow and IIP stock data. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Verification: The preparation of the IIP results involves considerable data checking against data available externally. The results are compared to other published information (e.g. banking and collective investment statistics published by the Central Bank and Financial Services Authority of Ireland, data from industry associations).
