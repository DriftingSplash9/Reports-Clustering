 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The estimates are based on concepts and definitions of the 1968 System of National Accounts(1968 SNA). Simultaneous with the rebasing of the national accounts to 2002, the compilation methodology will be brought in line with the 1993 SNA. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Economic activities classification is based on the 4-digit United Nations International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC rev 3). The activities included are: Agriculture; Forestry; Fishing; Mining and Quarrying; Manufacturing; Construction; Electricity and Water; Wholesale and Retail trade; Restaurants and Hotels; Transport, Storage and Communications; Finance, Insurance, Real Estate and Business Services, Government, Social, Cultural and Personal Services and the activities of Non-Profit Institutions Serving Households. Scope of the data The National Accounts covers the entire Republic of Ghana. The estimates are prepared in current prices as well as constant 1993 base year prices. The national accounts cover all institutional units within the territorial boundaries of Ghana. Transactions in goods and services are covered in the production accounts as well as expenditures in the expenditure approach, but there is no independent estimate of household final consumption expenditure, which is obtained as the residual. The production account of Ghana’s National Accounts figures are grouped into three broad economic activities, that is; Agriculture Industry Services Each of these groups is further subdivided, broadly according to first (classification) level of ISIC rev 3., but not to the full extent. The items under the expenditure approach include: Private Final Consumption Government Final Consumption Gross Capital Formation (GFCF), which includes changes in stocks(inventories) Imports of goods and Services Exports of goods and services Unrecorded activity No explicit adjustments are made annually for unrecorded activities, but estimates derived from the 1991/92 Ghana Living Standards Survey (GLSS3) have been included in the 1993 base-year information. 2.4.1 Valuation The national accounts are given at market prices, in purchasers' values. 2.4.2 Recording basis Timing of recording : The data are recorded on accrual basis except for Government data, which are on cash basis. Work is in progress, with help of the Government Finance Statistics (GFS) Manual 2001, to harmonize the government data to suit National Accounts purposes. Agricultural data are on crop year basis. Only cocoa production is modified from crop to calendar year by prorating the annual main crop over the two successive calendar years. Recording Period : The recording period for the national accounts is the calendar year. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The National Accounts of Ghana are compiled by National Accounts Section of the Ghana Statistical Service. With only infrequent extensive economic and household surveys being conducted, much of the information is derived from (short-term) indicators. In some areas, such as construction output and capital formation, the commodity flow approach is used. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording GDP by production approach (industrial activity) is based on a variety of sources, including surveys, administrative records and indirect proxies for output. Agriculture : The economic activities covered include: (i) growing of food crops, fruits, nuts, vegetables, coffee, rubber and other plantation crops mainly for exports (ii) cocoa production and marketing (iii) raising of poultry and livestock, hunting, trapping and gathering (iv) forestry and logging and (v) fishing. The main sources of data are the Ministry of Food and Agriculture (MOFA), Animal Health and production department, Ghana Cocoa Board, Forestry department and Fisheries department. Data from censuses and surveys like the Ghana Living Standard Surveys rounds are also used. The value added from Agriculture and allied activities is the sum of the value added from all the different activities included in this group. Industry: Included in this group are mining and quarrying, manufacturing, water and electricity and construction. Mining and Quarrying: The Ghana Minerals Commission and the Ghana Chamber of Mines are the main sources of data for estimating output and value added in both current and constant prices. For manufacturing , two categories exist (i) large scale formal sector establishment (ii) small scale informal sector activity. The most comprehensive source of information on large scale manufacturing enterprises is the report on the 1987 Ghana National Industrial Census (1991). Updated information will be available upon completion of the on-going 2003 National Industrial Census. The manufacturing data is updated by the monthly index of industrial output constructed for a panel of large firms. Electricity and Water: Volta River Authority (VRA) and Electricity Company of Ghana (ECG) provide data on both value and volume of electric power generated, transmitted and distributed in Ghana. The electricity sold to ECG by VRA is netted off as intermediate consumption in order to avoid double counting. The Ghana Water Company (GWC) provides information on water production, treatment and distribution, other similar data are obtained from local authorities not serviced by the GWC. Construction: Estimates of gross output and value added are obtained using a commodity-flow approach. The activities cover construction, repairs, alteration demolition of buildings, highways, etc. The 2000 Population and Housing Census provided current information to update the old benchmarks. Services. Included in this group are Transport, Storage and Communication, Wholesale and Retail Trade, Restaurants and Hotels, Finance, Insurance, Real Estate and Business Services, Government Services, Community, Social and Personal Services and Producers of Private Non- profit Services. Transport, storage and Communication: Information on land, air, water transport, telecommunication and Postal Services are obtained from recognized institutions and bodies. The Driver and Vehicle Licensing Authority (DVLA) provides data on the number of commercial and private vehicles, the daily sales of the commercial operators are obtained and multiplied by the number of vehicles in the various categories. The annual accounts of the large private operators together with state owned operators are analyzed. Ghana Telecom,the largest telecommunication operator in Ghana provide data on the number of fixed lines, etc. Estimates for Wholesale and Retail Trade, Restaurants and Hotels are composed of many components. For trade, these are generally based on the commodity flow approach, with shares of various types of commodities entering the trade channels estimated for the benchmark year and then projected forwards. The level of activities in the base period for hotel and restaurants was determined from a special survey of large hotels and the GLSS3 household survey data for small and informal sector units. These have been updated using tourism arrivals and population growth respectively. Banking and Insurance: Annual reports of all the companies are used as the information is timely and comprehensive. By law, by the end of the first quarter of the following year, the annual reports of the previous year should be ready. The National Insurance Commission, an umbrella body to supervise the insurance companies provides all the data needed for this activity. Real Estate and Business Services: House property and data relating to replacement cost and rateable value by type of house are collected by the Ministry of Local Government. The rate of growth in the housing sector was obtained from the 1984 population census is used in relation with the Prime Building Cost Index (PBCI). Output is taken to be equal to the imputed rent paid, while repairs and maintenance represent intermediate consumption. These values are inflated by the population growth rate and the PBCI. Business services are largely estimated on the basis of the number of registered practitioners in the various trades. Government output is derived from the analysis of the Government budget. The estimates for Community, Social and Personal Services are partially based on responses to a special questionnaire sent to some institutions, while the majority of the information has been obtained from the household survey. Annual estimates are mostly prepared using population growth. For Producers of Private Non- profit Services , data is mostly obtained directly from the organisations, either as annual reports or through a special survey. The number of domestic employees was obtained from the population census. Net indirect taxes are added to the activity-based estimates, but no provision has been made for including the FISIM adjustment in the estimates. Estimates for a number of components of the Expenditure approach are prepared, but no independent estimate for the largest component, household final consumption expenditure, is prepared. Government final consumption expenditures are obtained from the government budget. Gross fixed capital formation is derived from the output of the construction industry and imports (plus some local production) of capital goods. Estimates of Changes in Inventories are limited to cocoa, gold and some other major commodities as no other sources of information are available. Data on the Imports and Exports of goods and services are based on the information obtained from customs (goods) and the central bank (services in the balance of payments). As mentioned earlier, no independent estimates of household consumption are prepared and the numbers given for household consumption therefore also include the statistical discrepancy between the production and expenditure estimates. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The Statistical Service usually uses both the production and expenditure approaches. Reconciliation is done only for those activities and expenditures estimated using the commodity flows, viz. construction and fixed capital formation. Private consumption is obtained as residual. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs None
