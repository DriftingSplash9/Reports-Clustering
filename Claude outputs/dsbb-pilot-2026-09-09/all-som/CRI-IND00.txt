 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: In general, data are compiled following the guidelines of the SNA 2025. Definition The BCCR compiles the IMAE using a chained monthly Laspeyres-type index, it has as reference year 2022. Is a short-term indicator intended to approximate the progress of the gross value to a given month for the different industries included in the calculation of the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) at basic prices. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The economic activity is posted according the grouped of the sections defined in the International Standard Industrial Classification of All Economic Activities (ISIC), revision 4. The time series is available since 1991. Methodology of calculation Chained volume index If the monthly changes are linked together to form a time series, short-term volatility in relative price it may cause the chain-linked monthly measures to show substantial drift compared to corresponding direct measures. There is a positive correlation between the intensity and stability of the seasonal component of the series and the drift problem. The annual overlap technique ensures the monthly data aggregate exactly to the corresponding direct index. Seasonal adjustment The IMAE is highly seasonal. Hence, the series is adjusted by DEMETRA; TRAMO-SEATS or X12-ARIMA and broken it down into trend cycle, seasonality, and irregular component. C – Manufacturing The production of the manufacture distinguishes between two types of regimes: the definitive and special regime. Definitive regime The primary information used to calculate the monthly index is a combination of administrative data, production surveys carried out by the Economics Surveys Area unit and administrative records from the Ministry of Finance. This regime were grouped into five: i) miscellaneous food products (including beverages and tobacco); ii) textiles, clothing and leather industry; iii) paper and paper products, printing and publishing; chemicals and petroleum chemicals; iv) manufacture of metal products, machinery and equipment and v) other manufacturing (vehicles, parts and accessories of vehicles, wooden furniture, medical and dental instruments, other manufacturing products). Special regimes (include inward processing and free zones) Production data regarding special regimes are based on exports, cover all the establishments classified under these regimes, and consider a change in ownership. In order to keep consistency within the manufacturing industry, a similar aggregation to definitive regimen is used with one exception: regarding equipment and medical instruments products because their participation into the special regimen sales (over 50%). Basic data sources: General Directorate of Customs and administrative records from the Ministry of Finance. D,E - Electricity, water supply, sewerage, waste management and remediation activities. The monthly index of economic activity for the electricity and water sectors is constructed using data from the Costa Rican Electricity Institute (ICE) and the Costa Rican Institute of Aqueducts and Sewers (AyA). This index is based on volume indicators that measure the performance of both sectors, capturing their contribution to the overall economy. For the electricity sector, the index utilizes data on monthly sales in megawatt-hours (MWh), reported by ICE. These sales figures are disaggregated by demand groups, including residential, general, small industry, large industries, large high-voltage industries, and public lighting. The sales volumes serve as primary inputs, with weights assigned to each demand group based on their economic significance. This ensures that the electricity sector's contribution to the combined index is proportionate to its actual economic impact. For the water sector, the index incorporates monthly data on cubic meters of water demanded, provided by AyA. The data is disaggregated into aqueducts and sewer systems and further divided into consumption blocks such as residential, business, government, and preferential. Similar to the electricity sector, weights are applied to these consumption categories according to their relative importance, allowing the water sector's activity to be accurately represented within the index. By combining these volume indicators, the index reflects the overall economic activity of the electricity and water sectors as a unified measure. Seasonal adjustments are applied to account for regular fluctuations in demand, and the index is benchmarked to a base year to facilitate consistent comparisons over time. This methodology ensures that the index provides a comprehensive and reliable measure of the contributions of electricity and water services to the broader economy. F – Construction The activity of the construction is grouped according to the destiny of the production: private and public destination. The monthly index with private destination is calculated using as an indicator the meters actually built by the different buildings or building prototypes (Housing Social Interest, middle and upper class, wine cellar, buildings, business premises, shopping plaza, urbanization and repairs and renovations of prototypes), and for which the BCCR in conjunction with the Association of Engineers and Architects (CFIA) conducted a quarterly survey of Advancement of Projects with Private destination (ETAPCP). The basic information used for the statistical design of the study sample in the survey corresponds to the monthly records of professional responsibility of construction projects (which could have more than one project in progress) registered with the CFIA and the unit of measure them are the square meters. The monthly index of economic activity for public construction is constructed using data on budget execution reported by public institutions to the Comptroller General's Office (CGR) and the Ministry of Finance. This data serves as the primary input for calculating the index. To approximate the volume of public construction, the construction price index is considered. The sample of institutions included in the index consists of the Central Government, Municipalities, non-financial public corporations, and public trusts whose purpose is construction projects. The budget execution data is disaggregated by the construction activities undertaken by these entities, and each entity's contribution to the index is weighted based on the relative size and importance of their projects in the overall public construction sector. In constructing the index, the volume indicators reflect the financial resources allocated and spent on public construction activities, adjusted by the construction price index to account for price variations. Seasonal factors are considered, and the index is benchmarked to a base year, allowing for consistent comparisons over time. This approach ensures that the index accurately represents the economic activity of the public construction sector, offering valuable insights into its performance and contributions to the broader economy. Thus, adjustments are made, when possible, to the source data as part of the process of validating the data for compiling the accounts. special effort took place on the occasion of changing the reference year, as the consistency of the results from the surveys was reviewed and adjustments were made, including those due to undervaluation of the results. The corrections involved adjusting the valuation to account for underreporting of income and overvaluation of production costs. The opinion of accounting experts interviewed contributed to this exercise. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Timing of production observations: The information is gathered monthly from various sources. The data are collected in the first 10 days of each month and correspond to the month before last. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data are analyzed in the context of revisions. All material changes from available up-to-date data are incorporated into the national accounts statistics according to a revision cycle that prevents the accumulation of revisions over long periods of time. Editing procedures identify outliers and other atypical differences in periodic responses by individual survey units. Extreme values are confirmed with respondents. Source data are analyzed to correct for misreporting, particularly temporal consistency. Consistency with other related source data are checked, when possible. Also, the accuracy of government finance statistics, merchandise trade statistics, volume and price statistics, and other secondary sources used to compile the national accounts statistics are assessed. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Procedures for non-response: When certain industries occasionally (infrequently) fail to respond in time for completing the calculations for the IMAE, estimates for such enterprises are made in each case after checking these firms' history and on the basis of qualitative information supplied by other enterprises who do respond. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Time series components The whole time series is showed in their four components: Trend: A trend exists when there is a long-term increase or decrease in the data. A trend can be positive or negative depending on whether the time series exhibits an increasing or a decreasing long-term pattern. Cyclical component: Any pattern showing an up and down movement to a given trend is identified as a cyclical pattern. The duration of a cycle depends on the type of business or industry being analyzed. Irregular component: This component is unpredictable. Seasonal component: Seasonality occurs when the time series exhibits regular fluctuations during the same month (or months) every year, or during the same quarter every year. Detected discrepancies in intermediate data are assessed, investigated, and reconciled or removed. The resulting data are then entered into the supply and use table, in which framework a further balancing process takes place. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The supply and use framework is used to investigate discrepancies and eliminate them during the process of balancing the table. The possible causes of detected discrepancies are investigated and the revised figures are included. During the last phase of the process, the table is balanced by using changes in inventories as an adjustment variable. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Analysis of revisions is made monthly to validate and explain the changes between preliminary data, estimated data (as they are called when disseminated), and definitive data. Studies on long-term trends in the revision pattern to identify systematic biased revisions are occasionally performed. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Explanations to users on discrepancies between final and previous estimates are given only on special occasions. Revised series are generally disseminated without analysis of revisions. Studies on long-term trends in the revision pattern to identify systematic biased revisions are not performed. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Non-confidential information is provided upon request.
