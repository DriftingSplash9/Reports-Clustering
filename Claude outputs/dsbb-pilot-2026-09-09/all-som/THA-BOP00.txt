 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions BOP statistics systematically summarize, for a specific time period, all economic transactions between residents and nonresidents. Transactions for the most part between residents and nonresidents, consist of those involving goods, services, primary income, secondary income, capital account, financial claims and liabilities vis-à-vis the rest of the world. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification and sectorization is in line with the recommendations stated in the sixth edition of the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6). Scope of the data Transactions coverage: The data cover all transactions, in-cash or in-kind, between Thai residents and nonresidents. Geographical coverage: The data cover transactions of Thai residents vis-à-vis residents of all other countries. Main components of the balance of payments are also disseminated with country breakdown. Exceptions to coverage Not applicable Unrecorded activity Excluded from balance of payments statistics are shuttle trade, smuggling, and other unrecorded activities. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: Transactions are valued at market prices, or their proxies when actual market prices are not available. 2.4.2 Recording basis Conversion to unit of account: The exchange rate conversion is based on monthly average of the mid-rate between bid and offer rates quoted by commercial banks. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Most items in the current account are recorded on a gross basis. However, some transactions reported through the International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) could potentially be reported on a net basis, and hence could lead to understate gross flows for a few items for which ITRS is the sole data source. Financial account transactions are recorded on a net basis, with gross flows disseminated for major components. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The BOP compilation system combines data from various sources; namely, the Customs Department, the ITRS maintained by the BOT, data from other administrative sources (including government agencies, state enterprises, and other entities), securities database, and enterprise surveys. Goods : Exports and imports of goods are based on international merchandise trade data obtained from the Customs Department, supplemented with reports from relevant government agencies and state enterprises. Services : Main sources of data on services include the ITRS, administrative data (e.g., Ministry of Tourism and Sports, Immigration Bureau, Ministry of Public Health, Revenue Department, and Ministry of Higher Education, Science, Research and Innovation), business surveys, direct reports from enterprises, credit and debit cards data, money changers' data, money transfer operators' data, E-payment and the estimation model for travel, freight and insurance on imports, and financial intermediation services indirectly measured (FISIM). Primary income : Estimation model based on data from the Ministry of Labour (MOL) for compensation of employees, direct reports from enterprises, estimation model based on information from government agencies, securities database, as well as external debt survey for investment income, supplemented by the ITRS. Secondary income : Main sources of data are primarily the ITRS, supplemented with reports from relevant government agencies, as well as estimation models for workers' remittances based on the MOL's data. Capital account : Main sources of data are direct report from relevant government agencies, media, news and external debt survey, supplemented by the ITRS Direct investment : Main sources of data are IIP survey, external debt survey, trade credit survey, the Ministry of Finance (MOF) for public sector transactions, direct report from relevant agencies, and securities database, supplemented by the ITRS and corporate financial data obtained through administrative source. Portfolio investment : Main sources of data are the MOF, reports from securities database, external debt survey, and IIP survey, supplemented by the ITRS and corporate financial data obtained through administrative source. Other investment : Main sources of data are the MOF, reports from state enterprises, external debt survey, offshore deposits from the BIS, and IIP survey, supplemented by the ITRS. Data for trade credits are derived from trade credit survey and customs data. Reserve assets : Data on reserve assets (comprising monetary gold, SDRs, reserve position in the Fund, and foreign exchange) are administrative records from the BOT's Finance and Accounting Department. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data definitions, scope, and valuation are broadly consistent with the BPM6 . Adjustments are made in selected cases in terms of re-classifications, sectorization, and change of time of recording to bring about closer conformity to BPM6 recommendations. For ITRS and enterprise surveys, explanatory notes are provided to data reporters for better understanding of reporting requirements, as a measure to improve the quality of reported data. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Most inputs are available on a monthly basis, with one-month lag. Data from quarterly surveys (telecommunication survey, external debt survey, and preliminary IIP survey) are one-quarter lagged, while annual surveys (IIP survey based on audited financial statement, and trade credit survey) are eight-month lagged. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Data sources – including surveys, administrative records and ITRS – are routinely assessed and validated. Source data for BOP statistics are analyzed for underreporting/ misreporting, in particular to check for temporal consistency and consistency with other related data sources. Data providers are immediately contacted for clarification on doubtful data. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Goods : The Customs data record physical movement of goods across the Thai Customs boundary. Therefore, some adjustments are made in respect of coverage, valuation, and time of recording to bring the customs data into conformity with the BOP concept. Adjustments for both imports and exports are made to include aircraft, Thai military imports, satellite, and to exclude diplomatic shipments, personal effects, sample goods, and temporary imports/exports. Valuation adjustments are made to adjust imports from c.i.f. basis to f.o.b. basis by deducting freight and insurance from the c.i.f. value of general merchandise, fuel and monetary gold. Moreover, due to the information that has been discussed and agreed with the national account, non-resident operators carry 95% of freight, hence only 95% of freight costs is subtracted. Services : Freight and insurance on imports are estimated from invoice data and the publicly available information on international freight rates. Travel receipts and payments are compiled by applying stock of visitors remaining at the end of each day and the average expenditure per person per day, based on expenditure surveys of visitors carried out by the Ministry of Tourism and Sports (MOTS). The BOT makes some adjustments to the MOTS' estimates to (i) include travel receipts and payments for health-related travel; (ii) include travel receipts and payments for education-related travel; and (iii) include travel receipts and payments for acquisition of goods and services by border, seasonal, and other short-term workers. Primary income : Both inflows and outflows for compensation of employees cover mostly workers, whose work contracts are normally granted for a period of less than year. Interest income is estimated by applying average interest rate to respective debt positions, calculated separately for each sector/instrument. Reinvested earnings are obtained from IIP survey. Secondary income : Data sources include estimation based on MOL's data (on workers' remittances), supplemented by ITRS and administrative data on in-kind grants. Capital account : Acquisitions/disposals of nonproduced, nonfinancial assets (e.g. purchase/sale of embassies' land) and capital transfers (if any) are sourced directly from relevant government agencies, news and media, supplemented by the ITRS. Debt forgiveness is sourced from quarterly external debt survey. Financial account : Preliminary data based on ITRS, securities database, direct report and administrative data from relevant government agencies, and estimation models. Quarterly survey-based data (from external debt and IIP surveys conducted on nonbanks) replace preliminary ITRS-based data for applicable items. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Not applicable 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Intermediate results are cross-checked with related datasets as well as other data sources. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Related data series are cross-checked against each other. Data from ITRS are used to verify survey responses and used to derive estimates for non-response and non-sampled cases. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Errors and omissions are monitored in size, and direction to detect evolving pattern. Errors and omissions are also used as indicators to flag missing and overstated transactions. Compilers are committed to investigating any significant discrepancies that may be found. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses The DMD monitors the direction and magnitude of revisions and studies the effect of revisions on selected items and periods (including seasonal effects). Documentation on major revisions is maintained for internal use, with detailed information on causes of revision. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses The public is informed of major changes in methodology at the time the change is introduced through a press release and a notice placed on the BOT's website. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request In addition to the BOP statistics and related data routinely published (such as trade indices), more detailed series can be made available upon request. Unpublished (but non-confidential) specialized tabulations can be provided. Users are informed of the relevant contact information (division contact email) to submit inquiries. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The DMD publishes contact numbers and email addresses for corresponding tables. The DMD commits to responding to all requests within three working days. The assistance provided to data users is regularly monitored through users' satisfaction surveys. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The DMD provides a list of publications, documents, and other services, which are updated on an annual basis. Products are available free of charge.
