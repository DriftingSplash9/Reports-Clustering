 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Kuwait Foreign Trade Statistics are compiled according to IMTS2010. For trade statistics, the most important international agreement adopted by WTO is the WTO Agreement on Customs Valuation (Calculation of statistical value for import (CIF) and export (FOB)). FTS are mostly harmonized with international standards. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Statistical data are available by HS (Harmonized System) 201 7 SITC (Standard International Trade Classification) Rev.3 SITC Rev.4 ISIC (International Standard Industrial Classification) Rev.3.1 ISIC Rev.4 BEC (Broad Economic Categories) CPC (Central Product Classification) Rev.2 2.2.1 Scope Statistical data are available by HS (Harmonized System) 201 7 SITC (Standard International Trade Classification) Rev.3 SITC Rev.4 ISIC (International Standard Industrial Classification) Rev.3.1 ISIC Rev.4 BEC (Broad Economic Categories) CPC (Central Product Classification) Rev.2 Scope of the data The definition of trade system used in Kuwait is “Special Trade System, Relaxed definition”. There is no declaration or other documents for the trade between Free zone and rest of World, but Customs compiles declaration trade between Kuwait and free zone. Therefore, in practice trade data between Customs warehouses, free zone and the rest of the World, are not recorded. Moreover, there is only one free zone in Kuwait and no processing and industrial activities (only commercial). Though there is no specific area for inward processing, it is included in trade statistics. Exceptions to coverage Following goods are excluded from Foreign Trade Statistics: Monetary gold, Means of payment which are legal tender and securities, including means which are payments for services such as postage, taxes, user fees (The exclusion is based on HS 4907 00 31, HS 4907 00 32 codes, additional controls are made), Goods for or following temporary use (e.g. hire, loan, operational leasing), Software downloaded from the Internet, Goods supplied free of charge which are themselves not the subject of a commercial transaction, provided that their movement is with the sole intention of preparing or supporting an intended subsequent trade transaction by demonstrating the characteristics of goods or services such as: — advertising material, — commercial samples; Goods for and after repair and replacement parts that are incorporated in the framework of the repair and the replaced defective parts Means of transport travelling in the course of their work, including spacecraft launchers at the time of launching; Goods declared orally to Customs Authorities which are either of a commercial nature provided that their value does not exceed the statistical thresholds in terms of value and/or quantity, or of a non-commercial nature; Goods in transit Goods delivered to the ships, Goods supplied by and to enclaves of foreign governments and international organizations, Goods entering or leaving the economic territory of a country illegally, Military goods. 2.4.1 Valuation Statistical value : Statistical value for exports is FOB (Free on Board) basis while imports are CIF (Cost, Insurance and Freight). In addition, Import on FOB (since 2015) basis also available for analysis purpose. 2.4.2 Recording basis In terms of statistical value, majority of transactions were converted by Customs. If the terms of delivery are CIF-type in exports and Customs didn’t convert into FOB, CSB converts all into FOB. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The main data source is Customs declaration in Kuwait. Since having Customs Union with the GCC Member countries, uniform customs declarations have been used in all member countries since January 1st, 2003 and majority of declarations are captured electronically. -Customs (Kuwait General Administration of Customs) is the main administration responsible for the collection of customs declarations, for preliminary checks on declaration, for application of penalties, for publishing the National Commodity Code List including Harmonized System (HS) changes in accordance with GCC amendments. -Customs will send trade data by CSB rules and record layout electronically. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording There are 14 Customs centers in Kuwait. Except Abdeli and Land Port, which are designated for Exports to Iraq by Land, all Customs centers collect the data electronically. CSB has been allowed to login in Customs system through Customs Web Site using “User Name” and “Password” provided by Customs. In addition to Customs declarations, CSB collects also information from different sources. Oil and its Products: According to Kuwait Laws, oil and its products are not declared to Customs. All the information related to these products is collected by Ministry of Oil from Oil Companies in Kuwait. Aircraft: All aircraft are registered by Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) in Kuwait. Quarterly questionnaire are sent to CSB including type of aircraft, value, quantity, reference month, flow (imports/exports), type of trade (Operational Leasing/Financial Leasing/Temporary/Normal). Goods delivered to Aircraft: CSB has conducted a survey since 2014 to collect information on goods delivered to foreign aircrafts in Kuwait (as exports), and to national aircraft abroad (as imports). Exports data are compiled from national companies providing these services to foreign airlines in Kuwait Airport, while imports data are compiled from two national airlines, Kuwait and Al-Jazeera Airlines. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data are transferred to CSB monthly basis and in 20 days following reference month (T+20) with “Cancel declarations” which are cancelled by Traders or Customs for some reasons after Customs sent to CSB. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques CSB has been developing an Oracle package program which contains 5 main menus since 2015. Main menus in program are;  Data Processing  Data entry and update  Maintenance of classifications & Look-up tables  Data dissemination  Trade Indices 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures In terms of statistical value, majority of transactions were converted by Customs. If the terms of delivery are CIF-type in exports and Customs didn’t convert into FOB, CSB converts all into FOB. In terms of imports; if terms of delivery are FOB-type and Customs didn’t convert the statistical value in CIF, CSB converts to CIF type by using ratios calculated by mode of transport and main partner countries. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Validity checks are done for the following dimensions;  Commodity code,  Currency code for invoice, freight, insurance,  Terms of delivery,  Mode of transport,  Nationality of transporter,  Measurement unit,  Country of destination,  Country of origin,  Country of consignment,  Customs center,  Customs section,  Re-export code 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Every quarter, previous quarters’ figures are revised with current quarter’s news release according to  New data (customs declaration),  Corrections on declarations made by traders,  Late answers from traders,  Corrections on data,  Methodological changes Annual data are available in September following the reference year (September Y+1). 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Revised data are published with current period data. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request CSB provides foreign trade statistics data according to national and international users’ request. Also prepares tailor-made data on users’ requests.
