 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The overall structure of the GFS of Suriname is based on the concepts and definitions of the IMF’s Government Finance Statistics Manual 2001(GFSM 2001). This manual has been superseded by the GFSM 2014 that introduced, among other things, a different definition of general government, accrual recording in the main tables, updated classifications, and full balance sheets. The authorities have been considering introducing some elements of the more recent manual, in particular, accrual accounting. However, the statistics are reported first on a cash basis from the administrative financial records. Current budget legislation, the Organic Budget Law, supports recording expense on an “acquired right” (verkregen rechten) basis. This budget recording is on an accruals accounting basis. One exception is that certain payments can be allowed to be made after the end of the budget year. Currently it is being studied to what extent and in which phases to introduce the GFSM 2014 . The Outstanding Debt tables compiled by the SDMO are broken down by type of debt holder and by instrument. Debt is defined according to international recommendations (in practice the Commonwealth Secretariat Debt Management, Recording and Monitory System and the IFMIS, the External Debt Statistics, guide for compilers and users are utilized), as well as according to the Public Debt Act 2002 and the amendments of the Public Debt Act, dated March 2016. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification used in the Suriname GFS operation tables are broadly consistent with internationally accepted standards; and so are the published outstanding debt tables. The distinction between domestic and foreign counterparts to economic operations and debt is correctly made on the basis of residency, such as the international manuals recommend. 2.2.1 Scope The classification used in the Suriname GFS operation tables are broadly consistent with internationally accepted standards; and so are the published outstanding debt tables. The distinction between domestic and foreign counterparts to economic operations and debt is correctly made on the basis of residency, such as the international manuals recommend. Scope of the data Two types of statements are being produced: (i) a set of 15 tables summarizes and detailing central government operations for Revenue and Grants, Expenditure and Lending Minus Repayments, and Financing and (ii) a set of 6 tables that break down Outstanding Debt by holder and by instrument. The scope of the GFS of Suriname is limited to the central government operations in revenues, expenditures and financing. No statistics on the operations of parastatals (including social security funds) or local government agencies are compiled. However, statistics on subsidy and other transfers made from the central budget to these entities are compiled and published. Tables with a functional breakdown of expenditure are compiled for a few items. The SDMO also covers bank loans guaranteed by the government, which are assumed by parastatal agencies. Exceptions to coverage None. Unrecorded activity None. 2.4.1 Valuation Operations are valued at the amount of cash actually paid. Debt covers the value of the outstanding principal amounts, including fines on late payment and the value of construction work delivered but not yet paid. Accrued but not yet paid interest is not counted towards government debt. In practice none of the Suriname public debt instruments (including treasury bills) is traded on markets, so actual market prices do not exist. Transactions in foreign currency are converted to local currency using the mid-point exchange rate prevailing in the market at the moment they take place. Outstanding debt held by nonresidents is published in US$ values and the local currency equivalent in accordance with the stipulations of the Debt Act. 2.4.2 Recording basis Operations are recorded in the period that the related payments are made (cash basis), and subsequently the process to attribute payments to appropriate budget years is started. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures All operations are recorded on a gross basis, except for lending and financing transactions as well as corrective transactions (such as refunds of taxes and overpaid expenses), which are shown net in accordance with international guidelines. Debt is recorded on a gross basis. Some subsidies and netting schemes are included in the statistics, for which the authorities are now in the process of presenting these on a gross basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Difficulties in obtaining appropriate source data are a main reason for limiting the scope of the Suriname GFS to central government. Expanding the coverage to the general government sector is not planned in the short run. No register of public sector units is available yet. The timeliness of some of the available sources on budgetary government could be improved and budget classifications would accommodate GFS requirements after full adoption of the new Chart of Accounts (CoA). The main source for revenue data banks’ statements on the accounts held by the government. Revenue statistics are subsequently reconciled with the records of the tax administration, which tend to be on an accruals basis but reported with a lag. Nontax revenue is available from the bank sources. Data on foreign grants receipts are collected by the Ministry of Finance. The main source for financing transactions are the SDMO and the CBvS reports. The chart of accounts (CoA) is segmented and coded in a way which allows for mapping to GFS categories. The coa was recently revised to improve its suitability for appropriate bridge tables to GFS. These are now being constructed to facilitate the compilation of government finance statistics from the underlying accounting information. However, only expenditure data is captures in the IFMIS system. Revenue and financing data are being compiled in addition. The EAD monitors international standards, guidelines, and practices for changes that need to be taken into account in the compilation system for government finance statistics. The EAD consults the data supplying agencies in compilers’ meetings that are called regularly. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data are partially consistent with international guidelines. For instance, the time of recording and valuation in the sources meet the needs of GFS. The source data provide, to a large extent, sufficient detail on the counterpart’s operations to be able to consolidate Suriname’s GFS. However, as currently the statistics are limited to budgetary central government and ministries do not normally engage in payments among each other, consolidation is not an issue of any practical importance. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Some source data are not timely enough for the monthly GFS publication; hence, timeliness is set at 2 months. The data for the current year and the previous year are considered “preliminary.” If needed, data can still be revised back in time. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The accuracy of reported source data is checked for coverage, accuracy, inter-temporal consistency and consistency across different sources. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques All detailed information may not yet be available when the first monthly GFS estimates on a cash basis are being prepared. These are based on information derived from accounts that the MoF maintains with the CBvS and banks. Wages/salaries data are replaced by the corresponding data originating from the Central Bureau for Mechanized Administration and the proper adjustments are made to other categories. The interest payments, external disbursements, treasury paper issuance, and loan amortization data are received from the SDMO. The secondary administrative source that become available concern the budget execution and the tax administration data on an accruals basis. In practice, these reports are issued with delays. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures None. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Intermediate results are checked against other information if available. This is particular the case in preparing the publication of the Financial Note, which forms part of the documentation provided to the National Assembly on occasion of the presentation of the budget for the coming year. The Financial Note contains macroeconomic information from various sources and care is taken that these data are consistent to the extent possible. Validation of GFS against the financial statements of large mining enterprises, which (in the past) tended to pay considerable taxes to government, is an example of the type of substantiation that takes place using independent data sources. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data To some extent, reported financial flow data on nonfinancial and financial liabilities are reconciled with changes in the corresponding stock data. For instance, the amount of interest payments may be compared with the amount of outstanding debt 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The Suriname GFS includes a statistical discrepancy between the overall balance and financing which is shown separately. Checking the value and sign of the discrepancy is a standard component of the compilation process because it is an indicator of possible coverage and timing issues in the source statistics. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses The character of revisions to the data is reviewed, but this does not lead to formal studies into the sources of errors, omissions, and fluctuations in the source data. The administrative character of the sources does not indicate that such studies could benefit the compilation process or lead to the definition of a formal revision cycle. Adequate documentation on revisions is maintained, including (a) identification of the main aspects of the source data leading to the revisions; (b) the reasons for the revisions; and (c) the direction and magnitude of the revisions. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Documented internally. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request On a case by case basis, because of confidentiality agreements with certain tax payers. In general very extensive data details are published for expenditures.
