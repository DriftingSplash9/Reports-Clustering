 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The Central Bank of Bahrain (CBB) is in charge of collecting the source data and compiling and disseminating balance of payments (BOP). The compilation framework of Bahrain’s BOP follows the recommendations of the IMF’s Balance of Payments Manua l, sixth edition (BPM 6 ) . Although the sectorization of BOP follows the recommendations of the BPM 6 , all of the national publications (the CBB’s Monthly Statistical Bulletin, Economic Report , and Economic Indicators ) do not provide detailed sectoral breakdown. This is contained in the Balance of Payments Report, which is published annually. The CBB also provides detailed BOP data to the IMF or any other party when requested, according to recommendations of the BPM 6 . The signs used in the assets side of the financial account is reversed (negative sign means increase and positive sign means decrease). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification system of the BOP is consistent with the recommendations of the BPM 6 . For the national publications purposes, summarized tables are prepared to be included in these publications. Current Account: It covers all the imported and exported goods and services, primary and secondary income accounts in the balance of payments. • Primary Income includes all the investment income, direct investment, portfolio investment, and others. Whereas, Secondary Income cover workers’ remittances. • In current account, when credits exceed the debits, in other words, when the difference is positive the result is called as current account surplus. • In contrast, the result is called a deficit when the debits exceed the credits. when the debits exceed the credits, in other words, when the difference is negative the result is called as current account deficit. Capital Account: It covers capital transfers. Financial Account: It covers the changes in external financial assets and liabilities of a country and the corresponding records of these changes, it calculates the nets of the direct investment, portfolio investment, other investment, and reserve assets. On the assets side of the different items of the financial account, a negative sign means an increase in foreign assets compared with the previous period, while a positive sign means a decrease in foreign assets. On the liabilities side of the different items of the financial account, a negative sign means a decrease in foreign liabilities, while a positive sign means an increase in foreign liabilities. Only total assets and liabilities are provided in the national publications. The data provided to the IMF include further breakdown by sector and instruments. Those are re-disseminated by the IMF in its International Financial Statistics (IFS) and Balance of Payments Yearbook (BOPSY). 2.2.1 Scope The classification system of the BOP is consistent with the recommendations of the BPM 6 . Scope of the data Most transactions of the financial sector are captured and included in the BOP. However, because of insufficient source data, the coverage of transactions of the non-financial sector is less comprehensive. Nevertheless, the CBB has included recently the communication sector, which is a very important sector in the economy. Unrecorded activity Because of the lack of source data, transactions between the US military base and foreign embassies/consulates in Bahrain and transactions between Bahrain’s embassies/consulates abroad and nonresidents are not included in the BOP. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation rules are generally in line with recommendations of the BPM 6 . The BOP and IIP transactions are valued at market value, where applicable. Most financial account transactions of the banking sector are derived from stock data, as differences between current positions and previous positions. However, valuation and other changes are not excluded. 2.4.2 Recording basis The BOP and IIP transactions are recorded on an accrual basis, where applicable. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Current account transactions are presented on a net basis, except for goods. The Statistics Bulletin , the regular dissemination vehicle of the BOP, presents financial account transactions classified by assets and liabilities, as recommended by the BPM 6 . The data provided to the IMF are presented in line with the recommendations of the BPM 6 . 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Source data for transactions and investment position of the financial sector are mostly comprehensive because they are regulatory reports and surveys based on the CBB’s supervisory authorities over the financial institutions within Bahrain. However, source data of investment income received/paid by the financial institutions in Bahrain are not obtained. Instead, investment income data are estimated by multiplying positions with London Interbank Offer Rate (LIBOR) (for portfolio investment and other investment) or at a fixed rate of 12 percent for credit (income from direct investment assets) and 16 percent for debit (income from direct investment liabilities). Regarding transactions and investment position of the non-financial sector, source data are not comprehensive, because the CBB does not have legal authority to collect information from the nonfinancial sector. Thus the source data are basically various voluntary surveys to major enterprises. The CBB also collects source data from other government ministries, such as the trade and travel data (IGA), external public debt and official grants (MOFNE), and exports/imports of oil (Bapco Energies). 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording In the past the CBB did not have sufficient source data from many resident companies that are involved in significant cross-border transactions (e.g., telecommunications and insurance) resulting in insufficient coverage of data in some important items. However, recently the CBB has succeeded in capturing different sectors transactions such as communication, insurance, construction and other business sectors, thanks to the forms that have been designed purposely by the CBB which have been included in the FDI survey. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Source data for the BOP from the financial sector are mostly timely. There is no written agreement on the timing of data submission among data-producing agencies, although it is understood that the relevant data are provided as soon as available. In fact, the problem does not lie solely in providing the trade data in a timely manner. The problem is in the data itself which can’t be reliable as the system still depends on a manual data entry. Thus, the CBB should wait a long time until the bulk of the transactions are entered into the system. In other words, even an early submission will not solve the problem as long as the data are not complete and hence reliable. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Direct investment income is considered as 12 percent (credit) and 16 percent (debit) of positions. The 40 percent of the direct investment income is attributed to dividends and distributed branch income; the balance is attributed to reinvested earnings. These ratios come from a survey conducted in 1998. Portfolio investment income and other investment income are calculated by multiplying positions of the relevant financial institutions with annual average of 3-month LIBOR rate. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The fixed ratio of 10% (9% for freight and 1% for insurance) is used to adjust the c.i.f. base import data into the f.o.b. base data. This ratio is based on historical experience. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The CBB staff monitors developments in the errors and omissions item and seek to understand them. This item has declined remarkably in the recent years as witnessed in the last year's BOP data. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses From time to time, revisions are made.
