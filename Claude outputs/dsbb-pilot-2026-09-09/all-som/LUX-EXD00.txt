 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: Gross External Debt Statistics (EXD) are established using stock data contained in Luxembourg’s International Investment Position (IIP). The Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), in accordance with the conceptual framework of the Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) and the External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (2013), compile the Luxembourg External Debt Statistics. The Luxembourg external debt is broken down first by institutional sector (general government, monetary authorities, monetary financial institutions, other sectors), second by maturity (short and long term on an original maturity basis) and third by type of financial instrument (debt securities, trade credit, loans, currency and deposit, other liabilities). Intercompany loans under direct investment relationships are separately identified without sector attribution. Definition: The Luxembourg external debt is the outstanding amount of the liability side of the Luxembourg international investment position (excluding equities). Data cover all actual current, and not contingent, liabilities that require payment(s) of principal and/or interest by the resident debtor and that are owed to nonresidents. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: The Luxembourg external debt data are classified in accordance with the standard components identified in the BPM6 . 2.2.1 Scope Classification: The Luxembourg external debt data are classified in accordance with the standard components identified in the BPM6 . Scope of the data Coverage of external debt: data cover debt securities, trade credit, loans, currency and deposit, other liabilities and intercompany lending. Geographical coverage: In principle, data cover all external liabilities of physical persons and legal entities residing in the territory of the Grand-Duchy of Luxembourg. 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to unit of account: External debt data are expressed in millions of EUR. ECB’s reference Exchange rates prevailing at the end of the period are used to convert debt liabilities denominated in foreign currencies into the EUR. Valuation: All debt instruments are valued at nominal value. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Debt securities: Data on foreign securities held by residents are based on a monthly security-by security collection from all the main holding sectors (credit institutions, Mutual funds, Financial vehicle corporations, Captive Financial Institutions). Custodian reporting on a monthly security-by-security basis is used to compile positions from the remaining sectors (mainly households). For the monetary authorities and for the general government, data are obtained from administrative sources, which provide reports on a security-by-security basis. Data on non-residents investments in Luxembourg securities are computed by subtracting the amount of securities held by resident (as reported by the before mentioned security-by-security reporting schemes) from the totalamount issued as reported by the Centralized Securities Database (CSDB) managed by ECB. Other investment: Trade credits are based on a specific survey of a representative sample of enterprises. Loans, currency and deposits of the main debtor sectors (credit institutions, Mutual funds, Financial vehicle corporations, Captive Financial Institutions) are collected on the basis of quarterly detailed balance sheets collection. For the monetary authorities and the general government, data are collected from administrative sources. Loans, currency and deposits position of non-financial enterprises and households is derived from the Bank for International Settlements statistics. Intercompany loans: data on inward direct investment stocks of other capital are collected through a quarterly surve. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Debt securities: Data are computed by subtracting the amount of securities held by residents (as reported by the resident custodians or MFIs) from the total amount issued as reported by the reporting agents. Loans, currency and deposits: The BIS locational banking statistics are used to estimate borrowings from nonresident households domiciled in BIS-reporting area.
