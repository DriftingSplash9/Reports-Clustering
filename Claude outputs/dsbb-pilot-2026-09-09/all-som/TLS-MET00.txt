 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions - The statistical methods and concepts used to compile data for the monthly reports, and the annual report on External Trade Statistics closely follow the recommendations of United Nations’ publication International Merchandise Trade Statistics: Concepts and Definitions (IMTS, Rev.2) . - The compilation framework in place has been developed and implemented in 2004 with support from Statistics New-Zealand under the framework of a Technical Assistance project with the Asian Development Bank (ADB). - In general, it reproduces the Customs Statistics derived from ASYCUDA (Automated System for Customs Data) system and produces reports in publishable format, on a monthly basis. The ASYCUDA system became operational in the Timor-Leste Customs Service in October 2003. This system provides the basic data from which External Trade Statistics are prepared. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization DGE uses the same classification as Customs, which are based on the International Harmonized Commodity description and Coding System (HS). Scope of the data - The compilation framework used by DGE to compile ETS is known as the “general” system of recording trade statistics as defined in UN statistical paper M52. Under this system, imports include all goods entering the economic territory of a compiling country and exports include all goods leaving the economic territory of a compiling country. The “economic territory” includes bonded warehouses. - The system provides coverage of all imports and exports from the main airport and seaports plus parcel post IE overland. Some documentation from “small” ports is also processed at the Dili Customs Office, however it should be noted that approximately 4% of entries (those not lodged electronically via ASYCUDA), may not be included in the ETS. - Goods which do not permanently add to the material resources of a country are identified as “Non Merchandise” trade. Some common examples would be temporary imports, returnable containers, goods in transit and goods consigned to foreign embassies within a host country. - Exports of petroleum oil and gases from Timor-Sea (Joint Petroleum Development Area, and Timor-Leste’s Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) do not pass the Customs, and are thus not captured by the ASYCUDA System, and respectively not included in the DGE monthly reports on ETS. Exceptions to coverage It should be noted that goods crossing international borders without appropriate Customs documentation are not included in Trade Statistics. . Unrecorded activity Goods and services smuggled in or out of the country. 2.4.1 Valuation Imports are valued on c.i.f. basis, and exports on f.o.b. basis. The c.i.f value includes the cost of the good cost of insuring the goods during transportation and the cost of freight. The f.o.b value includes the cost of the goods plus their delivery on board ship ready for departure. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Both imports and exports are shown on a gross basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data are obtained from import and export documents lodged with the customs service of Timor-Leste. These data are then processed by the DGE to produce ETS that conform to United Nations statistical concepts. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Import and export data are compiled by date of assessment. Imports and exports are processed and recorded by Customs. The dates of dissemination of Customs declaration are used as an approximation for the time when goods enter or leave the national territory. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Customs data are not always received timely to enable publication three weeks after the end of the reporting periods. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data from Custom are routinely checked, and, if necessary, questions may be raised with the Customs authority. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Intermediate data are routinely checked. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Intermediate data are routinely checked. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Data are revised on ad hoc basis 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Yes, data are disseminated on demand.
