 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The Consumer Price Index (CPI) is a chained Laspeyres type index, covering the categories of the Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP), adapted to the needs of the CPI. The aim of the Consumer Price Index is to measure the change over time of the prices of consumer goods and services deemed to be representative of the resident household's final monetary consumption expenditure. As the Harmonized Index of Consumer Prices (HICP) is an indicator derived from the CPI, the latter is compiled in the framework of the HICP methodological requirements and good practices. The Commission Regulations in the field of consumer prices are available at https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/hicp/legislation Note: from January 2026, the CPI will be rebased to 2025=100 and begin using the COICOP 2018 classification. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: the consumption expenditures are classified according to the COICOP. Since January 2013 Statistics Portugal has adopted the harmonized European Union COICOP at five digit level. Sectorization: the CPI covers the whole household sector, more precisely the goods and services that are acquired by households. Scope of the data Population coverage: The index covers all resident households irrespective of income. Geographical coverage: The index covers the entire country of Portugal (seven regions, according to the second level of the Nomenclature of Territorial Units for Statistics (NUTS II), including the Autonomous Regions of Azores and Madeira). 43 localities were selected in accordance with population at the municipal level, data collected from the Interbank Service company on the volume of debit card payments on retail stores and data on the disposable income of the households and on the Gross Value Added by NUTS II. The results were cross checked with those of the municipalities' purchasing power index compiled by Statistics Portugal. Item coverage: All individual goods and services considered to be representative of the residents' Household Final Monetary Consumption Expenditure (HFMCE) structure. For each series or sub-series the categories covered are the following: Number of categories Level of breakdown 12 2 digits 43 3 digits 102 4 digits 225 5 digits In 2024 approximately 120,000 prices were observed on a monthly basis from around 12,500 retail outlets (centralized price collection and rents are not included). For centrally collected items, such as fuels, prices are collected every day for all the retailers in the mainland. The number of representative items (nine digits) is 1,294. Exceptions to coverage Own final consumption, income in kind, owner occupied housing, major repairs, conversions and extensions to owner occupied housing, mortgage repayments and mortgage interest, other interest payments, life insurance premiums, investment-related expenditures, business-related expenditures, social transfers in-kind of goods and services from government and non-profit institutions serving households. Institutional households are not explicitly covered. According to demographic statistics currently available the number of these households remains very small. In a great extent, their expenditures are supported by General Government, Non Profit Institutions and classical households. Therefore, given its presumably very small weight in total population and possible double accounting problems (as part of the expenditures incurred by classical households with institutional households may already be reflected in the index), for the time being, there is no explicit consideration of this category in the Portuguese CPI. However this issue continues to be under investigation, in particular, special attention will be devoted to the demographic developments taking into account the latest Census results. Unrecorded activity Not included. 2.4.1 Valuation The CPI covers the prices paid for goods and services in monetary transactions. Prices included are the transaction prices actually paid by households for individual goods and services. It includes all indirect taxes minus subsidies on products, reductions and discounts applied to consumers in general and it excludes interest and credit charges, regarding them as financing costs rather than consumption expenditure. Price reductions are considered in accordance with Commission Regulation 2020/1148 of 31 July 2020. 2.4.2 Recording basis Not applicable. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Insurance: the weights reflect the value of the service charge implicitly set. Gross insurance premium is used as a proxy of the service charge due to difficulty in defining the service charge for a particular policy. Lotteries and gambling: the weights refer to the amount of the household expenditure in lotteries and betting net of the part distributed as a prize – the observed prices correspond to the value of a bet as a proxy of the service charge. So, if the proportion of the total of the bets to be given out in the form of a prize is kept constant, the change in the unit value of each bet will be equivalent to that of the service charge. If the proportion changes, this is taken into account in the index compilation in order to reflect the change in the service charge. Health, education and social protection: the acquisition prices of health, education and social protection goods and services are in accordance with the concept adopted from HFMCE. They correspond to the amounts paid by consumers net of reimbursements. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Price collection methods: Prices are collected through a monthly survey of retail outlets and service providers, using tablet computers with specific price collection software. Social rent prices are collected directly from households by phone interview. Market rent prices are calculated using administrative records provided by the Portuguese Fiscal Authority. Some prices (such as administered prices and telecommunications) are centrally collected, by phone or internet. Web scraping is used to collect data from several retailers, mainly for clothing, footwear and furniture. Sample sizes: Approximately 120,000 prices are observed on a monthly basis from around 12,500 retail outlets (central price collection, rents and web scraping are not included; for some centrally collected items, such as fuels, prices are collected every day for all the retailers in the mainland). In 2024 the number of representative items is 1,294. Item selection: The selection of products is mainly based on the results of the HBS. The market brands and the characteristics of the products are the result of market research. The index is annually chained, allowing for new goods and services to be brought in at the end of each year using data sources other than the HBS, such as administrative data, and information provided by price collectors. Item/product specification: The specifications of the representative items are sufficiently broader to allow that prices are indeed collected. At a second level, the price collector has to choose the variety of the product for which the price will be collected and has to describe its relevant characteristics. Price collectors are instructed to price the most representative variety. The final decision on the inclusion of the observed price belongs, however, to the regional office. These decisions are taken by checking if the choice of the price collector is within the boundary of the specifications for each elementary aggregate. Outlet selection: There are around 12,500 outlets in the sample (central price collection, rents and web scraping are not included). The outlets, from which prices are collected, are chosen to represent the existing trade and services network and they are usually based on three main criteria: popularity with consumers, significant turnover from consumer sales and availability of goods and services included in the CPI basket. Internet shopping is included for some centrally collected items, such as air travel fares and hotel accommodations. Web scraping is used to collect data from several retailers, mainly for clothing, footwear and furniture. Sources of weights: National Accounts and the Household Budget Survey are the main source for the weights. Specific data sources are also taken into account in the annual update of weights (e.g. administrative data on medicines, tobacco and fuel sales, new cars, tolls, motorcycles). Weights for each year are price-updated using December of the previous year as the reference period for the updating. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Timing of price observations: Since January 2007 - price collection takes place across at least a one working week period at, or near, the middle of the calendar month to which the index pertains. Most prices are collected on a monthly basis. For a small set of products whose prices change marginally in the short run prices are collected every three months, on a rotation basis, meaning that in each month one third of the sample is collected. Non-processed food items, such as fruits, vegetables and fish, whose price changes in the short run may be substantial, are collected twice or three times per month, in different weeks. Prices for fuels are collected every day. Prices are collected on a yearly basis for those products that change typically once a year (e.g. education fees, social protection, public hospital services, road worthiness test). For some specific and more complex services (e.g. air travel and hotel accommodation) prices are collected prior to the use of those services, two and one month before and in the reference month, always in the same day of the week and for three predefined periods throughout the reference month. An average of the three prices is then taken into account in the CPI compilation. For CD’s, DVD’s, computer games and books an average of the top sales prices is considered. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness See the previous point. Source data timeliness allows producing statistical outputs whose timeliness meets applicable data standards. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data: Censuses: every 10 years National Accounts: every year HBS: every 5 years Administrative data: every year Relevant sample surveys: whenever available The quality of the CPI can be assessed to be very high. Its concepts and methodology have been developed in line with the Harmonized Index of Consumer Prices (HICP) methodology, according to international standards and using consumer price statistics experience from all EU Member States. The accuracy of the source data is monitored by assessing the methodological soundness of price and weight sources and the adherence to the methodological recommendations. There is a variety of data sources both for weights (National Account data, Household Budget Survey data, etc.) and prices (visits to local retailers and service providers and central collection via mail, telephone, e-mail and the internet are used, as well as web scraping). The type of survey and the price collection methods ensure sufficient coverage and timeliness. The outlets, from which prices are collected, are chosen to represent the existing trade and services network and they are based usually on three main criteria: Popularity with consumers, significant turnover from consumer sales and availability of goods and services included in the HICP basket. Resident households in the economic territory of the country are covered, irrespective of their income. Quality assurance is performed at the relevant levels of the organization. At regional level, there is ongoing contact between the regional delegates and supervisors and between price collectors and supervisors. Written documentation with guidelines is regularly provided by central coordination and immediately incorporated into field work. Regular meetings between central coordination and regional delegates, supervisors and price collectors take place every year. In order to reduce the sampling errors samples are very large taking into account the economic dimension of the country. For the computation of the Portuguese HICP, on average 120,000 prices are observed monthly, from around 12,500 retail outlets throughout the country (central price collection, rents and web scraping are not included; for some centrally collected items, such as fuels, prices are collected every day for all the retailers in the mainland). Statistics Portugal tries to reduce non-sampling errors through continuous methodological improvements and survey process improvements such as computer assisted price collection, which can help avoiding coding and typing errors. The results are cross-checked with other statistics, such as production and imports prices, data on indirect taxes and data from other sources. The Portuguese CPI figures are disseminated on the 8th working day after the end of the reference month following a predetermined timetable. A flash estimate is also published on the last working day of the reference month. User satisfaction surveys are regularly conducted by Statistics Portugal. Information under the theme Prices is one of the most requested. The control and quality assessment is ensured through the supervision of Eurostat to the procedures of the HICP which is an indicator that is derived from the Portuguese CPI. Member States must compile HICPs in compliance with HICP methodological requirements and good practices in the field of consumer price indices. In order to achieve this aim, Eurostat undertakes compliance monitoring visits to Member States with the aim to review HICP methodological issues. The last compliance monitoring visit to Statistics Portugal took place in June 2019 and the report was released in December 2019. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Treatment of missing prices: Missing prices are imputed according to the average increase in the same locality (or region, if there are no other prices collected in the same locality), for the same product, until the product-offer becomes available again. Selection of replacement items: As soon as it is known that a missing product will not be available again, it is replaced. Replacements are made according to product characteristics and representativeness. Adjustments: As the CPI aims at measuring 'pure' price changes, it should be unaffected by changes in quality of products. Prices are therefore adjusted for changes in quality of the goods and services. If the quality differences are considered to be minor direct price comparison is applied. Bridged overlap is the main quality adjustment method applied. Less frequently, option prices approach is also used. Introducing new products: New products are introduced whenever there is enough information to decide for the inclusion in the sample and for the re-weighting. Every year, Statistics Portugal identifies newly significant goods and services through administrative data and a judgmental process involving price collectors, supervisors, regional offices and central office. Newly significant goods are in most cases included in the index as new elementary aggregates with the annual chaining. Seasonal items: The methodology on the treatment of seasonal items is in accordance with Commission Regulation (EU) 2020/1148 of 31 July. Prices for out-of-season products are estimated using either counter-seasonal estimation (clothing and footwear products) or all-seasonal estimation (all other seasonal products, such as some fruit and vegetables). More detailed information is available in the 2011 press releases, in particular in the Portuguese version of the January 2011 press release. Additional data is available at Eurostat's website. Seasonally adjusted indices: The CPI is not seasonally adjusted. Owner-occupied housing: Owner occupied housing is not included in the index as it is an asset and not a consumption good. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Main stages of the compilation of the national index: calculation of localities' average prices (2), calculation of regional average prices (1), calculation of the elementary indices at regional (3) and national (4) levels aggregation of the regional sub-indices and regional all-items index (5), and aggregation of the national sub-indices and all-items index (6 and 6'). The computer system at local and central level provides some tools that allow for a regular validation. Extreme and unusual price changes are analyzed in detail, taking into consideration the qualitative information provided by price collectors and supervisors. Special attention is given to tariffs and regulated prices. Prices that remain stable for a long period are also checked. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Intermediate data are cross-checked with other statistics, such as production and imports prices. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs See the previous points. When significant methodological changes are introduced a priori tests are conducted and parallel calculations are performed in order to guarantee the precision of the results. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revision studies are carried out whenever significant methodological changes are introduced.
