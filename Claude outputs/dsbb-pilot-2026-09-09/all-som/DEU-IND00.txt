 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The industrial production index is an index based on the Laspeyres concept. It is designed to project the value added of the base year in constant prices on a monthly basis. Since these quantities however are only available annually, economic development is described on an improvised basis by the monthly registration of the quantities and values of products in the individual branches of economic activity. Aggregation of the branch indices is then done using the percentage of the gross value added of the base year. The important projection elements of the production indices are production quantities and values collected monthly for approximately 5000 types of goods defined by the national "Product Classification for Production Statistics, 2019 Edition (GP 2019)". The IPI is compiled according to the German "Classification of Economic Activities, 2008 Edition (WZ 2008)", based on the internationally classification; the Statistical Classification of Economic Activities in the European Communities (NACE) Rev. 2 for Industries. The concepts are consistent with those in the European Union regulation on short-term statistics (EU-STS). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The "Classification of Economic Activities, 2008 Edition (WZ 2008)" applies to the collection of institutional economic data. The monthly indices in the Production Industry are limited to the use of results for the four-digit level (="classes") of WZ 2008; at this level the classification encompasses 246 breakdown items. At the same time, this aggregation stage is identical to the NACE Rev. 2 collection classification which was prescribed as mandatory by the Statistical Office of the European Communities (Eurostat) for the national surveys of member states. In this way the contents of the national indices coincide with the contents of the indices published by Eurostat for the European Union and by other member states. The indices computed using WZ 2008 or NACE Rev. 2 can also be described by suitable aggregations in the branch classification of ISIC Rev. 4. The main groups are formed by direct assignment of classes (= four-digit level) of WZ 2008 to the main groups of the Production Industry: Intermediate goods industry; Capital goods industry; Durable consumer goods industry; Non-durable consumer goods industry; Energy. In the WZ 2008 classification there is no assignment of the higher aggregates (groups, divisions, subsections and sections) to these main groups; only the four-digit WZ identifiers are assigned to the main groups according to their principal activities. Scope of the data Monthly production data are collected by local units of the Production Industry. If the local unit has at least 50 employees and its principal activity (= value added, alternatively: number of employees) is located in the Production Industry, this local unit is obligated to deliver monthly reports. If a local unit is included in the monthly reporting obligation, it must report production amounts and values for each product which is produced by it and which occurs in the "Product Classification for Production Statistics, 2019 Edition (GP 2019)" to the Statistical Office of the Land where the local unit has its headquarters. The monthly survey collects also information on oil and gas products. The 16 Statistical Offices of the Laender for their part prepare Laender totals for the stipulated product list and deliver them to the Federal Statistical Office. The aggregate of the base year to be projected is the gross value added at factor costs. This institutional (i.e., for the branches of economic activity) defined quantity originates from the cost structure survey, a sample survey for enterprises of the Production Industry. These statistics cover enterprises with 20 or more employees; for purposes of index calculation (weighting structure of the base year for compilation of branch indices) the results are expanded to the reporting group of "all enterprises". The value also includes the costs of packaging, even if it is charged separately. Conversely, the charged turnover and excise taxes, the freight charges billed separately, and the rebates granted to customers are not included. The contractor reports the compensation paid by the principal as the value of contract processing. 2.4.2 Recording basis Local units obligated to report provide information on the amount produced and the value of marketing production to the responsible Statistical Office of the Land for each product contained in the Product Classification. These data are transferred to the Federal Statistical Office. After the Laender data are compiled into a federal result, the production index is calculated and delivered to the Federal Ministry of Economics and Energy and the Deutsche Bundesbank. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The concepts of calculation are consistent with those in the European Union regulation on short-term statistics (EU-STS). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The monthly production survey carried out for local units encompasses the following for approximately 5,000 types of products: Amount and value of production intended for sale. This is generally the marketable output, including contract processing. Amount of processing production. This is the production which is intended for further processing in the reporting local unit, in another local unit of the same enterprise or in outsourcing in another enterprise. The value of production intended for sale is based on the selling prices achieved in the reporting period or the selling prices which can be achieved at the time of sale, ex works. The value also includes the costs of packaging, even if it is charged separately. Conversely, the charged turnover and excise taxes, the freight charges billed separately, and the rebates granted to customers are not included. The contractor reports the compensation paid by the principal as the value of contract processing. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The source data are consistent with the definitions, scope, classifications, and timing of recording as required by the EU-STS regulation. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Source data are timely. A report for the current month must be submitted by each local unit obligated to report. If it is not possible for a local unit to deliver its production report on time, the responsible Statistical Office of the Land will use corresponding estimates. This estimation of missing data is generally only done for comparatively small local units; larger local units are always currently included in the result of the respective reporting month. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The source data for the weights are surveys of all enterprises with one or more employees. The monthly production data are collected for local units, if the local units have at least 50 employees and their principal activity (= value added, alternatively: number of employees) is located in the Production Industry. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques To compute the index, the data of the reference period are put in relation to the results of the base period and value relatives are formed. In the case of the use of values as updating series, the value relatives must be divided by the adequate producer price index (the results are volume relatives). At the second level of computation the volume relatives are summed up to form 246 indices for branches of economic activity of the production industries. To this end, the share of the production value of an individual updating series in the total production value of all updating series of a specific branch is used. The third stage of computing production indices aggregates the indices of the lowest level of breakdown to form aggregates at higher levels in accordance with the breakdown of the classification of economic activities. For this aggregation of production indices, the share of a value-added quantity in the value added of all branches of economic activity required for index construction in base year 0 is determined. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The Federal Statistical Office computes production indices as original values, values adjusted for working-day variations, seasonally adjusted results and the trend component. Adjustment for working-day variations balances the irregularities in the original values which are caused by the different number of working days in each reference month. Seasonally adjusted results try to eliminate the annual regularities in the time series. In both cases, the method X13JDemetra+ is used to gain the results. The trend component is identified by using the Berlin method, version 4 (BV 4). 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results During the compilation process, the results are compared with the results of other indicators as the index of new orders received. Additional information is also obtained from the press and other sources 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data The reasons for unusual index movements as strikes are provided in the regular press releases. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs IPIs for branches and aggregates (NACE) are derived directly from the lowest level (four-digit) branch indices by a single, progressive hierarchical aggregation process, so discrepancies between aggregates cannot occur. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses A distinction needs to be made between short-term corrections of any errors, and regular index revisions resulting from a change in the index weighting base period, modifications of the classification, as different procedures are applied. In the situation in which errors are detected in a published aggregate for a particular month, a retrospective correction is made in the subsequent month’s release. In the case of regular index rebases (as a rule every five years) the results and changes in the calculation methodology are presented in detail in the FSO monthly journal “Wirtschaft und Statistik”. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses The revised indices are published on the Internet and together with the preliminary indices for the following month in "Wirtschaft und Statistik" (Economics and Statistics). 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Only in exceptional cases special tabulations may be provided upon request. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Via Internet: https://www.destatis.de/EN/Service/Contact/Contact.html All publications containing the production index data include information about contact points (names, telephone numbers, facsimile numbers, and e-mail addresses).
