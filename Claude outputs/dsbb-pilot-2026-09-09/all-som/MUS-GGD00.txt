 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions General government gross debt is compiled based on the definitions and concepts as per GFS Manual 2014, Public Sector Debt Statistics Guide 2013, External Debt Statistics Guide 2014 as well as provisions as per the Public Debt Management Act 2008 and the Finance and Audit Act, which are consistent with the System of National Accounts 2008 (SNA 2008). General government gross debt definition is equal to the sum of liabilities at the end of the period relating to loans, debt securities and SDR allocations. The debt figures are reported at face value. They are consolidated between and within subsectors. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The general government sector and its sub-sectors are defined in accordance with and are consistent with the GFSM 2014 and 2008 SNA. It consists of the central government (budgetary central government plus extra budgetary units and social security funds) and the local/regional government. The classification of instruments and the general government sector is in line with the requirements of the SDDS plus. The debt instruments consist of loans, debt securities and SDR allocations for each sub-sector. It consists of the central government (budgetary central government plus extra budgetary units and social security funds) and the local/regional government. The classification of instruments and the general government sector is in line with the requirements of the SDDS plus. The debt instruments consist of loans, debt securities and SDR allocations for each sub-sector. Please see the Transition Plan for meeting the full requirement by 2027. Scope of the data The data covers budgetary central government, extra-budgetary units (including social security funds), local government and regional government. The debt positions are consolidated between and within subsectors. General Government Gross Debt comprises the debt instruments namely loans, debt securities and SDR allocations. The debt is also reported by currency of denomination and residence of the creditor. Domestic debt are debt liabilities owed by residents to residents of same economy. Debt liabilities owed by residents to non-residents are external debt. Mauritius government does not contract insurance and deal in financial derivatives. Deposits, pensions and standardized guarantee schemes, and other accounts payable are not reported as debt, as the required data are not available. Thus, the reported debt figure does not include accrued i nterest . Transactions originally denominated in foreign currencies are converted into Mauritian rupees using the prevailing rates of exchange. The conversion is made using the exchange rate on the day of the transaction obtained from the BOM. Debt stocks in foreign currencies are converted at the exchange rate prevailing at the end of the reference period, which are obtained from the BOM. 2.4.2 Recording basis The debts are recorded as per the valuations stated at 2.4.1. Government’s accounts are maintained on a cash basis. However, for computation of government budget deficit, interest on accrual basis is used. Appropriate adjustment is made for the difference between cash and accrual interest to come up with the government borrowing requirements. For recording of debt stock, the principle of accrual accounting is applied. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The outstanding debts are compiled and disseminated on a gross basis. However, the computation of General G overnment Gross D ebt takes into account intersectoral and intra - sectoral consolidations. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The sources for Government external debt data are loan agreements and disbursements and repayment notifications received from creditors. For Government debt securities, the data source is the BOM, which issues securities on behalf of Government. The data in respect of loans contracted by EBUs are obtained directly from them on a monthly basis by the DMU. Part of the data (deposits, pensions and other accounts payable) for Government, EBUs, local and regional governments are obtained from administrative and accounting returns through surveys carried out by the Treasury Department/Statistics Mauritius on a yearly basis. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The definitions used in the source data are consistent with statistical definitions and it is ensured that there is a common understanding of such definitions among all organisations. The classification of organisations to sectors/sub-sectors is the responsibility of Statistics Mauritius, which publishes the full classifications in its Digest of Public Finance, Statistics and same is used by all data producing entities. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data collection and processing are adequate to meet timeliness and periodicity for the dissemination. Respondents are made aware of the deadlines set for data reporting. The DMU maintains strict follow-up procedures, including direct communication with respondents, to ensure timely receipt of comprehensive debt data. However, for the reporting of certain components within the recent period, an estimate may be used. These are replaced with actual data upon availability. 3.2.1 Source data assessment All the debt data, whether available internally or obtained from creditors, EBUs and local/regional authorities, BOM and SM are assessed for their consistency and completeness. All the debt data are audited annually by the National Audit Office. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques None 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures NA 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Data reported are validated and routinely compared with other available sources including financial statements and press reports. In case of doubt or unusual trend, the providers of the data are questioned, any errors are rectified, and data are actualised. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data All data are subject to verification and validation in consultation with their providers . Where appropriate, a n organisation may be requested to submit a copy of bank statement certifying the loan amount, interest and the outstanding debt stock. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs All discrepancies are investigated and resolved prior to dissemination. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses On a quarterly basis.
