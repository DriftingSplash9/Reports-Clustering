 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Definition: The net external debt position is Austria's overall assets in countries abroad minus the outstanding liabilities owed to these countries. Basis for the external debt data are reports from Austrian residents on their gross assets/liabilities vis-a-vis the rest of the world. Analytical framework: External debt statistics is in line with the guidelines in the Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) as well as with those in the manual of External Debt Statistics: Guide for compilers and users (2013) . Austria's external debt statistics is an extraction of the International Investment Position and is based on the same methodology. Other conceptual issues: Sector attribution of external debt liabilities: External debt liabilities are attributed to the sector of the immediate borrower. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Other classification issues: Maturity: The data are compiled on original maturity basis. Sector classification of external debt liabilities : External debt liabilities are classified under the sector category of the debtor and not of any guarantor. Arrears cannot be separately identified under "short-term, other debt liabilities". Coverage of short-term debt: This category covers all external debt positions with an original maturity of one year or less (e.g. money market instruments, loans, cash and deposits). Intercompany lending between entities in a direct investment relationship is separately identified in the total external debt position without sector attribution. Scope of the data Instrument coverage: Compared to the International Investment Position, the quarterly external debt data do not include any financial assets and liabilities arising from participation rights. Not included are equity instruments like shares and mutual funds, direct investment equity capital, and reinvested earnings as well as real estate. Geographical coverage: The geographic coverage is the rest of the world from the Austrian perspective. Other coverage issues: Classification by type of debt instrument. Deposits of nonresidents with domestic banks are included. Instruments included under "other debt liabilities" : arrears, trust transactions, bills of exchange, assignment transactions. Trade credits owed to nonresidents are included Financial leases with nonresidents are treated as loans and included in the gross external debt position. Arrears are included in the gross external debt position. Domestic and foreign currency debt owed to nonresidents are included in the external debt data. The Sector “Monetary financial institutions” covers deposit taking corporations and money market funds. Foreign currency-linked external debt: no domestic/foreign currency breakdown is available but not published. Securities issued in the domestic markets by residents and purchased by nonresidents. External debt includes securities issued in the domestic markets by residents and purchased by nonresidents. Debt liabilities of the offshore enterprises - including banks - that are resident in the reporting economy: By definition, offshore enterprises are no residents of Austria. Dissemination of asset data for offshore enterprises: not applicable. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: Assets and liabilities are mostly valued at market prices which holds true for most of portfolio investment data and reserve assets. For loans and deposits nominal values are the main source of valuation. Conversion to unit of account: Assets and liabilities are reported in original currencies and are converted at middle rates on the last day of the reporting date. 2.4.2 Recording basis Recording basis: As the external debt statistics is part of the IIP, the accrual principle is applied here as well (except for income on loans and deposits where income can be on a cash basis). Other recording issues: Outstanding liabilities arising from periodic interest costs that have accrued and are not yet payable are included in the gross external debt position. Those liabilities are recorded as part of the value of the underlying debt instrument. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Debt securities: A comprehensive and reliable system is used for compiling stocks (and flows). The system is based on data collection on a security-by-security basis using the International Securities Identification Number (ISIN). Based on the reported ISIN code in connection with the securities database, all necessary EXD (IIP and BoP) classifications (by financial instrument, sector, and country) are made. Depository institutions (banks) report monthly on their own (stocks) and on their customers deposits (stocks and flows) without any reporting threshold. Information on repos and reverse repos is part of the reporting requirements.The information from banks is supplemented by compulsory reports by non-banks (corporations and households) on securities which are held outside resident depository institutions, which means securities held at non-resident custodians or in own custody. Reporting by non-banks is required quarterly, for all securities held on deposits abroad if the stock exceeds Euro 5 mio. The reported information on securities held outside resident depository institutions is enriched with data on Third Party Holdings in the Euroarea from the ESCB Securities Holdings Statistics (SHSS) as well as estimations for securities held outside the Euroarea based on data on deposits from the BIS. Trade credits, loans, deposits: data are based on monthly reports by banks and non-banks. Both report on stocks and possible write-offs broken down by country and by currency. For non-banks, there are separate reports for trade credits and for "other instruments" (loans and deposits). For each of the two reports a separate threshold of Euro 10 mio of foreign assets or foreign liabilities applies. If the threshold is exceeded, a full report is required. The reports are broken down into assets and liabilities as well as into counterparts within or outside the enterprise group (affiliates and third parties). Intercompany loans and trade credits: As mentioned above, data on non-equity capital between affiliates are collected as a separate category within the "other investment" collection scheme and are therefore included in external debt. Other data collection issues: External debt transactions: data are available to identify or estimate external debt transactions. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Debt securities: Stocks data include accruals (calculated on a security-by-security basis). Interest rate components derived from discount prices and redemption at higher prices are included in the accruals calculation and therefore in the stocks. This mechanism was especially designed for zero-coupon bonds, but it also works for all other kinds of short and long-term securities. Stocks and flows are closely reconciled. Exchange rate and price adjustments are calculated security-by-security, using information from the securities database (mainly quotations and exchange rates). Other adjustments (due to reclassifications between sectors and instruments are also identified automatically in this context. The remaining differences are calculated as residual adjustments. Debt securities stocks are valued at market prices. Loans, deposits and trade credits: Assets and liabilities primarily classified as other investment are based on nominal values. Reports by banks and non-banks are almost exclusively based on stock reports broken down by currency and country. Specifically, reported data regarding loans and deposits of households and corporations are supplemented by estimates using mirror data. Separate reporting items for short-term and long-term original maturities exist. Exchange rate conversion is made by the compiler, not by the reporting agents. Reserve assets, debt securities and deposits: Data on reserve assets are prepared by the Accounting Department of the OeNB in special monthly reports. These reports comprise stocks, transactions, and all kinds of adjustments. The data are taken directly from the accounting database. Discrepancies between accounting principles and BOP/IIP concepts are seen as being insignificant. All positions are valued at market values using closing mid-market exchange rates 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Other issues: Traded debt instruments issued by residents abroad but in hands of residents are excluded from the gross external debt position, by applying the residual approach: outstanding amount minus domestic portfolios = external liabilities.
