 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The concepts and definitions are consistent with those laid down in the 1993 “Systems of National Accounts”. The Department adopted the SNA 1993 in 2005 and provided revision of National accounts statistics based on the new system (SNA 1993) back to 1996. The aggregates compiled and presented include the following: Gross domestic product (GDP) at market prices is estimated from the production side as the sum of values added at basic prices for 16 major economic activities (industries) plus taxes on products less subsidies . Sequence of accounts for General government and Households have been compiled up to Use of Income account. Rest of the world sequence of account is also compiled. The rest of the institutional sectors’ sequence of accounts are not compiled yet. Gross domestic product (GDP) at basic prices is also estimated from the production side as the sum of values added at basic prices for 16 major economic activities (industries). Gross Value Added at basic prices for the general government is also available. It is useful to note that Gross Value Added at basic prices minus other taxes on production gives Gross Value Added at factor cost (which is equivalent to the previous GDP at factor cost). This means that the value added by industry is measured at basic prices and no longer at factor cost as has been the case. In practice, however, the difference between factor cost and basic prices is negligible. Estimates are provided at current as well as at constant 2001 prices. Production accounts are provided at current prices detailing gross output, intermediate consumption, remuneration of employees and operating surplus. Expenditure on GDP is compiled and presented as follows: Import of goods and services Export of goods and services Gross fixed capital formation Change in stocks\ Government final consumption Private final consumption Private consumption is no longer derived as a residue as was previously the case. Gross Fixed Capital Formation and Private consumption are calculated through the commodity flow approach. Estimates of GDP and its components are provided at current as well as at constant 2001 prices. Gross National Income (GNI) is calculated at current prices by adding Primary income from abroad and deducting Primary income paid abroad from GDP at market prices Gross National Disposable Income(GNDI) is calculated at current prices by adding current transfers receivable from the rest of the world and subtracting the transfers payable to the rest of the world 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Economic activities classification is based on the 4-digit United Nation International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC rev 3) with some slight modification. The activities captured are: Agriculture and Forestry; Fishing; Mining and Quarrying; Manufacturing; Construction; Electricity and Water supply; Wholesale and Retail trade, repairs; Restaurants and Hotels; Transport and Communication; Financial Intermediation; Real estate, Renting and Business services; Public Administration and defense; Education; Health and Social work; Other community, social and personal services; Private households with employed persons. Classification of imported and exported goods and services was revised in accordance to Standard International Trade Classification (SITC) of 1986. Scope of the data The national accounts cover the entire Republic of Kenya. Transactions in goods and services are covered as well as other transactions recorded in the production account, i.e. remuneration of employees and operating surplus. The national accounts cover in principle all institutional units that are resident in Kenya Unrecorded activity Currently, the coverage excludes illegal activities such as prostitution which are not easy to capture. However, informal activities and some horticultural activities which were previously not included in national accounts compilations have been incorporated. 2.4.1 Valuation Output is recorded at basic prices, Intermediate consumption at Purchaser's prices, exports at f.o.b. while Imports are at c.i.f. 2.4.2 Recording basis All transactions of goods and services are recorded on accrual basis with exception of government services, which are recorded on a cash basis without adjustments. The recording period refers to calendar year. Since Government data are recorded in fiscal year which covers the period July 1st - June 31st, adjustments are usually made by taking the average of two fiscal years to represent the calendar year. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The national accounts for Kenya are compiled and published by the National Accounts Section of the Kenya National Bureau of Statistics. Production Approach: The Central Bureau of Statistics maintains a central register of establishments (the business register). The Register serves as the frame on which surveys of the formal sector are based. Due to the dynamism of the economy, the rate of entry and exit of firms is high and this makes the tracking of the firms difficult. There is also a problem of non-response. In addition, the following new data sources, which were previously not used for the national accounts estimates, have been included: The 1997 Welfare Monitoring Survey (WMS 1997); The 1998 Labour Force Survey LFS 1998); The 1999 National Micro and Small Enterprise Survey (MSE 1999); and The 1999 Population Census. A recent survey of manufacturing, carried out by Kenya Institute of Public Policy Research and Analysis (KIPPRA) has also been used. The estimates for central government are based on accounting records, provisional budget and revised budget estimates published or unpublished. These records undergo a considerable amount of analysis before being used for national accounts compilation. Since the Government data are recorded in fiscal years, adjustments are made by taking the average of two fiscal years to represent the calendar year. The estimates for local government are based on accounts from local authorities gathered by the CBS. Constant prices are derived by extrapolation with employment numbers. Agriculture: Estimates are product based instead of being based on data from agricultural holdings (establishments), and output data are derived from commodity flows. Growing of cereals and other edible crops is principally done by small-scale holdings, i.e. household enterprises. Therefore, benchmarks for production for own consumptions have been derived from household surveys (1997 WMS) and additional sources for marketed produce are used for maize, wheat, barley and rice. At present there are no estimates of hunting. Tea and coffee production are estimated from data on deliveries to respective marketing boards. Livestock production is measured from returns compiled by department of Livestock Production. Marketed milk data is obtained from the Dairy Board of Kenya and Kenya Co-operative Creameries (KCC). Marketed maize and wheat data is obtained from National Cereals and Produce Board and millers while an estimate of total production is obtained from the Ministry of Agriculture. Estimates of the small-scale farmers are based on the past bench mark data, which have been brought forward with indicators for large-scale farming. Intermediate consumption is based on data collected from agricultural suppliers. Constant prices are estimated by double deflation using price and volume data from the sources mentioned above. Forestry: The Forestry Department supplies data on its wages and numbers employed, which are the basis for the estimates for forestry operated by government. The estimates for private logging are based on royalties and other payments from the private sector. Estimates at constant prices are derived from quantitative data on trees felled and areas planted. The non-monetary sector is estimated through extrapolation of an old benchmark (time use survey 1972) by the changes in rural population and by applying an average rural wage. Fishing: Marketed output both at current and constant prices, are based on data from the Fisheries Department on fish landed by quantities and values. Constant prices are derived using catches of the four major categories as volume indicators. Intermediate consumption is assumed to be 35% of output based on the 1998 Labour Force Survey. Unreported output (includes production for own-consumption) is assumed to be 10 per cent of market output. Compensation of employees; A benchmark for 1998 has been calculated by multiplying average earning with an approximate number of employees based on the 1998 Labour Force Survey. Compensation of employees is assumed to have a fixed ratio to output. Mining and quarrying: The estimates are based on data on production obtained from the Mines and Geology Department. These data include values and quantities of ores and minerals and are used for estimates of output and value added at current and constant prices. Manufacturing: A benchmark estimate of output for 2001 (the base year) based on various sources and judgmental estimates is used The ratio of intermediate consumption over output is estimated on the basis of KIPPRA survey data and other sources for some sub-sectors. Constant prices are calculated by extrapolation using volume index for manufacturing. Thus, single deflation is used assuming a fixed ratio of intermediate consumption over output. The Monthly Survey of Industrial Production (MSIP) collects data on quantities and value of output for major products. Current prices are then derived from constant values by reflating with various price indices, normally the same for output and intermediate consumption. However, for some sub-sectors the current estimates have been used as provided by the source data. Compensation of employees is calculated on the basis of the annual Labour Enumeration Survey (LES). Finally, 10% are added to take into account the social contributions. A commodity flow is compiled for each sub-sector. Exports are from the foreign trade statistics Electricity and water: The annual reports of KPLC and KenGen contain both financial and quantitative data for all operators. These data are for financial years ending 30 June. Calendar years are calculated as the average of two adjacent financial years. Output at constant prices is calculated by extrapolation with the quantities sold broken down by the different categories of users. Compensation of employees (COE) for KPLC and KenGen is derived from the relevant items in the income statements. For IPP (Independent Power Producer), EPP (Emergency Power Producer) and REP (Rural Electrification Programme) COE is assumed to be the same ratio over output as for KenGen. Water: Central government includes services provided for rural water schemes, while local government is responsible for distribution of water to households, industries and other consumers. Sum of cost approach is used to compute the output at constant and current prices. Intermediate consumption is deflated by CPI, non-food excl. VAT. Compensation of employees is deflated with a salary index derived from earnings and employment in local government according to the LES. Water collection : all household without piped water are assumed to collect water. Output is computed as product of number of households, average time spent per day (WMS 1997), and earning per day (based on gazetted monthly basic minimum wage for Agriculture, unskilled labour under 18 years) for 360 days per year. Constant prices are calculated by multiplication with the minimum wage for the base year. Construction: The estimates both at current and constant prices are based on the commodity flow approach. Imports sorted by Broad Economic Categories (BEC) include a category for industrial supplies, among which goods mainly used for input in construction have been identified. This is recorded as intermediate consumption by market producers with deduction of intermediate consumption by central government. Supply from domestic producers originates mainly in quarrying and certain sub-sectors of manufacturing. Additions have been made for inputs not typical for construction (for example fuel). A fixed ratio of value added to intermediate consumption at constant prices is used to derive output, value added and intermediate consumptions. Compensation of employees is based on the annual Labour Enumeration Survey. Current prices are then derived by reflation with the total building cost index. Own account construction : A benchmark for 2003 has been estimated separately for the two house types by multiplying the costs with number of households and dividing with the average life span. The cost for replacement of the roof for type 1) has been added. An addition of 10% has been made for the building of ancillary structures such as kitchens and latrines. Annual estimates at constant prices are derived by projecting with the annual growth of households. Current prices are derived by reflation: valued added with an index based on the gazetted minimum wage for rural workers and intermediate consumption with the average of the building cost index for timber and hardware. Sum of cost approach is used for the government account. Wholesale and Retail trade; repairs: Output is calculated as the sum of trade margins in the commodity flows both at current and constant prices. An addition of 10% is made for a likely underestimation of margins on goods sold for intermediate consumption and for services that are not included in the trade margins. Intermediate consumption is estimated at 54% of output (derived from 1999 SME survey). Repairs: Output. A benchmark for 2001 estimated by multiplying the number of cars with assumed average cost for repair and maintenance is extrapolated with the number of vehicles for the estimates of constant prices. Current prices are derived by reflation with CPI, transport. Intermediate consumption is estimated at 55% of output Compensation of employees (including repair activities) is estimated by extrapolating with employment numbers then reflating with the gazetted minimum wage. Hotels and restaurants: Hotels Output. The estimates are based on average room rates and volume indicators. Intermediate consumption is 65% of output both at current and constant prices. Compensation of employees is 45% of valued added. Constant prices are derived by deflation with the average of CPI, all items and the exchange rate for US Dollars. Restaurants output: A benchmark at constant prices is projected forwards with the volume index for hotels. Current prices are then derived by reflation with an index calculated as the average of CPI, food and CPI, alcoholic beverages. Intermediate consumption is assumed at 75% of output; 70% of this is assumed to be food. Compensation of employees is estimated at 80% of valued added. Informal sector : A benchmark for the year 1998 is based on the 1998 LFS. The annual increase at constant prices is assumed at 4% and CPI, all items is the deflator. Compensation of employees is estimated at 10% of valued added. Transport and Communication: Estimates are done separately for railway, road passenger and freight, pipeline, water and air transport. In addition, estimations for harbours, airports, auxiliary transport activities and communication are provided. Railway transport : All the necessary data are available from the income statement provided by Railway corporation. Various volume indicators are used to derive constant estimates. Intermediate consumption at constant prices is assumed to move with output. Road passenger : Estimates are based on 2001 benchmark output for 2001( matatu industry). The annual increase at constant prices is assumed to be equal to the population growth while current prices are derived by reflation with CPI, transport. Intermediate consumption is assumed at 58% of output both at current and constant prices. A benchmark estimate of output for 2001 is based on annual survey of transport (taxis, buses, tour operators). Freight transport: A benchmark estimate of output for 2001 is estimated on the basis of an indirect indicator, the sales of diesel. Ratios of intermediate consumption and compensation of employees over output are according to annual survey of freight transport. A volume index is calculated as a straight average of the quantities of diesel sold and trade margins at constant prices. For transport via pipeline all necessary data is available from the Kenya pipeline company. Water transport: Estimates are based on annual survey of transport. For the estimates at constant prices a volume index is used, calculated as the average of employment and net registered tonnage. All necessary data are available from Kenya Ports Authority on the activities at the habours. Constant price estimates are calculated using a volume index which is computed as a weighted average of number of ships (25%), tonnes of exports (15%), and tonnes of imports (60%). Intermediate consumption at constant prices is assumed to follow output . Communication : Information contained in the respective companies’ annual reports and surveys are used to generate the required variables. Relevant CPI indices are used to deflate. Financial Intermediation: Current price data on financial intermediaries are timely and detailed. Questionnaires are sent out to financial institutions and the response is complete. Annual reports of all the companies are used as a complimentary data source. Estimates of insurance companies, agents and brokers are based on wages and salaries obtained from the employment survey. Constant prices for both financial intermediaries and insurance are derived by deflating with CPI non-food excluding VAT. Output of insurance services at constant prices is calculated by volume extrapolation (employment index adjusted by 1% for productivity) Real Estate, Renting and Business services: The estimates are based on the number of households in different types of houses according to the 1989 and 1999 population censuses. Rental values are either obtained from price collection or imputed. Output is calculated by multiplying the rental values with the number of households in each category. Constant prices are calculated by projection with the number of households. Intermediate consumption is estimated at 10% of output for modern houses and 5% for other houses. No intermediate consumption is calculated for traditional houses. Renting and Business services : The estimate based on benchmark is projected at constant prices with a volume index calculated as changes in employment (LES) with an addition for an annual increase in productivity with 2%. Intermediate consumption is estimated at 28% of output and Compensation of employees at 60% of value added. Government account: Output is calculated as sum of costs. Constant prices estimates as the sum of costs: Intermediate consumption is derived by deflation with CPI, non-food excluding VAT. Compensation of employees is extrapolated with employment in the public sector according to LES. Public administration and defence: Three sub-sectors of government public administration and defence services: central government, local government, and social security institutions and output is based on sum of cost approach. Output at constant prices as the sum of costs: Intermediate consumption is derived by deflation with CPI, non-food excluding VAT. Compensation of employees is extrapolated with employment in the public sector according to LES (Government services; Law and order; Defence; and Other public administration). Education: Private sector (Primary and secondary education): The estimates at current prices are based on the number of private schools. Output at current prices is calculated as the sum of intermediate consumption and compensation of employees. Consumption of fixed capital and surplus are ignored. Compensation of employees is calculated as the percentage of private and secondary schools of the government schools is multiplied with the compensation of employees in government schools. Intermediate consumption is assumed at 30% of compensation of employees. Output at constant prices is calculated by extrapolation with enrolment numbers with a higher weight for a secondary school student. Intermediate consumption is deflated with CPI, non-food excluding VAT. Tertiary education(Private): The estimates at current prices are based on enrolment numbers in private teacher training colleges, technical schools and universities. The average cost per student is assumed to be 10% higher than in the public counterparts. Output at current prices is then calculated as the sum of intermediate consumption and compensation of employees. Consumption of fixed capital and operating surplus is ignored. Output at constant prices is calculated by extrapolation with enrolment numbers. Intermediate consumption is deflated with CPI, non-food excluding VAT. Government (some of cost approach is used): The major part of government schools are sponsored by the Teachers’ Salary Commission. This is a statutory body, which pays the salaries. Local authorities also operate some schools. Central government operates teachers’ training colleges and some technical schools. The universities are statutory bodies that are controlled and mainly financed by central government Health and social work: Private sector: Based on 1996 output The constant price output is brought forward with employment numbers according to the annual Labour Enumeration Survey (LES). CPI, medical services is used for reflation. Intermediate consumption is assumed at 30% of output at constant prices and reflated with of CPI, non-food excluding VAT and CPI, medical services. Compensation of employees (a benchmark based on 1998 LFS) is brought forward with employment numbers and the CPI, non-food. Government: Public health facilities are operated mainly by central government but some health activities are also carried out by local government. Output at constant prices is calculated as the sum of costs: Intermediate consumption is derived by deflation with CPI, non-food excluding VAT. Compensation of employees is extrapolated with employment in the public sector according to LES. Other services: Private :Estimates based on 2001 benchmark for outpu . At constant prices, this is projected with a volume index calculated as changes in employment according the annual Labour Enumeration Survey (LES) with an addition of an annual increase of 2% in order to take changes in productivity and the informal sector into account Intermediate consumption is estimated at 34% of output and Compensation of employees at 60% of value added. Government (sum of cost approach): Minor activities are carried out by central government in field of culture. The major part includes the activities of local government in the fields of sewage and refuses collection. Output at constant prices is calculated as the sum of costs: Intermediate consumption is derived by deflation with CPI, non-food excluding VAT. Compensation of employees is deflated with a salary index. Domestic services: A benchmark has been calculated for 1998. Number of persons employed in domestic work is available from the 1998 Labour Force Survey and has been multiplied with the gazetted minimum wage for a general labourer, other municipalities. Employment is assumed to have increased with 2% per year since 1990 and the average salary to change with the gazetted minimum wage. Meals during work are assumed at 40% of the wages at constant prices; this is reflated with CPI, food. Expenditure Approach Expenditure on GDP is compiled and private consumption is no longer derived as a residue as was previously the case. Private consumption: Private final consumption expenditure is made up of final consumption expenditure by households and by non-profit institutions serving households. Both current and constant price values are calculated through the commodity flow approach. The commodity flows allow a disaggregation of private consumption by a few categories: Food, beverages and tobacco Clothing and footwear Housing, water, electricity and fuels All other goods All other services Direct purchases abroad by resident households Less: Direct purchases in the domestic market by non-residents Public consumption: Government consumption is derived in the commodity flows connected to the ISIC-categories specified for government Final consumption by non-market producers in government is defined as their output minus any sales or fees paid for its services. The sales and fees are paid either by households or enterprises (producers), in the former cases recorded as private consumption and in the latter cases as intermediate consumption. Payments for licences to own or use vehicles, boats or aircraft and also licences to hunt, shoot or fish are treated as taxes, while payments for all other kinds of licences, permits, certificates, passports, etc., are treated as purchases of services and included in household consumption or intermediate consumption. Government consumption should be classified according to COFOG (classification of functions of government). Three categories are currently specified in Kenya’s national accounts: Health (COFOG 07) Education (COFOG 09) All other other categories Gross Fixed Capital formation (Total GFCF): GFCF is derived in the commodity flows, which allow disaggregation by type of asset: Buildings and structures: 90% of market output in the construction industry is assumed to be GFCF. The remaining 10% are minor repair works. In addition all output for own construction by households is GFCF Transport equipment: Vehicles are assembled in Kenya to a minor extent. 95% of these are estimated as GFCF with a mark-up of 5%. Most transport equipment is imported and GFCF is calculated in the commodity flow for imports. Estimates are made for three categories: Passenger motor cars: 75% of imports is estimated as GFCF with a mark-up of 30% Other road vehicles are all GFCF; a mark-up of 20% is added. Other transport equipment is mainly aircraft; no mark-up is added. Other machinery and equipment: GFCF in equipment produced in Kenya is estimated in two commodity flows for manufactured products. 25% of output for the domestic market in the sub-sector Manufacture of machinery, apparatus and equipment is recorded as GFCF and, in addition, 50% of the output of furniture. Most machinery and equipment is imported and is specified in the classification by Broad Economic Categories (BEC): 41 Machinery and other capital equipment. 25% is added for installation costs and margins Cultivated assets: Moreover, the expenditure of the Forestry Department on plantation development is recorded as GFCF Intangible assets: Expenditure on mineral exploration is recorded as GFCF and output of business activities. No estimates are made of GFCF in other intangible assets. Estimates of GFCF by general government are based on the previous average ratio of GFCF of government to total GFCF. Changes in stocks: Currently, there are no comprehensive estimates of changes in inventories. The commodity flows are made to balance disregarding inventory changes. Most probably estimates of those would reflect mainly in the estimates of private consumption and intermediate consumption in the commodity flows. Currently, changes in inventories are estimated only in a few commodity flows: For a few minerals as a balancing item between output and exports. For crude petroleum and refined petroleum products, based on quantitative data on supply and use. Exports of goods : The major source for exports of goods is customs data delivered by the Customs Department and processed by the CBS. However, it has not been possible to fully classify all exports by industry and a discrepancy is recorded between the sum of the commodity flows and the Customs data. The various components of CPI are used as deflators or volume indicators for the calculation of constant. The discrepancy described above is deflated by the implicit deflator for all exports recorded in the commodity flows. Re-exports at constant prices are calculated by deflation with the appropriate implicit deflator for imports c.i.f. Exports of services: The source for exports of services is the balance of payments (BOP) compiled by the KNBS. They are recorded in the commodity flows for Land transport, Sea transport, Air transport, Communication and Business services. Constant prices are derived by deflation with implicit output deflators for these services. Direct purchases in Kenya by non-residents comprise expenditure by foreign embassies and consulates, other foreign official agencies and international organisations. They are deflated with CPI, all items. Imports of goods: The major source for imports of goods is customs data delivered by the Customs Department and processed by the CBS. Adjustments are made for categories of imported goods that are not covered by customs data. These categories are contained in a separate commodity flow; they include: The Export Processing Zones Military imports Electricity imports from Uganda Maintenance and repair of aircraft Procurement in airports In general, the commodity flows are compiled as follows. Re-exports are recorded as exports and are valued c.i.f. Additions to the c.i.f.-value are made for: import duties, excise duties (in a few cases), margins and, for private consumption, VAT. The use is given by the BEC-classification Constant prices are calculated in either of two ways. Except for private consumption, specific volume or price indices are used for imports c.i.f.; to this are added imports duties, excise duties, margins and VAT derived by multiplication with base year percentages. Private consumption at purchasers’ prices is deflated with a suitable CPI. VAT, margins, excise duties, and imports duties are deducted from this derived by multiplication with base year percentages. Re-exports at constant prices are calculated by deflation with the implicit deflator for imports c.i.f. for the different BEC-categories. The adjustment for c.i.f. / f.o.b. is deflated with the implicit deflator for total imports c.i.f. Import of services: The source for imports of services is the balance of payments (BOP) compiled by the CBS. Two commodity flows are compiled. Direct purchases abroad by residents include all transactions recorded under Travel in the BOP and Embassies/consulates, recorded under Government services in the BOP. Travel, personal is recorded as private consumption and Travel, business as intermediate consumption as well as expenditure by Kenyan embassies and consulates. All other services. Imports of road passenger services and life insurance services are recorded as private consumption and the rest as intermediate consumption. Constant prices are derived by deflation with an index based on the overall weighted index for exchange rates increased with 2% for assumed inflation. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Production Approach: Output data are estimated as indicated in section 3.1.1.
