 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The dataare compiled in broad accordance with the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6). Definition: The balance of payments is a statistical statement summarizing the economic and financial transactions of Hungarian residents with the rest of the world during a reference period. Analytical framework:Eleven broad categories of data under three main headings: Current account: exports and imports of goods, services, primary income and secondary income; Capital account: capital transfers and acquisition/disposal of nonproduced, nonfinancial assets; and Financial account: assets and liabilities for direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives and employee stock options, other investment, international reserve assets (and net errors and omissions). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification system: Standard classification of the BPM6 . 2.2.1 Scope Classification system: Standard classification of the BPM6 . Scope of the data Transactions coverage: The data cover all transactions between residents and nonresidents. Intrastat/Extrastat cover merchandise entering and leaving Hungary. From January 2006 on a quarterly basis the MNB has also been compiling and publishing the balance of payments and international investment position statistics which include SPEs, to enable the analysis of international data by mirror statistics. However, from the perspective of analysing the external developments of Hungary, the balance of payments and international investment position excluding SPEs are more fit to purpose. Both sets of statistics are available for users on the MNB's website. In its publications and reports, the MNB analyses the data excluding SPEs. In 2020, under the leadership of the IMF, a new internationally approved SPE definition was elaborated. This SPE definition provides a wider coverage of the range and the activity pursued by special purpose entities, which means a majority or decisive control instead of an exclusive relationship with the rest of the world, and the monitored activity is not limited to passive financial activity. In 2021, the earlier SPE definition applied in Hungary has been revised with retroactive effect due to the implementation of the SPE definition approved by the IMF BOPCOM. Geographic coverage: No geographic regions are excluded from recording. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation principles: In general transactions are recorded at market prices where it is required by the standards. Conversion to unit of account: Data reported in foreign currencies converted to domestic currency (Hungarian forint) at monthly average exchange rates. Goods: Consistent with international methodological standards, exports and imports of goods are valued f.o.b/f.o.b. However, Intrastat/customs statistics record goods trade at c.i.f/f.o.b. The c.i.f. value of imports should be replaced by the f.o.b. value in the balance of payments. The MNB receives the f.o.b. data for goods imports from the Hungarian Central Statistical Office (HCSO). No other adjustment is made to data derived from the foreign trade statistics. With regard to unlisted enterprises’ equity, the valuation is based on the own funds at book value (OFBV) principle, i.e. as it is stated in the subsidiaries’ balance sheet: for outward FDI in the balance sheet of the non-resident enterprise and for inward FDI in the books of the resident enterprise. Regarding listed companies’ equity assets and liabilities, valuation is at market value 2.4.2 Recording basis Recording of income: Investment income is recorded on an accrual basis. Accrual-based interest is monitored directly (reported by the data suppliers). EU transfers are recorded on an accrual basis from September 2009. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Goods: Merchanting is recorded on a net basis, in accordance with international statistical standards. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Hungary has switched to a direct reporting system on January 1st 2008. The compilation system is based on: direct reporting of economic entities (enterprises, central bank and deposit taking organization, Government Debt Management Agency); Intrastat system and customs statistics for trade in goods (HCSO); trade in services statistics (HCSO); border surveys for travel (HCSO); HCSO report for compensation of employees; HCSO report for transfers of government and household sectors; and securities statistics for portfolio investments and their incomes; Goods: Data derived from Intrastat system as regards trade with EU member states and customs statistics for non-EU countries. Both statistics compiled by Hungarian Central Statistical Office (HCSO). The incorporation of Intrastat/customs statistics provides that goods for processing are treated also under services. Certain transactions in goods in the balance of payments (such as goods not crossing the frontier and repairs on goods) are not recorded in the Intrastat/customs statistics but derived from questionnaires on trade in services. Services: Data derived from border surveys for travel and from direct reporting and administrative sources for the remaining services items (compiled by HCSO): manufacturing services on physical inputs owned by others, transportation, communication services, construction services, insurance services, financial services, computer and information services, royalties and license fees, other business services, personal, cultural and recreational services, government services. Primary income: Compensation of employees: The source of data is the report of HCSO. Direct investment income: The source is the direct reporting of the economic entities (monthly or quarterly). The recording of reinvested earnings is derived from annual direct investment surveys and corporation tax declarations. Until this information is available,the income on FDI equity is estimated by the MNB. Portfolio investment income: The main source is the securities statistics. As a supplementary source data on income on bills receivable/payable and participation not embodied in securities is collected within the questionnaires on other investments (monthly and quarterly). Other investment income: The main source is the direct reporting of the economic entities (monthly or quarterly). Secondary income: There are two main sources: HCSO provides data for transfers of government and household sectors direct reporting of economic entities for all other sectors. Capital account: There are two main sources: HCSO provides data for transfers of government and household sectors direct reporting of economic agents for all other sectors. Direct investment: The source of information is the direct reporting of the economic entities (monthly, quarterly and annual). Portfolio investment: The primary source of information is the securities statistics which compiles monthly data on security-by security basis using the International Securities Identification Number (ISIN). As a supplementary source data on bills receivable/payable and participation not embodied in securities is collected within the questionnaires on other investments (monthly and quarterly). Financial derivatives and employee stock options: The source of information is the direct reporting of economic entities (monthly and quarterly). Other investment: The source of information is the direct reporting of the economic entities (monthly, quarterly). Submitted data are supplemented by estimations if necessary. Reserve assets: The primary source is the Magyar Nemzeti Bank's report on end-month outstanding positions for SDR, RPF and all those foreign exchange external assets (except securities) which are recorded in its books and are readily available for direct financing of payments imbalances, for indirectly regulating the magnitude of such imbalances through intervention in exchange markets to affect the forint exchange rate, and/or for other purposes. Data on securities is derived from the securities statistics. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The source data characteristics from various reporters are as follows: The most important data providers have to report monthly. These economic entities are designated by the MNB, their reporting is compulsory. They have to send reports on transactions and stocks of FDI, other investment, financial derivatives and employee stock options, transactions on transfers, nonproduced, nonfinancial assets and stock of securities. The remaining data suppliers are obliged to report their data on a quarterly and annual basis, depending on pre-defined (in the MNB decree available) thresholds. Annual FDI reports have to be sent by economic entities designated by the MNB on their stocks and reinvested earnings. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The timeliness of the source data is as follows: monthly direct reports: 10 working days after the end of the reference month; quarterly direct reports: 10 working days after the end of the reference quarter; annual direct reports on FDI: 30 June after the reference year; securities statistics: monthly, approximatelly 40 days after the end of the reference month; Intrastat system and customs statistics for trade in goods: monthly, 43rd day after the reference period; HCSO transfers: quarterly on non-EU transfers, 60 days after the end of the reference quarter; HCSO trade in services: quarterly, 60 days after the end of the reference quarter; HCSO travel: quarterly, 60 days after the end of the reference quarter. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Formal computerized checks of individual reports are built into the data reception process. All the consistency requirements within one report and between different reports of a single reporting entity are clearly identified in the instructions attached to each reporting form. High-value transactions are routinely checked with respondents. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Transportation services are adjusted with a value equal to the adjustment in the terms of delivery of goods, but with the opposite sign. The estimation and allocation are carried out by the HCSO. Income: Accrual-based income for the reference period is reported by the data supplier. COPC (current operating performance concept) approach is used in recording income related to direct investment. Secondary income: No adjustment is made to the value that is reported by economic entities. Direct investment: A macro-based estimate is applied for income on equity before the processing of data derived from annual direct investment questionnaires (based on balance sheets and profit and loss account). Other investment: In consequence of accrual accounting, interest accrued but not paid in the given period must be recorded as a claim or liability of the adequate instrument. (When the interest is paid a decrease of assets or liabilities are recorded in the financial account.) Reserve assets: Flows computed from end-month outstanding positions in the book of the MNB are adjusted to exclude changes in positions due to valuation, no other foreign asset is classified as a reserve asset than the MNB's liquid external assets, which observes the criteria set in the BPM6. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Seasonal adjustment: The data have been seasonally adjusted on a regular basis using the TRAMO and SEATS programs built in JDemetra+. Geographic detail: Since 2000 (as a reference period) the MNB reports to Eurostat the geographical breakdown of the balance of payments (Vademecum Questionnaires), but it does not form part of the MNB's standard statistical publication through its website (except FDI data flows). Other: All the financial account data and investment income data are available by sectors: Central Bank; General Government; Deposit taking corporations except the central bank, Money market funds, Financial corporations other than MFIs and Non-financial corporations, households, and non-profit institutions serving households 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Plausibility checks are carried out on both individual reports and aggregate levels. At both these levels, time series analysis is an essential tool for this purpose. Consistency checks with other statistics are also carried out at an aggregate level. Should there be any inconsistency at this level, there is a feedback to the individual report level and the reporting entity is asked to clarify the reason for the inconsistency. Staff closely monitor the scale of errors and omissions and seek to minimize them by carrying out cross-checks between current, capital and financial accounts. The behaviour of the BOP statistical time series is routinely assessed against related series, e.g. financial flow data are validated with the corresponding stock of external debt data, and reported data on investment income are assessed in relation to the corresponding stock in the IIP statistics. Information reported in the financial press is used to verify high-value FDI and other transactions. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished and non-confidential data can be obtained upon request.
