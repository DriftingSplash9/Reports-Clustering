 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Relationship to international guidelines : The compilation of gross ED is in accordance with the sixth edition of the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual, 2009 (BPM6) and External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users, 2013 (EDG, 2013). Starting from April 2016 the new BPM6/EDG, 2013 methodology was implemented and used to disseminate the ED statistics. Historical data back to 2009 have been converted and published in the statistical section of the NBM website. Additionally, ED data for the World Bank's Quarterly External Debt Statistics (QEDS) have been reported by the NBM starting with the data for 2012. Gross ED positions are compiled on a quarterly basis and are presented under the following categories: for each of the four institutional sectors (central bank, general government, deposit-taking corporations except the central bank, other sectors further detailed into two sub-sectors: (1) other financial corporations and (2) nonfinancial corporations, households and non-profit institutions serving households). Data are broken down by original maturity (short- and long-term) and by debt instruments (debt securities, trade credits and advances, loans, currency and deposits and other debt liabilities, SDR allocations). Data on intercompany lending under direct investment relationship, and namely debt liabilities of direct investment enterprise to direct investors are separately identified by maturity, short and long term. ED is also broken down by the original currency of debt instruments, and by the main creditors for loans only. Definition : The gross ED of the Republic of Moldova, at a given time, is the outstanding amount of all current, and not contingent, actual liabilities that require payment(s) of principal and/or interest by the debtor at some point(s) in the future and are owed to non-residents by the residents of Moldova. ED comprises both domestic and foreign currency debt owed to non-residents and, since 2009, the country's liabilities on the allocations of SDRs. Financial leases with non-residents are treated as loans and are included in the gross ED position. Intercompany lending between entities in a direct investment relationship includes debt liabilities to direct investors and to affiliated enterprises and is identified separately in the total ED position. Government securities owned by non-residents, customers of domestic licensed banks in national currency are included in the ED. ED liabilities payable in goods and services are also included in the ED position. According to the EDG 2013 , arrears on ED service are no longer recorded as short-term debt, but are included in the outstanding amount of long-term or short-term liabilities under the main underlying debt instrument. The long-term debt data refers to financial instruments with original maturity of more than 12 months, and short-term – to those with a maturity of 12 months or less. The ESSD compiles ED statistics of the Republic of Moldova on a quarterly and annual basis, in millions of US dollars (USD). Quarterly and annually ED data are also recalculated from USD into EUR at quarter-end / year-end cross exchange rates. The data in EUR are presented in annexes to the quarterly Analytical Notes and in International Accounts (IA) Yearbook. Coverage, definitions and classification are identical for all periods. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification : Gross ED statistics is compiled according to the recommendations of the EDG, 2013 . The disseminated data include: debt securities, loans, SDR allocation, trade credits and advances, currency and deposits, other debt liabilities, direct investment: intercompany lending. 1. Debt securities include debt securities issued by Moldovan residents, held by non-residents, including debt securities issued by the government in national currency in order to be placed in the domestic market of the Republic of Moldova and purchased by non-residents. However, according to the Law of the Republic of Moldova "On public debt, state guarantees and on-lending from state borrowing" No 419-XVI of 22.12.2006, such securities are attributed to the internal governmental debt by the MOF. Debt securities are classified by sector (issuer's sector) and by the type of financial instrument. The outstanding position in liabilities on debt securities at any given time is the sum of debt securities issued by Moldovan residents and held by non-residents, minus overdue securities plus accrued interest for the reported period. 2. Loans comprise the following components: - central bank loans – loans contracted from the IMF and used by the NBM; - general governmental loans (state loans and loans of administrative-territorial units (ATU)) – external loans contracted and used by the central and local government on behalf of the Republic of Moldova; - loans guaranteed by the Government – external loans of the private sector under the guarantee of the Government; - loans of public corporations – external loans contracted by economic agents where the government and/or ATU holds more than 50% of the equity capital; - private loans – external loans contracted by the private sector (deposit-taking corporations, except central bank, and other sectors) with the exception of loans from affiliated nonresident companies reflected under Direct investment: intercompany lending item. The position of liabilities in the form of loans at any given time is the amount of the external loans contracted and used by Moldovan residents minus actual repayments of principal, plus interest arrears up to the respective date. 3. SDR allocations are the Government liabilities to the IMF. 4. Trade credits and advances consist of external liabilities arising from direct extension of credit by the suppliers of goods and services to their customers and advances for work that is in progress (or is yet to be undertaken) and prepayment by customers for goods and services not yet provided. They include: - payments in advance for exports of goods and services received by economic agents from non-residents; - trade credits for imports (unpaid imports) offered by non-residents to resident economic entities; - liabilities on imports of services not paid in due time. 5. Currency and deposits consist of non-residents deposits in the Moldovan banking system. 6. Other debt liabilities cover: - the historical debt for imports of goods and services; - liabilities to non-residents on transactions with investments; - liabilities on dividends distributed but not yet paid to non-residents. 7. Direct investment - intercompany lending. The position of intercompany lending at any given time is the amount of the external loans contracted and used by Moldovan subsidiaries/direct investors from their parent companies/branches minus payments of scheduled principal. It includes: - loans from parent companies or from branches located abroad; - trade credits and advances - liabilities to foreign direct investors; - other debt liabilities to foreign direct investors. The position of liabilities in the form of intercompany loans at a given time is the amount of external loans contracted and used by economic entities of a country from their direct investors minus actual principal payments, plus interest arrears. Debt positions between affiliated enterprises on trade credits are derived from legal entities' financial statements and are recorded in annual 1-Invest report collected by NBS. The ED is disaggregated by institutional sectors following the BPM6 BOP classification: central bank, general government, deposit-taking corporations, except for central bank, and other sectors. Other sectors' debt includes debt liabilities of public corporations and other enterprises to non-residents. The ED liabilities are attributed to the sector of the immediate borrower. For analytical purposes the ED is presented specifying the debtor's sector that is: public sector, publicly guaranteed private sector debt and nonguaranteed private sector debt. Public sector debt comprises Governmental and the NBM's debt liabilities to non-residents and debt resulting from external loans of administrative-territorial units (ATU) and public corporations (publicly owned enterprises and business entities where the government or ATU hold more than 50% of the equity capital). External governmental (state) debt represents the total amount of outstanding foreign liabilities (including owed interest), contracted by the Government on behalf of the Republic of Moldova through the MOF, and consists of the direct external governmental (state) debt (including liabilities on SDR allocations) and the publicly guaranteed assumed ED. The direct external governmental debt includes the external liabilities of the government, when the government acts in the capacity of the debtor. Publicly guaranteed assumed ED includes external governmental liabilities arising from the government's duty to execute the state guarantee when the debtor (guarantee beneficiary) fails to meet its obligations towards the creditor, according to the provisions of the state guarantee contract. ED of ATU includes current and mature external liabilities in the form of loans, as well as unpaid interest due on them, owed and outstanding, contracted and used by local public authorities. The debt of the National Bank of Moldova includes loans from the IMF used by the NBM. The ED of public corporations includes current and mature external liabilities in the form of loans and unpaid interest due on them, owed and outstanding, contracted and used by business entities where the government and / or ATU hold more than 50% of the equity capital, and by public enterprises. Liabilities not meeting the definition of public debt are classified as private debt. The external private debt includes the liabilities of the private sector (deposit-taking corporations, except the central bank and other private enterprises including private companies with foreign capital) to non-residents, contracted with or without governmental guarantee and serviced by the private sector. For analytical purposes the ED is also presented by maturity on an original maturity basis: long-term refers to liabilities with an original maturity of more than 12 months, and short-term – to those with a maturity of 12 months or less. Additionally, according to QEDS requirements, the ED data are also presented on the remaining maturity basis. Changes in the external liabilities positions like reclassifications by institutional sector, rescheduling, debt forgiveness are clearly identified in an analytical table. Scope of the data Unit Coverage: The gross ED largely covers all positions of external financial liabilities of residents to non-residents. Foreign embassies and consulates, representatives of international organizations accredited in Moldova and representative offices of foreign enterprises located in Moldova are treated as non-residents. Data Coverage: The gross ED of Moldova includes the positions of external liabilities for all institutional sectors: central bank, general government, deposit-taking corporations, except the central bank and other sectors, further detailed into two sub-sectors (1) other financial corporations and (2) nonfinancial corporations, households and NPISHs. Transaction Coverage: The gross ED of Moldova covers, in principle, all external financial liabilities to non-residents incurred by resident central bank, general government, deposit-taking corporations, except the central bank and other sectors, separately for other financial corporations and nonfinancial corporations, households, and NPISHs . Geographic Coverage: The economic territory of Moldova consists of the geographic territory administrated by the Government of Moldova, including Moldovan embassies, consulates and representatives abroad. Embassies, consulates, representative offices of foreign institutions and of foreign governments placed on the territory of the Republic of Moldova are treated as non-residents. Exceptions to coverage Coverage of external debt : Financial derivatives and employee stock option liabilities, all equity (shares and other equity) and investment fund shares are not included in the ED position. Households' external liabilities with non-residents may not be fully covered by the existing sources and methods. Geographical coverage : The ED data do not include external indebtedness of residents from the left side of the Nistru River and the municipality of Bender. Unrecorded activity None 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to units of account : The gross ED data are compiled and published in millions of USD. The positions in external liabilities denominated in other currencies are converted into USD at the official exchange rate set by the NBM at the end of the reference period. The impact of foreign exchange rate fluctuations of original currencies against the USD, price changes and other adjustments are calculated evaluating the positions. These adjustments are calculated according to the IMF methodology. Sectorial presentations of ED is also available in EUR converted at the cross exchange rate at period-end. Annual EUR-denominated data are available starting with reference year 2013 (presented in the annexes to the Yearbook for 2017) and for quarterly data – starting with data for 2024 (presented in the annexes to the Analytical notes). Valuation: Tradable ED instruments are reported at nominal value. The book value is used for the valuation of liabilities when the market value is not appropriate or not available. 2.4.2 Recording basis Recording basis: The recording basis for the ED position is accrual accounting, except for loans, where due-for-payment basis is used. The outstanding liabilities arising from periodic interest costs of debt securities that have accrued and are not yet payable are included in the gross ED position as part of the value of the underlying debt instrument. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The main data sources for gross ED compilation are the data received from the MOF, some departments of the NBM, licensed banks, the NBS, and residents contracting external loans. Debt securities : The NBM uses the data on debt securities from the Debt Management and Financial Analysis System (DMFAS), supplemented with data from the International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS), the Central Securities Depository, and from the NBM's Risk Monitoring and Reporting Division. Loans : Data on loans are produced based on the DMFAS, administered by the MOF (for public debt, except NBM's loans from IMF) and by the NBM (for private loans and NBM's loans from IMF). DMFAS data are supplemented with the data from the ITRS. Data on intercompany lending are compiled based on DMFAS and supplemented with the ITRS data. SDR allocation : reports from the MOF and the DMFAS data. Trade credits and advances : Quarterly and annual data on advances received by economic agents from non-residents are calculated based on consolidated data from the legal entities' balance sheets, collected by the NBS. Since 2008 quarterly estimations are made based on the comparison of payments and receipts recorded in the ITRS with external trade statistics. Liabilities on imports of goods and services not paid in due time – NBM's estimations based on the ITRS and external trade in goods and services data comparison. - Quarterly positions on trade credits are estimated by adding flows to positions in a particular period; - Annual positions on trade credits are reconciled with general consolidated data from the legal entities' financial statements, collected by the NBS. Currency and deposits : Data are compiled based on the licensed banks' reports collected by NBM. Other debt liabilities: Data on the historical debt (with a maturity of more than 1 year) of legal entities on imports/exports of goods and services are received from the surveys of legal entities – monopolies in the strategic imports of goods (e.g., energy resources) or services (e.g. railway transportation). Data on liabilities to non-residents in transactions with investment and deferred payments of dividends are compiled using the report of the National Commission for Financial Markets and the ITRS. Direct investment: intercompany lending data on loans to subsidiaries from foreign direct investors/branches are derived from the DMFAS and are supplemented with data from the ITRS. The data on the direct ED of the government and the publicly guaranteed ED are presented by the MOF on a quarterly basis. The data on external non-guaranteed private sector debt are compiled by the NBM on the basis of the DMFAS, which is supplemented with the ITRS data. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data generally comply with methodological standards and guidelines for compiling quarterly ED statistics. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The source data are timely for compiling and disseminating quarterly ED statistics. For surveys, the enterprises are expected to report promptly and follow-up procedures to ensure the timely receipt of data. Non-respondents are followed up accordingly. Data from the ITRS are available on a timely basis. Formal arrangements are in place to ensure the timely basis of administrative data. 3.2.1 Source data assessment All data go through validations with related sources. Individual records with high values are cross-checked with the ITRS or other sources. Necessary follow-up is done either to correct misreported data or to obtain missing data. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The NBM monitors the compatibility of data and ensures the internal integrity of the ED position. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Seasonal adjustment: ED data are not seasonally adjusted. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Intermediate results are checked against various alternative data sources and estimates. Collection of data from multiple sources enables the ESSD to validate statistical outputs. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data The behaviour of time series is cross-checked with related series or indicators, where available. Financial flows are reconciled with changes in positions. Whenever surveys or administrative data are used, they are cross-checked with ITRS data. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Data on changes in ED position are reconciled with data from the BOP, integrated IIP, governmental financial statistics and monetary and financial statistics. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revision studies : Analyses and studies of revisions are carried out on a routine basis and used to improve sources and statistical techniques. The information regarding revisions, by items and periods affected, together with the reasons for revisions is published in the International Accounts of the Republic of Moldova Statistical Yearbook. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Information on revisions in terms of the reasons, items and periods affected is published in the International Accounts of the Republic of Moldova Statistical Yearbook . 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Ad hoc ED statistics are routinely provided upon request to ministries and to international organizations, embassies, international rating agencies, etc. The ESSD also provides data in response to requests from students and researchers. Customized tabulations are provided free of charge to meet specific requests. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The International Accounts of the Republic of Moldova Statistical Yearbook provides a clear reference to the division in charge of compiling and disseminating ED statistics, along with contact details of the person who could assist users. Contact information for public relations and media is also available on the NBM's website, together with an online feedback form for analytical materials and questions. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The NBM publishes all NBM's statistical publications on its website, which also includes a comprehensive list of publications.
