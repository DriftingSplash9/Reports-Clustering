 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Data on international reserves and foreign currency liquidity in accordance with international standards set out in the International Reserves and Foreign Currency Liquidity: Guidelines for a Data Template (IMF 2013) and in the sixth edition of The Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (IMF, 2009) (BPM6). Data on international reserves and foreign currency liquidity represent a set of economic indicators enabling to compare balance of financial assets and other scheduled revenues in foreign currency on the one part, and existing liabilities and other scheduled expenditures in foreign currency, on the other part. International reserves of the Republic of Belarus are defined as highly liquid foreign assets comprising stocks of monetary gold, special drawing rights, reserve position of the Republic of Belarus in IMF, and foreign currency reserves, that are readily available for meeting the balance of payments financing needs, for intervention in exchange markets to affect the currency exchange rates, and for other related purposes. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification used are in accordance with recommendations of the International Reserves and Foreign Currency Liquidity: Guidelines for a Data Template (IMF 2013). International reserves of the Republic of Belarus include highly liquid foreign assets (investment grade and above under Fitch Ratings, Standard and Poor's and Moody's Investor Service rating scales) consisting of: monetary gold (including gold deposits); special drawing rights of the Republic of Belarus with the IMF; the reserve position of the Republic of Belarus in the IMF; foreign currency reserves. Foreign currency reserves consist of the following highly liquid foreign assets in freely convertible foreign currency: cash and foreign exchange and payment documents that are nonresidents' obligations; correspondents' accounts and demand deposits in nonresident banks; deposits in nonresident banks with an original term to maturity of up to one year inclusively; liquid securities issued by nonresidents; other claims relative to nonresidents with an original term to maturity of up to one year inclusively. Scope of the data Institutional coverage: Data on international reserves and foreign currency liquidity include foreign currency assets and liabilities (off-balance sheet including) of the National Bank of the Republic of Belarus (the National Bank), the Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Belarus (the Ministry of Finance) and the Agency of Deposit Compensation (state-owned institution) in regard to both residents of the Republic of Belarus and nonresidents. Data on international reserves include monetary gold, special drawing rigths, reserve position in the IMF and foreign currency assets of the National Bank and the Ministry of Finance. International reserves include foreign assets in the currencies of foreign countries (the U.S. dollar, the euro, the pound sterling, the Japanese yen, the Chinese renminbi, the Swiss franc, the Australian dollar, and the Canadian dollar), with maturity of nonresidents' liabilities up to one year (inclusive), placed with financial corporations that are nonresidents of the Republic of Belarus or into securities issued by nonresidents, with long-term credit rating not lower than the investment grade according to grading scales of international rating agencies such as Fitch Ratings, Standard and Poor's, and Moody's Investors Service. Exceptions to coverage International reserves do not include assets placed with resident banks; securities used as collateral for repo transactions; pledged assets and assets with nonresident banks in which the National Bank has equity interest. 2.4.1 Valuation Transactions are valued by the cost of conducting thereof; foreign currency reserves are recognized at market price. The cost of monetary gold is valued at market price calculated on the basis of price of gold fixed by the London Bullion Market Association. Data are compiled in US dollars. Foreign assets are converted into US dollars using the cross-rates of US dollar to other foreign currencies based on the official BYN to respective foreign currencies rate, fixed by the National Bank as of reporting date. 2.4.2 Recording basis The accrual principle is used in the recording of investments. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Data on international reserves and foreign currency liquidity are recorded on gross basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The following are main sources of information used for compilation of data on international reserves and foreign currency liquidity: the National Bank's balance sheet data on foreign assets and liabilities of the National Bank of the Republic of Belarus, balances of precious metals of the National Bank of the Republic of Belarus with nonresident banks and the National Bank's central vault; information of the Ministry of Finance on monetary gold stocks; data on principal and interest repayment schedule for forward currency raising and placement transactions of the National Bank, on a transaction-by-transaction basis; information on the Ministry of Finance's external public debt repayment and service schedule; information on forthcoming principal and interest repayments on foreign currency loans and borrowings provided by the Government of the Republic of Belarus; information on repayment and service schedule of the foreign currency debt guaranteed by the Government of the Republic of Belarus; Website of the International Monetary Fund ( https://www.imf.org/external/ ) in terms of data on the relationship between the Republic of Belarus and the International Monetary Fund. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Data on international reserves and foreign currency liquidity include data on official reserve assets, other foreign currency assets, predetermined net drain on short-term foreign currency assets, contingent net drain on short-term foreign currency assets; and other information on expected foreign currency flows. The value of international reserves of the Republic of Belarus is calculated as the sum of assets in special drawing rights and other foreign currency, monetary gold, booked on the balance sheet account of the National Bank, and monetary gold of the government of the Republic of Belarus and the reserve position in the International Monetary Fund. Monetary gold is defined as gold at the disposal of the National Bank and the government of the Republic of Belarus, in the form of bank gold bullion bars and gold weighted bullion bars and stored in the national bank for gold commemorative coins and bullion coins of at least 995/1000 fineness. Special drawing rights represent international reserve assets created by the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and allocated to members to supplement to existing official reserves. Reserve position of the Republic of Belarus in IMF is comprised of deposits placed by the Republic of Belarus on the IMF General Resources Account. Foreign currency reserves are comprised of highly liquid foreign assets and may take the form of foreign cash and other payment facilities that constitute liabilities of nonresidents; available account balances with nonresident banks with original maturity up to one year (inclusive); marketable securities issued by nonresidents; assets held in trust; other claims on nonresidents with original maturity up to one year (inclusive). Other foreign currency assets represent assets than do not meet criteria established for international reserves. As opposed to international reserves, such assets include claims both to nonresident and residents. Predetermined short-term net drain on foreign currency assets comprise proceeds from and payments of principal and interest on available financial foreign currency assets and liabilities anticipated within one year, and foreign currency flows expected within the same period coming from forward contracts, futures contracts, and swaps. Contingent short-term net drain on foreign currency assets show contingent exchange inflows and outflows within one year caused by contract liabilities associated with prospective or potential future accumulation of financial assets and liabilities in foreign currency. Undrawn commitments, issued or obtained guarantees, and option contracts come under such type of potential currency flows. Other information on expected foreign currency flows includes additional information on aforementioned foreign currency flows. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The timetable of source data input ensures timely compilation of data on international reserves and foreign currency liquidity and comply with the requirements of the Special Data Dissemination Standard of the International Monetary Fund (SDDS) pertaining to dissemination of data of the foreign sector. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Compilers of international reserves assess source data on a consistent basis and initiate the source data modification process when necessary, to improve statistical reporting. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The data is not seasonally adjusted. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Intermediate results are regularly checked against other reports at the disposal of the National Bank. Statistics on international reserves and foreign currency liquidity are compared with monetary statistics, government finance statistics, balance of payments statistics, and international investment position. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Intermediate data are analyzed in terms of internal and temporal consistency. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs When unaccountable wide variation of data occurs, its causes are studies through direct contacts with information providers. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Published data are final and not subject to revision. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished non-confidential statistics may be provided on request. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The metadata placed on the National Bank's official Internet site specify the name, telephone numbers, e-mail address of the contact person. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The list of publications of the National Bank is available on the National Bank's official Internet site.
