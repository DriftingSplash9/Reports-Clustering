 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The external debt is developed in compiled in accordance with international standards set out the External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (IMF, 2013), and the sixth edition of The Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (IMF, 2009) (BPM6). Gross external debt of the Republic of Belarus , at any given time, is the outstanding amount of those actual current, and not contingent, liabilities that require payment(s) of principal and/or interest by the debtor at some point(s) in the future and that are owed to nonresidents by residents of the Republic of Belarus. For a liability to be included in external debt, it must exist and be outstanding. The principles and concepts used for compilation of the external debt correspond to the principles of the balance of payments compilation and the international investment position of the country (principles of economic territory of the country, residence, principles governing the valuation and the time of recording of transactions, etc.). The structure of data of the external debt corresponds to structure of the balance of payments financial account and the international investment position (in terms of external debt liabilities). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification used are in accordance with the External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (IMF, 2013) and BPM6. External financial liabilities with respect debt securities, trade credit and advances, loans, currency and deposits and other debt liabilities, broken down by four sectors of the economy, are identified separately in the data on external debt of the Republic of Belarus: general government, central bank, deposit-taking corporations, except the central bank, and other sectors (other financial corporations; nonfinancial corporations, households, and NPISH). Loan transactions of the banking sector are classified separately from currency and deposits transactions of this sector. All interbank positions other than securities and accounts receivable/payable are classified under deposits. The breakdown into long- and short-term maturity component is made according to the initial maturity of the financial instrument. Direct investment: intercompany lending are attributed to long-term debt. The following institutional sectors are distinguished: general government sector includes all general government bodies of the Republic of Belarus, local executive and administrative bodies of the Republic of Belarus, foundations, institutions, nonmarket nonprofit organizations controlled by government, and other publicly controlled organizations engaging in nonmarket activities. Diplomatic and other official representative offices of the Government of the Republic of Belarus located outside the Republic of Belarus also belong to the general government sector of their home country; central bank includes the authorities that exercise control over key aspects of the financial system, in particular, issuing currency, managing international reserves and regulation of activity of deposit-taking corporations. The National Bank of the Republic of Belarus belongs to such authorities; deposit-taking corporations , except the central bank include organizations engaged in financial intermediation related to fundraising for deposits as their principal activity. Banks and non-banking credit and financial institutions (NCFI) belong to this sector in the Republic of Belarus; other sectors include (1) other financial corporations, as well as (2) nonfinancial corporations, households, and nonprofit institutions serving households, unrecorded in the above sectors. Beyond that, data on external debt of the public sector are compiled in a broader definition, covering public and publicly guaranteed debt. The external debt of the public sector in the extended definition includes external debt liabilities of government bodies, the central bank, financial and nonfinancial corporations of the Republic of Belarus, in the authorized funds of which the share of state ownership is 50 percent or more. Debt liabilities of other institutional units that do not fall under fit the definition the public sector in the extended definition are classified as external debt of the private sector . The publicly guaranteed external debt of the private sector is defined as private sector external debt liabilities, which servicing is guaranteed by government bodies and the central bank. External liabilities fall under the direct borrower sector. 2.2.1 Scope Classification used are in accordance with the External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (IMF, 2013) and BPM6. Scope of the data The external debt covers debt liabilities of all sectors of economy of the Republic of Belarus to nonresidents, regardless of currency in which such debt is denominated. External liabilities fall under the direct borrower sector. Interest costs that have accrued but not yet payable is included as part of the value of the underlying debt instrument. Arrears are included to basic instruments. Data on gross external debt are compiled on a consolidated basis for the Republic of Belarus as a whole. External debt liabilities comprise the following debt instruments: special drawing rights allocations; currency and deposits (including unallocated gold accounts); debt securities; loans, insurance, pension, and standardized guarantees schemes; trade credit and advances; and other accounts payable. Intercompany lending between entities in a direct investment relationship is separately identified in the total external debt position. Financial leasing with nonresidents is classified as loans. Preferred shares held by nonresidents are classified as an external debt component in the context of the issuer undertaking to pay dividends on them, though the issuer does not undertake to buy them out at market price. Geographic coverage : The economic territory of the Republic of Belarus covers the country's geographic area under the jurisdiction of the Government of the Republic of Belarus, within which labour, goods and capital can move freely. The country's economic territory includes land and air space, territorial enclaves in other countries (distinct areas situated in other countries, which belong to or are leased by the Government of the Republic of Belarus for diplomatic, military, scientific or any other purposes, free zones or customs warehouses and enterprises used under control of customs authorities). The economic territory of the Republic of Belarus does not include enclaves of foreign governments and international organizations located within the geographic area of the Republic of Belarus. Exceptions to coverage External debt does not cover guarantees, opened but yet undrawn credit lines, financial derivatives, and other contingent liabilities, as well as equity (other than preferred shares held by nonresidents, which are recognized in the debt securities line). 2.4.1 Valuation Debt liabilities as of the reporting date are measured at their nominal value. Securities are carried at fair value. Data of external debt are compiled in US dollars. Positions of external debt liabilities denominated in other currencies are converted into US dollars using the cross-rates of US dollar to other foreign currencies based on the official BYN to respective foreign currencies rate, fixed by the National Bank as of reporting date. 2.4.2 Recording basis External debt liabilities are recorded separately per each financial liability. Time of transaction recording is determined on an accrual basis. According to the accrual basis of accounting, transactions are recognized by the parties simultaneously, and the interest charged on deposits, loans and securities is included in the available amount of financial liabilities and not treated as a component of other accounts payable. When arrears occur, they continue to be shown in the same debt instrument until the liability is extinguished. Arrears are included to basic instruments. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The following are main sources of information used for compilation of the external debt: reporting on foreign economic activity submitted to the National Bank by deposit-taking corporations of the Republic of Belarus (relating to foreign economic transactions of their customers that are residents of the Republic of Belarus, and their own foreign economic transactions) and by organizations that are residents of the Republic of Belarus; reporting on foreign economic transactions of organizations that are residents of the Republic of Belarus submitted to the National Statistics Committee of the Republic of Belarus (the Belstat): on attracting of foreign investment into the national economy, and providing Belarusian investments abroad by other financial and nonfinancial corporations of the Republic of Belarus; other official sources of information of the Republic of Belarus: the Belstat, the Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Belarus (the Ministry of Finance), the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the Republic of Belarus, other government bodies; quarterly information from the Central Bank of the Russian Federation submitted in accordance with the agreed rules of information exchange between the Central Bank of the Russian Federation and the National Bank; data from international organizations; mass-media, internet resources. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The main source data were specially compiled for external debt calculation and generally follow the recommendations of the External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (IMF, 2013) and BPM6. Data on external debt liabilities as of the reporting date are classified according to institutional sectors, maturity, types of debt instruments. Classification of external debt liabilities by financial instruments covers: special drawing rights (allocation); currency and deposits (including unallocated gold accounts); debt securities; loans; trade credit and advances; other debt liabilities (insurance, pension, and standardized guarantees schemes, other accounts payable). Debt liabilities to direct investors and direct investment enterprises are included in external debt statistics and recognized in a separate item "Direct investment: intercompany lending". Special drawing rights allocation is an obligation of the Republic of Belarus arising in response to allocation by the IMF of a special type of asset: special drawing rights created by the IMF and allocated between the IMF member-states as supplement to available reserve assets. Allocation of special drawing rights is recognized on the balance sheet of the National Bank. Data derived from the National Bank and International Monetary Fund are used. Currency and deposits cover liabilities of deposit-taking corporations of the Republic of Belarus and other financial corporations of the Republic of Belarus, for accounts and deposits of nonresidents. Monthly reporting of the National Bank, banks and NCFI, as well as reporting of other financial corporations is used. Debt securities represent liabilities for negotiable instruments evidencing of debt existence. They cover bills, bonds, notes, negotiable certificates of deposit, commercial papers, debentures, asset-backed securities, money market instruments, and similar instruments generally traded on financial markets. Bills are defined as securities that give the holders the unconditional rights to receive stated fixed sums on a specified date. Data are compiled based on reporting of banks and NCFI of the Republic of Belarus, other financial corporations of the Republic of Belarus, data of the Ministry of Finance, the Belstat survey on investment in the Republic of Belarus from abroad, the IMF data broken down by countries within the Coordinated portfolio investment survey, data derived from the partner countries. Loans show the outstanding principal amount of raised loans and borrowings. Loans include liabilities arising when a lender directly lends a certain amount of financial or material values to a borrower (debtor) and in evidence receives documents that are not negotiable. Financial leasing agreements are treated as loans. he following sources of information are used for compilation of data: for the central bank – the National Bank's reporting on foreign assets and liabilities; for the general government sector – reporting of the Ministry of Finance on external public debt; for the sector of deposit-taking corporations – the consolidated balance of banks and NCFI; for other financial corporations – reporting of other financial corporations; for other sectors – the Belstat survey on foreign loans and borrowings based on survey of nonfinancial corporations; data derived from the partner countries. Trade credit and advances include debt amounts, related to deferred payment provided by nonresident suppliers of goods and services to their clients residents of the Republic of Belarus (unpaid import), as well as prepayments by nonresidents for goods and services (export) not yet provided (prepaid export). The Belstat survey of nonfinancial corporations, and reporting of other financial corporations is used. Other debt liabilities include debt related to insurance, pension, and standard ized guarantees schemes, and other accounts payable, not qualified as trade credit or other debt instruments. Insurance, pension, and standardized guarantees schemes consist of nonlife insurance technical reserves, life insurance and annuity entitlements, pension entitlement, claims of pension funds on pension managers, and entitlement to nonpension funds, and provisions for calls under standardized guarantees. For data compilation, reporting of insurance companies is used. Other accounts payable cover liabilities for taxes, purchase and sale of securities, securities lending fees, gold loan fees, wages and salaries, dividends, and social contributions that have accrued but not yet paid. For data compilation reporting of the National Bank, banks and NCFI, reporting of the Belstat, the Ministry of Finance, and reporting of other financial corporations are used. Intercompany lending covers liabilities related to attraction of borrowed funds and the provision of lending resources (debt securities, trade credit and other loans, other debt liabilities) available between direct investors and their foreign branches, affiliated companies and associated enterprises. Intercompany lending within direct investment is classified according to the relationship between an investor and the entity receiving the investment: (1) investment by a direct investor in its direct investment enterprise; (2) reverse investment by a direct investment enterprise in its own immediate or indirect direct investor; and (3) investment between resident and nonresident fellow enterprises. For data compilation the Belstat reporting on investment in the Republic of Belarus from abroad, the IMF data broken down by countries within the Coordinated direct investment survey, reporting of the partner countries are used. The foreign debt liability classification by maturity differentiation between long term and short-term liabilities is based on the original maturity criterion. Short-term liability is liability with original maturity of one year and less or liability paid at call . Long-term liability is liability with original maturity exceeding one year. Short-term remaining maturity of outstanding external debt includes repayments of debt due to be paid in one year or less. It is calculated by adding the value of outstanding short-term external debt (original maturity) to the value of outstanding long-term external debt (original maturity) due to be paid in one year or less. External debt classification by currency implies breakdown by liabilities denominated in domestic currency and liabilities denominated in foreign currencies. Domestic currency debt is debt that is payable in the domestic currency and not linked to a foreign currency. Foreign currency debt is defined as debt which the value of flows and positions is fixed in a currency other than the domestic currency. The net external debt position is equal to gross external debt less gross external assets in debt instruments. Foreign assets in the form of debt instruments do not include assets in the form of shares and other equity, and assets in the form of financial derivatives and bullions of monetary gold. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The timetable of source data input ensures timely compilation of data on gross external debt and comply with the requirements of the Special Data Dissemination Standard of the International Monetary Fund (SDDS) pertaining to dissemination of data of the external sector. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Compilers of the external debt assess source data on a consistent basis and initiate the source data modification process when necessary, to improve statistical reporting. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The external debt statistics are compiled on the basis of summarized statistical information. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Data on gross external debt future payment schedule (principal and interest), currency and country-structure of external debt liabilities for all sectors of economy are compiled. The external debt data are not seasonally adjusted. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Intermediate results are regularly checked against other reports at the disposal of the National Bank: reporting of the National Bank, other deposit-taking corporations, other financial corporations; reporting of the Ministry of Finance, data derived from the partner countries are compared with monetary statistics, government finance statistics, and national accounts statistics. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Intermediate data are analyzed in terms of internal and temporal consistency. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs When unaccountable wide variation of data occurs, its causes are studies through direct contacts with information providers. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Data for the reporting period are intermediate when first released. The data revision policy for the external debt statistics is determined by data revision rules and described in the National Bank's quarterly data analytics bulletin "The Balance of Payments, International Investment Position, and Gross External Debt of the Republic of Belarus", though notes are made that the external debt statistics may be modified in future publications. A separate annex contains the list of modifications introduced in the external debt, indicating the reason and period for which they have been introduced (quarterly), per each item. Modifications may be introduced due to update of reported data, changes in methodology or methods of calculation of certain indicators, development of new sources of information, reclassification of instruments (indicators), update of reported data upon agreement with the partner countries, for other reasons. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Historical data series of external debt from 1996 to 2012 were made accessible in accordance with the External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (IMF, 2003) and starting from 2000 to the present day in accordance with the External Debt Statistics Guide (IMF, 2013) and BPM6. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished non-confidential statistics may be provided on request. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The metadata placed on the National Bank's official Internet site and the quarterly data analytics bulletin "The Balance of Payments, International Investment Position, and Gross External Debt of the Republic of Belarus" specify the name, telephone numbers, e-mail address of the contact person. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The list of publications of the National Bank is available on the National Bank's official Internet site.
