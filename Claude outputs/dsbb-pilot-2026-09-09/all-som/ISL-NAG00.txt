 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework : The Icelandic National Accounts follow the structure, concepts, definitions, and classifications of the System of National Accounts 2008 (2 008 SNA ) and the European System of Accounts 2010 ( ESA 2 010 ), implemented in 2014. Historical series have been revised back to 1995 according to the new system. Definition : GDP measures the value generated by all resident units engaged in productive activity before the deduction of provision for the consumption of fixed capital. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification systems: ESA 2010 both provides a methodology and defines classifications that Statistics Iceland uses for compiling national accounts on comparable bases, e.g., the ESA sector classification and institutional unit structure (see 3.3). In compiling the production accounts, the classification system in use is ÍSAT08, the Icelandic version of NACE rev.2. ÍSAT08 is a 5-digit classification which at the 4-digit level fully matches the 4-digit NACE rev.2 but the fifth digit adds the possibility of further breakdown if necessary. In the expenditure accounts, household final consumption uses the Classification of Individual Consumption According to Purpose (COICOP) system in a breakdown of three levels. General government consumption expenditure is classified according to purpose. The classification by purpose follows the Classification of the Functions of Government (COFOG) in a breakdown of 3 levels. Scope of the data Transaction coverage: All activities within the 2008 SNA/ESA 2010 production boundary are in principle covered Geographic coverage: The economic territory of Iceland. The economic area of Iceland encompasses the island of Iceland, including smaller surrounding islands, with the addition of Icelandic ships and aeroplanes in international traffic, Icelandic fishing boats fishing in international waters and Iceland's embassies. Conversely the embassies of foreign countries in Iceland are counted as foreign territory. Account coverage: Iceland's current system of national accounts have only been drawing on one of the three approaches accepted to evaluate gross domestic product for national accounts, the expenditure approach. The production approach and expenditure approach are used independently and the results from the production approach adjusted to fit the results of the expenditure approach. However, income breakdown of GDP is compiled by industries using the operating surplus as the residual in the final balancing. As in the Icelandic BOP, exports are valued at f.o.b. basis and total and detailed imports are available at both f.o.b. and c.i.f. bases 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: Transactions are in general recorded on an accrual basis. Exports and imports of goods are in practice recorded when the goods cross the border as registered through custom declarations. All data on the general government is recorded on accrual basis. Recoding period: The reference period of the figures in the annual national accounts is the calendar year and for the quarterly national accounts the quarter in which the economic activity occurs. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The data collection process occurs through several transmission protocols: Statistic's Iceland Online Data Collection Portal Database mirroring between institutions Cloud services Email The transmission protocols vary between source data and intra data, external sources and internal sources. A feature of the Icelandic national accounts is their reliance on administrative data. Most frequently these data are collected for purposes other than statistical, such as taxation. Direct inquiry entirely for national account's purposes hardly exist and processing of tax assessment data, both on a sample basis and by using the total population, is the most common procedure. Figures on quarterly GDP are only available following the expenditure approach. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Expenditure approach to quarterly GDP: In the quarterly accounts for the final consumption expenditure of households the most important indicators are the imports of consumer goods and the VAT turnover. Direct figures of the sale of agricultural products are available monthly. Foreign exchange statistics on travel exist on a quarterly basis. For government final consumption expenditure, the main sources are monthly government accounts data on a cash basis. For gross fixed capital formation, export and import statistics are the most important sources as well as the financial accounts of companies. Financial accounts of companies listed on the stock exchange are available on a quarterly basis, but more detailed data of almost total coverage is available for the calendar year from the tax authorities. Balance of payment statistics are available on a quarterly basis for calculating exports and imports of services and property income to and from abroad. The quarterly national accounts are based on a variety of sources that are either collected in the form of surveys, direct inquiries or are obtained from public records. These public records are as mentioned in 18.1. When available, the data is collected, quality tested and adapted into the process of data compilation. For example, data and information on credit card turnover, VAT turnover and imports of goods are used in assessing the development of household consumption expenditure and capital stock, data from Registers Iceland on construction progress is used in assessing the development of housing investment and data from the Financial Management Authority is used to assess government consumption expenditure and public investment. In all cases, the data collected is adapted to the definitions and conceptual framework of national accounts as well as being quality tested. Data on foreign trade is obtained from official statistics published by Statistics Iceland. The annual national accounts are largely based on aggregate quarterly measurements. Later, when the main data sources for the year are available, the annual figures can change and the quarter results are adjusted where appropriate. These data sources include sources such as the financial accounts of central and local governments, tax turnover and annual accounts of enterprises and government agencies. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness National account data should be made available to users as soon as possible. The ESA 2010 transmission programme defines the required timeliness for all national account statistics, where quarterly data should be made available 2-3 months after the end of the quarter. Statistics Iceland publishes data on each quarter 60 days after the end of the quarter. For official statistics, there are two quality dimensions that are of the utmost importance; timeliness and accuracy of results. It is common for official statistics, including nation accounts, for the results to be revised as it is often based on provisional data, due to the importance of timeliness. The reliability and accuracy of the results usually increases over time as newer and better data becomes available. Therefore, in the case of dissemination of preliminary results, users need to be informed and interpret preliminary results according to its nature. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Expenditure approach: The Icelandic quarterly national accounts estimates are based on non-seasonally adjusted source data. The quarterly estimates are compiled from the expenditure side. The compilation level is generally more aggregated on a quarterly basis than the annual one. However, in some cases like foreign trade statistics, the breakdown is the same both for quarterly and annual figures. Seasonal adjustment: Seasonal adjustments of national accounts time series seek to identify and remove seasonal fluctuations in data. The main aim of seasonal adjustments is to ease the comparability of data between years and adjacent quarters. Statistics Iceland uses indirect seasonal adjustment techniques. When using the indirect adjustment technique, the individual time series (private consumption, government consumption expenditure, etc.) are seasonally adjusted and linked together to obtain the seasonally adjusted main aggregates such as GDP or national income. When preliminary data is revised, changes will also affect the seasonally adjusted results. Geographical detail: Neither the annual nor the quarterly accounts contain any geographical breakdown. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results In addition to the quality and verification tests performed on the data received and used in the compilation of national accounts, data and results are validated through out and in all areas of the compilation process. Among other things, it is done to ensure internal consistency in the results and minimise the risk of errors. In some cases, data validation is done by using corrisponding time series (criterion variables) to capture possible deviations or signs of items that need further examination. The scope and depth of the data validation process is highly dependent on the time limits set for processing, which is narrower in terms of dissemination of quartely national accounts than annual ones.
