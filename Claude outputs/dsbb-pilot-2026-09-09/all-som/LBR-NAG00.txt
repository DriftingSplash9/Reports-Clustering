 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The National Accounts for Liberia are compiled and disseminated by Liberia Institute of Statistics and Geo-Information Services (LISGIS). The framework is based on the 1993 “System of National Accounts” (SNA). The LISGIS is in the process of adopting the 2008 “System of National Accounts” (SNA). The main data published during the 2008 GDP compilation are at current and constant 2008 prices by sector and by expenditure. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Economic activities of producer units are classified according to the third revision of the International Standard Industrial Classification of all Economic Activities (ISIC Rev 3) with national adaptations in the breakdown within agriculture. The major activities distinguished in the national accounts are as follow: agriculture, livestock production, forestry, logging, fishing, hunting, rubber production, coffee and cocoa production, major crops, especially rice the staple food and cassava, other agricultural production and agricultural services; mining and quarrying; manufacturing; electricity and water; construction; wholesale and retail trade/hotels and restaurants; transport, storage and information/ communication; finance and insurance activities, professional, scientific and technical acts, real estate and business services; community, social and personal services; producers of government services; government final consumption expenditures; and public administration and defense, education, health and social works activities, activities of household, other service activities, extraterritorial organizations. Imputed banks service charges are treated as intermediate consumption by a nominal industry. Scope of the data Transaction Coverage: Trained interviewers from the LISGIS, using well-designed questionnaires, collect information on all transaction categories from a complete census of all establishments employing twenty persons and above and selected units (establishments) on a sample basis. Geographic Coverage: The national accounts are estimated at national level (which include the rest of the Country). They are not produced at the sub-administrative division levels or by gender of ownership of establishments, etc. However, currently an attempt is being made to collect national accounts information in a disaggregated manner. Currently there are some data available for this purpose. Unit Coverage: The national accounts cover all units (establishments) residing in Liberia that employ at least 20 or more persons and/or has monthly sales over Liberian $10,000 per month and has its own accounting section or has hired an accounting firm to keep track of its financial activities. In some cases, few selected units (establishments) were not cooperative in completing the questionnaires on time. However, LISGIS makes frantic efforts to collect outstanding completed questionnaires. However, more comprehensive data are available from tax records which are used to compliment the data for GDP compilation. Estimates for informal and non-monetarized production are included, based on periodic benchmarks. The national accounts figures are available at the national level for the years, 2008-2015. Estimates figures were prepared by the IMF for the years, 1996-2000. Figures are now being prepared for each year, starting from the year 2000-2005 like the ones prepared by the IMF from 2008-2015. The main data published during the 2008 GDP compilation are at current and constant 2008 prices by sector and by expenditure. Expenditure on GDP includes a line for “Unrecorded Trade and Statistical discrepancy.” The data are published in millions of United States Dollars. All transactions in goods and services are recorded at market prices (US $). Output is recorded at producers’ prices, domestic expenditure at purchasers’ prices, while both imports and exports are valued free on board (fob). The recording periods refer to calendar years; the LISGIS (Liberia Institute of Statistics and Geo-Information Services) estimates the transactions of central government by July-June fiscal year. With a few exceptions, agricultural production is allocated to the harvest year. Unrecorded activity No proper estimates are made for illegal transactions such as illegal drugs, prostitution etc. 2.4.1 Valuation The prices used to value flows are purchasers’ and market while stocks values are based on producers’ and historical prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis In principle, all transactions are recorded on an accrual basis; however, the basic data for general government, which are on a cash basis, are not adjusted to an accrual basis. All transactions in goods and services are recorded at market prices (US$). Output is recorded at producers’ prices, domestic expenditures at purchasers’ prices, while both imports and exports are valued at free-on-board (fob). The recording periods refer to calendar year; the transactions of central government are based on the fiscal year, July-June, the government’s fiscal year or the fiscal year of the business establishment. Agricultural production is allocated to the harvest year. For the Budget, for example, the figures for 2003-04 are allocated to calendar year 2003. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The Ministry of Commerce and Industry maintains a Central Registration of Establishments (CRE) for the country, which has been constructed and updated each year from the annual business registry exercise. Additionally, the LISGIS compiles Directory of Establishments through an establishment census, with the first post-war conducted in 2007 while another round is currently ongoing in 2013. The LISGIS also collects periodically list of educational institutions from the Ministry of Education, health, from the Ministry of Health and Social Welfare, Bureau of Fisheries from the Ministry of Agriculture and forestry establishments from the Forestry Development Authority. However, the data collection processes may not be too effective as before due to logistical problems with some of these institutions. Agriculture, Forestry, Fishing, Hunting Crops: Volumes and prices on cash crops, fishing and hunting crops, livestock and main food crops are collected from the National Accounts Survey that is conducted by LISGIS annually and supplemented by annual statistical survey conducted by the Ministry of Agriculture; while data on forestry, including logging, firewood and charcoal are from National Accounts Survey that is conducted by LISGIS annually and also supplemented by data from the Forestry Development Authority. Mining and quarrying Data gathered from mining and quarrying are becoming more reliable from National Accounts Survey that is conducted by LISGIS annually due to viable economic condition and lifting of international sanctions. However, data for gold are supplemented by trade data. Value added is derived using ratios derived from the enterprises that respond to the annual enterprise survey. Manufacturing Data are available from the annual enterprise survey supplemented with information from tax records. Electricity and water supply The activity of the national electricity supply is covered; enterprises relying on their own generators are also covered. Water supply is also severely limited, but the activity of the state owned company is covered. Construction Data from formal enterprises are available. This is supplemented by information on huts from local authorities, that is the number constructed and average costs. Wholesale and retail trade; hotels and restaurants These activities are available from the business survey and tax records. Transport, storage and communication Formal businesses are available from the enterprise survey and tax records. Licensing data are used to provide estimates for informal transport. Finance and insurance Data for banks are supplied by the Central Bank of Liberia (CBL). Other activities are covered by the annual enterprise survey. Other private services These activities are estimated from the enterprise survey and tax records. Government services Data are available from the Budget in sufficient detail to derive the required figures. Taxes and subsidies Data on taxes are obtained from the Tax Records. The government does not currently provide any subsidies. Imports and exports Trade data are calculated in LISGIS by external trade section and the Bureau of Customs. Data for services are obtained from enterprise survey and CBL. Government final consumption These figures are obtained from the Budget. NGO final consumption Data are obtained from the NGOs via a special survey conducted by LISGIS. Capital formation These figures are obtained from the enterprise survey, Budget and tax records. Household final consumption This is derived by residual from total GDP measured from the production approach. Consequently, it includes the net errors and omissions from the other activities. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Production approach : Outputs at current prices are derived as discussed under section IV. Value added is usually not available directly, but its ratio to output is derived from periodic surveys of enterprises; the last of such survey was in 2012. Currently, value added including gross capital formation and other indicators are being derived from the establishment survey conducted in 2012. GDP at factor cost is being calculated as the sum of all value added estimates less Financial Intermediation Services Indirectly Measured (FISIM). A distinction may be made between monetary and non-monetary value added in the activities Agriculture, Construction, and Owner-occupied Dwellings. GDP at factor costs plus net taxes on products gives GDP at market prices. Constant price figures for agriculture, forestry and mining are based on available volume data. Government services are derived using the number of employees. All other activities are deflated by components of the CPI. Expenditure approach : Current price figures are derived as described in section IV. Constant price estimates are not currently produced. Reconciliation : The only possible reconciliation is to check that household final consumption, which derived by residual, is sensible. Revision and dissemination policy : For every year, two estimates are prepared. Provisional estimates are ready by the end of the year following the reference year. One year later, the final estimates are available. Overall revisions of the national accounts methodology are made annually. The Liberia Institute of Statistics and Geo-Information Services (LISGIS) publish the “National Accounts of Liberia” at the end of the year. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Data derived for National Accounts estimates (benchmarks) are validated through stakeholders consultation involving the Ministry of Finance, The IMF, World Bank, LISGIS and other relevant stakeholders. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Intermediate data are assessed by reviewing data sources with the direct involvement of key data compilers of administrative records, methodology, and outputs.
