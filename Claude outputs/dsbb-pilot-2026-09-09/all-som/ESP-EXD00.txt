 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Concepts and definitions of the External Statistics are compiled following the guidelines published in the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual and taking into account the requirements of the External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (2013 EDS Guide). The Guide defines gross external debt as follows: “Gross external debt, at any given time, is the outstanding amount of those actual current, and not contingent, liabilities that require payment(s) of principal and/or interest by the debtor at some point(s) in the future and that are owed to non-residents by residents of an economy”. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The External Debt is presented according to the classification of the External Debt Statistics Guide. External Debt data are classified into four sectors (General Government, Central Bank, Deposit Taking Corporations except the Central Bank, and Other Sectors). The classification used to compile Spain's International Investment Position and External Debt is essentially the one set forth in the sixht edition of the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6) and is fully consistent with the SNA2008 (System of National Accounts). The sector breakdown, functional categories, instrument and term classification are fully consistent with the BPM6. Scope of the data The data cover the entire Spanish territory. The data covers all debt liabilities of the Spanish residents to the rest of the world consistently with the International Investment Position. Exceptions to coverage As regards the direct reporting of the Other Residents Sector to the Banco de España, if the amounts of transactions during the immediately preceding year, or balances of assets and liabilities on December 31 of the previous year, are less than €1 million, the form is only to be sent at the express request of the Banco de España within a maximum period of two months from the date of request. The non-reported data according to this threshold are considered to be non-significant. Unrecorded activity There is no unrecorded activity. 2.4.1 Valuation Following BPM6 recommendations, the stocks are valued at market prices at the end of the reference period as far as they are available. Loans, deposits, trade credits and advances and other accounts payable/receivable are valued at nominal value (with the exception of Monetary and Financial Institutions where face value is used). Conversion to unit of account: in general terms, stocks are converted into euros at the average market exchange rate (the average of the buying and selling rates) of the closing quarterly date. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The sources of information are those used to compile the International Investment Position for debt liabilities. See metadata 3.1.1 for International Investment Position. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The definitions, scope and classifications used in the source data are broadly in line with the sixth edition of the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6). In some cases, adjustment procedures exist to ensure that the source data conform to the coverage, classification, valuation and timing conventions of the BPM6. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness In general, the source data are timely as regards the compilation of International Investment Position and External Debt statistics. According to the established threshold, some direct reporting declarations of the Other Resident Sectors are made on annual basis 2 months after the end of the reference year. Previous stocks are kept constant through quarters until yearly data are available, and then the variation is distributed linearly. The benchmark used for assets in deposits of Other Resident Sectors, mirror data from the Bank for International Settlement's statistics "Locational banking statistics by residence", are available not for the first publication of each quarter, but for the first revision. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The sources of information are those used to compile the Internation Investment Position for debt liabilities. See metadata 3.2.1 for International Investment Position. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Portfolio Investment: Stocks in negotiable securities are assigned to the related institutional sector on the basis of the resident issuer in the case of liabilities, and to the resident sector underwriting or buying the securities in the case of assets. The liabilities of debt instruments in portfolio investment begin to be estimated using the residual approach for all sectors, on a security by security basis since 2020 data. This estimate is obtained as the difference between the total issuance and the portfolios of resident investors. Data from Securities Issues Statistics - CSEC (issuance statistics with information from the ECB's centralized securities database, security by security, and including principal indexation in inflation-indexed issuances) and data on holdings, also security by security, of resident investors are used. Other Investment: For deposits abroad of the Other Residents Sector, the data of the direct reporting system has potential coverage problems and therefore the Bank for International Settlements' statistics "Locational banking statistics by residence", Balance Sheet Items (BSI) statistics (ECB statistics based on aggregated or consolidated balance sheet of the monetary financial institutions sector) and Form 720 of the Tax Agency is used. To estimate insurance and pension fund technical reserves hold by households, mirror data compiled by ECB from insurance corporations and pension funds under statistical reporting regulation is used. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs 1. Consistency with: Rest of the World sector in Financial Accounts; Balance Sheet Items statistics (ECB statistics based on aggregated or consolidated balance sheet of the monetary financial institutions sector) and Securities Holdings statistics (ECB Statistics collected on a security-by-security) 2. Several bilateral comparison exercises with mirror data of other countries (FDI European Network) ESCB and ESS Quality reports 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions are stored and assessed on an ongoing basis. Data are considered definitive in the fourth year after the reference year, according to the revisions calendar specified in 4.3.1. Monitoring of revisions is considered to be a normal part of the compilation process. Studies of the causes, magnitude and direction of revisions over a given period of time are part of the set of quality indicators that the compilers go through in every dissemination cycle. This kind of quality indicators is also included in the ECB's biannual quality report and Eurostat's annual quality report. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses A detailed analysis of differences between revised and preliminary data is only performed for internal use as part of every compilation/pre-dissemination cycle. Studies and analyses of the revision with explanation of the reasons underlying changes and of the impact on the data are disseminated only when major methodological changes occur, or when there is a break due to a new collection system. This is done in the monthly Balance of Payments press-release, or in the Balance of Payments/ International Investment Position quarterly press-release, and/or in the news section of External Statistics, the methodological note or a specific statistical note posted at the website in the External Sector section of ‘Statistics'. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Assistance on statistics compiled and/or disseminated by the Banco de España can be requested by users by means of the telephone (34 91 338 5651) or by a contact form ( https://app.bde.es/gnt_seg/en/contacto?a=NwbElZKVDdF0cMf6ebybP9BztpFPLW91 ).
