 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The economic transactions are grouped following the product classification defined by El Salvador National Accounts System (SCNES from its Spanish name: "Sistema de Cuentas Nacionales de El Salvador"), as it has done for the institutional sectors identification. The Economic activities are classified according to the International Standard Industrial Classification of All Economic Activities (ISIC Rev. 4); goods are classified according to the Central Product Classification (CPC Ver. 2). Locally, the National Statistics and Census Office (ONEC) made its own classification for economic activities and goods, for the former it has the Classification of Economic Activities of El Salvador (CLAEES) and the Classification of Products of El Salvador (CPES). At the same time, the National Accounts Department has developed an internal classification for the economic activities and goods: the first one, El Salvador Economic Activities Classification (NAES, from its Spanish name: "Nomenclatura de Actividades Económicas de El Salvador") and El Salvador Products Classification (NPES, from its Spanish name: "Nomenclatura de Productos de El Salvador") The quarterly GDP figures are published for economic activities division and not for goods. 2.2.1 Scope The economic transactions are grouped following the product classification defined by El Salvador National Accounts System (SCNES from its Spanish name: "Sistema de Cuentas Nacionales de El Salvador"), as it has done for the institutional sectors identification. Scope of the data · Data scope: The scope is, in general terms, consistent with international accepted norms, guidelines or practices. The data consider all El Salvador's economy and economic activities. · Transaction coverage: It includes all transactions of goods and services established on 2008 SNA as part of the production possibility frontier. It covers: - Annual estimates of Gross Domestic Product (GDP) by the production, the expenditure and the income approach, component of expenditure and production, at current prices and by chained volume. The base year is 2005 and the reference year 2014. - Quarterly GDP estimations by the production approach and component of expenditure at current prices and the chained volume indices. The base year is 2005 and the reference year is 2014. The quarterly GDP estimations follow the annual GDP methodology. There are 19 economic activities plus net taxes in the production approach; and by the expenditure approach the GDP is estimated by Final private and public consume; gross fixed capital formation, the exports and imports. The change in inventory are treated as a result. - The quarterly GDP is not estimated by the income approach. · Geography coverage: all the economic territory of the country. Exceptions to coverage 2.4.1 Valuation The flow and stocks valuation rule is the same as in SCN1993 and SCN2008. The production is valued at basic prices, the intermediate consumption is valued at purchasers’ prices, excluding the deductible value added tax basis, the GDP is valued at market prices. The household final consumption and the gross fixed capital formation expenditure are valued at purchasers’ prices, including the trade margins and excluding the deductible value added tax, mainly in the fixed capital formation. The exports are valued at FOB (Free on Board) prices and the imports are valued at CIF (Cost, Insurance and Freight) prices. The balance of payment data is collected as recommended by the IMF Balance of Payments Manual, 6th Edition. The BCR has adopted de IMF Balance of Payments Manual. 2.4.2 Recording basis The national accounts are registered on an accrual basis. The government data comes from El Salvador’s Government Accounting System, and it could be measured on accrual or cash basis. The rest of the world transactions are accounted when the goods come-in or get-out the economic territory as they are registered at customs. The period of accounting for the quarterly GDP is the calendar quarter. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The unit internal transactions are registered at gross value. The annual national accounts register taxes separated from subsidies, however the quarterly national accounts register net taxes. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs P r oduction approach: A. They are the process to validate the data. To choose the National Accounts base year (2005) was made a detailed analysis to evaluate all the available data such as economic censuses and surveys. They were used for offer and demand consistency. The product and activity classifications were adapted to fulfill international agreements on national accounts systems. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The BCR statistical reports are published on due time. The BCR is fully responsible of them. The monetary, foreign trade, balance of payment and financial system figures are published under the IMF Special Data Dissemination Standard (SDDS). Also, price indeces and the national accounts figures are published on SDDS. CPI and IPP are published on due time. Non-financial private sector data not always comes on time so the data is revised as the records are completed. The original data is periodically supervised by national accounts compilers to check out veracity of data. There is a forward publishing calendar of statistical reports. This forced the compilers to get data to estimate some figures for the national accounts statistical reports. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The data validation and consistency process includes a detailed analysis of administrative records, for the economic sectors estimated by these; to guarantee data consistency in terms of definition, validation and time of reference. The ONEC surveys data is revised and processed to be used for national accounts purposes. There are criteria to revise and consistency of data supported by related indicators. The enterprises are asked when some data looks abnormal or is missing. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The data compilation processes are based on criteria to validate data for guaranteeing data consistency through their codes and abnormal data identification to minimize processing errors. The economic activity output and product classification is made using National Accounts Nomenclature (NCN for is Spanish name: “Nomenclatura de Cuentas Nacionales”). It is derived from ISIC Rev. 4. The imputation procedure when data is missing is based on calculus over the sample 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The salvadoran economy is grouping in all ISIC Rev. 4 sections to estimate the quarterly output. The intermediate consumption estimation is only on annual basis and it is estimated for all the economic activities as described in the Salvadoran NCN. The using of fixed production structures is considered a limit for the current output estimations. There are innovations to estimate the GDP, they come from the SCN2008 recommendations, such as: - SIFMI ( from its spanish name " Servicios de Intermadiación Financiera Medidos Indirectamente" ) treatment - Work-in-progress - Central Bank output cost approaches The accrual basis methodology is applied to estimate the output for the economic activities, government income and expenditures, and taxes and subsidies on products. The GDP volume measures are estimated through the double indicator methods using Price indeces to deflate the current economic activity output, or to inflate the output volume indicator in the Quaterly Accounts. The value added tax volume measure comes from multiplying the tax rate by the deflated taxed flow value. The independent GDP components estimations make by the expenditure approach are: Household Final Consumption Expenditure (HFCE), Government Final Consumption Expenditure (GFCE), Gross Fixed Capital Formation (GFCF), Exports and Imports. And the Change in Stocks component is estimated as the rest. In general, the quarterly GDP growth comes from the estimation of Denton methodology of chained volume indeces (this process is called Benchmarking). This mixes the volume indices and the GDP annual estimations. The seasonal adjusted series are estimated using statistical methods to temporary adjust the series and taking off the series irregular components (X-13 ARIMA program). 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The data validation process is periodically. The data is compared with other economic indicators as they are available. The intermediate data is validated using many sources. For example, the agroindustrial output, the exported products and the main inputs are compared with foreign trade data or agricultural or industrial surveys. The main input data is validated using administrative records and surveys; fiscal data and independent sources such as producer associations, government agencies and business associations. The production chain prices are validated by producer and consumer price surveys, they are compile by different agents. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data There is a process to evaluate data statistical differences. It is called Consistency verification process, and here are made any needed adjust on data. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs If after the data is validated a discrepancy is detected, the needed adjusted is made. The change in stocks is estimated as the rest. This guarantees GDP is the same if it is estimated by output approach or by the expenditure approach. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses The annual series are revised in the next three years after the publishing year. They are classified as “estimation” in the first year of publishing. After, when more data is available, they are classified as “preliminary.” And when more data is gotten the series are classified as “definitive.” If new data is obtained after the series is classified as definitive with the Supply and Use Tables in the Annual Accounts, there is a note to explain why the series has changed. They are published in BCR Revista Trimestral (Quarterly magazine) and BCR website. The quarterly figures on the current year are estimated. Quarterly GDP figures are revised every quarter, and they are also adjusted when the annual figures are revised. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses The publishing norms says that any further revision of revised figures must publish a technical note to explain why the change occurs. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request When it is requested, the data could be delivered as non- published detailed tables if the data is not classified as confidential.
