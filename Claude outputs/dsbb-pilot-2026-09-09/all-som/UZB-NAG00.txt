 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The Statistics Agency generates GDP on an annual and quarterly basis, in accordance with the recommendations of the System of National Accounts (SNA) 2008. Gross domestic product (GDP) is one of the most important indicators of the System of National Accounts, characterizing the final result of the production activities of resident economic units, which is measured by the cost of goods and services produced by these units for final use. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The SNA of Uzbekistan identifies the following sectors of the national economy: non-financial corporations; financial corporations; general government; households; non-profit institutions serving households. The interrelations of sectors of the domestic economy with other countries are reflected in the "rest of the world" accounts, which unite all non-resident institutional units in the part in which they interact with residents of the national economy. When assessing GDP using the production method, the national classification on kinds of economic activities of the Republic of Uzbekistan is used as an industry classification. When assessing final consumption expenditures, international classifications are used as classification: "Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose" (COICOP), "Classification of Functions of Government" (COFOG), "Classification of the Purposes of Non-Profit Institutions Serving Households" (COPNI). 2.2.1 Scope The SNA of Uzbekistan identifies the following sectors of the national economy: non-financial corporations; financial corporations; general government; households; non-profit institutions serving households. The interrelations of sectors of the domestic economy with other countries are reflected in the "rest of the world" accounts, which unite all non-resident institutional units in the part in which they interact with residents of the national economy. When assessing GDP using the production method, the national classification on kinds of economic activities of the Republic of Uzbekistan is used as an industry classification. When assessing final consumption expenditures, international classifications are used as classification: "Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose" (COICOP), "Classification of Functions of Government" (COFOG), "Classification of the Purposes of Non-Profit Institutions Serving Households" (COPNI). Scope of the data The system of national accounts of Uzbekistan currently includes the following accounts, developed by type of activity and institutional sectors of the national economy: - the goods and services account; - the production account by type of economic activity, which reflects the formation of added value; - the generation of income account by type of economic activity to analyze the cost structure of GDP; - the allocation of primary income account; - the secondary distribution of income account to determine the total amount of income received as a result of production activities and redistribution processes; - the use of disposable income account, which reflects final consumption expenditures of households and government bodies; - the capital account, reflecting the formation of resources for capital expenditures and their use for accumulation. Currently, national accounts data cover all resident units of the economic territory of the Republic of Uzbekistan by type of economic activity. An assessment is made of statistical indicators for the informal sector of the economy, including the production of products for own final use, excluding indicators of the "shadow" economy and illegal activities. Exceptions to coverage There are no exceptions. Unrecorded activity An assessment is made of statistical indicators for the informal sector of the economy, excluding indicators of the "shadow" economy and illegal activities. 2.4.1 Valuation The valuation rules used to account for flows and stocks are broadly consistent with the 2008 SNA: the cost of market products is indicated in basic prices; output for own consumption is valued at equivalent market prices; imports are valued based on CIF prices and exports based on FOB prices; transactions in foreign currency are translated at the average market exchange rate at the time of their implementation; accounting of economic transactions in the SNA is carried out mainly on an accrual basis in prices at the time of the transaction (at current prices); non-market goods and services are valued using the market price for similar goods and services sold on the market, if it can be determined, or using production costs if a market price is not available. In particular, the services of government and non-profit institutions serving households are assessed based on production costs. 2.4.2 Recording basis Transactions and flows are accounted for on an accrual basis in accordance with the recommendations of the 2008 SNA. Data for the general government sector are recorded on a cash basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures GDP is calculated on a gross basis, that is, gross value added equals output minus intermediate consumption. Gross value added of industries is calculated on a gross basis, that is, including consumption of fixed capital. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Production Approach Sources of information for estimating GDP using the production approach are: short-term surveys of types of economic activity, a network of sample surveys of small enterprises, micro-firms and individual entrepreneurs, household questionnaire data, structural surveys of enterprises and organizations, as well as administrative data on the execution of the state budget, customs and tax authorities, financial indicators of extra-budgetary funds, the Central Bank and others. Quarterly, statistical data is generated in full on the basis of statistical reporting of large enterprises and on the basis of a sample survey of small enterprises and micro-firms. To obtain annual and quarterly estimates, the corresponding data from statistical observation forms and financial statements of enterprises are used. Expenditure Approach Household expenditures on final consumption are determined by groups of goods and services based on statistical reporting data on retail trade, statistics of market services provided to the population and household budget surveys. The value of goods produced for own consumption is recorded on the basis of current market prices for goods. The assessment of government expenditures is based on data from the report on the execution of the state budget. Final consumption expenditures of NPISHs include expenditures on consumer goods and services that they provide to households free of charge. This category also includes expenses of educational, health, and cultural institutions financed by enterprises and organizations to provide free services to their employees. To calculate this indicator, data obtained when calculating GDP using the production approach in the amount of costs for maintaining social and cultural facilities is used. The volume of goods and services provided by non-governmental non-profit organizations to households is determined on the basis of statistical observations of non-profit organizations. Indicators of gross accumulation and changes in inventories of material working capital are determined according to statistical observations. Volumes of exports and imports of goods and services are taken into account based on balance of payments data. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The source data is consistent with the definitions, statistical coverage and classifications of national accounts, and in terms of timing, reporting periods and valuation principles. In order to improve the calculation of national accounts, statistical observation forms are regularly revised. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Initial data are received in a timely manner, the deadlines for data submission are determined in the Statistical Program. 3.2.1 Source data assessment When compiling national accounts, the following data are used as initial information: data from statistical observations of legal entities (enterprises and organizations), results of regular surveys of the informal sector by economic sector, provided for by the Statistical Program, household questionnaires data, data on the execution of the state budget, administrative data from tax and customs authorities, balance of payments of the Central Bank and other administrative sources. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques GDP by Production Approach Calculation methods in constant prices. The calculation of GDP components in constant prices is carried out using two main methods: - deflating data for the reporting period in current prices with the corresponding price indices; - extrapolation of data in current prices for the corresponding period of last year by indices of physical volume or natural indicators that adequately reflect the dynamics of development of this type of economic activity. In some cases, the direct revaluation method is also used, the calculation of indicators in constant prices is carried out by multiplying the quantity of products produced in the reporting period by the prices of the corresponding period of the previous year. When calculating GDP components, the deflation method is predominantly used; in the absence of suitable price indices, the extrapolation method is used. Since the components of GDP are aggregates, the methods for estimating them are a combination of the above methods. GDP by Expenditure Approach Calculation methods in constant prices. Calculation of components of final consumption expenditures in constant prices is carried out using two main methods: - deflating data for the reporting period at current prices with the corresponding consumer price indices for goods and services in accordance with COICOP; - extrapolation of data in current prices for the corresponding period of last year by indices of physical volume or natural indicators that adequately reflect the dynamics of development of this type of economic activity (when calculating the expenses of government and NPISHs). Since the components of final consumption expenditure are aggregates, the methods for estimating them are a combination of the above methods. To recalculate the indicators of the use of GDP in constant prices, the following are applied: - consumer price index – for household final consumption expenditures; - natural indicators, including changes in the number of employed industries - for expenditures of government agencies; - price index for investments in fixed capital – for the accumulation of fixed capital; - producer price indices in industry - for changes in inventories; - price indices for export and import products - for export and import of goods; - consumer price indices for services – for exports of services; - weighted average consumer price indices for services of the relevant partner countries - for imports of services. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The annual and quarterly national accounts do not measure illicit economic activities. Informal activity is assessed using data from special surveys, administrative data and the Household Budget Survey. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Individual data sources are reviewed to check for outliers. If outliers are identified, compilers contact the appropriate departments to reconcile the data. Source data is analyzed and checked for dynamic consistency. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Possible discrepancies in the underlying interim data are analyzed and adjusted to resolve the discrepancies. In particular, the following coefficients are carefully checked: intermediate consumption/output, gross value added/output. In addition, the structure of value added by type of activity is also checked for chronological consistency. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Existing statistical discrepancies between GDP calculated by the production approach and GDP by the expenditure (final use) approach are analyzed. Supply and use tables of goods and services are compiled and analyzed annually. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses The schedule for revising GDP indicators is reflected in the annually approved Statistical Program. Annual (preliminary) GDP estimates are presented one month after the end of the reporting year. These GDP estimates are updated on the basis of annual statistical reporting 15 months following the reporting year. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Revised (final) data are disseminated through Statistics Agency publications and on the official website. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request According to the Law of the Republic of Uzbekistan "On Official Statistics", the Regulations for organizing work on the preparation and dissemination of statistical information and other regulatory documents, statistical data is presented to users upon their request in the prescribed manner.
