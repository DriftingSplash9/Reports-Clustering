I. Analytical Framework, Concepts, Definitions, and Classifications
 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: The compilation of the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) estimates is based on the recommendations set out in the System of National Accounts (2008) and Quarterly National Accounts Manual of the International Monetary Fund. 
 
 Definition: GDP is a measure of the total value of goods and services produced by an economy during an accounting period (usually one year or one quarter). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization For the expenditure-based GDP, classification of goods and services for household final consumption expenditure is in accordance with the Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP). International merchandise trade is classified according to the Nomenclature for the External Trade of Macao/Harmonized System, 6 th Revision . International trade in services is broadly based on the classifications recommended in the Balance of Payments Manual, 6 th Edition . 
 
 For the production-based GDP, all the economic activities are classified according to the Classification of Economic Activities of Macao (CAM, Rev. 1) , which is an adaptation of the International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC, Rev. 3) . 

II. Scope of the data
 2.2.1 Scope For the expenditure-based GDP, classification of goods and services for household final consumption expenditure is in accordance with the Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP). International merchandise trade is classified according to the Nomenclature for the External Trade of Macao/Harmonized System, 6 th Revision . International trade in services is broadly based on the classifications recommended in the Balance of Payments Manual, 6 th Edition . 
 
 For the production-based GDP, all the economic activities are classified according to the Classification of Economic Activities of Macao (CAM, Rev. 1) , which is an adaptation of the International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC, Rev. 3) . Scope of the data Cover resident non-financial corporations, financial corporations, government units, households and non-profit institutions serving households, as stipulated in the SNA 2008. 
 
 Cover (as far as possible) all activities within the production boundary outlined in the SNA 2008. 
 
 Cover the entire territory under the administration of the Government of the Macao Special Administrative Region. Unrecorded activity Certain unrecorded activities (for example, unreported merchandise imports and exports) are included in the GDP estimates by adjustments or imputations to the existing components/activities. 

III. Accounting Conventions
 2.4.1 Valuation Transactions of the expenditure-based GDP are generally valued at purchasers’ prices. For the production-based GDP, value added is measured at producers’ prices to provide a more accurate measure of the industry’s contribution to the economy (gross output at producers’ prices and intermediate consumption at purchasers’ prices); owner-occupied housing services are valued at comparable rental rates, and non-market services are valued at cost. Value added at basic prices is also compiled and made available to users as reference. 2.4.2 Recording basis Generally, transactions are recorded on an accrual basis, while the cash basis is used for government expenditure. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Transactions between establishments within the same enterprise are recorded on a gross basis. 

IV. Nature of the Basic Data Sources
 3.1.1 Source data collection programs For the expenditure-based GDP, primary sources of data and their respective collection programs for each component are as follows: 
 
 Private consumption expenditure: Information from the Household Budget Survey (HBS) conducted every 5 years is the main source of data, which is supplemented with latest figures from demographic statistics, Consumer Price Indexes (CPI), retail sales, employment, tourism, external merchandise trade, results from other DSEC surveys, as well as administrative records; 
 
 Government final consumption expenditure: Government accounts and other administrative records; 
 
 Gross fixed capital formation: Sources of data include public investment expenditure in government accounts and other administrative data; annual and quarterly surveys, mainly on construction activities of the private sector; data collected directly from companies undertaking large-scale construction projects; and the external merchandise trade statistics; 
 
 Changes in inventories: Data from annual economic activity surveys; 
 
 Exports and imports of goods: External merchandise trade statistics are the main source of data that is supplemented with results of annual economic activity surveys; 
 
 Exports and imports of services: Sources of data include gaming receipts figures of the Gaming Inspection and Coordination Bureau; hotel indicators from Hotel and Similar Establishment Survey, Visitor Expenditure Survey and other tourism indicators. In addition, results of the various annual economic activity surveys; administrative records; quarterly data from major companies; and data on the financial sector from the Monetary Authority of Macao (AMCM). 
 
 For the production-based GDP, main sources of data include results of annual economic activity surveys, household surveys, quarterly and the annual Survey on Interest Income/Expense of Financial Sector conducted by the AMCM, administrative records from government departments and private organizations. Financial Intermediation Services Indirectly Measured (FISIM) is compiled and distributed to different economic activities by the Reference Interest Rate method in the major GDP revision carried out since 2010. DSEC has a register of establishments (the Business Register), which is updated regularly with latest information from administrative records. The Register serves as the sampling frame on which surveys of the formal sector are based. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording A comprehensive range of monthly, quarterly and annual surveys covering the principal sectors of the economy has been undertaken regularly. In most cases, sampling is applied and the large establishments are included. Questionnaires are designed to lessen the reporting burden of respondents, as well as taking care of the basic data requirements of the national accounts. The definitions, classifications, valuation and time of recording of the different questionnaires are in line with the recommendations of SNA 2008. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness For the expenditure-based GDP, source data are usually available within 40 days after the reference quarter. However, the estimates of the 4th quarter, which are published together with the annual estimates, are available within 70 days after the end of the reference year. 
 
 For the production-based GDP, results of the annual surveys are available in separate periods, with classification of activity details ranging from two- to five-digit level of CAM Rev.1. To accommodate the tax reporting period of the establishments and enterprises from June to August, survey results are available within 10 months after the reference year for use in the national accounts. The majority of the results are conformed to national accounts requirements and used directly, some require adjustments before use. 3.2.1 Source data assessment In general, source data are subject to internal assessment and checked for inconsistency before being incorporated. Besides, survey results will be cross checked with each other to identify reporting errors. 

V. Compilation Practices
 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Sources of data for the expenditure-based and production-based GDP estimates are originated from survey results, administrative records and data collected from the private sector. Reconciliation is made when necessary. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures For the expenditure-based GDP, estimates are carried out separately for private consumption expenditure, government final consumption expenditure, gross fixed capital formation, changes in inventories, exports and imports. Value added by industrial activity is calculated as gross output less intermediate consumption, basically compiled at the 2-digit level of the CAM Rev.1 , with detailed breakdown of pillar industries, such as those related to the gaming and tourism sector. 
 
 a) The expenditure-based GDP estimates (quarterly and annual) are compiled at current prices and in chain volume measures. 
 
 a.1) Current price estimates: 
 
 Private consumption expenditure: sum of household final consumption expenditure (in the domestic market and abroad) and the final consumption expenditure of non-profit institutions serving households (NPISHs): 
 Household final consumption expenditure in the domestic market is estimated from the results of the latest Household Budget Survey (HBS), i.e. the 2012/2013 HBS , other surveys and administrative records. Household final consumption expenditure abroad is mainly based on the data from the HBS and the Survey on Expenditure of Overseas Government Offices, departures and arrivals of Macao residents, outbound travel of Macao residents and other relevant indicators, as well as survey results of financial sector conducted by the AMCM in estimation of FISIM. Final consumption expenditure of NPISHs is estimated from the amount of Government transfer to those institutions. 
 Government final consumption expenditure: sum of compensation of government employees and the purchases of goods and services by the Government, less the sale receipts from providing goods and services. 
 
 Gross fixed capital formation: sum of investment in construction projects, costs of ownership transfer, real estate developers’ margin, and investment in machinery and equipment, transport equipment and other fixed assets. 
 
 Investment in construction projects encompasses value of construction of new buildings. Construction by private sector is estimated according to the total floor area built and the construction costs by type of buildings. Construction by public sector is based on administrative data. Other types of construction include investment of major construction projects collected directly from enterprises, and estimates on renovations, refurbishment and maintenance according to survey results. 
 
 Costs of ownership transfer refer to the charges and fees paid to the private sector and to the Government for transfer of ownership of fixed assets and non-produced assets, such as real estate agents’ commissions, lawyers’ fees, charges paid to real estate developers and stamp duties for transfer of properties, etc., which are mainly based on the administrative information about the transaction of properties and data from government accounts, etc. 
 
 Real estate developers’ margin is the difference made by real estate developers through acquisition of land, arrangement of construction of buildings, financing of project development and marketing of new property, which equals market value of property less cost of land and developing cost of the real estate project, excluding profits or losses realized from resale of existing property. Real estate developers’ margin covers difference related to the construction of property within the reference period, irrespective of whether these buildings have been fully or partly sold, awaiting sale, or have been sold before the reference period. 
 Investment in machinery and equipment, and transport equipment is estimated from respective import and export data, survey results and administrative records, less the amount related to visitors’ spending and households. Investment in other fixed assets include cultivated assets (greyhounds and horses used for racing purposes) and intangible fixed assets (computer software, etc.) that are estimated according to information collected from private enterprises and external merchandise data. 
 Changes in inventories: Estimated from relevant data of various annual economic activity surveys. Indicators are applied for quarterly estimates. 
 
 Exports and imports of goods: Estimated from the external merchandise trade valued f.o.b. Imports and exports of goods are adjusted with an equilibrium analysis of Supply & Use to capture those not being recorded or declared properly . In addition, fuels purchased in ports outside Macao are also counted as imports of goods. 
 
 Exports and imports of services: Expenditure of inbound visitors is estimated based on the gaming receipts and other relevant tourism data on visitors’ spending. For household final consumption expenditure abroad, please refer to “private consumption expenditure”. Services other than travel are estimated based on data on imports and exports of services collected from various sources. Indicators are also applied for the quarterly estimates. 
 
 a.2) Chain volume measures: 
 
 Volume estimate of each GDP component is compiled using the price weights of preceding year, which is done by deflating the current price value of the sub-components at the most detailed levels by the relevant preceding-year-based price indices; volume estimates of these sub-components are aggregated to the component levels, which are then added up to obtain the volume estimate of GDP valued at preceding year prices. 
 
 The annually re-weighted volume measures of all years are chain linked together to obtain a continuous time series of the chain volume indices of GDP and its major components. 
 
 a.3) Reference year and update cycle: 
 
 Usually, a more recent year is chosen as the reference year and updated forward every year. The retrospective time series data are available from 1982. 
 
 b) The annual production-based GDP is also available at current prices and in chain volume measures. 
 
 b.1) Current price estimates: 
 
 Production-based GDP refers to the sum of the gross value added of all economic activities in Macao, plus relevant taxes. Gross value added is the difference between gross output and intermediate consumption that is estimated mainly from the results of the various annual economic activity surveys. Adjustments on informal economic activities are made according to the relevant indicators. Domestic services are derived from the number of employees in households and an estimate of average pay. An imputed figure on rentals for owners of premises in their individual capacity is added to the national income calculations to reflect the value of the services rendered. Such rentals are estimated according to the area and average market rent of the dwellings. 
 
 b.2) Chain volume measures 
 
 Chain volume measures are released for the value added of broad economic activities, but not for the single production-based GDP estimate to avoid confusion. In general, double-deflation method is applied to the industry, where output and intermediate consumption are deflated independently with appropriate preceding-year-based price indices, and the difference between the two is obtained as the volume measure of the industry’s value added. 
 
 The value-added volume measures of all years are chain linked together in a similar manner as those of the expenditure-based estimates to obtain a continuous time series. 
 
 b.3) Reference year and update cycle: 
 
 To ensure consistency with the expenditure approach, the same reference year is chosen for the industries’ chain volume measures. The retrospective time series data are available from 2008. 
 
 b.4) At producers’ prices 
 
 The published value added of each economic activity is valued at producers’ prices, as in Macao, some product taxes can be considered as the outcome of the economic activities, which are levied as deductions from the gross receipts or sales of the industries, such as the concession taxes collected from gaming, water supply, generation of electricity, telecommunications, as well as management of public parking. On the other hand, GDP at basic prices are also compiled and provided as reference to users. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data There is no procedure in place for regularly assessing and investigating the behavior of intermediate data against related series. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs As the sources of data, scope of coverage and estimation methods are different between the expenditure-based and production-based GDP, minor discrepancies do exist. The expenditure approach is considered as the principal measure of the GDP of Macao; therefore, the discrepancy in current price estimates, which is generally within the 5% range, is usually adjusted to the estimate of the production-based GDP, whereas for the chain volume measure, the single GDP estimate figure is published for the expenditure approach only.

Information on the latest GDP revision is available in Major Revision of Gross Domestic Product, 1982-2014 , which can also be downloaded at website. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Subject to feasibility and quality assessment of the data, dissemination of special statistical outputs is available upon request.
