 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Definition: The external trade in goods cover Iceland's trade (import of goods and exports of goods crossing the border) with the rest of the world. In general, SI follows the guidelines in the "United Nations: International Merchandise Trade Statistics 2010, Concepts and Definitions" as regards what to include in external trade statistics, how and when. The statistics extend to merchandise trade, and by a general definition any imports or exports which add to or subtract from the stock of material resources of a country should be included in external trade statistics. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: The basic classification and the most detailed one is the Icelandic Customs Tariff, which is an eight-digit classification that complies with the six digits of the HS classification, with the addition of two digits that are used in some instances for more detailed breakdown according to Icelandic requirements. Data is also published by SITC, Rev. 4 and Broad Economic Categories (BEC) as well as by some Icelandic classifications. Statistics Iceland defines countries according to ISO-3166 alphabetic code. Scope of the data Transactions coverage: In Iceland, the special trade system is employed from mid year 1998, excluding goods imported to customs bounded warehouse or free zones until they are imported into the country. Transactions included both in aggregates and at detailed levels include non-monetary gold, oil, fuels purchased abroad by national vessels and aircraft, unissued banknotes and securities, goods for processing, returned goods, goods under financial lease (one year or more), goods with high value of service content (computer software, etc.), ships and aircraft, fish landed abroad from Icelandic vessels, parcel post, transactions below threshold value (it is estimated that minor trade is excluded as the threshold is very low, ISK 500) and major improvements. Geographic coverage: Trade with all countries are included, countries defined according to ISO-3166. Exceptions to coverage Transactions that are not included are goods in transit, goods on temporary admission, illegal trade, goods consigned by governments to its embassies and diplomatic representatives abroad and goods supplied to foreign embassies and diplomatic representatives stationed in Iceland, monetary gold, goods on operational lease, samples of small value, aid, goods in export processing zones, migrants' personal effects and entrepot trade. Unrecorded activity No estimates are made for under reported or missing trade such as smuggling activity or shuttle trade. Unrecorded activity No estimates are made for under reported or missing trade such as smuggling activity or shuttle trade. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: The value of an imported item of good is presented at cif and fob value while exports are presented at fob value only. If the price is not determined at the time of departure, an estimated selling price is used. 2.4.2 Recording basis Conversion to unit of account: Figures on the value of exports and imports are reached by converting the foreign currency value of the commodity to Icelandic krónur (ISK) based on the weekly midrate of the currency concerned. The reference rate of exchange is a so-called customs exchange rate, which is the official exchange rate as registered by the CBI every Monday. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The information is primarily based on customs declarations for imports and exports, filled in by importers and exporters, which are extracted from the Customs Authorities database. Information on registration of ships and aircraft is based on sources obtained from the Icelandic Transport Authorities collected monthly. SI also surveys monthly trading of ships and aircrafts and purchases of fuels abroad by national ships and vessels. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Sources: The customs declarations for imports and exports are registered when the import/export is cleared by the Customs. Information of registration of ships and aircraft is based on information from the owner and/or the operator of the ship or aircraft. The surveys trading of ships and aircrafts and purchases of fuels of national ships and aircraft is directed to the seller and buyer of ships and aircrafts and the buyer of fuels abroad. Type of data collected: Data collected in the customs declaration include eight digits customs tariff numbers, values, quantities, unit values, currency, exchange rate and country of origin/destination as well as some other information used for data checks or further describe the data (for example dates, id-numbers, customs duty exemption codes etc). Survey data includes date of transaction, amount and weight. Registration data includs new registration and changes in registration. Basis of country data: Data are published according to country of origin for imports and country of destination for exports. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Timing of recording transactions: In the data from the Customs the goods are recorded when they are cleared by the Customs. Data from other sources are recorded by the time of the transaction. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Adjustments to improve coverage/classification/timing/valuation: The data undergoes various quality controls during each month processing, for example testing the value, weight, the classification and the country code compared to historical data. There are also ongoing projects with the Customs Authorites and the Ministry of Finance to improve coverage, classification and timeliness of the data. Processing system: SI uses national system Vakinn (system for importing data, testing, correcting and publishing results). Site of processing: Data are processed at the statistical office, SI. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Seasonal adjustment: Data on external trade are not seasonally adjusted. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Reconciliation: Comparison with Customs VAT data each month.
