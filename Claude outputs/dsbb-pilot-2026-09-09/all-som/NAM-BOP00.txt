 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The balance of payments for Namibia is a statistical statement summarizing the economic transactions of residents of Namibia with the rest of the world. The Bank of Namibia (BoN) is responsible for the compilation of the balance of payments statistics. The legal basis for compilation of BOP is the BoN Act no.1 of 2020. The balance of payments presentation and methodology are largely in conformity with the sixth edition of the IMF Balance of Payments Manual (BPM 6 ) . 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The balance of payment statistical framework closely follows that of BPM 6 . The collected data are aggregated into two main accounts: the current account, and the financial account; the data for the International Investment Position (IIP) is also derived from the BOP surveyed data. Current account Includes goods (merchandise), services (transportation, travel, insurance and other business), compensation of employees , investment income (direct investment, portfolio investment and other investment), current transfers (government and private) and capital account ( consists mainly of transfers of aid (to general government), which is linked to projects of capital in nature, and migrant's transfers). Financial account The financial account is divided into five main items, namely, transactions in direct investment in Namibia and abroad, portfolio investment, Financial Derivatives (Other than Reserves) and Employee Stock Options, other investment (short term and long term), and reserves assets. The reserve assets section includes SDRS, reserve position of the Government in the Fund and Foreign exchange. Scope of the data The Bank of Namibia (BoN) is responsible for the compilation and dissemination of balance of payments statistics. The transactions recorded in the balance of payment statistics are those of Namibian residents with non-residents. The sources of the data are surveys of enterprises, administrative records, government departments, parastatals and ITRS. In principle, all the major resident units of the reporting economy that have transactions with the rest of the world are included in the BOP. Data on travel, government services and some categories under current transfers are currently compiled using BOPCUS. In addition, data on imports are sourced from Namibia Statistics Agency (NSA) and reconciliation is done on the data to ensure correct adjustments and coverage to comply with BOP requirements and the Standards of National Accounts (SNA). The exports data are also obtained from NSA, with some of the data obtained from the surveys. Exceptions to coverage Smuggling activities especially those for diamonds and other precious items are also not included in the coverage. Data on OFC is still on ongoing especially the reconciliation between BOP and MFS particularly data by pension fund and financial derivatives. While portfolio investment of non-residents in the Namibian economy is estimated to be low, there is currently no data on that. Data on other capital between related financial corporations, mostly banks are excluded from foreign direct investment and included in other investment. Unrecorded activity Data on informal trade are currently covered as estimates in the trade data. In the capital account, data on debt forgiveness for both the government and other sectors are captured. In the Financial account, estimation of foreign currency, especially the Rand, which might be in the hands of residents, also needs to be estimated and included in the data. 2.4.1 Valuation The data on balance of payments are mainly collected in Namibian Dollars. However, in cases where data are reported in foreign currencies, these are converted to Namibia Dollars by using average exchange rates of the concerned months in that quarter or year. Exports and imports are valued at Free on Board (FOB) with other transactions valued at market prices. Three methods of valuation are employed for the valuation of flows in the financial account, i.e. market value, net asset value, and company director's evaluation. 2.4.2 Recording basis This follows the guidelines of BPM 6 : Goods are recorded at the time of changing ownership (accrual basis). Dividends are recorded when declared Services are recorded at the time the service is rendered. Arrears and financial transactions are recorded when transactions become due, but not when paid. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Data is recorded on a gross basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The data sources for the different components of the balance of payments are listed below. Goods Imports Cost, insurance and Freight (CIF) data are obtained from the Namibia Statistics Agency (NSA), which NSA receives from the Namibia Revenue Authority (NamRA). Export data are also mainly sourced from NSA coupled with enterprise surveys and administrative records. Import figures are captured CIF and then adjusted to FOB with adjustments of 7.6 percent for freight and 0.85 percent for insurance for those goods that are sourced from countries that do not share a border with Namibia. Services: Travel This data are currently sourced from ITRS. Transportation This data are currently sourced from ITRS and survey data. Other services (communication, insurance, royalties, financial, computer, professional, technical amp; government) are based on administration records and surveys. Income: The main source for direct investment income, portfolio investment income and other investment income is the enterprise survey of foreign owned companies, the depository corporations survey, and administrative records of the Bank of Namibia and Ministry of Finance and Public Enterprises. Current Transfers Data on current transfers are mainly obtained from the surveys on, non-governmental organizations (NGOs) and government administrative records. The ITRS is also a good source for a breakdown of grants for both government and NGOs. Capital Account: Main source of data for capital transfers are government administrative records and surveys of NGOs. The ITRS is also a good source for an analysis of capital transfers for both government and NGOs. Financial Account Direct investment in Namibia and abroad are obtained from surveys on foreign direct investment entities. The survey collects flows and levels of foreign assets and liabilities at the beginning and end of each quarter, with separate information for equity, retained earnings and other capital. Portfolio investment into and by the private sector is partly collected through surveys of fund managers, while the other part is collected from the BON surveys and government administrative records. Financial Derivatives (Other than Reserves) and Employee Stock Options are sourced from MFS data. Other investment information is collected through the BON surveys of enterprises, Bank of International Settlement and government administrative records. Reserve assets are obtained from the Bank of Namibia's administrative records. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The BOP data are broadly in line with the scope, classifications, valuation and time of recording required as per international practices. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The BOP data is collected and compiled on a quarterly basis. The quarterly survey are send out two weeks after the end of each of quarter and expected to be received about four weeks after the end of the quarter. The data is normally received in a timely manner from the reporting agents with a reasonable response rate achieved which makes it easier to compile the BOP. Namibia currently meets the GDDS requirements and is in the process of migrating to SDDS. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Sample surveys are assessed over time (every 10 years ). However, new companies entering the market are added to the sample through the media or through research and meetings with stakeholders. Dummy surveys for the non-responded companies based on their previous submitted data are in place and compiled with the rest of the received data, while follow up for actual data continues and once received, replacing the previously estimated data. The bank of Namibia continues to engage sources of administrative data, especially the Customs office and NSA. The three institutions conduct annual workshops with clearing agents and customs officers to explain the importance of accurate recording of data. The BOP section also regularly engages the Exchange Control department to improve the misclassification of data in the ITRS 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Goods: The data obtained from the survey are crosschecked with the data on economic activities collected by the real sector section within the Research and Financial Sector Development department. For example, data on mining, fishing and agriculture are always crosschecked among BoP, MFS and Real sector for consistency. The import figures are captured CIF and then adjusted to FOB. The complete data sets for merchandise statistics are obtained from NSA. Services: Data is largely sourced from the from the ITRS as well as quarterly surveys. The data on freight and insurance debit is estimated based on merchandise imports. Current Transfers, and Capital Transfers: Administrative records of government and NGO's surveys data are the main source for the data. However, data are grossed up for non-response. Financial Account: For consistency, all items in the Financial account are edited and checked against the previous year's data, fiscal accounts, MFS, and other sectors' statistical series for direct investment, portfolio investment and other investment. This is done by running reports from the IERS to crosscheck balances of the previous periods with recently submitted data. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Data on fiscal year are converted to calendar year. No seasonal adjustments are made. Stock of international reserves are adjusted for capital gains and losses. The reserve data used to calculate imports cover are currently inclusive of valuation changes. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Constant verifications are made to confirm consistency and correctness of data among various data. Also, the Bank of Namibia has a MOU with NSA in place on data issues, while the quarterly meetings to discuss data are also attended by the Ministry of Finance and Public Enterprises to understand each other's data and the consistency of such data across the three institutions. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Assessment and follow up are constantly made to confirm consistency and correctness of data among various data producing agencies and a lot of issues has been picked up and corrected through this process. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Discrepancies in various data are being picked up and steps are taken to rectify if necessary. Discrepancies in trade data are the major example, and necessary efforts are put in place to do away with such discrepancies by reviewing the source data and methodology used to compile the data as well as adjustments made to the data. In addition, the quarterly reconciliation meetings between the section and staff at the NSA also aid in rectifying the variance as per the MOU. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions to the released data are only done on a quarterly basis through the Quarterly Bulletin for the data released during the previous quarter and footnotes are also used to indicate data that have changed from the previous release. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Analyses of the revised data are disseminated in each Quarterly Bulletin. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request None
