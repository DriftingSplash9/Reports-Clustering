 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The conceptual framework for the national accounts is set out in the United Nations’ System of National Accounts 2008 (SNA2008). The National Institute of Statistics of Rwanda (NISR) publishes annual estimates of the GDP and its components, with desegregation by activity (30 activities branch of ISIC revision 4) and by expenditure. Base year estimates are made using supply and use tables. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Activities are classified throughout according to the single letters of Revision 4 of the International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC Rev4). 2.2.1 Scope Activities are classified throughout according to the single letters of Revision 4 of the International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC Rev4). Scope of the data Transaction Coverage: Goods and services and associated transactions Geographic Coverage: National geographic territory. Unit Coverage: Estimates cover all institutional residents (financial and non-financial enterprises, government, NPISH and households). The National Institute of Statistics of Rwanda (NISR) publishes every year, in March, the GDP estimates of the previous year on two approaches: production and expenditure approaches. Quarterly estimates are also published 90 days after the Quarter in question is over. GDP is estimated at current and constant price of 2014 the benchmark year The reference period is from January to December of the 2014 year (calendar year). All economic activities at national level are covered (enterprises, public administration, informal sector, households, banks and insurance). Unrecorded activity Forbidden activities are not taken in account. Informal activities and production for own-consumption are taken in account. 2.4.1 Valuation Imports are recorded at c.i.f. price (i.e. cost, insurance and freight price). It is the price of a good delivered at the frontier of the importing country, including any insurance and freight charges incurred to that point, or the price of a service delivered to a resident, before the payment of any import duties or other taxes on imports or trade and transport margins within the country. Exports are recorded at f.o.b. price (free on board price). It is the c.i.f. price less the costs of transportation and insurance charges, between the customs frontier of the exporting (importing) country and that of the importing (exporting) country. The period of registration is the calendar year (of January 1st to December 31). 2.4.2 Recording basis Most operations are recorded on the basis of accounting of rights and obligations. The purchases and the returns of the public administrations are recorded on the basis of the roadbeds and organization. Production and local supplies are recorded at basic prices. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The level of GDP is established in Rwanda using a Supply and Use Table, currently for 2014 (the fourth in Rwanda). Each SUT has been associated with the corresponding Enquête Intégrée des Conditions de Vie (EICV) completed in the years 2001, 2006, 2011 and 2014. SUTs for 2006 and 2011 have formed the basis for the annual series of estimates of GDP and its components between 2001 and 2006 and for the annual and quarterly series since 2006. However, 2006 SUT comprised only of summary version, and excluded the intermediate demand components. The most recent SUTs (2011 and 2014) includes the intermediate consumption matrix. They also incorporates several new sources of data not previously available, notably the Integrated Business Enterprise Survey (IBES) and detailed data from Ministry of Finance’s Financial Management Database. For Rwanda, the most significant change in the SNA2008 is the use of the latest version, Revision 4, of the International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC Rev.4). While there are a number of changes at the detailed level of ISIC, at the level of the published categories the main difference is the introduction of a new category for “Information and Communication Services”. This category includes publishing (previously in Manufacturing), film making and broadcasting; telecommunications (previously with Transport) and IT services (previously part of Business Services). Another change is in the treatment of “Imputed bank service charges” nowadays known as Financial Intermediation Services Indirectly Measured (FISIM). Previously these services (essentially the difference between the interest received by banks and the interest paid) were deducted as a global adjustment from the total value added of producers (interest charges being excluded from intermediate consumption). Now however FISIM has been allocated between users of bank services and the need for an overall adjustment has gone. All these changes were incorporated since SUT 2011. In SUT 2011 a number of further methodological improvements have been introduced to bring the estimates closer to full compliance and they were also considered in SUT 2014. These include: Estimates of informal cross-border trade. Revised CIF/FOB adjustments to the Customs value of imports. First estimates for the final consumption expenditure of NGOs. The development of new benchmark estimates for the construction sector. Better estimates for the output of the livestock sector including increases in stocks (capital formation). Fixed capital formation in banana, tea and coffee plantations and in IT systems. Improved analysis of government expenditure. Subsidies to public sector enterprises introduced since 2006. Estimates of crop production from the Seasonal Agricultural Survey, Business income tax data to supplement VAT data. Complete information on the activities of the Telecommunications sector The new rebased series, growth in GDP has been kept unchanged for years up to and including 2012 (because the previous weights, for 2006 and 2011, are more relevant over that period). For 2013 and subsequent years, the new weights for 2014 have been applied to the detailed estimates of growth to produce the revised GDP series, in 2014 constant prices. From some categories, the switch between ISIC Rev.3 and ISIC Rev.4 Improved sources and methods There are three possible methods of extrapolating the 2014 benchmark estimates of total output using indicators. These are Indicators based on both values and quantities (volumes) of production Indicators based on values of production and price indicators for deflating these Indicators (or proxy indicators) of quantities and price indicators for inflating these to estimate the current values Value added (GDP by activity) is derived by applying input-output ratios to the resulting estimates of output. In the absence of more up-to-date information the input-output ratios remain fixed. The sources and methods currently used in compiling the quarterly indicators of total output are summarised in Annex A. The main improvements were as follows: The Annual Seasonal Agricultural Survey (SAS) was used for the first time in a GDP rebasing exercise in Rwanda. The previous rebasing, relating to 2011, took place in 2014, too early to take advantage of the SAS, which was first undertaken in 2013. The SAS significantly improves the estimates of the volume of crop production used in the SUT and in the estimates quarterly GDP. The estimates of many of the key ratios needed to compile the SUT were for the first time complied using NISR’s Integrated Business Enterprise Survey (IBES). For example, the input output ratios and margin rates. In previous rebasing exercises, e.g. for the 2011 SUT, these ratios were derived from much less complete data sources. The IBES also provided a basis for measuring the secondary (and other) activities of businesses, for example the trading activities of manufacturers. This leads to a significant improvement in the conceptual quality of the estimates of GDP, since the best-practice methodology for measuring trading activities differs from that for other forms of production. The detail and data quality of the estimates of purchases available from the 2014 IBES survey also allowed of the development for the first time of a comprehensive ‘intermediate use’ matrix in the SUT, leading to further improvement in the conceptual quality of the estimated level of GDP. Government Financial Statistics (GFS) data from MINECOFIN’s IFMIS accounting system was used in full for the first time in a rebasing exercise in Rwanda. This includes information on the income and expenditures of not just Central Government, but also of Local Authorities and Extra Budgetary Units. The inclusion of these data is a major improvement in the estimates of government output and expenditure, and provides greater consistency with GFS accounting standards. Data on sales turnover from the Rwandan Revenue Authority’s (RRA) Business Income Tax and Value Added Tax databases were used for the first time to establish the level of formal sector economic activity and to quality assure the results for the IBES survey. Data on compensation of employees from the RRA’s Pay As You Earn (PAYE) database, used to estimate the output of NGO providing services to households, was used for the first time in an SUT in Rwanda. The new activity classification Countries belonging to the East African Community have undertaken to switch from Revision 3 of the International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC Rev.3) to Revision 4. The main innovation in ISIC Rev.4 is the introduction of a new Information and Communication category. This category includes publishing, broadcasting, and film making; telecommunication services; and information technology services. Previously these were included with manufacturing, transport and communications, and business and other services. Since SUT 2011 these transitions have been done. To obtain value added, benchmark input-output ratios (based on previous estimates) are used, crop by crop. All agricultural production is regarded as informal (marketed) and non-marketed. Estimates of the non-marketed production (own-account consumption) are extrapolated from benchmark estimates using the same indicators as for overall production. It is thus assumed that the proportion of each crop that is marketed remains constant until the next EICV survey results. In mining and quarrying, the benchmark estimates are based mainly on the value of exports, but these have been adjusted in the annual estimates of GDP to take account of unrecorded imports. Otherwise local production is likely to be overstated. Estimates of the total output of the formal sector are available from the VAT returns made by mining companies to the Rwanda Revenue Authority. The remainder is assumed to be informal. For Quarrying activities , benchmark values are mainly obtained from the analysis of informal activity section of EICV and extrapolated using construction output. In manufacturing, total output at current price are mainly given by VAT data. Informal sector is mainly forecasted using inter EICV trend adjusted with import, exports data. Constant price outputs are calculated by using Producer price index (PPI) by witch output figures at current price are divided. For food processing and Beverages and tobacco, quantity indicators are multiplied by corresponding PPI or CPI to obtain current price output. To obtain value added, benchmark input-output ratios are used In electricity, up-to-date data are available on the total quantity supplied (both imported and locally produced). These figures are multiplied by the standard price and used to extrapolate the benchmark data to estimate the total output at current prices for the most recent period. Total output at constant prices is obtained by extrapolating benchmark value by the quantity index. In Water & waste management, up-to-date data are available on the total quantity produced. These figures are multiplied by the standard price and used to extrapolate the accounting data to estimate the total output at current prices for the most recent period. Total output at constant prices is obtained by dividing the current price figures by a price index derived from the standard price. In construction, annual and quarterly estimates of the overall value of modern-type output at current prices is extrapolated using a value index derived from a quantity indicator multiplied by a corresponding price indicator. The quantity indicator is a weighted average of two indicators: an index of road construction and an index of the quantity of cement consumed in the country. Out of the total modern-type output, formal output is estimated using turnover from the VAT system, and the remainder is regarded as informal. Quantity indicators for these sub-sectors are obtained by dividing the value indices (based on the values) by the price index. Benchmark total output of the traditional-type construction is extrapolated using a value index derived from a quantity indicator multiplied by the same price indicator as above. The quantity indicator is based on the estimated rural housing formation. In maintenance and repair of motor vehicles, annual and quarterly estimate is based on the quantity indices multiplied by a corresponding price indicator. The quantity indicator is based on the estimated stock of vehicles. In wholesale and retail trade, annual benchmark total margins are extrapolated using an index of values, derived by adding together the values of the following: marketed crop production marketed manufacturing, assumed to be 80% of total output (from previous estimates) marketed imports, assumed to be 73% of the value of imported goods. A corresponding quantity index is derived using the quantity indices that measure the development over time of each of the three categories, weighted together using the current price values in the base year. A price index for trade margins is calculated by dividing the value index by the quantity index. In transport services: Annual and quarterly estimates of the formal sector output at current prices are based on data from the VAT system. The informal output is based on composite quantity indicator of freight and passenger. The quantity indicator is multiplied by the price index to obtain the current price estimates. Freight transport indicator is based on quantity indicator from wholesale and retail trade, while passenger transport is mainly based on mid urban population growth. In hotels and restaurants , annual and quarterly estimates of the formal total output is based on the hotels survey data on room sold for the accommodation and VAT for catering services. The informal sector is based on the trend between EICVs on accommodation and catering services consumption. A value index is derived and divided (deflated) by a price index to obtain a corresponding quantity index, while quantity index derived from room sold is multiplied by a price index to obtain value index. In Information and communication, annual and quarterly output at current price is based on extrapolating output by the value index from VAT. The entire sector is believed to be formal. The value index is divided by the price index to obtain the constant price estimates. In financial services, at current prices, total output, intermediate consumption and hence GVA (by subtraction) are estimated directly (for the benchmark and every quarter) from returns made by all the banks and insurance companies operating in the country. FISIM (part of the banks’ total output) is also calculated from these returns. In real estate, the benchmark value is derived mainly from the EICV and VAT. Total output at current price is extrapolated from the benchmark using quantity indicator multiplied by the corresponding price index. The quantity index is composite indicator derived from rural housing index, urban housing index, rental index (from EICVs) and deflated VAT turnovers for the formal sector. In Professional, scientific & technical activities, benchmark estimates, at current prices, is extrapolated using VAT turnover for the formal sector. The informal sector is derived from the quantity indicators (from inter EICV trend) multiplied by the corresponding price index. The same methodology applies on Administrative &support services activities. In public administration, because the output of government is not sold for profit, total output in defined as the sum of intermediate expenditure, compensation of employees and (in principle) the (imputed) consumption of fixed capital. No attempt has been made to estimate the latter, so GVA consists of compensation of employees. The benchmark exercise involved an analysis of government expenditure, especially intermediate consumption. Estimates were made of total output, making allowances for expenditure by local authorities, social security administration, and technical assistance. In education and health, services are supplied partly by government, partly by non-government organizations and the private sector, often non-profit institutions. They are paid for partly by government, partly by households or individuals through fees, and partly by charities. For the benchmark, total demand was estimated using information from the government accounts and from the EICV for household expenditure. Expenditure approach: Government final consumption expenditure: Government final consumption expenditure at current prices is calculated by summing the following components. Total output of the public administration activity Less the sales of government services, taken as the “non-tax” revenue Government spending on education. Government spending on health. Imports of technical assistance services as recorded in the balance of payments accounts. The equivalent figures at constant 2011 prices are obtained by deflating each of the above components and then adding them. The first two and the last components are deflated using the public administration deflator (activity L). The third uses the education services deflator and the fourth uses the health services deflator. Private final consumption expenditure The annual estimates at both current and constant prices are currently derived as a residual. The result is therefore affected by any errors in the data and the assumptions used to estimate all other components of GDP. It must therefore be treated as a very rough estimate, to be reviewed when the next benchmark is prepared. Starting with GDP at market prices estimated from the production side, capital formation and net exports (see below) are deducted to obtain final consumption expenditure in total. Finally government final consumption expenditure is deducted to give the required result. Gross fixed capital formation (GFCF) Annual estimates of GFCF are obtained by extrapolating the benchmark.
