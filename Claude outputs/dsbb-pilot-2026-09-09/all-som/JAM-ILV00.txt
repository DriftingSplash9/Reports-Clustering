 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Gross foreign reserve assets are defined consistently with the Sixth Edition of the Balance of Payments Manual and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6) as readily available claims on nonresidents denominated in foreign convertible currencies. They include the (Bank of Jamaica’s) BOJ’s holdings of monetary gold, SDR holdings, foreign currency cash, foreign currency securities, liquid balances abroad, and the country’s reserve position at the Fund. Excluded from reserve assets are any assets that are pledged, collateralized, or otherwise encumbered, claims on residents, claims in foreign exchange arising from derivatives in foreign currency vis-à-vis domestic currency (such as futures, forwards, swaps, and options), precious metals other than gold, assets in nonconvertible currencies, and illiquid assets. Data are also disseminated on the BOJ's foreign liabilities to the IMF and others and on net international reserves. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Official reserve assets are broken down into convertible foreign currency categories consistent with the IMF’s Data Template on International Reserves and Foreign Currency Liquidity, namely: Official Reserves Assets; IMF Reserve Position; SDRs; Gold; and Other Reserve Assets. Note: The monetary authority of Jamaica does not hold reserves in the form of monetary gold. 2.2.1 Scope Official reserve assets are broken down into convertible foreign currency categories consistent with the IMF’s Data Template on International Reserves and Foreign Currency Liquidity, namely: Official Reserves Assets; IMF Reserve Position; SDRs; Gold; and Other Reserve Assets. Note: The monetary authority of Jamaica does not hold reserves in the form of monetary gold. Scope of the data A broader concept of international reserves is also disseminated. This broader coverage includes BOJ’s gross and net reserves, government holdings of reserve assets (which have been negligible in recent years) and net foreign assets of commercial banks. The components of the stock of international reserves that are disseminated in the BOJ balance sheet and include IMF related assets, convertible currencies and current accounts abroad, and time deposits. Jamaica has held no reserves in the form of monetary gold in recent years. All foreign exchange assets are revalued at the end of month exchange rates, but reserve assets are not marked to market prices. Exceptions to coverage Jamaica does not hold reserves in the form of monetary gold. 2.4.1 Valuation All foreign exchange assets are revalued at the end of month exchange rates, but reserve assets are not marked to market prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis Foreign currency instruments are valued on the basis of the rate of exchange of the Jamaica Dollar for the United States dollar, which is determined by the weighted average rate of trades reported by authorised dealers and cambios and the rate at which the Bank itself buys US dollars. The rates of exchange for other currencies are derived from the US$ rate using rates published by The World Markets Company Plc (WM Reuters). Monetary assets and liabilities denominated in foreign currencies at the reporting date are translated at the foreign exchange rates prevailing at that date. Transactions in foreign currencies are translated at the foreign exchange rates ruling at the dates of those transactions. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data on international reserves come from the BOJ's balance sheets. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Procedures are in place to check the accuracy of the translation from foreign currency to domestic currency.
