 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Concept : Gross official international reserves are external assets that are readily available to and controlled by the monetary authorities and the Government for the purpose of macroeconomic management and as safeguards against external shocks. Definition : Official reserve assets comprise the reserve holdings of the Bank of Mauritius, the country's reserve position in the IMF and the foreign assets of the Government. Relationship to international guidelines : Data are compiled on the basis of definitions contained in the sixth edition of the IMF’s Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6) and the IMF’s International Reserves and Foreign Currency Liquidity – Guidelines for a Data Template. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: Instruments included within the definition of reserve assets are classified into monetary gold, holdings of foreign currency, Special Drawing Rights, the reserve position in the IMF and the Government's foreign currency balance held abroad. Holdings of foreign currency include securities as well as deposits with foreign monetary authorities, commercial banks, external fund managers and supranational bodies. 2.2.1 Scope Classification: Instruments included within the definition of reserve assets are classified into monetary gold, holdings of foreign currency, Special Drawing Rights, the reserve position in the IMF and the Government's foreign currency balance held abroad. Holdings of foreign currency include securities as well as deposits with foreign monetary authorities, commercial banks, external fund managers and supranational bodies. Scope of the data Institutional coverage : The Bank holds and manages the official foreign reserves of Mauritius, with the exception of the reserve position in the IMF and the Government's foreign currency balance held abroad. Instrument coverage : In addition to the reserve position in the IMF and the Government's foreign currency balance held abroad, the official reserves are held in the form of securities, deposits, repurchase agreements, monetary gold and SDR. Banks’ foreign exchange deposits held with the Central Bank on account of the cash reserve and liquidity coverage ratio requirements are currently being reported as contingent short-term net drains on foreign currency assets, specifically in Section III, item 1.b as ‘Other Contingent Liabilities’ up to one month maturity. Exceptions to coverage The Bank's equity participation in non-resident private corporations is excluded from international reserves given that these assets are neither liquid nor readily marketable. Unrecorded activity The IRFCL does not contain any estimate made for illegal activity and for other transactions for which data are not available. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: The values for SDRs and reserve position in the IMF are determined at the IMF basket valuation for the SDR. Gold is valued at the price in the London bullion market on the last business day of the month. The foreign currency value of other overseas assets is based, where applicable, on market quotations; accrued interest is normally taken into account. Conversion rates: The foreign currency value of overseas assets is converted at end-month market exchange rates. Aggregate data are disseminated in Mauritian rupees and US dollars. Frequency of calculation and revaluation: Reserve assets are revalued on a daily basis. 2.4.2 Recording basis Recording is carried out on an accrual basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures None 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data are largely obtained from the Bank's balance sheet, with the exception of data on the reserve position in the IMF. Data on the country's reserve position in the IMF are made available to the Bank in its capacity as agent for the Mauritian Government for its transactions with the IMF. For the international reserves and foreign currency liquidity template, the main data source for the ‘official reserves assets’ is the Bank's balance sheet while some of the data on ‘predetermined short-term net drains on foreign currency assets (nominal value)’ and ‘contingent short-term net drains on foreign currency assets (nominal value)’ are obtained from the MoF. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The source data are consistent with the definitions, scope and classification, set out in the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6) for the underlying concept of international reserves and are in line with the IMF’s International Reserves and Foreign Currency Liquidity – Guidelines for a Data Template. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The data received from the Accounting and Budgeting Division of the Bank for the dissemination of gross official international reserves are timely. For the requirements of the international reserves and foreign currency liquidity template, the data are provided within a reasonable timeframe by both the Accounting and Budgeting Division of the Bank and the MoF to enable timely reporting and dissemination. 3.2.1 Source data assessment None, since all official sources are covered. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques None. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures No adjustment is made to the data. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results None 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data None 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs None 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses No formal revision studies are undertaken. Published data are mostly final. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses None 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Not applicable. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points All publications contain a specific e-mail address relating to the subject field being disseminated. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs None
