 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Merchandise trade statistics records the value of goods traded in both directions across Libya’s international borders. Goods traded are classified by export and import categories. Exports are defined as goods sold by Libya to the rest of the world; and imports are defined as goods purchased by Libya from the rest of the world. The GAI has followed the Harmonized System of Foreign Trade Data Classification, issued by the World Customs Organization (WCO), from 2007 onwards 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The GAI followed SITC Rev. 2 prior to 2004; it followed SITC Rev. 3 from 2004 to 2006; and it followed the Harmonized System of Foreign Trade Data Classification from 2007 onwards (SITC is Standardized International Trade Classification). Scope of the data Most goods traded are covered in the GAI’s foreign trade statistics. These include dutiable goods passing through customs, and government imports and exports. Exceptions to coverage The following categories of goods are not covered: goods sent by Libya to its diplomatic missions abroad; goods sent from abroad to foreign diplomatic missions in Libya; personal effects of travelers and tourists; animals for racing; items meant for exhibitions only; samples and packages meant for re-exports; goods shipped for temporary storage; monetary gold; negotiable securities and bank notes; means of transport used for travel and shipping; provisions and fuel provided to foreign carriers; provisions and fuel provided to national carriers abroad; transit trade in both the directions. Unrecorded activity Shuttle trade (for which no customs documentation exists) is unrecorded trade; it is excluded from merchandise trade statistics; estimates of unrecorded trade, however, are made by the CBL and included in the balance of payments statistics. 2.4.1 Valuation Import trade statistics are compiled on the c.i.f. basis, and export trade statistics are compiled on the f.o.b. basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The GIA derivs the source data for the merchandise trade statistics from the following three sources: monthly statements of the Customs Departmnet; annual reports of the National Oil Corporation (NOC); and annual reports of the National Suppliers Corporation (NASCO). The GAI represntatives stationed at Al Khoms and Mesurat sea ports, as well as those at Ras Judair (near the border with Tunisia) and Benghazi (in the eastern region), provide customs data directly to the GAI. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques No statistical techniques are used. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures No other statistical procedures are used.
