 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Definition: Following the "International Trade Statistics: Concepts and Definitions" UN 1998 (UN ITS), exports/imports are defined as the value of goods which subtract from/add to the stock of the country's material resources. Relationship of national practices with international and regional guidelines: Compilation of merchandise statistics is in line with the recommendations of UN ITS and IMF's Balance of Payments Statistics Manual (6th edition). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Products are classified at the 8-digits level of the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS). For analytical purposes, the trade data are classified according to the Standard International Trade Classification (SITC, Rev. 3). The Classification by Broad Economic Categories, Rev. 3 (BEC) is used for end-use classification. The classification of the direction of trade statistics follows the alphabetic ISO (international Organization for Standardisation) coding at the 2-digits level. Imports and exports are both distinguished by country of dispatch and destination. HMRC can provide more detailed data at a 6 or 8 digit HS level, or SITC basis on their web site. h ttps://www.uktradeinfo.com/find-commodity-data/help-with-classifiying-goods/ Scope of the data Transactions coverage: All movements of goods into or out of the customs territory of the UK (or Great Britain for EU trade) are covered which involve a change of ownership or permanent relocation. For trade between Northern Ireland and the EU: All transaction in scope above a designated threshold are included. Transactions below this threshold are estimated, based on VAT returns. The threshold is reviewed annually and published on uktradeinfo.com/trade-data/overseas/ Specific trade in gold i.e. gold coin and monetary gold (gold transferred between national government or authorities) is excluded from the statistics of total exports and imports. However, trade in ores and concentrates and finished manufactures of gold (e.g. jewellery) are included in total exports and imports. Currently, adjustments are made to include industrial gold in trade statistics - in exports, the adjustments reflect the value added in refining gold and producing proof coins; in imports the adjustments reflect the value of gold used in finished manufactures (such as jewellery and dentistry). Geographical coverage: The territory of Great Britain and Northern Ireland coinciding with the customs territory. All petroleum, natural gas and related products are included in merchandise trade, including those from the UK Continental Shelf. Oil imported for processing and exported after processing without change of ownership is reflected in import and exports data. Exceptions to coverage Transactions coverage: The exceptions include: monetary gold (gold transferred between national government or authorities) and gold coin, Transactions involving gold deposits (and lending) in the London Bullion Market are considered broadly equivalent to banking transactions as they do not necessarily involve physical movement of gold in or out of the country. Further exceptions include securities, bank notes and coins in circulation, goods for temporary use or for limited period, goods in transit, goods imported by embassies, commercial samples and goods sent free to replace defective goods. Geographical coverage: It excludes the offshore islands (the Channel Islands and the Isle of Man). Unrecorded activity Estimates for smuggling of alcohol and tobacco are added to import data. Adjustments are made to import figures for trade in goods to allow for the impact of VAT MTIC fraud. The adjustments were introduced for the first time in the UK trade May 2003 First Release published on 9 July 2003. An article explaining the fraud and the impact on the trade figures was also published on 9 July 2003. Under the EU legislation, there is no estimate of trade between EU countries for enterprises below the VAT threshold. From January 2022, these adjustments are no longer included. Further details can be found in Methodology changes to trade in goods statistics from March 2022 . (https://www.uktradeinfo.com/news/methodology-changes-to-trade-in-goods-statistics-from-march-2022/) 2.4.1 Valuation Exports are valued free on board (F.O.B) and imports on cost, insurance and freight (C.I.F) basis. Conversion to unit of account: Amounts expressed in foreign currency are converted to sterling by the trader using a system of ‘period rates of exchange' published by HMRC. These rates are normally operative for a four weekly period unless there is a significant movement in the exchange rate. Exports and imports of petroleum, natural gas and related products are valued FOB. The adjustments for the cost of freight and insurance on oil and gas imports is projected from data formerly supplied by the Department for Business, Enterprise and Regulatory Reform. 2.4.2 Recording basis ONS apply the ‘special trade' as of 1st May 2016, before which the ‘general trade' system (as described in the IMTS) to compile statistics. As a result of this change, goods are only recorded once they are removed from free zones and enter circulation or certain customs procedures. No adjustments are made as a result of this change. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Netting is not used generally. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The data for trade with non-EU countries and GB trade with EU countries are derived from Customs declarations. For the data on Northern Ireland trade with EU countries the source is the Intrastat survey. This is also true for petroleum and natural gas and related products (other than gas and electricity through pipelines to or from the EU and Norway). The methodology used to collect EU data on Natural Gas and Electricity was amended by the Commission of the European Communities (EC regulation no 1982/2004). As a result, HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) changed to collecting information relating to the trade in Natural Gas and Electricity directly from the pipeline and grid operators. This removed the need for individual companies to submit import and export declarations for these goods. The methodology records the physical flow of Natural Gas and Electricity between the UK and its EU trading partners, as well as with Norway. HMRC calculate any prices that have not already been provided on a declaration, estimated using the average spot price per month for gas and electricity. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Sources: For trade with non-EU countries and trade between GB and the EU, the importers' and exporters' customs declaration data is provided via the customs CDS system (formerly CHIEF), which is equivalent to a full on-going census of trade in goods. The Intrastat survey (monthly enterprise survey) for the data on trade between Northern Ireland and EU countries, which requires only large companies (in terms of their value of EU trade) to submit detailed monthly declarations on their trade. The data on the total value of trade for smaller traders comes from the periodic (quarterly or annual) VAT declarations, which all companies have to submit if they are above the VAT threshold. Types of data collected: Value of imports and exports in Pounds and by country of origin/destination. Information is also collected on the types of transaction, transport and on invoice currencies (non-EU data only). Basis of country data: Imports and exports are both distinguished by country of dispatch and destination. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Timing of recording transactions: For non-EU trade and GB – EU trade that is recorded through the customs CDS system (formerly CHIEF) the timing is determined by the date of processing of the customs declarations. For Northern Ireland - EU trade, the timing is determined by a combination of the invoice date and the date of movement of the goods. 3.2.1 Source data assessment In addition, on-going exercises are carried out to record the level of error in the data either through incorrect completion of the customs documentation (for example, incorrect valuation basis used) or through failings in the processing system (for example, keying errors). 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Adjustments to improve coverage/classification/timing/valuation: The customs data are regarded as complete, as such only minor adjustments are made to allow for trade transactions outside the scope of the customs system (for example, UK purchases of ships which are delivered abroad - such merchandise may not cross the customs boundary and thus may not appear in the customs data). Certain adjustments are incorporated to correct for identified deficiencies in the recording of trade and to bring the data into line with the definitions set out in the sixth edition of the IMF's "Balance of Payments Manual". For Northern Ireland trade with EU countries, the Intrastat system includes automatic correction for non-response in the survey in the case that traders above the Intrastat threshold do not fulfil the legal requirement to submit the returns within the deadline. The correction is also used to estimate the trade data of small businesses. In both systems, in-built validation and credibility checks are applied to the raw data received, using combinations of value/volume ratios with particular attention given to high value transactions. The methodology used to collect EU data on Natural Gas and Electricity was amended by the Commission of the European Communities (EC regulation no 1982/2004). As a result, HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) changed to collecting information relating to the trade in Natural Gas and Electricity directly from the pipeline and grid operators. This removed the need for individual companies to submit import and export declarations for these goods. The methodology records the physical flow of Natural Gas and Electricity between the UK and its EU trading partners, as well as Norway. Value data are estimated using the relevant market prices for gas and electricity. Adjustments have been made in the past for rigs and production platforms entering/ leaving the territory for one year or more. They are not necessary at the present time. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Seasonal adjustment: The data are seasonally adjusted using X13-ARIMA procedures. Other: Price indices for imports and exports are no longer computed as of July 2018. These have been replaced with Implied deflators (IDEFs), which are calculated from current prices (CP) and chained volume measures (CVMs) to calculate the implied effect of inflation on prices. Processing system: Customs data are collected automatically in the CDS system (formerly CHIEF). Both Customs and Intrastat data are processed by the Trade Statistics Unit in HMRC. Site of processing: Office for national Statistics. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results HMRC (TSU) conduct regular analysis of mirror statistics, i.e. asymmetry studies, between the UK and rest of the EU, plus some ad hoc studies with other countries. Eurostat also carry out asymmetry analysis for member states. Users can also raise issues with the data for investigation by TSU. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Reconciliation: The data are cross-checked with partner countries' trade data and compared with other indicators (for example, data from trade opinion surveys, exchange rates, etc.). They are further checked in the balancing exercise for national accounts compilation. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs ONS continues to engage with the international community to build understanding of trade in services asymmetries with partner countries. A table in the release shows summary information on the size and direction of the revisions which have been made to the data covering the period from January 1997 to the month of latest release. Revisions to the UK trade statistics are most often down to 1 of 2 reasons. Firstly, the initial estimates may change due to late return of documentation by businesses. The other main reason for revisions is changes to methodology and systems improvements to ensure more accurate data. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses The aggregate effect of revisions is highlighted in the notes that accompany each trade news release.
