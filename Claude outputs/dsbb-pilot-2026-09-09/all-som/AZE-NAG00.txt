 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The methodological soundness dimension is assessed against the guidelines outlined in the System of National Accounts 2008 (2008 SNA). Concepts and definitions: All concepts, definitions and standards are taken from the international methodology – System of National Accounts 2008 (2008 SNA). The System of National Accounts 2008 (2008 SNA) is the internationally agreed standard set of recommendations on how to compile measures of economic activity in accordance with strict accounting conventions based on economic principles. The recommendations are expressed in terms of a set of concepts, definitions, classifications and accounting rules that comprise the internationally agreed standard for measuring such items as gross domestic product (GDP), the most frequently quoted indicator of economic performance. The accounting framework of the SNA allows economic data to be compiled and presented in a format that is designed for purposes of economic analysis, decision-taking and policymaking. The accounts themselves present in a condensed way a great mass of detailed information, organized according to economic principles and perceptions, about the working of an economy. They provide a comprehensive and detailed record of the complex economic activities taking place within an economy and of the interaction between the different economic agents, and groups of agents that takes place on markets or elsewhere. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification and sectorization used in the compilation of national accounts are in broad conformity with internationally recommended systems. Classification system: All transactions, flows, and institutional sectors are classified according to the classifications, recommended in the SNA 2008. The types and usage of classifications are: · NACE – statistical classification of economic activities in the European Community (NACE); · Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP) – to classify the final consumption of households; · Classification of the Functions of Government (COFOG) – to classify the final consumption of general government; · Classification of products by Activity (CPA) – to classify products and services; Sectorization system: · S.11 – Non-financial corporations; · S.12 – Financial corporations; · S.13 – General government; · S.14 – Households; · S.15 – Non-profit institutions serving households; · S.2 – Rest of the world. 2.2.1 Scope The classification and sectorization used in the compilation of national accounts are in broad conformity with internationally recommended systems. Classification system: All transactions, flows, and institutional sectors are classified according to the classifications, recommended in the SNA 2008. Scope of the data The production boundary: In the SNA, production is understood to be a physical process, carried out under the responsibility, control and management of an institutional unit, in which labour and assets are used to transform inputs of goods and services into outputs of other goods and services. The SNA includes within the production boundary all production actually destined for the market, whether for sale or barter. It also includes all goods or services provided free to individual households or collectively to the community by government units or NPISHs. The accounts of the SNA are compiled for resident institutional units grouped into institutional sectors and subsectors. An institutional unit is said to be resident within the economic territory of a country when it maintains a centre of predominant economic interest in that territory, that is, when it engages, or intends to engage, in economic activities or transactions on a significant scale either indefinitely or over a long period of time, usually interpret ted as one year. The GDP of a country, viewed as an aggregate measure of production, is equal to the sum of the gross value added of all resident institutional units engaged in production (plus any taxes, and minus any subsidies, on products not included in the value of their outputs). This is not exactly the same as the sum of the gross value added of all productive activities taking place within the geographical boundaries of the national economy. Some of the production of a resident institutional unit may take place abroad, for example, the installation of some exported machinery or equipment or a consultancy project undertaken by a team of expert advisers working temporarily abroad. Conversely, some of the production taking place within a country may be attributable to nonresident institutional units. The asset boundary: The boundary of assets is defined according to the SNA 2008. Geographic coverage: The economic territory of the Republic of Azerbaijan is the geographical territory being under the jurisdiction of the country's government where there are free movements of people, goods and capitals. The economic territory includes the land area, airspace, territorial waters, including jurisdiction over fishing rights and rights to fuels or minerals. The economic territory also includes territorial enclaves in the rest of the world. Currently, the data compilation does not include economic activities in Nagorno-Karabakh and other surrounding regions, temporary occupied by Armenians. The scope of the national accounts statistics covers 2008 SNA accounts/aggregates. There are some of tables and accounts, that are determined as minimum requirement for its implementation and they should be compiled on a regular basis. - Annual value added components at current and constant prices by activity; - Annual GDP at current and constant prices by production method; - Annual expenditures of GDP at current prices; - Gross national income aggregates at current prices with an annual frequency; - Sequence of accounts for the total economy with an annual frequency; · production account; · generation of income account; · primary distribution of income account; · secondary distribution of income account; · use of disposable income account; · production and generation of income account; · capital account; - Annual rest of the world account; · external account of goods and services; · account of primary incomes and current transfers from abroad; · external by capital account; - Production account allocation by sectors at current prices with an annual frequency; - Annual value added by sectors at current prices; - Quarterly GDP by production approach, at current and constant prices by activity; - Quarterly GDP by expenditures approach, at current prices. Exceptions to coverage No exceptions. Unrecorded activity National accounts statistics includes an estimation of non – observed economy. Estimation on illegal activities is not included. 2.4.1 Valuation Production output is valued at basic prices. Intermediate consumption is valued at purchasers’ prices. Gross fixed capital formation is valued at purchasers’ prices. Total GDP is valued at purchasers’ prices, while value added is valued at basic prices. GDP components are estimated at current and constant prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis Transactions and flows are generally recorded on an accruals basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Grossing/netting procedures in accordance with SNA 2008 rules. Data on value added are compiled and published on the gross and net basis (annual estimates). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The common structure of the source data for national accounts is based on the methodological guidelines of SNA 2008. The main sources for estimation of gross domestic product (GDP) by production approach are: - Business statistics and regular surveys in different types of activities (monthly, quarterly and annual), reports on production and expenses submitted to the SSC by enterprises; - Reports on execution of state budget (monthly, quarterly, annually) of the Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Azerbaijan); - Balance of payments from the Central Bank of the Republic of Azerbaijan); - Data from Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FIMSA); - The State Register of Statistical Units (Statistical Business Register); - Ad hoc surveys in various fields of statistics; - Household budget surveys; - Labour force and wages surveys; - Financial statistics by the Central Bank of the Republic of Azerbaijan. Business register is an automated system for collecting, accumulating and processing data on legal persons of all types of ownership, and organizational and legal forms of business, physical persons (entrepreneurs). The Register is maintained on base of information from the administrative registers from Ministry of Tax and other state authorities. Currently, the Business register has a function both of administrative and statistical registers. It contains statistical indicators and information about the principal and secondary activities of an enterprise and entrepreneurs. Actually, the data source for GDP estimation by production approach related on quarterly data from types of economic activities. These types of economic activities are based on international classification by types of economic activities ( NACE Rev.2) . 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The indicators of input data are reconciled with definitions, scope of coverage and classifications of the SNA 2008. During the revising process of the state statistical reports, the main requirements for the compliance of indicators according to the principles of SNA 2008 are taken in order to obtain the necessary data for the national accounts compilation. The annual national accounts are compiled in accordance to the data of reference year, while the quarterly accounts are based on discrete data for the reference quarter excluding the reports on execution of the budget reports of the Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Azerbaijan (state and local budgets) presented by cumulative basis. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Currently, the full set of accounts is regularly compiled annually only. The quarterly estimates of the GDP by production and use methods at current and constant prices are completed on the 90 th day after the end of the reference quarter. Monthly 'flash' estimates of cumulative GDP are produced and disseminated within two weeks following the end of the reference period. Preliminary annual estimates of GDP are submitted two weeks after the end of the reference year. The full range of the system of national accounts is published annually in the "National Accounts of the Republic of Azerbaijan" annual publication. The main tables in MS Excel format, also as the e-version of the publication are available at the official website of SSC of the Republic of Azerbaijan. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data for national accounts is systematically assists for quality and coverage using the alternative data sources and balancing methods. The results of the assessments are used for the improvement of adjustments related to the data sources. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Estimation process of the national accounts indicators always has some techniques adjustments like extrapolation or forecasting analysis. This is especially noticeable in the calculation of quarterly GDP by production and expenditure approaches. The main requirement in estimation process of quarterly national accounts is to do some adjustments for equalizing with annual data, to have benchmarking. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Annual GDP by production and expenditure approaches is calculated at current and constant prices. Quarterly GDP by production approach is calculated at current and constant prices. Quarterly GDP by expenditure approach is calculated at current prices. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The validation process of the data of national accounts is based on various types of statistical data: reports of enterprises, various administrative data, and annual reports of state agencies (Ministry of Finance, State Oil Fund and etc.). During the validation process special pay attention is given to the balance for change in value, volume, and price from the previous year; changes from the previous national accounts estimates for the same period; balancing procedures using the data from supply and use tables. Moreover, GDP results according to the production and expenditure are compared for identify the statistical discrepancy between two approaches. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data The discrepancies in intermediate data are periodically investigated, and measures are taken to remove them. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Supply and use tables are compiled every five year. Input data are revised, the reasons for discrepancies are explored, and certain adjustment to the scheme to produce the SNA indicators is made. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Annually and quarterly estimates of national accounts statistics are always revived and analyzed. The revision process of data is based on confirmed methodological statements by Board of SSC which called as the "Regulation on review of the data". Moreover, the timeliness of revision of preliminary final data is published at the official site of SSC of the Republic of Azerbaijan. https://www.stat.gov.az/menu/6/information_regulations/regulations_en.pdf (also download an excel file from: https://www.stat.gov.az/menu/6/?lang=en – publications- determination calendar of the data ) The official methodology study; revisions and analyses studies about national accounts are published at the official site of the State Statistical Committee of the Republic of Azerbaijan. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Revisions are measured, assessed, and explained in the statistical publication and in the database accessible by users. https://www.stat.gov.az/source/system_nat_accounts/?lang=en 5.1.5 Dissemination on request National accounts statistics is disseminated officially at the website of SSC and on publications. Moreover, there are some of users, like teachers, researches; students and etc. send their official request to the official e-mail address of the State Statistical Committee of the Republic of Azerbaijan.
