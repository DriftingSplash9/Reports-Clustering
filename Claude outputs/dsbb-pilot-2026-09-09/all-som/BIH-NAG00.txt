 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework : The national accounts statistics of BiH are compiled following the conceptual framework of the European System of Accounts 2010 (ESA 10 ) , which is consistent with the System of National Accounts of the United Nations 2008 ( 2008 SNA ) . Definition : GDP is a measure of the total value added generated by all resident producer units plus net taxes on products during the reference period. GDP is also defined as total domestic final consumption corrected by the external trade balance on goods and services. Gross output is defined as the market value of all produced goods and services that are generated by resident producers during an accounting period. Intermediate consumption at purchase prices is the value of goods and services which are transformed or used in the production process. Gross value added is equal to the difference between gross output and intermediate consumption. Value added for institutional units is expressed at basic prices. FISIM (financial intermediation services indirectly measured) presents the value of services of financial intermediation, which are indirectly measured. The calculation of FISIM is based on reference interest rate. FISIM was allocated to intermediate consumption and final consumption . The allocation of FISIM represents one of the important conceptual adjustments in the calculation of GDP. New FISIM calculations is included into the officially GDP calculation. Household final consumption expenditure consists of expenditure incurred by resident households on consumption of goods and services for direct satisfaction of human needs and wants. In the System of National Accounts, the total volume of this expenditure is the largest of the GDP, calculated by the expenditure method. Gross fixed capital formation consists of producers’ acquisitions less disposals of fixed assets during the reference period plus certain additions to the value of non-produced assets realized by the productive activity of producers. The obtained investment goods can be new or already used, purchased or received without compensation from third persons in the country or abroad, produced in own arrangement or obtained through financial leasing. Final consumption expenditure of general government includes two categories of expenditure: - Non-market output other than own-account capital formation, which is measured by production costs less incidental sales . - Purchases by general government of goods and services produced by market producers that are supplied to household as social transfer in kind. Final consumption expenditure of general government is measured as difference between non market output and occasional sales on the market plus purchase of goods and services for free distribution. Exports and imports of goods occur when there are changes of ownership of goods between residents and non-residents, regardless of whether there are corresponding physical movements of goods across borders. Exports and imports of services consist of all services rendered by residents to non-residents or vice versa. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The Classification of Activities NACE Rev.2 is used to classify the activities. Other classifications have been introduced for the purpose of compiling GDP by expenditure approach, i.e. the Classification of products by Activity (CPA 2008) and the Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP) for household consumption expenditure. BHAS is the main institution responsible for the sector classification of institutional units in BA according to the ESA 2010, and the technical implementation is divided among BHAS, FIS and RSIS. Sector codes at the most detailed level are being assigned to all active institutional units in SBR, apart from the units belonging to the General Government sector. The list has been published. 2.2.1 Scope The Classification of Activities NACE Rev.2 is used to classify the activities. Other classifications have been introduced for the purpose of compiling GDP by expenditure approach, i.e. the Classification of products by Activity (CPA 2008) and the Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP) for household consumption expenditure. BHAS is the main institution responsible for the sector classification of institutional units in BA according to the ESA 2010, and the technical implementation is divided among BHAS, FIS and RSIS. Sector codes at the most detailed level are being assigned to all active institutional units in SBR, apart from the units belonging to the General Government sector. The list has been published. Scope of the data Unit coverage : Data cover resident non-financial corporations, financial corporations, government units, households and non-profit institutions serving households, as stipulated in the 2008 SNA / ESA 10 . Transaction coverage : In principle, all transactions in the current and the capital accounts are included. All transactions in goods and services covered by the SNA/ESA are included in the national accounts, apart from those related to illegal activities. Thus, production includes also the goods and services produced for own-consumption and own-produced capital assets, the imputation for the rental services of owner-occupied dwellings, barter transactions, as well as all related market transactions in goods and services. Data cover activities within the production boundary outlined in the SNA 2008 /EAS 10 . Geographical coverage : National accounts data cover the whole territory of Bosnia and Herzegovina. Separate production-based GDP data for the entities are compiled for the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina and for Republika Srpska. GDP estimates for the Brcko District and for the state level of public administration are compiled by BHAS and included in the aggregate data at the countrywide level. Exceptions to coverage Illegal activities are not included in national accounts of BiH. Projects financed by foreign donors are partially included in general government expenditures. Unrecorded activity Non observed economy (NOE) estimations are included in calculation of GDP. The estimate of imputed rental of owner –occupied dwellings is included in calculation. 2.4.1 Valuation Market prices are used to value flows and stocks. Output is valued at basic prices and intermediate consumption is valued at purchaser’s prices. Under the expenditure approach purchaser’s prices are used. In the experimental expenditure-based GDP calculations, import and export of goods and services are based on the balance of payments data taken over from Central bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina. The export value of goods is calculated by fob, while import value of goods is calculated by cif. 2.4.2 Recording basis All transactions are recorded on an accrual basis except for the government’s revenues, taxes, and subsidies which are recorded on cash basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Since the main source data for GDP compilation is based on the financial statements of enterprises, the transactions between establishments of the same enterprise are recorded net. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Production-based GDP The main approach used when calculating the annual GDP is the production approach. Current and constant prices GDP as well as the aggregates of the production account are compiled by the entity statistical institutes. Data aggregated at two-digit level, using the European Classification of Economic Activities (NACE Rev.2), are transmitted to BHAS. The basic sources used in the entities for GDP calculation by production approach are mostly financial reports of enterprises, administrative data for budgetary units, as well as results of regular statistical surveys. BHAS completes the estimates of the entities by adding data for state level units including central government institutions and the Central Bank. The components of the production account compiled by BHAS are allocated to the entities and the Brcko District using the coefficients established for the distribution of Value Added Tax. There are differences between the entities regarding the modalities of data collection and processing. Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina - FIS collects data for the legal entities using a form specially designed to suit the needs of the national accounts compilation - the statistical annex to the financial reports of legal units. The statistical annex is designed by FIS and contains all necessary data for the statistics of national accounts. Data for banks and insurance companies are collected by a statistical questionnaire. Data for budgetary users and non-budgetary funds are taken from regular annual financial statements. For collection purposes, the FIS has designed a special reporting form to be used by the municipalities (the Tax Bureau within the municipality) in reporting data on the entrepreneurs (unincorporated enterprises). This form would cover aggregated data by economic activity taking into accounts that the entrepreneurs use a more simplified accounting system. As with agriculture most of the production is undertaken by individual units, and data are collected via a special form requesting information on production (quantities and values) and associated costs (provided by experts in agriculture from the municipality offices). Special forms are also used for collecting data from municipalities for entrepreneurs and households engaged in other activities. Republika Srpska - The main sources of data used by the RSIS are also annual financial statements of legal entities (non-financial enterprises, non-profit institutions, Banks and insurance companies and public enterprises and agencies – indirect budget users). The main source data for direct budget users is Budget Report for local and entity levels of government. Agricultural statistics has been used as the main source data used for agriculture production. Estimates for the activities of entrepreneurs are based on the number of owners and their employees and self-employment obtained from administrative sources, i.e., the Health Insurance Fund (HIF) and the Pension Insurance Fund (PIF). These sources, of relative reliability, provide information broken down by legal category and economic activity. GDP by income approach is produced using the same data sources and similar procedures as in case of GDP by production approach. Estimates of Gross National Income and of Gross National Disposable Income have been carried out on the countrywide level by BHAS, using information produced by the Central Bank of BiH (net primary income from the rest of the world and net current transfers from the rest of the world). Expenditure-based GDP GDP by expenditure approach is compiled on the countrywide level by BHAS, since some data sources, such as Balance of Payments, are only available for the country as a whole. The main data sources for compilation of GDP by expenditure: - Household budget survey - Foreign trade data - PRODCOM survey - Balance of payment - Output by economic activities - The investment survey - The public investment program - Changes in inventories by economic activity - Various basic statistics - Various administrative sources The benchmark year estimate is partially based on the results of the Household Budget Survey and partially on a commodity flow approach. Some components of household consumption expenditure are obtained directly from the enterprises providing the services to households, like in the case of utility services and communications. For years without a household budget survey, mainly a commodity flow approach is used complemented with relevant indicators of price and volume. The estimates of consumption of NPISHs are based on the respective estimates of output of these institutions compiled by the entities statistical institution within the framework of their work in compiling GDP estimates based on the production approach. The output of these institutions is measured by the sum of their cost components. General Government consumption expenditure is based on the analysis of the data collected and processed by statistical institutions of BiH on the execution of the budgets by all government institutions comprising the state, entities, cantons, local authorities and extra budgetary funds. Gross Fixed Capital Formation (GFCF) on machinery and equipment estimation is based on commodity flow approach. The main data sources for estimation GFCF are foreign trade statistics and the annual survey on investment. Estimates on investment in construction assets by enterprises are obtained from an annual survey on investment conducted by the Entities’ institutes of statistics. Partial data on construction by individuals and by entrepreneurs derived from statistical surveys provide the basis for the respective estimate of capital formation by individuals and entrepreneurs. Data on the stock of in inventories at the opening and closing dates of the annual balance sheets are obtained from the financial reports of enterprises submitted to the statistical agencies. Estimates of total imports and exports of goods and services were taken from the corresponding estimates of BoP (Balance of Payments) statistics compiled by the Central bank. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording In general, the source data are consistent with the definitions, scope and classifications of NA statistics. Value added by economic activity is done by the main activity of the institutional units, and not by the establishments. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Generally, source data are collected and processed by entities to meet the timeliness and periodicity requirements of the national accounts compilation. Deadlines for the provision of source data by reporting units are indicated in the agencies’ work programs. FIS usually receives data on enterprises (including those in agriculture) on April 30, while data for the small entrepreneurs are received on May 30 of the year following the reference period. In RS, data become available in April or May, allowing however the Agency to prepare the preliminary results of national accounts by the end of June. In both entities, initiatives are taken to prevent late response including contacting respondents, in particular those with a significant impact on an industry’s activity or by checking with complementary sources. On the other hand, statistics compiled at the entity level are in turn considered the main source data for the countrywide national accounts. Therefore, the release schedule of FIS and RSIS are, in principle, harmonized with that of the BHAS (July for the preliminary estimates and November/December for the final ones). 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data are regularly monitored by both entities to identify any changes in the classification and to correct for underreporting or misreporting by checking against related source data (number of employed, wages, salaries, production turnover etc.), consistency of data against previous year information, etc. Special attention is generally paid to distortions, such as outliers, and respondents may be contacted in case of need. However, coverage of the informal sector, in particular for agriculture or for certain services, remains weak. Compilation techniques allow for data consistency checks. Identified outliers are verified first for possible errors of data entry and then with complementary data if possible. If necessary, the enterprise is directly contacted. Adjustments to data are made as needed. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Production-based GDP GDP at current prices is compiled as the sum of gross value added across all economic activities less the imputed services of financial intermediaries indirectly measured, plus net taxes on imports and taxes on products. The value added of non-financial enterprises is calculated as the difference between the value of output and intermediate consumption. Output is measured as sales plus the changes in inventories of finished and unfinished products. Intermediate consumption is measured as the value of goods and services purchases to be used in production less the change in inventories of raw materials and other materials used in production. Value added of unincorporated enterprises of households (entrepreneurs and individuals) in the construction, transport, trade, and business and personal services industries are obtained by summing up the estimates of the components of their respective generation of income accounts. Estimates are based on the number of people engaged in each activity and imputations of their compensation of employees together with ratios of gross operating surplus to compensation of employees. Estimates of agriculture output by entrepreneurs and individuals are obtained by product by multiplying quantities produced by prices. Value added is estimated for the total industry applying the input-output ratios derived from the financial reports of enterprises and detailed data on production costs collected at the municipality level. Output of general government and NPISHs is measured at cost. Due to the lack of data on consumption of fixed assets, a fixed coefficient is used in both Entities, the district of Brcko, and the State statistics. Expenditure-based GDP Estimates of household consumption expenditure on manufactured goods are based on the analysis of detailed data on production, imports and exports of goods, by product. Consumption of electricity, gas and water is based on the reports of the utility companies. Consumption of agriculture products are based on the household survey and extrapolation for most recent years using detailed data on the supply of agriculture products and changes in their respective prices. For the consumption of services, the main source data is the Household Budget Survey and volume and price indicators for most recent years. The value of the imputed rents of owner-occupied dwellings was estimated for the year 2004 using a cost approach. For most recent years, these estimates are obtained by extrapolation of the base year estimate using an indicator of the changes in the stock of dwellings and rents. Estimates of general government consumption expenditures and of the consumption of NPISHs are based on the estimates of output compiled by the entities’ institutes of statistics and the BHAS, based on the financial reports of the respective institutional units. Government consumption also includes expenditures on foreign financed projects classified as current expenditures of government. Gross fixed capital formation on machinery and equipment is estimated based on a commodity flow approach. Construction assets acquired by enterprises are estimated based on the results of the annual survey on investment. Partial information on the construction of dwellings by individuals and assumptions are the basis for the estimates of capital formation in dwellings. Changes in inventories have been calculated as the change in book values obtained from the financial reports of enterprises. Exports and imports of goods and services are obtained from the balance of payment statistics in local currency. Estimates at constant prices Production-based GDP Estimates of GDP by production at constant prices are based on a single indicator method. In most cases, the volume indicator of output is used. Estimates at constant prices are calculated at prices of the previous year. The single indicators method was used for the GDP calculation at constant prices. For the GDP calculation at constant prices, the single deflation method or single extrapolation method for output were used, depending on available indicators. Intermediate consumption at constant prices has been calculated by applying intermediate consumption shares in output for the previous year at current prices to output at constant prices. The reason for the calculation of intermediate consumption at constant prices in this way is the unavailability of data on the structure of intermediate consumption at the relevant level of disaggregation. GVA at constant prices is calculated as the difference between output at constant prices and intermediate consumption at constant prices. In general, constant price calculations still need improvements, which also means there is a need for improvement in the various price statistics. Expenditure-based GDP The most important indicators that have been used for estimates of GDP by expenditure approach at constant prices are CPI, export and import indices, Construction deflator, General government deflator, PPI. Estimates of household consumption expenditure are calculated at detailed level of products or four-digit levels of the COICOP classification, by deflation of the values by the adequate components of the consumer price index or using a revaluation method when quantities are available. , estimates at constant prices were calculated at the four-digit level of the COICOP classification Expenditures of resident households abroad were deflated by relevant components of the consumer price indices of the countries of main destination of tourism. The consumption of non-residents was deflated by relevant components of the CPI. NPISHs consumption expenditure: estimates at constant prices were obtained as the sum of the cost component of consumption recalculated at prices of previous year, where purchases were deflated by the general CPI and remuneration of employees at constant prices are obtained by extrapolation of the previous year data by the change in the number of employees. General government consumption expenditure: were obtained as the sum of the cost component at constant prices. Purchases of goods and services were deflated by the general CPI and the remuneration of employees was based on the changes in the number of employees. Gross fixed capital formation: Estimates of construction assets (buildings and structures) were deflated using the implicit deflator of output of the construction industry. Calculations of acquisition of machinery and equipment at constant prices were obtained by deflation of the current values. As the deflator for domestically produced equipment, the corresponding producer price index was used. Imported machinery and equipment was deflated by producer price index from country of origin, weighted by share in total import of machinery and equipment. Valuables: Estimates of valuables at constant prices were obtained by deflation of the current value by consumer price index. Exports and imports of goods and services: Unit value indices of a Paasche type for exports and imports were used for the deflation of the respective values of exports and imports at current prices. Exports and imports of services were deflated by price indices. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Estimates of the non-observed economy, including illegal activities, were made during 2004 to 2007 within the framework of a Eurostat-OECD project to measure the non-observed economy in the Western Balkan countries. As a result of these activities, the statistical institutes of BiH have estimated the size of the non-observed economy to be included in the National Accounts calculations. However, illegal activities were estimated due to the weak data sources not included in the official GDP. In the framework of the same Eurostat/OECD project, imputed rents for owner-occupied housing were estimated, following the user cost approach. The statistical institutions of BiH have followed the recommendations of the OECD manual ‘Measuring the Non-Observed Economy’ and Eurostat’s ‘Tabular Approach to Exhaustiveness’ in their estimations of the non-observed economy as closely as possible. The team focused on identifying the adjustments that are needed to improve the output measures of the economic (NACE) activities. Adjustments in the non-observed economy concerning expenditures have not been made yet. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Validation of data is undertaken separately by each statistical institution. At the entity level, the FIS and RSIS validate their estimates by comparing them with related alternative information as available. For example, dubious estimates that are based on the financial statement data are compared with other administrative sources, whenever possible. In the compilations of constant prices, the implicit deflators obtained are compared with available price indices. However, there are no systematic and rigorous procedures for checking the estimates. At the country level, BHAS is mandated to verify the accuracy of data received from the entities. However, due to the tight deadlines for publishing the data combined with occasional delays in data provision and other obstacles in data collection process (only aggregated data at 2-digit level), a thorough assessment of data is not routinely undertaken. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Discrepancies in intermediate data are investigated when they occur, and efforts are made to remove discrepancies. Other techniques for assessing results have been adopted more recently with the support of an IMF’s advisor. These include assessment of data by calculating ratios such as average salary per employed, share of intermediate consumption in output, share of total wages and salaries in output, value of purchased goods against value of goods sold are used to check accuracy of data produced. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Although, theoretically, the calculations of GDP by using different approaches should coincide, in practice, discrepancies arise due to differences in the data sources and the methods used. In the case of BiH, the discrepancy between the absolute values of the expenditure-based GDP estimates and the respective estimates of GDP by production have been stabilized at a level of 5 %. However, there are no significant differences in the nominal and volume growth rates between the two estimates. BHAS does not yet try to balance both estimates so that the discrepancy between the two approaches is published as it is. The reconciliation of the two estimates of GDP would be extremely important but is not feasible with the currently available information, in particular because of the lack of supply and use tables. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished non-confidential data are made available to the public upon request (usually in writing). Statistical institutions of BiH meet requests for additional information free of charge. If other users (i.e. faculties, institutes, individuals-students, etc.) have specific needs, they will submit the request for data with detailed description for which purposes they will be used. Prices are defined according to statistical institutions price lists published on their official web sites.
