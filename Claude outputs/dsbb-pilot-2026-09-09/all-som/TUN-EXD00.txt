 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: Tunisia's gross external debt position is compiled in accordance with the recommendations contained in the IMF's External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (2013 EDS Guide), the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual, Sixth Edition, (BPM6) and the Monetary and Financial Statistics Manual and Compilation Guide (MFSMCG2016). Definition: Tunisia's gross external debt position encompasses, at any given time, the outstanding amount of those actual current, and not contingent, liabilities that require payment(s) of principal and/or interest by the debtor at some point(s) in the future and that are owed to nonresidents by Tunisian residents (Conform with EDS 2013). The criteria used for determining whether a liability is a debt instrument or not: Tunisia's gross external debt position encompasses the amount outstanding of all actual current, and not contingent liabilities requiring future payment of principal and/or interest to nonresidents. External debt liabilities payable in goods and/or services are included in the external debt position. External debt data measure both domestic and foreign currency debt owed to nonresidents. Extent to which intercompany lending between entities in a direct investment relationship is included in the gross external debt position: It is included to cover both debt liabilities to affiliated enterprises and debt liabilities to direct investors. Coverage of short-term debt included in the gross external debt position: All external debt liabilities with an original maturity of one year or less. Extent to which arrears are included in the gross external debt position: It is included to cover unpaid amounts falling due in principal and/or interest. Positions in financial derivatives, equity securities, and equity capital are excluded from the gross external debt position. External debt includes securities issued in the domestic markets by residents and purchased by nonresidents. The nondebt securities, such as equity securities and equity capital, are excluded. Financial leases with nonresidents are treated as loans and included in the gross external debt position. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: Tunisia's gross external debt position is presented (with a minor difference) in accordance with the classification contained in the IMF's External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (2013 EDS Guide), the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual, Sixth Edition (BPM6) and the Monetary and Financial Statistics Manual and Compilation Guide (MFSMCG2016). Scope of the data Coverage of external debt: The gross external debt statistics cover all liabilities included in the international investment position (IIP) except for financial assets arising from equity holdings and other similar liabilities, in line with the recommendations of IMF's External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (2013 EDS Guide). Geographical coverage: The data cover the whole Tunisian economic territory, which reflects the concepts of residence and centers of economic interest as defined in the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual, Sixth Edition (BPM6). External debt data are compiled based on covering debt positions between residents and nonresidents. They encompass all Tunisian liabilities to nonresidents, in accordance with the residence concept contained in the sixth edition of the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual. 2.4.1 Valuation Unit of account: The data are expressed in millions of Tunisian dinars. They are converted at the exchange rates on the last business day of the reference period. Valuation: Apart from certain exceptions (market value or other), the assets are valued at their nominal value. 2.4.2 Recording basis Treatment of outstanding liabilities arising from periodic interest costs that have accrued and are not yet payable: they are included in the gross external debt position Those liabilities are not recorded as part of the value of the underlying debt instrument. The evaluation is based on the use of the accruing period, the interest rates and the outstanding amount. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources: Long-term loans and bonds issued on capital markets abroad: For all sectors, data on these liabilities are obtained individually from the external debt database managed jointly by the Ministry of Finance and the Central Bank. These liabilities represent the amount outstanding of debt disbursed, in terms of both principal and interest (accrued and not yet payable), as well as arrears. Direct investments: These data are derived from statements to the central bank, either from the banking system or via the annual enterprise survey. These data only cover loans between affiliated enterprises. Other liabilities: General government: information on these instruments is obtained monthly from the Ministry of Finance accounting data; Central Bank: information on these instruments is derived from the central bank monthly accounting data; Financial sector: information on these instruments is derived from the financial system's accounting data; Other sectors: commitments in the form of financial assets are derived from banking system statements and are reflected in the monetary statistics ; commitments in the form of nonfinancial assets are deduced from the balance of payments position. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Comments on various headings: Direct investments: this heading only includes loans between affiliated enterprises. The components of equity capital (equity securities or reinvested earnings) are not included. Portfolio investments: these liabilities correspond to money market instruments and securities and bonds (except for those issued on markets abroad). Other investments: these data fall under the following headings: Loans: this encompasses bilateral debt, multilateral debt, bank debt; Trade credit and advances; Debt securities issued on markets abroad; Special drawing rights (allocations) ; Currency and deposits " and " Other liabilities " : at present it is not possible to provide a breakdown of these headings between short- and long-term, for the Deposit-Taking Corporations, except the Central Bank. Data are not available to allow disaggregation of "other sectors" into three subsectors (Other financial corporations, nonfinancial corporations, and NPISH). Data are available to allow disaggregation into public sector guaranteed external debt and publicly guaranteed private sector debt, but only for the long-term loans. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Compilation follows the methods described in the IMF's External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (2013 EDS Guide) and the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual Sixth Edition (BPM6) as closely as possible. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request If Table 2 (SDDS encouraged table on domestic/foreign currency breakdown) is/would be prepared, the foreign currency external debt would include foreign currency-linked external debt; that is debt payable in domestic currency with the amounts to be paid linked to a foreign-currency.
