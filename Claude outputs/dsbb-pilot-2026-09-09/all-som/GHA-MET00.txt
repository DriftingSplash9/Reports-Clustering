 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Most import data are compiled by CEPS based on the Harmonized System with 10-digit. GSS uses the General Trade System, which covers all imports, to produce data for publication. Scope of the data Approximately 95 percent of general merchandise imported into Ghana is captured by CEPS. Smuggling is estimated to be rather small because of Ghana's inaccessibility of the land border crossing to motorized transportation. Exceptions to coverage In data processing, the transactions among the companies operating in the free zones and the transactions between these companies and non-residents, and the transactions between residents are excluded and treated as special transactions. 2.4.1 Valuation GSS's published data on imports are valued on a c.i.f. basis, while data on exports are valued on an f.o.b. basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The Ghana Statistical Services (GSS) compiles monthly, quarterly, and annual data on merchandise trade using data on non-oil imports and exports collected and compiled by the Customs Excise and Preventive Services (CEPS). Since 2003, CEPS has used the Ghana Community Network (GCNet), which is a new data capturing system, to capture the customs data collected by all major custom offices throughout the country. Data on crude oil imports are obtained from the Tema Oil Refinery on a monthly basis; data on energy imports and exports are provided by the Volta River Authority. GSS downloads the data from the GCNet in a standard format and uses a software developed by EUROSTAT known as EUROTRACE for processing the data.
