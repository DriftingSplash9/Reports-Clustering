 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Concept: This indicator shows the evolution and trend of national productive activity in the short-term. The National Institute of Statistics and Informatics (INEI) completed the base year revision for the national accounts in February 2014. The new base year is 2007 and is used to estimate the production index. The estimates are based on a framework similar to that of the National Accounts. ( System of National Accounts 2008 SNA 08 ). The global Production Index is obtained as a weighted average of the indices of the different sectors. The weighting factor is the annual percentage structure of the 2007 base year value added. Definition: The monthly production index is estimated by the INEI at constant prices using 2007 weights. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classifications: INEI and the International Standard Industrial Classification ( ISIC Rev. 4 ) are used for classifying economic activities. Scope of the data The index is derived from production volumes reported by the corresponding ministries. In particular, the Ministry of Production provides manufacturing data from 93 industrial sectors ( ISIC Rev. 4 , 4 digit level). Data for the other sectors are provided by the appropriate sectoral ministries. Coverage: The monthly Production Index data reported by the Central Reserve Bank of Peru (BCRP) are disaggregated into eight economic sectors: agriculture and livestock, fishing, mining and hydrocarbons, manufacturing (further divided into manufacturing in the form of raw materials processing and nonprimary manufacturing), electricity and water, construction, trade and services. The services sector includes product taxes and import duties. 2.4.1 Valuation GDP estimated using the production method is valued at constant prices, in accordance with the guidelines of the 2008 version of the ISIC. Indirect taxes are not considered as part of this activity. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The index is derived from production volumes reported by the corresponding ministries. · Agriculture and livestock: Data is collected nationwide regarding farms (temporary, permanent, and semipermanent; human and industrial consumption), producer prices, area sown and cultivated, area harvested, and productivity. Animal slaughter and livestock subproducts are also considered, along with agriculture and livestock's services. · Fishing: Fish extraction volumes are considered, categorized by type and use: for direct human consumption (fresh, frozen, canned, and cured), industrial consumption (fishmeal and fish oil) and for open-sea fishing and inland fishing for human consumption. · Mining and hydrocarbons: In the case of metallic mining, account is taken of the extraction of minerals such as copper, gold, zinc, silver, lead, iron, tin, and molybdenum. In hydrocarbons, data cover the extraction of crude petroleum and natural gas. Coverage is national and includes all enterprises in the formal sector involved in large-, medium- and small-scale mining. Since 2023, it also includes an estimation of the informal gold extraction activity. Oil and gas natural auxiliary extraction activities, as well as mineral extraction activities are also included. · Manufacturing: The data is gathered by the Office of the Vice-Minister of Industry in the Ministry of Production. Given the importance of primary activities, the data are broken down into manufacturing in the form of raw materials processing and nonprimary manufacturing. Primary industries are connected to the evolution of the agriculture and livestock, fishing, and mining and hydrocarbons sectors. · Construction: Estimations are conducted indirectly, based on domestic cement consumption and expenditure programs related to the construction, rehabilitation and maintenance of rural roads and highways. · Trade and services: This category encompasses commercial activities, financial services, insurance, transportation and communications, business and government services, restaurants and hotels, housing rentals, personal and housing services, health services, and private education. Estimates of trade and services are prepared by the INEI using direct methods and the indirect flow of goods method. Product taxes and customs duties: Estimated by the INEI using data provided by the National Superintendency of Tax Administration (SUNAT). Sources of weights: The national accounts and the input-output table with 2007 as base year. Item selection: The selected items are representative of each branch of productive activity and thus largely explain output value. Unit selection and sample sizes: Agriculture and livestock: Data is compiled monthly by the Agricultural Statistics Office of the Ministry of Agriculture. The sector is divided into two subsectors: agriculture, which considers a sample of 71 products; and livestock. Fishing: The General Office of Economic Studies and Impact Evaluation of the Ministry of Production compiles information on the extraction of all the industrial enterprises (census method) dedicated to the elaboration of products that use hydrobiological resources for human or industrial consumption. Mining and hydrocarbons: The General Office of Mining of the Ministry of Energy and Mining compiles information on extraction of 8 metals (copper, gold, zinc, silver, lead, iron, tin and molybdenum) and Perupetro (a state-owned firm that oversees the hydrocarbons sector) compiles information on 3 hydrocarbons (crude oil, natural gas and natural gas liquid). The coverage is nationwide and includes all formal enterprises as well as the informal gold extraction activity. Manufacturing: The General Office of Economic Studies and Impact Evaluation of the Ministry of Production compiles information on 93 industrial classes using a national sample of 3230 industrial establishments. Construction: The estimates are based on cement consumption and physical progress in road infrastructure (construction, maintenance, etc.) The information is provided by the Association of Cement Producers, the Ministry of Transport and Communications and the Ministry of Finance. · Electricity and water: The information is provided by enterprises dedicated to the services of water and electricity. Trade and services: The INEI calculates these using both the indirect flow of goods method and the direct method. The former accounts for the commercial margins and the demand for services from various economic sectors as well as of imports, based on the coefficients in the 2007 input-output table. The latter method is applied to those services for which there is adequate data coverage or proxy indicators of business evolution. Product taxes and customs duties: The INEI estimates product taxes (sales and excise taxes) and import duties using the flow of goods method. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Nature of indicators used: Output volumes, gross output value indices, and value added. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Periodicity of production observation: The basic data is compiled on a monthly basis. They are collected, starting from the third week of each month, from the statistics offices of various ministries (Agriculture, Production, Energy and Mines, Transportation and Communications; Housing and Construction), public and private sector producer associations, and the INEI. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Nature of weights: The weights and price structure used are based on the national accounts base year. For each activity, outputs are aggregated using base year prices, yielding to gross output value. Value added is calculated under the assumptioning that the ratio of a sector's value added to gross output value remains constant at the previous year's level. Frequency of weight updates: The previous base year was 1994, which has been updated to 2007. The results were published in February 2014 and have been updated to reflect the change in base year for the national accounts. Computation of the most detailed indices: The output of the various sectors is valued by multiplying the average base year price of each product by the volume of production. The index for each sector is then calculated by comparing the output value in the current month with the monthly average base year value. Aggregation: The overall production index is derived by calculating the weighted sum of the indices of the various sectors. The weighting factor is the annual percentage structure of the base year value added. Reference period: 2007 = 100. None
