 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Definition: Australia's external debt show the debt liabilities of all institutional sectors to the rest of the world. Analytical framework: Australia's external debt statistics are a subset of the International Investment Position (IIP), which is compiled in accordance with the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6). Criteria used for determining whether a liability is a debt instrument or not (i.e. the future requirement to make payments—principal and/or interest) following the External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (EDG) and BPM6. External debt liabilities payable in goods and/or services are included in the external debt position. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification system: External debt presents debt liabilities to non-residents in accordance with the requirements of the EDG. To comply with EDG, financial derivatives, which are classified as debt in Australia's IIP, are excluded. In addition to this presentation, Australia publishes transactions in and levels of debt in its quarterly publication Balance of Payments and International Investment Position, Australia with a detailed sector classification, together with detail on the domicility of the debt. 2.2.1 Scope Classification system: External debt presents debt liabilities to non-residents in accordance with the requirements of the EDG. To comply with EDG, financial derivatives, which are classified as debt in Australia's IIP, are excluded. In addition to this presentation, Australia publishes transactions in and levels of debt in its quarterly publication Balance of Payments and International Investment Position, Australia with a detailed sector classification, together with detail on the domicility of the debt. Scope of the data Geographic coverage: In Australia's external debt statistics, the external territories of Christmas Island, Cocos (Keeling) Islands and the Australian Antarctic Territory are regarded as part of the Australian economy. Debt liabilities of the offshore enterprises—including banks—that are resident in the reporting economy are included in the gross external debt position. Asset data for such enterprises also are disseminated in other releases. Coverage of activity: Australia's external debt shows the liabilities of all institutional sectors to the rest of the world. However, the borrowing activities of households may not be fully captured by existing sources and methods. External debt data compiled on the basis of covering debt positions between residents and non-residents. External debt presents debt liabilities to non-residents. Debt is allocated to the institutional sector of the enterprise which has a liability with a non-resident. External debt data measure both domestic and foreign currency debt owed to non-residents. Intercompany lending between entities in a direct investment relationship is fully included in the gross external debt position. The coverage of short-term debt included in the gross external debt position is all debt of original maturity of one year or less. Arrears are fully included in the gross external debt position, however, they are not separately identified within other liabilities. Positions in financial derivatives, equity securities, and equity capital are excluded from the gross external debt position. External debt includes securities issued in the domestic markets by residents and purchased by non-residents. Financial leases with non-residents are treated as loans and included in the gross external debt position. 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to unit of account: Transactions originally denominated in foreign currencies are converted to Australian dollars using prevailing market rates of exchange. Transactions are recorded on the basis of conversion using the mid-point of the buying and selling exchange rates on the day of the transaction. Valuation: Debt is valued at current market prices wherever possible. The nearest proxy valuation is accepted if full market valuation is not possible. 2.4.2 Recording basis Recording is on the basis of accrual accounting. Outstanding liabilities arising from periodic interest costs that have accrued and are not yet payable are included in the gross external debt position. The liabilities are recorded as being in arrears and included under other liabilities. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The main source of Australia's external debt data is the Survey of International Investment (SII) conducted quarterly by the ABS. This is an electronic form dispatched quarterly which collects data from enterprises. Enterprise consists of all related legal entities that are classified to the same resident institutional sub-sector. The SII obtains information about foreign financial assets and liabilities acquired as principal by financial intermediary enterprises, corporate trading enterprises and financial auxiliary enterprises, as well as any foreign financial assets and liabilities acquired using funds managed by the enterprise on behalf of other Australian enterprises, governments or individuals. Details of the international investment activity of the Commonwealth Government and the Reserve Bank of Australia are collected using separate forms which capture all of their balance of payments and international investment activities. Information is collected about foreign portfolio investment in Australian assets where the investment is held by a nominee enterprise in Australia on behalf of a foreign investor. Most of these data are collected on a security by security basis (through nominee collection and form 85L). In most cases, transactions and levels data are reported by transactors within the reconciliation format of the IIP. However, in some instances, such as foreign investment in tradable securities registered with domestic nominees, the transactions information is estimated by the ABS based on nominee holdings on behalf of non-residents which is reported quarterly, together with public information on price changes for the relevant security portfolios. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording External debt presents debt liabilities to non-residents in accordance with the requirements of EDG . However, in some instances, such as foreign investment in tradable securities registered with domestic nominees, the transactions information is estimated by the ABS based on nominee holdings on behalf of non-residents which is reported quarterly, together with public information on price changes for the relevant security portfolios. A wide variety of verification processes are adopted. As the source data are collected within the IIP framework, self-verification by the transactors is part of the reporting to the ABS. The data for cross-border transactions and investment positions are also confronted with data reported on total balance sheet positions and financial transactions in other ABS collections (for flow of funds and balance sheet presentations in the national financial accounts, including summary market statements) and in prudential reporting. Data are also routinely compared with company reports and press reports. Historical annual series, commencing in 2001 are released with each issue. Quarterly external debt data are shown for the full quarterly time series (from September quarter 1988). 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results All data submitted by businesses in surveys or through administrative sources are subject to rigorous editing procedures, verifying the validity of the data. Where available, data are also confronted with regulatory prudential information, company reports, and information gathered from the financial press. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Aggregates and individual business unit data are assessed for their plausibility and may result in further investigation of individual business data. These assessments include assessment with alignment with concepts as well as confrontation within the BoP framework and alignment with accounting identities and sensibilities. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs There is an ongoing program to confront reported data and aggregates to identify and remove causes of statistical discrepancies. This includes ensuring consistency across the macroeconomic accounts regarding the recording of large transactions. Staff involved in compiling the financial account monitor errors and omissions to confirm that no consistent biases are becoming apparent and that the magnitude of errors and omissions is not increasing over time. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request More detailed quarterly, annual statistics, and time series data are available on request via subscription from the listed contact person.
