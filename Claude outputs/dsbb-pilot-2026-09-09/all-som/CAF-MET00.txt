 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Trade statistics are, in theory, prepared in accordance with the Manual International Merchandise Trade Statistics: Concepts and Definitions , published by the United Nations Scope of the data The data are published in millions of CFA francs. Trade statistics cover “special” trade: merchandise entering the Central African economic territory for domestic consumption and merchandise fully or partly produced in the Central African Republic and definitively leaving the economic territory. The data are not seasonally adjusted. The data are published in the Bulletin Trimestriel de Conjoncture (Quarterly Economic Survey), showing the total value of imports and exports, and the balance; in the Bulletin Trimestriel de Statistiques (Quarterly Statistics Survey), showing imports and exports in weight and in value, by group of products, implicit price of some exports, and main partner country; and in the Annuaire du Commerce Extérieur (Trade Yearbook), showing imports and exports in weight and in value, by country, by product, by country x product, and by product x country, in the classification of the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS). Exceptions to coverage Coverage does not include transactions with a declared value of less than CFAF 5,000; merchandise sent for repair or processing; temporary shipments for use in the performance of work; merchandise brought into the country or exported, exempt of all duties and fees, to meet the needs of embassies and other nonresidents; and military equipment. Imports are broken down by product and by country of origin; exports, by product, and by the latest known destination. 2.4.1 Valuation Statistical value is the value of the goods when they cross the CAEMC borders in the case of imports, including c.i.f., and when they cross the national border in the case of exports, f.o.b. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The Division of Statistics and Economic and Social Studies (DSEES) in the Ministry of Economy, Planning, and International Cooperation is responsible for producing and disseminating trade statistics. Data on special trade are received from Customs and published quarterly for imports (CIF) and for exports (FOB). 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Adjustments are made with respect to certain products. For instance, wood data are derived from a comparison between the data provided by (i) the Ministry of Water and Forests, (ii) the monitoring company Société Générale de Surveillance, and (iii) customs declarations and payments provided by the revenue offices in Bangui and Berberati. As regards diamond exports, the data are provided by the Gold and Diamonds Consulting and Marketing Office (BECDOR) in the Ministry of Mines and Energy. Similarly, the data on coffee and cotton are reported, respectively, by the public coffee marketing office and the cotton company. The data on certain imports are cross-checked with the declarations of importers. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data None
