 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The gross external debt position is a subset of Germany’s overall financial relations with countries abroad. Data are disseminated in millions of Euro on gross foreign debt liabilities. The data are compiled in accordance with the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual ( BPM6 ) taking into account the requirements of the External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users ( EDG ) . The concept of residency applied in the German External Debt Statistics follows the recommendations of the BPM6 . 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization External debt presents debt liabilities to non-residents in accordance with the requirements of the External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users. The quarterly data are broken down by sectors (general government, monetary authorities, monetary financial institutes (MFIs) and “other sectors", such as financial and non-financial corporations), by debt instrument (loans and deposits, trade credits, debt securities and other debt liabilities) and by maturity (short term/ long term). Scope of the data Germany’s external debt shows the liabilities of the main institutional sectors to the rest of the world. In comparison to the liabilities side of the i.i.p. the quarterly external debt data do not include any financial liabilities arising from shares and other equity (investment fund certificates, equity capital and reinvested earnings within direct investment as well as real estate) and financial derivatives. Geographic coverage: The economic territory of Germany is defined in the Foreign Trade and Payments Act. Territory in this sense is the Federal Republic of Germany within its national borders and that part of the continental shelf which is part of the Federal Republic under international agreements. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: Tradable debt is valued at market prices. Conversion to unit of account: Bank liabilities are reported in original currencies and converted at spot middle rates on the reference date. Liabilities of enterprises are reported in Euro and are converted at rates which enterprises use in their internal accounting. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources: Germany's external debt data is compiled mainly on the basis of several stock statistics. The main data sources are: the monthly stock statistics on the external (assets and) liabilities of deposit taking corporations (part of banking statistics), enterprises and general government; the monthly securities holdings statistics (SHS) of securities deposited with German banks; the monthly securities issuances statistics of domestic securities; the monthly data from the Bundesbank accounting and other inhouse source the modified accumulation of b.o.p. transactions in cases where no source data for stocks are available; secondary / supplemental data sources. These sources allow for a sectoral classification between the monetary authority, other MFIs, general government and other sectors. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Direct investment: Debt instruments include inter-company loans only; related positions in securities between affiliates are not included here, but under portfolio investment. Inter-company loans (including trade credits) are reported in monthly stock statistics on the external assets and liabilities of domestic enterprises, which separately identifies loans between affiliated and those between non-affiliated enterprises. Exchange rates effects for all types of inter-company loans are estimated by the Bundesbank as the currencies of denomination are collected with the information on the foreign claims or liabilities. If an affiliate belongs to the MFI sector, positions are not included under FDI, but recorded as other investment. Portfolio investment: covers long-term debt securities (=original maturity of more than one year) and short- term debt securities (original maturity of up to one year). Since September 2018 the portfolio investment liabilities relating to debt have been calculated as real stocks from the "residual approach"; i.e. by subtracting residents' holdings of domestic securities from the overall issuances of German debt securities. The data for the total issuances are derived from the Bundesbank's securities statistics. The data for residents' holdings of domestic papers are available from the Bundesbank's securities holdings statistics which also provides the stock data for portfolio investment assets. All data used are based on security-by-security data collection. The change in compilation was introduced for data back to end-2015. Data prior to that date were derived from the modified accumulation of b.o.p. transactions (including regular revaluation price and exchange rate changes): (a) B.o.p. transactions by countries/regions, instruments and sectors were converted into "nominal" transactions using average market prices and exchange rates for the reporting period. (b) The "nominal" transactions were added to the "nominal" stocks (converted in the same way) of the previous reporting date. (c) Finally, the "nominal" stocks were re-converted using the end-of-period market prices and exchange rates. The category other investment takes into account information from the monthly stock statistics on the external assets and liabilities of domestic MFIs and of domestic enterprises, and general government. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The Bundesbank compares its data with counterpart data compiled by international organizations. CPIS data are used to correct the geographical allocation of German portfolio investment liabilities in the i.i.p.. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions are analysed in the context of checking i.i.p. data. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request In principle, external debt information is available vis-à-vis the rest of the world and vis-à-vis groups of countries. This information is, however, not regularly published. Customized tabulations and more detailed data is made available, on request. In most cases, the data are provided free of charge. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points In the Balance of Payments, International Investment Position division, a separate unit responds to users’ requests for information on the German balance of payments and i.i.p. statistics. The press office, which also maintains a central email account for queries addressed to the Bundesbank, forwards any external debt related questions to this unit. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The Monthly Report provides an overview of all Bundesbank publications. All relevant statistical publications containing data and methodological notes are also listed on the Bundesbank’s balance of payments website. Publications and time-series downloads as well as email-based requests are free of charge. Orders for regular subscriptions can be placed with the Bundesbank’s press service.
