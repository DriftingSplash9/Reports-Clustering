 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The analytical framework used for compiling monetary and financial statistics is based on the MFSMCG 2016. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification of financial instruments and the principles of sectorization applied in the monetary statistics compiled by the BOM are fully consistent with the guidelines of the MFSMCG 2016 . 2.2.1 Scope The classification of financial instruments and the principles of sectorization applied in the monetary statistics compiled by the BOM are fully consistent with the guidelines of the MFSMCG 2016 . Scope of the data Data comprise the accounts of the Bank of Mauritius. Exceptions to coverage Unrecorded activity Not Applicable. 2.4.1 Valuation In line with the general recommendation of the MFSMCG 2016 , the valuation of financial assets and liabilities is based on market prices or, for financial assets and liabilities that are not traded or traded infrequently, on market-price equivalents (fair values). Loans and deposits, however, are included in the statistics on the basis of book value, which includes the nominal value of the principal augmented by the accrued interest. This practice is consistent with the MFSMCG 2016 guidelines. In line with the MFSMCG 2016 recommendations, loans are reported on a gross basis, without netting of provisions for expected losses. Financial instruments denominated in foreign currency are converted into national currency values at the mid-point market exchange rate prevailing at the balance sheet date or, for transactions, at the transaction date. This practice is consistent with the recommendations of the MFSMCG 2016 . 2.4.2 Recording basis In accordance with the accrual accounting principles recommended by the MFSMCG 2016 , interest accruing on deposits, loans, and securities other than shares is incorporated into the outstanding amount of the underlying financial asset or liability. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Data are collected and compiled on a gross basis. The central bank accounts are compiled and disseminated on a gross basis. Reciprocal asset/liability positions between the BOM and the other depository corporations are separately identified. Consistent with the standard recommended in the MFSMCG 2016 , netting and consolidation adjustments are made only in presenting the analytical survey (the DCS). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The source data for compiling the central bank accounts are the accounting records (balance sheet) of the BOM prepared by the Accounting and Budgeting Division of the BOM. These reports are generated from an electronic general ledger system based on complete reporting. Source transactions are captured and the reports are generated from a predetermined classificastion of each account. The Balance sheet is presented in different levels of detail for various purposes and user groups. For the purpose of compiling monetary statistics, the BOM sectoral balance sheet is augmented by detailed schedules (Certificates of Balances). The data reported are based on the accrual accounting concept, in line with the IMF's Monetary and Financial Statistics Manual and Compilation Guide 2016. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data used for compiling monetary statistics provide a reasonably good approximation of the concepts, definitions, scope, classifications, and recording principles for compiling sound monetary statistics that are recommended in the MFSMCG 2016. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The data collection system allows sufficient time for the compilation of monetary statistics within the timeframe that is necessary to meet the SDDS timeliness guidelines. The accounting records of the BOM are provided to the Monetary and Financial Statistics Section within a week after the end of the reference month for purposes of monetary data compilation. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Automated procedures are used to facilitate the monitoring of the accuracy of data reported by the Accounting Department. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Compilation procedures rely on automated consistency checks to minimize processing errors such as coding, editing, and tabulation errors. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Given that data are fully based on accounting records, no estimation techniques are employed to adjust data for missing balance sheet items. Also, no seasonal adjustments are applied to monetary aggregates. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The quality of monetary statistics is monitored throughout the compilation process, which is automated. The source data from banks are subject to a number of built-in internal consistency checks and validation routines. Any inconsistencies and unexplained large data variations are investigated with the reporting bank. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Internal consistency checks are employed for ensuring consistency and quality of intermediate data. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The procedures for ensuring internal and intertemporal consistency that are built in the data processing and compilation system provide adequate safeguards against unexplained fluctuations and large distortions in monetary statistics. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Monetary statistics are considered final when published and rarely subject to revisions. Studies and analyses of revisions are carried out infrequently or ad hoc. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Given the infrequent revisions to monetary data, no formal revision studies and analyses are prepared and made available to the public. Even in the case of a major revision of the broad money data in the DCS as of the end of 2006, which was related to the modification of the national definition of broad money, no specific commentary was provided to explain this change in the definition of broad money and resulting data revisions for the past period. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished and non-confidential data are made available to users upon request. This policy, however, is not publicized and the BOM only occasionally receives user requests for historical and unpublished data. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Data users can contact the Director of the Economic Analysis & Research and Statistics Department as well as the staff of the Monetary and Financial Statistics Section for assistance. They are guided through phone or e-mail. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The Monthly Statistical Bulletin is posted on the website of the Bank as from December 1998 and as from July 2011 onwards hard copies are no longer disseminated to the public. Monetary statistics data are also disseminated in Annual Report and Monetary Policy Report.
