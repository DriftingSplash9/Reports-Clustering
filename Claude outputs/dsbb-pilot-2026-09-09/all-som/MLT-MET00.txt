 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Recommendations followed include: UN International Merchandise Trade Statistics: Concepts and Definitions (Series M, No 52, Rev. 2) 1998 and the updated version IMTS 2010. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Product classifications The Combined Nomenclature is the goods classification used within the EU for the purposes of foreign trade statistics. It is also used by the Directorate General for "Taxation and Customs Union" of the European Commission for customs duty purposes. The classification is maintained by Eurostat for the statistical aspects and by the Taxation and Customs Union Directorate-General for the tariff aspects. The product classification codes are based on the Combined Nomenclature (CN) which is based on the World Customs Organization's Harmonized System Code (HS). Headings in the CN are identified by means of an eight-digit numerical code the first six of which are in full agreement with the HS code. The very considerable numbers of subdivisions within the CN were introduced with the EU's specific customs and foreign trade statistics requirements in mind. The CN is revised annually and, as a Council Regulation, is binding on the Member States and appears in all of the EU official languages. Standard International Trade Classification (SITC Rev.4) used for aggregated data dissemination and analytical purposes. This nomenclature is managed by the United Nations. A link is available from SITC Rev.4 to CN. Use of other nomenclatures such as SDG which is part of the Standard International Trade Classification (SITC Rev 4). SDG stands for Section, Division and Group; and the United Nations Statistical Division's BEC code (Broad Economic Classification) for end-use classification and data dissemination purposes. Country and region classification The country nomenclature in use is the Geo-nomenclature (based on the ISO 3166 alpha - 2). Scope of the data Since Accession to the EU on May 1, 2004 Foreign Trade statistics in Malta have been made up of Extra and Intra trade statistics covering both non-EU and EU trade respectively. Foreign trade statistics are inclusive of both these concepts and are presented as complete to users. Up till the end of April 2004, Customs Declarations were the main source of all International Trade Statistics data. As from May 2004 the Customs Department collects mainly by electronic means, the basic data for non-EU Trade. At the end of each month an electronic extraction of customs declarations covering non-EU movements of goods across the customs territory of the Maltese Islands was transferred to the National Statistics Office (NSO). Intra-EU Trade data are collected by means of the Supplementary Declaration System. All transactions above the exemption threshold, established by the VAT Department and covering arrivals and dispatches of goods between Malta and the other EU Member States are to be recorded. No estimations of below threshold trade are carried out because of a low exemption threshold of €700 in place. Notwithstanding the fact that the VAT Department is the legal owner of the data derived from this System, the NSO is responsible for managing the Supplementary Declaration System. The Intrastat System, which was created as a means of collecting Intra-EU trade data came into operation on 1 January 1993 for EU Member States. Intrastat declarations are collected through a secure web based online system which can be accessed with a username and password on https://intrastat.nso.gov.mt/ The NSO is responsible for checking, analysing and preparing statistical reports for dissemination. Foreign trade can be classified as: General trade The goods that are imported directly into, or exported directly from, the free circulation area plus goods that are imported into warehouses or re-exported from warehouses are included in foreign trade statistics as used for national reporting and dissemination purposes. Special trade (relaxed definition) The goods that cross the boundary of free circulation area plus the goods that are imported for, or exported after, inward processing is included. All transactions between foreign countries and customs-bounded warehouses and commercial free zones are excluded. This format is adhered to when sending monthly External Trade data to Eurostat. In foreign trade statistics, information on quantities and value of imported and exported commodities are classified and described mainly by commodity types using the CN 8-digit code and other related nomenclatures, as well as by partner countries. Primarily the information that is collected for Intra-EU and Extra-EU foreign trade data includes the value, commodity type (CN8 and Taric), the quantity in kgs and/or a supplementary quantity, and trading partners which are considered as follows: For Imports: Country of Origin of the goods for External Non-EU Trade For Arrivals: Country of Consignment of the Traded Goods between EU Member States For Exports: Country of Final Destination for External Non-EU Trade For Dispatches: Country of Final Destination for Intra-EU Trade The treatment of specific movement of goods is as follows: Repair of means of transport - Not included. Supplies to own armed forces overseas - Not included. Industrial plant - Not included. Staggered consignments - Not included. Bunker supplies - Included. Ship's stores - Included. Joint productions - Not included. Postal consignment - Included. Vessels and aircraft - Included. Deep-sea fishery - Not included. Motor vehicle and aircraft parts - Included. Installations at sea - Not included. Military goods - Not included. Petroleum products - Included. Exceptions to coverage Goods and transactions excluded from the foreign trade data include: Direct transit trade Personal and household effects except those attracting duty, Fresh fish caught by Maltese fishing craft, Goods sent or received by diplomatic missions in Malta, Repairs, Services, Monetary gold, legal tender (securities, bank notes, coins in circulation), Advertising materials and commercial samples provided they are not subject to a commercial transaction, Temporary transactions where no processing is planned or made, and duration is no longer than 24 months, Emergency aid for disaster areas These exclusions are generally in accordance with the international recommendations and do not therefore represent an exception. 2.4.1 Valuation Imports/Arrivals are valued on cost, insurance and freight (c.i.f.) basis. Total exports/Dispatches are valued on a free on board (f.o.b.) basis. For non-EU trade, the transactions are recorded at Customs in the currency of transaction and converted to € at the exchange rate in use on the date of customs clearance. Data provided to the NSO is in € and includes information on the currency used. For Intra-EU trade, all Supplementary Declarations are required by law to be submitted in euro (€ ) after 1 st January 2008. 2.4.2 Recording basis Intra-EU and Extra-EU trade data are compiled on a monthly basis. The calendar month is the period within which the physical Imports/Arrivals and Total Exports/Dispatches of goods occur. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures No adjustments are made. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The main sources attributed to the collection of Foreign Trade Statistics are the following: Extrastat: collection via Customs Department The source of international trade data covering non-EU countries is the Customs Administration, which provides an electronic extract of customs declarations data to the NSO. The data are collected by an automated system which replaced ASYCUDA. The current system in use since May 2004 is designated as Customs Electronic System (CES/MEX) covering both Imports and Exports. This system handles all customs declarations where both importers and exporters are obliged by law to report all their transactions with non-EU countries using electronic data transmission. Intrastat: direct company declaration As described in 2.2.1.1 the Supplementary Declaration System (Intrastat) is used to collect trade data covering transactions between EU Member States. Arrivals cover goods that are shipped from an EU country or which are in free circulation with respect to EU Customs Law that are received in Malta, while dispatches record the same designated community goods that are sent to other Member States. Traders who annually exceed the exemption threshold of €700 are to submit their monthly declarations within 10 working days after the end of the reference month. Most traders follow the NSO's recommendation to declare on a per consignment basis. Failures to declare in time or not at all are checked and noted by the CfR Department which according to the VAT regulation can initiate a regulatory offence procedure which may result in a fine for defaulters. Data providers can submit their information Electronically through a web-based form. Traders opting for this practice will be provided with their own username and password that would allow them to compile and transmit their data in a highly confidential manner and within a very secure environment. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording As previously described. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness For data related to Non-EU trade the data are available and complete at the end of each month. Data are extracted electronically from Customs by the NSO. For the Intrastat system the data are entirely collected on line and are available earlier due to the prevailing per consignment usage of the system. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Validations at data source are carried out by the ITGS unit on the CES and Intrastat systems, on the most important data elements that are required. These include the verification and validation of the CN/Taric code, the country information, the value, net mass and more. Additionally ITGS staff review and analyse the plausibility of the data by checking the statistical value and weight of each commodity. Various other checks on data have been introduced since 2021. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Adjustments to improve coverage are applied to some categories of specific movements such as the inclusion of trade in sea vessels and aircraft which by their nature may never cross the local customs borders. Additional information is checked with national registers for ships and aircraft. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures No seasonal adjustments are applied. Quarterly volume indices are computed at the 1digit level of the SITC, Rev 4 data and totals. Customs and Intrastat data are processed at the NSO by means of the integration of data from various databases – Intrastat’s Supplementary Declarations and the Customs Electronic System. Some trans-codification of variables is necessary to fully integrate the data. Data validation is carried out in two main steps, first by checking of all records with high values. Checks are also carried out on selected product codes such as fuel, gold and currency notes, and certain Countries of Origin or Destination. As a second run all records are checked for correctness with various IT tools and statistical concepts. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Since 2022 , with the introduction of the Micro Data Exchange (MDE) system, asymmetry studies and non-compliance by local traders are continuously analysed and investigated, and action is taken where necessary to reduce the asymmetries as much as possible. The published results do not contain estimates for any trade flow. Intra-EU trade does not include any estimation of below threshold trade which is deemed to be low at €700. Invariably small traders with an annual trade under the threshold also declare. The data may be revised due to new or missing information sometimes due to non-response which are discovered by the ITGS unit. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data The Micro-Data Exchange (MDE) is used to carry out asymmetry issues. Since the introduction of MDE in January 2022, the ITGS unit is more able to identify asymmetries and non-respondents. However, the MDE exercise is still in its initial phases and still has numerous issues of inaccuracies to be solved, however, it is a good indicative tool. One of Malta's main drawback in resolving asymmetries with other member States is the level of thresholds in the other partner country which would include estimates for below the threshold trade with Malta in that particular Member State. Nevertheless asymmetry exercises serve to indicate where potential discrepancies can be discovered. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Micro-Data Exchange (MDE) has been introduced as from January 2022 with the purpose of being a tool to identify asymmetries between Malta and other EU Member States. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Studies and analyses of revisions are carried out on an ad hoc basis. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Revision studies and analyses are undertaken as needed in order to improve data quality and coverage. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Ad hoc reports for imports/exports data are disseminated (in various formats) by the Dissemination Unit and are subject to cost-recovery charges. Confidentiality of data suppliers is observed at all times. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The NSO’s Dissemination Unit is the point of contact for the Merchandise Trade data users. The contact details can be found on all releases. If this unit is unable to answer an inquiry, the user is directly referred to the International Trade Statistics Unit. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs International Trade news releases are available online in PDF and XLS from the NSO website . As from 2023 the International Trade News release commentary and Salient Points are also made available in the Maltese language. In 2024 an annual Trade in Goods Publication has been published and can be accessed from the Publications Section on the ITGS website .
