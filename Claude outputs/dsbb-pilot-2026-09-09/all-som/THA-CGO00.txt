 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytic framework: The data are compiled broadly in accordance with the methodology set out in the IMF's Government Finance Statistics Manual 2014 (GFSM 2014). The core analytic framework applied for the analysis of the central government sector consists of the following financial statements: · the Statement of Government Operations; The statement is still being implemented in the current analytical framework since most of the data are still available on a cash basis. The operating balance equals revenue minus expenses. The gross operating balance can be derived in the event that expenses do not include consumption of fixed capital (i.e., "GFS depreciation"). The net operating balance can be calculated if the consumption of fixed capital for all levels of government is included in the data. The net operating balance minus the net acquisition of nonfinancial assets equals net lending/borrowing. The overall fiscal balance is the net lending/borrowing deemed to be for public policy purposes. The overall fiscal balance is calculated as net lending/borrowing minus the net acquisition of financial assets. Typically, these are loans to (and repayments from) other units/sectors in the economy (including other public sector units), as well as the acquisition and disposal of shares and other equity. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: The statement provides the detailed classification of revenue transactions by type of revenue; expense transactions by economic type; nonfinancial assets by type of asset; and financial assets and liabilities by type of instrument. Scope of the data Institutional coverage: Central government: Consolidated Central government: budgetary operation; and extrabudgetary fund Exceptions to coverage Not applicable Unrecorded activity Not applicable 2.4.1 Valuation Flow data are recorded at market prices while available stocks—currently only liabilities—are valued at face value. Based on the migration path, valuation of stocks at market prices will be implemented over the next few years; first for liabilities, followed by financial assets, and then nonfinancial assets. Transactions in foreign currency are converted to Thai Baht using the mid-point exchange rate prevailing in the market at the time these transactions take place. The outstanding stock of debts are converted from foreign currency to Thai Baht using the mid-point exchange rate prevailing on the last working day of each corresponding month. 2.4.2 Recording basis Accounting basis: the data are available on both an accrual basis and a cash basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Grossing/netting procedures are consistent with the GFSM 2014, where all transactions are shown on a gross basis, except for transactions in financial assets and liabilities. Corrective transactions, such as refunds, are netted against the original transactions. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources: Monthly: Summary reports of budgetary transactions of central government and extrabudgetary funds. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording With a few exceptions, the source data reasonably approximate the definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording required by the GFSM 2014. The exceptions are discussed below. For all central government units, the source data follows the respective charts of accounts, which are based on the International Public Sector Accounting Standards (IPSAS). For annual central government data, the source data provides sufficient information to allow detailed classifications according to the GFSM 2014. The new budgetary central government chart of accounts has been developed in close collaboration with the GFS compilers, to ensure that adequate details are available for GFS compilation, including details to consolidate the various levels of government. For quarterly and monthly data, the available source data are sufficiently detailed to compile a complete Statement of Government Operations. Nonetheless, the central government source data have the following shortcomings in terms of scope, valuation, and time of recording (see also 3.1.1) because the migration to accrual accounting is not yet completed while several problems are experienced with the implementation of the Government Fiscal Management Information System (GFMIS): · Valuation and time of recording: Budgetary central government and local government source data are recorded on an accrual basis and a cash basis as well as available stock data are valued at face value, not market values. In addition, no information is yet available to compile all the GFSM 2014 statements and tables (e.g., a complete Balance Sheet and a Statement of Other Economic Flows); Detailed bridge tables exist between all the source data and the GFSM 2014 classifications, and, through these, compilers are aware of the differences and adjustments processes. The GFMIS has been developed to take account of these links between the source classifications and the GFSM 2014 classifications. Until the GFMIS is fully functional, the GFS compilers are using the bridge tables in MS Excel spreadsheet to compile the GFS data from the source data. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Central government source data becomes available in one month after the reference period. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The source data are routinely assessed. The FPO uses internal, automated consistency checks to verify the source data when they are reported. Because the FPO is a major source data user (not a compiler of source data), such data are normally consistent and accurate because they have already been verified at the administrative and accounting levels. The source data are also verified against the BOT data on central and local government financing. Through the GFS Committee and direct contacts, the FPO has regular access to the source data compilers to resolve any issues or questions about the source data. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Once the GFMIS is fully functional, the GFS compilation will be almost completely automated for the budgetary central government data. In the meantime, GFS compilation is done using MS Excel spreadsheets which have built-in formulas to verify internal consistency (both vertical and horizontal checks). All data are corrected when more accurate data become available (i.e., preliminary data are replaced with final data, with appropriate labeling, accordingly). The GFS compilation procedures are described in detail in a document—posted on the MOF and FPO websites—called Thailand's GFS: Information on Methodology, Data Coverage, and Compilation Practices. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The FPO cross-checks all the results, but in particular the financing data, with the BOT's Data Management and Analytics Department. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Through the GFS Committee, formal procedures are in place to resolve data classification and sectorization issues within the public sector statistics as well as across the various sets of macroeconomic statistics. Since only debt stock data for the consolidated central government is currently available, reconciliation between the stocks and flows is partially conducted for debt data. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Cross-checking technique: Net balance of lending/borrowing is equal to total financing, which is calculated as total transactions in financial assets minus total transactions in liabilities. In other words, the net balance can be cross checked with the result of revenue - expense - net acquisition of nonfinancial assets - net acquisition of financial assets + liabilities, which should be zero. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses As explained above, revisions primarily involve replacing preliminary data with final data, or estimates with actual (preliminary or final) data. If such revisions are significant, they will be explained in footnotes. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request The GFS data disseminated is comprehensive. Nonetheless, the FPO often responds to individual requests for data not regularly disseminated. Users may visit the GFS website to contact the FPO for such data request services. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The MOF and FPO websites include complete contact information for the contact points about GFS (names, telephone numbers, facsimile numbers, and e-mail addresses).
