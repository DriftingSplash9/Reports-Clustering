 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The data are compiles in million of Djibouti Francs (DJF) based on the special trade system, i.e., flows of goods are recorded using the residence criteria defined in the Balance of Payments Manual (BOP5). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Trade data are classified according to the Harmonized System (HS) 2002 and a classification by major group, specific to Djibouti, was prepared with the cooperation of UNCTAD. It includes: Food and beverages Raw and processed tobacco Khat Noncombustible minerals Hydrocarbons and derivatives Chemical products Plastics and derivatives Papers, books, journals Textiles and shoes Articles made of stone, cement, ceramic, glass Wood and wood products (other than furniture) Metals and metal products Furniture (made of wood or other materials) Miscellaneous machines and electrical devices Vehicles and transportation equipment Other miscellaneous merchandise Special transactions Scope of the data The data cover all transactions recorded by the customs declarations. External trade data are derived from customs declarations submitted to the General Directorate of Customs and Indirect Taxes of the Ministry of Economy and Finance, in charge of Industry and Planification (MEFIP). External trade data covers the “national territory” and is based on the special trade concept. The “national territory” includes enterprises and individuals that are resident on the economic territory of Djibouti and would exclude embassies, international or regional organizations, and foreign military basis established in Djibouti. Until now, only goods imported directly by foreign military basis and its personnel have been excluded from imports statistics. Goods imported by a resident enterprise and purchased by foreign military personnel are usually included in imports statistics and not treated as re-exports. 2.4.1 Valuation Imports are recorded at c.i.f. (cost, insurance, freight) value, while exports are recorded at f.o.b. (free on board) value. 2.4.2 Recording basis Data are recorded at the time they enter the economic territory of Djibouti (the date reported on the customs declaration). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs External trade data are derived from customs declarations submitted to the General Directorate of Customs and Indirect Taxes of the Ministry of Economy and Finance, in charge of Industry and Planification (MEFIP). 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Customs data are transmitted to the DISED every quarter within approximately one week from the end of the quarter. The Excel files contain roughly 10 to 30,000 declarations per month accounting for all movements of merchandise and sufficient data elements to enable the information to be processed. For example, all merchandise in transit is identified, including the cost of transportation and insurance. The file contains information on quantities and volumes of merchandise as well as the country of origin. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The data are transmitted to the DISED within roughly one week from the end of the reference quarter. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The source data are reviewed and validated. Certain corrections are made at the time of data entry, which is validated based on an analysis of the data and knowledge of the modes of importation. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures To track trends in the country’s import levels, the DISED also produces two series of indices (price index and quantity index, base year 1990) determined using the Laspeyres formula. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Time series data are reviewed and validated at the level of the HS-1 classification as well as at the level of the Djibouti’s key groups. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Data transmitted by Customs authorities are not revised.
