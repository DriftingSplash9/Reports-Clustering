 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical Framework: The balance of payments is prepared in accordance with the recommendations of the sixth edition of the Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) . The presentation of the balance of payments includes the current account, the capital account and the financial account. The current account is divided into four headings: goods, services, primary income and secondary income. The capital account shows credit and debit entries for nonproduced nonfinancial assets and capital transfers. The financial account records transactions that involve financial assets and liabilities. It is classified according to five functional categories: direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives, other investments and reserve assets. Finally, a line for net errors and omissions is added as a balancing item. Concepts: The balance of payments presentation incorporates the standard components specified in BPM6 . 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Goods . This heading covers all merchandise transactions whose economic ownership is changed between a resident and a nonresident. It is divided into three components: general merchandise, net exports of goods under merchanting and nonmonetary gold. Services . Twelve items are distinguished for services: manufacturing services on physical inputs owned by others, maintenance and repair services n.i.e., transport, travel, construction, insurance and pension services, financial services, charges for the use of intellectual property n.i.e, telecommunications, computer, and information services, other business services, personal, cultural, and recreational services, government goods and services n.i.e. Manufacturing services on physical inputs owned by others and maintenance and repair services n.i.e. are shown as new service items in line with the change of ownership principle. Transportation services are broken down by form of transport (sea, air and other) and by nature (transport of goods and transport of passengers). Also are included postal and courier services. For travel, transactions are classified as business or personal travel for receipts and expenses. The classification of insurance services is adapted to the recommendations of the BPM6 (direct insurance and reinsurance charges are considered as insurance services, net insurance premiums and nonlife insurance claims are included as secondary income). In accordance with recommendations of the BPM6, goods and services acquired locally by nonresident construction enterprises are included under construction and financial intermediation service indirectly measured (FISIM) are identified and included in financial services. Services supplied by "call centers" are classified according to the type of service provided. Primary income . The primary income item covers investment income, including reinvested earnings and rent of natural resources to another non-resident institutional unit. The investment income is broken down by functional categories. The primary income account records "pure interest" by eliminating the FISIM component from "actual interest". Compensation of employees is negligible and therefore not disseminated separately. Secondary income . Secondary income is broken-down as public and private transfers. Net nonlife insurance premiums and claim payments are introduced as secondary income. Capital account . In the Moroccan balance of payments, this account covers capital transfers as well as the acquisition or disposal of non-produced, non-financial assets. The personal effects, financial assets, and liabilities of persons changing residence are no longer covered by a capital transfer. Financial account . The column headings have been changed to net acquisitions of financial assets and net incurrence of liabilities (instead of credits and debits, respectively). Financial transactions cover direct investments (broken down by type of financial instruments) including reinvested earnings, portfolio investments, financial derivatives (which have been introduced as a separate item) and other investments (Other equity, currency and deposits, insurance, pension and standardized guarantee schemes, loans and Trade credit and advances, other accounts payable/recievable and special drawing rights -SDR-). Reserve assets . This heading records the change in reserve assets held by the central bank, due to balance of payment transactions (after taking into account price and exchange rate revaluations). 2.2.1 Scope Goods . Scope of the data The balance of payments records all transactions between residents and nonresidents in accordance with the methodology of the sixth edition of the IMF’s Balance of Payments Manual ( BPM6 ): the data disseminated covers imports and exports of goods and services, primary income, secondary income, direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives, other investment, and change in reserve assets. 2.4.1 Valuation The values used are those stated in customs declarations, bank settlements, and other documents. Valuations are carried out in accordance with the international methodological alternatives. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: The balance of payments records transactions primarily when the settlement takes place (or customs clearance for merchandises, actual payments for other operations). 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Current account items are recorded on gross basis whereas financial account items are recorded on net basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Balance of payments statistics are mainly compiled on the basis of an international transactions reporting system (ITRS) that captures balance of payments transactions. For transactions in goods, the information is provided by the customs administration (customs declarations filled by transactors at the time of each import or export operation). For payments between residents and nonresidents, the data are collected via a system that records international settlements resulting in purchases and sales of foreign currencies as well as movements of credits and debits on foreign currencies accounts held by residents. The source data come from the banks that are licensed to act as intermediaries and to conduct foreign exchange transactions. These data are provided to the FEO through regular and comprehensive reporting, regardless of the sizes of the payment. The data are verified, whenever it is possible, to ensure that they are consistent with information collected from banks and other operators. The data derived from these primary sources are supplemented by information from other sources: main operators, administrative departments (Ministry of Finance and Bank Al-Maghrib...), which are used to compile data on transactions relating to public foreign debt and reserve assets. Measurement of trade services relies on information that is reported both from ITRS, and on a variety of data sources, including administrative sources and enterprise surveys, and estimation techniques. Data on trade credit are collected through enterprise surveys. Such surveys are conducted on a sample basis. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Refer to headings 2.1.1, 2.2.1 and 2.3.1 above. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Customs declarations are submitted on a daily basis over a direct line between the customs stations and the FEO. Information on payments abroad is received every ten days or every month (depending on the medium used) with a lag of no more than ten days after the end of the reference period. These data reporting procedures are set out in the Decision of the Minister of Economy and Finance No.2840-09 and Instruction No. 05 of the FEO to the licensed intermediary banks. Arrangements are in place to ensure that all customs declarations and bank statements are received on time. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Many comparisons , analysis and cross-checkings are performed between data gathered from the various sources to improve coverage, including the comparison of data from settlements with those of statistical surveys. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The reception and processing of source data is computerized (using broadband line and secured protocols such VPN and Electronic Data Interchange). Computer controls are performed and these are supplemented by visual inspections to ensure data accuracy. The layout of the data reporting forms has been negotiated with the partners concerned. Verification procedures include checks for comprehensiveness, the validity of codes used, compatibility and plausibility, the exchange rate used, etc. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Customs declarations and bank statements are numbered by customs office and by banking institution, using a serial numbering system. This numbering system makes it possible to detect missing documents. The information taken from these documents is cross-checked against the data collected directly from the transactors and agencies concerned, as well as from Moroccan government offices. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Data from the ITRS are verified by crosschecking with information reported by other agencies or operators: for example, in the case of the public foreign debt, data on drawings and repayments are compared with those from the Department of the Ministry of Finance responsible for public debt management or with public enterprises and institutions. Data from the Department of Tourism on numbers of tourists are used to construct more details on travel information. Information reported in the press is used to monitor the realization of certain transactions, particularly in the field of foreign investment. The volume of external transactions reported by the banking system is also confronted with data on external assets and liabilities of banks obtained from monetary statistics. Data on trade credit collected from enterprise surveys are compared with those obtained from the ITRS. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The “net errors and omissions” item is monitored closely. For the ongoing work there is crosschecking between the different categories of data in order to identify possible sources of net errors and omissions, ie interest on private loans and the stock of private foreign debt, etc. Reconciliations with data from other countries and from international agencies are performed for trade in goods, under the program for harmonizing Moroccan statistics with those of his main partner. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions are carried out on an on-going basis until the data are finalized on an annual basis. In order to avoid a number of preliminary data, the FEO strives to incorporate periodically the revisions. The Balance of Payments Division relies on statistical staff to verify and crosscheck the information between the banks and that of the economic operators. These checks assist in incorporating the required corrections in a timely manner. The main revisions are conducted when there are major discrepancies between data.
