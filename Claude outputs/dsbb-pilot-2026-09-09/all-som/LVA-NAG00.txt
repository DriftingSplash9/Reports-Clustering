 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: Latvian National Accounts are compiled in accordance with the methodology set out in the European System of Accounts 2010 (ESA 2010). Definition: The annual and quarterly estimates of GDP are obtained by the production, expenditure and income approach. Following ESA 2010, GDP at market prices by the production approach (the main approach in Latvia) is defined as the sum of gross values added from all kinds of economic activities plus taxes on products and excluding subsidies on products. From the expenditure side, GDP is defined as the sum of total final expenditure at purchasers' price, gross capital formation, export of goods and services less import of goods and services. The GDP by the income approach is defined as the sum of compensation of employees, gross operating surplus and mixed income and taxes linked to production and imports less subsidies. The gross operating surplus and mixed income are derived as residual value. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification systems: Institutional sectors are classified according to requirements defined in the ESA 2010. Economic activities are classified using NACE Rev.2. Government expenditure is classified according to the Classification of the Functions of the Government (COFOG), individual consumption expenditure of households - according to the Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP 2018) and consumption expenditure of non-profit institutions serving households (NPISHs)-classification of the purposes of non-profit institutions serving households (COPNI). International transactions of the merchandise trade are classified using the Combined Nomenclature. Sectorization: Quarterly National accounts data by institutional sectors are produced only for the General Government (S.13) and the Rest of the World (S.2) sectors. Annual National accounts data are produced by institutional sectors S.11, S.12, S.13, S.14, S.15 and the Rest of the World (S.2) sector. 2.2.1 Scope Classification systems: Institutional sectors are classified according to requirements defined in the ESA 2010. Economic activities are classified using NACE Rev.2. Government expenditure is classified according to the Classification of the Functions of the Government (COFOG), individual consumption expenditure of households - according to the Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP 2018) and consumption expenditure of non-profit institutions serving households (NPISHs)-classification of the purposes of non-profit institutions serving households (COPNI). International transactions of the merchandise trade are classified using the Combined Nomenclature. Sectorization: Quarterly National accounts data by institutional sectors are produced only for the General Government (S.13) and the Rest of the World (S.2) sectors. Annual National accounts data are produced by institutional sectors S.11, S.12, S.13, S.14, S.15 and the Rest of the World (S.2) sector. Scope of the data Transactions coverage: Coverage includes all activities within the ESA 2010 production boundary. Geographical coverage: The Latvian national accounts cover the economic territory of the Republic of Latvia. Unrecorded activity Estimates are made for non-response, non-registered units, under-reporting to fiscal administration, income in kind, tips, illegal activities (prostitution, narcotics, smuggling of tobacco, alcohol and fuel), VAT fraud, etc. The ratios of the level of unrecorded activity to the total are diversified by the various institutional sectors, activities and GDP components. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation is performed according to ESA 2010 guidelines: output and gross value added are valued at basic prices, output for own final use - at basic prices except for agricultural production for own use of households, which is evaluated at purchasers' prices at which it could be sold if offered for sale, non-market output - as the sum of costs, intermediate consumption and gross fixed capital formation - at purchasers' prices, export and import of goods - at f.o.b. at the border of the exporting country. Components of GDP by production and expenditure approach are produced at both current and constant (previous year price, chain linked) prices. GDP from the income approach is produced only at current prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: The collected statistical data are predominantly available on an accrual basis following business accounting regulations. The administrative data for the government sector are available on a cash basis, which for the purpose of compilation of government sector indicators are adjusted to an accrual basis. A one-month time-adjusted cash method is applied to all main tax categories and social contributions. After the reconciliation with annual national accounts' figures, they are revised. Recording period: Calendar quarters or year. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Grossing/netting procedures are made in line with the ESA 2010 guidelines. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Production approach: The main sources for calculating the quarterly GDP are: the quarterly information from the Institute of Agricultural Resources and Economics, monthly and quarterly reports on agricultural production and prices (CSB), monthly volume indices of industrial production and the absolute values of output in terms of value (Mining and quarrying, manufacturing, electricity, gas, steam and air conditioning supply), quarterly volume index of construction production (construction), monthly retail trade statistics, monthly wholesale statistics, quarterly business trends in Forestry and logging, Transportation and storage, Accommodation and food services, Information and communication, Real estate activities, Professional, scientific and technical activities, Administrative and support service activities, Education (market services), Human health and social work activities (market services), Arts, entertainment and recreation, Other service activities, quarterly labour statistics about the number of employees and wages and salaries, quarterly information from Latvijas Banka (BoP, Quarterly Financial Report 1-FP, Profit or Loss Account of Latvijas Banka, the monetary financial institutions' financial report (Profit or loss statement and balance sheet positions), Profit or loss account of investment brokerage firms and investment management companies, Profit or loss account of pension funds, etc), Ministry of Finance of the Republic of Latvia (taxes on products), the principal sources of general government financial statistics are monthly and annual reports of the Ministry of Finance on the implementation of state and local government budgets and statistical form information on the activities of state and local government-controlled enterprises. Quarterly business statistics are based on survey data (information is collected from large units - net turnover and number of employees are determined as the main criteria). Statistical information provided by Latvijas Banka (information on credit institutions, insurance companies, and pension funds) is used to evaluate the output and intermediate consumption of financial sector. The main data source for non-market activities is monthly-based general government expenditures on central and local government. The main annual data sources for the GDP production approach: the statistical survey ‘1-Annual', collected from business enterprises, the "Annual Report on Central Government Budget Execution and on Local Government Budgets of the Republic of Latvia" is used to estimate components of the General government sector, administrative records on central and local government units are used to estimate components of the general government, data on non-profit institutions serving households are obtained from administrative records on an annual basis, Information on credit institutions, insurance companies, and pension funds provided by Latvijas Banka is the main data source for the measurement of Financial sector components, information from the Institute of Agricultural Resources and Economics, General Government Consolidate Budget Execution from the Treasury provides information about taxes on products and subsidies. Expenditure approach: Household Final Consumption Expenditure (HFCE) is compiled using several data sources: annual data from the Household Budget Survey (HBS), retail trade statistics, additional sources (mainly for services), and model-based estimates using the commodity flow (CF) method. Because of low response rates, HFCE is currently not primarily based on HBS data. Instead, each HBS result is reviewed individually to assess how reliable and usable it is. Although HBS data are not used in the final calculation of HFCE by consumption groups, they remain important for analytical purposes and for comparing detailed results from other data sources at the ECOICOP/COICOP 2018 group level. Final consumption expenditure of NPISHs is compiled using output and sales data obtained from the administrative records of the State Revenue Service. These data are available only on an annual basis. Quarterly estimates are derived by dividing the most recent annual data by four. Final consumption expenditure of general government is primarily compiled using monthly reports from the Treasury of the Republic of Latvia. For enterprises that have been reclassified from the non‑financial corporations sector to the general government sector (based on the 50% financing criterion), quarterly data are obtained from surveys collected by the Central Statistical Bureau (CSB). Annual data are compiled using information from the "Annual Report on Central Government Budget Execution and on Local Government Budgets of the Republic of Latvia."; Gross fixed capital formation (GFCF) is primarily compiled using data from the quarterly survey "Report of Investment," which collects information from large enterprises. Additional data sources are used to improve estimates, particularly for dwellings, including quarterly information on newly completed residential buildings and the house price index. Annual data on GFCF for non‑financial and financial corporations are obtained from the annual sample survey of non‑financial investment and construction. For general government, administrative records from central and local government units provide supplementary information used to estimate GFCF components. For non‑profit institutions, administrative data from the State Revenue Service are used. Changes in inventories: For changes in inventories only annual information is available. Quarterly estimates are derived as a residual item, given the absence of dedicated quarterly inventory information. Exports and imports of goods and services: The total value of exports and imports of goods and services is sourced from the Balance of Payments, compiled by the Latvijas Banka. Income approach: Compensation of employees: the main data source for calculating wages and salaries, as well as employers' social contributions, is the quarterly Enterprise Survey. Additional information is obtained from the State Revenue Service, the Annual Report on the Central and Local Government Budget Implementation, the Labour Force Survey, the Annual Survey on Enterprise Activity, the Treasury and Latvijas Banka. Taxes on production and imports, subsidies: information on taxes on products, other taxes on production, and subsidies is taken from the monthly and annual General Government Consolidated Budget Execution reports provided by the Treasury. Quarterly data on subsidies received from the European Union are sourced from the Balance of Payments. Operating surplus and mixed income are balancing item in the quarterly accounts. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Definitions, scope and valuation of source data are based on international bookkeeping standards and European Regulations. International and European classifications are used as well. The time of recording is on an accrual base. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness In general, source data are in time for the regular quarterly estimates in T+2 months. For flash estimates 29 days after the reference period information only of two months of the respective quarter for some branches is available. (Publication of the GDP flash estimate on the 29th day after the end of the relevant reference quarter is temporarily deferred. The first annual figures on National Accounts are released 2 months after the end of the reporting year as a sum of quarters. The first Annual Sector Accounts (mainly based on short-term sources) are released 9 months after the end of the reporting year. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The first routine assessment of the quarterly source data is the annual figures. The second and main process is the balancing procedure in the Supply and Use Table. As far as necessary, the results of these assessment procedures are given as feedback to the source statistics. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Production approach: The value added at current prices is compiled using benchmarking and extrapolation with various turnover and volume indicators. Gross domestic product at market prices is defined as the total value added of industries plus taxes on products and excluding the subsidies on products. Constant prices. Components of various industry groups are deflated using producer price indices (PPIs) in industry, consumer price indices (CPI), export unit value indices, import price indices, services producer price indices, and construction cost indices. Extrapolation with volume indicators is used where price indices are lacking or are not applicable. Direct volume projection is used for transport, and fishing, while input indicators (changes in the number of employees) are used for non-market services and part of market services. Constant prices are evaluated at the previous year's prices and chain-linked volumes with the reference year of 2020. Adjustments to the source data: conceptual and exhaustiveness adjustments are made for annual data. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Reconciliation: As the GDP estimate from the production approach is considered as the main approach of GDP, the changes in inventories are adjusted to bring the GDP from the expenditure approach to the level of the estimate from the production approach. In 2019 CSB revised its benchmarking method from pro-rata to the proportional Denton method based on the "Denton-Cholette" algorithm. Seasonal adjustment: The CSB uses a model-based seasonal adjustment procedure TRAMO/SEATS implemented in the software JDemetra+. The seasonal adjustment in quarterly national accounts (QNA) is made and updated whenever a new observation is added to the time series. All time series are directly adjusted, which means that the QNA aggregate and each of its components are seasonally adjusted separately. QNA seasonally adjusted series are published at both current prices and chain-linked volumes. Geographic data: Quarterly GDP data refer exclusively to the Republic of Latvia as a whole. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results During the compilation of quarterly GDP, intermediate results undergo validation by sector and branch specialists. Particular attention is given to ensuring coherence between price and volume developments, verifying the time‑series comparability of estimates, and identifying any irregularities or inconsistencies. During the balancing process, experts actively participate in making the necessary adjustments to ensure consistency across the entire national accounts framework. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data The balancing process of the annual national accounts helps identify and revise weaknesses in the quarterly data. These issues mainly relate to the absence of detailed intermediate consumption information and the under‑coverage of gross fixed capital formation (GFCF) and household final consumption (HFC). 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs There are no discrepancies between estimates of GDP from production, expenditure, and the income approach. However, a certain amount of statistical discrepancy between financial and non-financial accounts due to different reasons is unavoidable. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revision analyses for GDP and the main expenditure components are done on both a quarterly and annual basis. The annual and quarterly revision triangles were established to accumulate the size of revisions of the main indicators as well as the reasons behind them. These triangles are published on the Official Statistics Portal: Quarterly , Annual . 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Detailed documentation on major revisions is provided in different media: as a methodological note in the metadata section of the Official Statistics Portal website, as a link to the note in the GDP press release, and as dedicated seminars to main users. The main users of national accounts data have become acquainted in advance with revisions related to major changes in the methodology before the change is implemented. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Key statistics are available on the website of the Official Statistics Portal .
