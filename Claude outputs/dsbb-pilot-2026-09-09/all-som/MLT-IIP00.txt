 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The compilation of International Investment Position (IIP) statistics is broadly in line with the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual ( BPM6 ) concepts. Resident institutional units are defined in conformity with BPM6 guidelines and these include special purpose entities. The IIP is a statistical statement that shows at a point in time the value and composition of financial assets and liabilities on a separate basis which are sub-divided under the functional categories: Direct investment Portfolio investment Financial derivatives Other investment Reserve assets 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Institutional units are classified and attributed to the relevant sector according to the BPM6. No distortion has been identified. The Central Bank of Malta stands for the monetary authorities; the deposit-taking corporations comprise the deposit money banks, the international banking institutions and money-market funds; the General Government sector comprises the central government, the EBUs, and the local councils. Other sectors have been sub-divided as financial and non-financial entities. Scope of the data All resident/non-resident positions as specified in the BPM6 are covered in IIP statistics in principle. Exceptions to coverage Unrecorded activity Illegal activities are not recorded. 2.4.1 Valuation On a general basis, stocks are stated at market price. The questionnaires sent by respondents distinguish between changes due to transactions and revaluations due to market price and exchange rate variations. The valuation criteria used for equity securities are market values, but book values are used for equities not listed on the Malta Stock Exchange (MSE). All other direct investment instruments such as intercompany loans are recorded at book value (using end-of-period exchange rates). Trading securities are valued at the lower book value or market value, but investment securities are based on book value. 2.4.2 Recording basis Data on stock positions of loans, securities and deposits are recorded according to the accrual principle. Positions of direct investment include reinvested earnings. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The financial account is compiled on a gross basis but net stocks are automatically calculated by the compilation system, with assets and liabilities being compiled separately. Direct investment is compiled similarly according to BPM6 guidelines. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The IIP compilation relies on survey data collection and administrative data sources. Direct Investment: positions are compiled using survey data of both financial and non-financial enterprises as well as the Tax Index of Financial Data (TIFD). Portfolio Investment: positions are compiled using survey data of both financial and non-financial enterprises as well as the Tax Index of Financial Data (TIFD). Purchases of domestic equity and debt securities by non-residents on the Malta Stock Exchange (MSE) are reported directly by the MSE. Other Investment: positions are compiled using survey data of both financial and non-financial enterprises as well as the Tax Index of Financial Data (TIFD). Financial derivatives: positions are compiled using survey data of both financial and non-financial enterprises. Reserve Assets: Data is provided by the Finance Office at the Central Bank of Malta. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording As is generally the case for direct reporting, source data correctly approximate the desired definitions, classifications, valuations, and time of recording. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The timeliness is mainly determined by international requirements of the ECB, Eurostat and IMF. For survey data collection, reporting agents are provided with a deadline that balances the needs of both parties, giving IIP compilers sufficient time for analysis and compilation while ensuring agents have ample time to fulfil their reporting obligations. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The reporting tool used by reporting agents in most financial sectors incorporates various validation checks to ensure the input data is as accurate as possible. The source data for the rest of the financial and non-financial sectors (surveys and administrative sources), are assessed using self-developed tools designed to identify errors or outliers. When discrepancies or anomalies are detected, reporting agents are contacted to revise or confirm the figures as needed. Additionally, where feasible, IIP data is cross verified annually against the financial statements. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Survey data is designed to fit directly into the IIP framework, and do not normally require specific statistical techniques , while data from administrative sources are mapped into the framework using a pre-determined mapping. Data are grossed up where necessary and data for non-respondents are imputed. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Intermediate results are validated using several tools. Information provided directly by reporting agents is checked against financial statements when available and where necessary. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Inconsistencies within data submitted by respondents are immediately investigated directly with respondents. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Errors and omissions are closely monitored. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions in IIP data are unavoidable due to timeliness of administrative sources, late submissions by reporting agents, revisions of initial estimates and methodology improvements, amongst other reasons. Revisions are monitored on a quarterly basis as part of the compilation process, but no research studies are done. When significant revisions are made, the NSO provides comments on these revisions through press information notices and meetings with the users. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Unless the revisions are substantial, no explanation is provided on the nature of the revisions. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request When available, international investment position data that are not disseminated and are not considered confidential can be made available to users. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points There are two contact points: first, the NSO’s Dissemination and Communications Unit, the reference of which is indicated in the NSO’s News Release and second, the NSO’s official website, which contains a specific module where users must fill in a request form; this is subsequently channelled to the relevant technical unit of the NSO. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The Balance of Payments and international investment position data are easily accessible through the NSO's official website which provides comprehensive information on how users can contact the NSO for assistance.
