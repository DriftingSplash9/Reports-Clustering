 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework and concepts: The data on central government operations (income, expenditure, deficit/surplus, financing) are organized for analytical purposes according to the 1986 GFSM system of definitions and classifications. Likewise, the data is exposed as established in the Argentine budget classification manual approved by Resolution No. 304/22 Relationship with international guidelines: The data published in the SDDS follow the basic guidelines of the GFSM 1986 . We also are working in a new budget classification manual order to follow the basic guidelines of the GFSM 2014. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification System: Income is classified as either current or capital income. Current income consists of tax and non-tax income. Capital income is derived chiefly from sales of assets, sales of shares, and loan repayments. Expenditures are grouped according to the economic classification, i.e., by differentiating between current and capital expenditure. Financing consists of the difference between financial sources and uses. The chief component of the financial sources category is debt, while for uses it is debt repayment. Scope of the data Institutional coverage of central government in reported data: Central government comprises the budgetary accounts, the extra-budgetary accounts, and social security. The budgetary accounts encompass the Legislature, the Judiciary, the Office of the President of Argentina, and the ministries and secretariats of the national executive branch. The extra-budgetary accounts include government agencies. Social security covers the National Social Security Administration, the Federal Police Retirement and Pension Fund, and the Institute for Financial Assistance for the Payment of Military Retirement Benefits and Pensions. Exceptions to coverage 2.4.1 Valuation Accounting basis: Income and expenditure published on the Argentine NSDP are recorded on a cash basis. Complementary period: No complementary period is used. 2.4.2 Recording basis Accounting basis: Most central government transactions are recorded on a gross basis. Income and expenditure published in the SDDS are recorded on a cash basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources: The source of the data published is the Office of the National Accountant-General and the General Treasury of Argentina. The data are based on the actual accounting records of the various units and are recorded on a cash basis. The data are presented on a consolidated basis, with the transactions between the various sectors eliminated from the data. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Adjustment for grossing and netting of transactions: Most central government transactions are recorded on a gross basis. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Consolidation: The data are presented on a consolidated basis. Consolidation procedure consists of eliminating transfers between the various sectors of the central government and recording them in a different category defined as expenditure and representative contributions, depending on the sector of origin or use, respectively. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results 5.1.5 Dissemination on request
