 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The French external debt is the outstanding amount of the liability side of the French international investment position excluding equities and financial derivatives. The French external debt is compiled by Banque de France according to the conceptual framework of the External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users . The French external debt is broken down by institutional sector - general government, monetary authorities, monetary financial institutions (MFIs) and other sectors - , by maturity (short and long term on an original maturity basis) and by type of debt instrument (debt securities, trade credit, loans, currency and deposit). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification system: The French external debt data are classified according the standard components identified in the BPM6. Sector classification: four sectors (general government, monetary authorities, banks, and other sectors) can be separately identified. 2.2.1 Scope Classification system: The French external debt data are classified according the standard components identified in the BPM6. Sector classification: four sectors (general government, monetary authorities, banks, and other sectors) can be separately identified. Scope of the data Instrument coverage : The criteria to be used for determining whether a liability is a debt instrument or not (i.e., the future requirement to make payments—principal and/or interest) will be based on the futurerequirement to make payments- principal and/or interest.The maturity of each debt instrument is known through our collecting system. The short-term/long-term classification is based on the maturity at issuance.For debt instruments issued by residents abroad, the part held by residents is reported by custodians on a security by security (s-b-s) basis. The part held by nonresidents is calculated as the difference between the total amount issued by resident and the amount held by residents. This information is completed by a quarterlysurvey (s-b-s) of resident banks which reports French securities issued on international markets and held by residents in securities accounts opened in resident banks. Transaction coverage : external debt liabilities payable in goods and/or services are not included in the external debt position. Intercompany lending between entities in a direct investment relationship is included in the gross external debt position to the extent that “Other capital” included in direct investment istaken into account in the external debt position. Coverage of short-term debt is included in the gross external debt position. Intercompany lending between entities in a direct investment relationship is separately identified in the total external debt position. In the case of outstanding liabilities arising from periodic interest costs that have accrued and are not yet payable are included in the gross external debt position,securities are covered, but not loans (deemednot very significant in terms volume for France). Institutional sectors : The French external debt covers all sectors.External debt data are compiled on the basis of covering debt positions between residents and nonresidents.External debt data measure both domestic and foreign currency debt owed to nonresidents. External debt liabilities are attributed to the sector of the immediate borrower and they areclassified under the sector category of the debtor and not of any guarantor.Monetary authorities and general government items are clearly identified; in the present compilation system,the disaggregation of "other sectors" into three subsectors (nonbank financial corporations, nonfinancial corporations, households and NPISH) is not possible. Data is not available to allow disaggregation into public sector external debt and publicly guaranteed private sector debt. While there is no available information on external debt liabilities of households, the amount are deemed not of a significant magnitude. Debt liabilities of the offshore enterprises —including banks—that are resident in the reporting economy are not included in the gross external debt position. “The private sector” debt position is included but not separately identified.External debt data are collected for small enterprises (stocks and flows). Coverage of external positions : The coverage of liabilities is in line for most of financial instruments with the guidelines provided by the IMF. The French external debt is the outstanding amount of the liability side of the French international investment position excluding equities and financial derivatives.Deposits of nonresidents with domestic banks are included andtrade credits owed to nonresidents are included, while positions in financial derivatives, equity securities, and equity capital are excluded from the gross external debt position.External debt includes securities issued in the domestic markets by residents and purchased by nonresidents. Financial leases with nonresidents are treated as loans and included in the gross external debt position . No arrears are included in the gross external debt position, thus arrears cannot be separately identified under "short-term, other debt liabilities". Geographical Coverage : The economic territory of France for the external debt statistics includes metropolitan France, the Overseas Departments (Martinique, Guadeloupe, Guyane, Réunionand Mayotte) and territorial units(Saint Pierre et Miquelon, Saint Barthélemy and Saint Martin) to which is added the Principality of Monaco. Exceptions to coverage Instruments coverage : Traded debt instruments issued by residents abroad, but in hands of residents are excluded from the gross external debt position. Foreign currency external debt does not include foreign currency-linked external debt; that is debt payable in domestic currency with the amounts to be paid linked to a foreign-currency. 2.4.1 Valuation Following guidelines of the BPM6, stocks are valued at market prices, as far as they are available. When a stock is calculated by adding a flow, the stock of the previous end-reference period and the flow are, as far as possible, revalued with exchange rate and stock exchange changes. Portfolio investment: The quarterly stock includesthe value of accrued interest, with stocks and flows both valued with an index. Conversion to unit of account: The exchange rate prevailing on the day to which the external debt relates is used to convert foreign currencies into the unit of account. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Quarterly stocks are collected for monetary authorities, MFIs, and partly for other sectors (trade credits) and for general government. Debt securities: External debt data on portfolio investment cover bonds and notes and money market instruments. Stocks are compiled from a quarterly survey of resident banks which report notably on the portfolio held on behalf of their non-resident clients. A very limited number of firms report directly on money market instruments on a yearly basis. Data are also collected quarterly from clearing houses for public debt instruments. Other investment: Quarterly stocks of monetary authorities are directly reported by Banque de France. Stocks data of the general government sector are partly reported on a monthly basis by the reporting agencies and partly estimated with the balance flows. Quarterly stocks of monetary and financial institutions (MFIs) are derived from banks’ and monetary mutual funds’ balance sheets. For the other sectors, stocks are compiled from an annual survey of companies and flows are derived from the quarterly Balance of Payments. Stocks of trade credits are reported on a quarterly basis from a stock survey of a sample of firms. Intercompany lending: Data on inward direct investment stocks of other capital are collected through an annual survey. Flows are derivedfrom the quarterly Balance of Payments. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Portfolio investment: Custodians are responsible for reporting quarterly securities stocks, security by security with the indication of the ISIN code. Other investment: On a sectorial basis, quarterly stocks are available for monetary authorities, MFI and partly available for general government. In addition quarterly stocks are available for trade credits. For the other components of the other investment, quarterly stocks are the result of an aggregation of stock plus quarterly flow. Intercompany lending: Quarterly stocks are the result of an aggregation of stock plus quarterly flow. None
