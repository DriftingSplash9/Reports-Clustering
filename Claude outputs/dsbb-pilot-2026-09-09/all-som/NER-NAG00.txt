 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions National accounts track the production and flows of goods and services by the various national economic actors. The Concepts and definitions are harmonized in accordance with the System of National Accounts (SNA 2008), the AFRISTAT methodological guide, and international classifications of economic activities. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The Nomenclatures: NAEMA-rev1 and NOPEMA are adapted to the National Nomenclature of Activities and Products (NAP-Niger) and are also inspired by the CITI-rev4. This harmonization facilitates the comparison and analysis of economic data at the national and international level, ensuring consistency in the classification of economic activities. 2.2.1 Scope The Nomenclatures: NAEMA-rev1 and NOPEMA are adapted to the National Nomenclature of Activities and Products (NAP-Niger) and are also inspired by the CITI-rev4. This harmonization facilitates the comparison and analysis of economic data at the national and international level, ensuring consistency in the classification of economic activities. Scope of the data It covers all sectors of the national economy. Exceptions to coverage The economic sectors not covered are those of the shadow economy (drug trafficking activities, begging, etc.). Unrecorded activity Online commerce activities and other data on social networks, etc... The SCN2025 can provide more detail on activities not concerned. 2.4.1 Valuation Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is developed using three approaches: the production approach; the expenditure approach; the revenue approach. It is valued at current prices and at constant prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis The National Accounts take into account the flows and transactions of economic actors at producer prices and at acquisition prices. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The sources are collected annually and include: the Public Administration; Household Surveys; specific surveys; Non-profit institutions (NPOs); Financial and non-financial enterprises; Merchandise trade and Balance of payments. This exhaustive inclusion allows for a complete overall view of the economy. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording After each rebasing or revaluation of GDP, the data are restated in accordance with the methodology and international standards of the 2008 National Accounts (NAG). The data are only comparable for the period 2000-2022 for which restatement work has been carried out. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Each year data are collected from institutional sectors (Households, Public Administration, NPOs, The Rest of the World, financial and non-financial corporations for the elaboration of Rapid, Provisional and Definitive Accounts. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Corrections are made during the arbitration and pre-arbitration work. These phases of the economic data compilation process allow for the detection and rectification of errors, inconsistencies, and statistical discrepancies, thus ensuring the quality and reliability of the final data. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The choice of sources, statistical techniques, and decisions made regarding the National Accounts are based on the manual of the System of National Accounts (2008 SNA). 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The national accounts are developed using the following software: Excel ; Resource-Employment Balance of Input-Output Tables (ERETES); For account reconciliation, the Racking-Ratio or Iterative Proportional Fitting (RAS) method is used. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The comparison of intermediate data with other sources, such as surveys, indices (Standard CPI series, Industrial Production Index (IPI) etc…), data from the West African Central Bank (BCEAO), mining data, and data from the Table of Central Government Operations (CGO) (TOFE), is an essential step in the data validation and verification process for economic data. This comparison allows for the detection of discrepancies, the identification of potential inconsistencies, and ensures the quality and reliability of the produced economic information. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Statistical discrepancies are consistently analyzed and explained in the provisional and final accounts. This practice aims to provide users with a thorough understanding of the variations or divergences between the preliminary and final data, identifying the factors that contributed to these discrepancies. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Statistical discrepancies are analyzed and explained in the provisional and definitive accounts. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses National Accounts Revision studies and analyses are carried out during the base year 2015. These activities have made it possible to take into account economic trends, new products, and the requirements of the 2008 SCN. Specific surveys are regularly conducted and used to reassess the level of GDP. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request The INS provides disaggregated and aggregated economic data to institutions and users upon their request through their questionnaires.
