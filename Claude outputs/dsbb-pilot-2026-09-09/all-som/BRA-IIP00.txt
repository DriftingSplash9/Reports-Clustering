 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The International Investment Position (IIP) comprises the external assets and liabilities of the reporting country, in accordance with the methodology set out in the 6th edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual – BPM6. The assets are broken down into five functional categories: direct investment; portfolio investment; financial derivatives (other than reserves) and ESOs; other investment; and reserve assets. The liabilities are broken down into four functional categories: direct investment; portfolio investment; financial derivatives (other than reserves) and ESOs; and other investment. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization IIP published data are broken down by functional category, financial instrument, institutional sector of the resident party, maturity (in the case of debt instruments) and currency. 2.2.1 Scope IIP published data are broken down by functional category, financial instrument, institutional sector of the resident party, maturity (in the case of debt instruments) and currency. Scope of the data The IIP covers the subset of financial assets and liabilities that have an international character. In most cases (except for gold bullion), the international character of a financial asset or liability arises because, of the two parties, one is a resident and the other is nonresident. Exceptions to coverage Direct investment liabilities in the form of real estate are currently not covered. Short-term commercial credits and advances are currently not covered. Unrecorded activity None. 2.4.1 Valuation The external debt liabilities statistics are valuated at nominal values, except for debt securities issued by the Republic in the international market, which are valued both at market values. Valuation of equity and investment funds shares liabilities depends on data availability. When actual market values are not available, a book value, as net worth, is used. Investments in Brazilian Depositary Receipts, which are securities of nonresident corporations traded in the Brazilian stock exchange, are recorded at market value. Market prices are mostly used to value assets. Assets are recorded at market value. 2.4.2 Recording basis Transactions are mostly recorded on a cash basis. Exceptions are interests on securities included in reserve assets, which are recorded on an accrual basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Grossing/netting procedures are broadly consistent with BPM5. Assets and liabilities are clearly and separately identified. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Assets: Survey Brazilian Capital Abroad, International Reserves Operation Department of BCB and Ministery of Economy. Liabilities: Survey Census of Foreign Capital in Brazil, Electronic Declaratory Registry - Financial Operations Registry (RDE-ROF), Brazilian Securities and Exchange Comission (CVM), Brazilian Stock Exchange (B3), Open Market Department of BCB, Central Bank Accounting Plan of the National Financial System Institutions (COSIF), BCB´s accounting data. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data obtained from the various sources are consistent with the definitions, scope, classifications, and time of recording of the IIP statistics. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data collection and processing timetables are adequate to meet timeliness and periodicity for disseminating the IIP statistics. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The quality and comprehensiveness of the data sources are continually analyzed. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Compilation procedures minimize processing errors such as coding, editing, and tabulation errors. Data sources are received and processed electronically through IT systems. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Until information from Surveys on Brazilian Capitals Abroad and on Foreign Capitals in Brazil is received, ITRS flows and exchange changes are used to adjust stocks. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Intermediate results are checked across a wide range of data sources. IIP and balance of payments data are used for crosschecking the consistency of datasets. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data The behavior of series is routinely crosschecked with related series and indicators. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs BCB routinely verifies debt data against data of multilateral organizations on external debt, mainly IDB and the World Bank. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions are common ground to the Survey on Brazilian Capital Abroad, due to the fact that only preliminary figures are available at the time its data are first published within the IIP. Reserve assets and all liabilities are subject to revision in case of methodology changes or factual errors. When estimating positions using BoP flows, the revision procedures follows those of the BoP, i.e. quarterly reviews. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses The general policy of the Central Bank of Brazil is that major changes in methodology are announced at the time they are introduced. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Statistics not routinely disseminated are made available to users upon request, following data disclosure rules. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Prompt and knowledgeable service and support are available to users. Contact points for enquiries are available on the metadata section of each statistics series available at the BCB website (www.bcb.gov.br), including e-mail addresses of the staff in charge. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs All Brazilian IIP data are available to the public on the BCB website at no charge.
