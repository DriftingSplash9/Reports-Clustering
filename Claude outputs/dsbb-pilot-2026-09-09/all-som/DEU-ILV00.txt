 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Concept and definition: The dissemination of the International Reserves complies with the International Reserves and Foreign Currency Liquidity - Guidelines for a Data Template of the IMF, the BPM6 and taking into account the specific conceptual framework of international reserves for euro area participating Member States which is defined and explained in the following documents: Decision (EU) 2016/2247 of the ECB (recast) of the ECB of 3 November 2016 on the annual accounts of the ECB (ECB/2016/35) Guideline (EU) 2016/2249 of the ECB of 3 November 2016 on the legal Framework for accounting and financial reporting in the European System of Central Banks (recast), (ECB/2016/34) Principles for the financial statement of the Deutsche Bundesbank (Grundsätze zur Rechnungslegung der Deutschen Bundesbank), available at: https://www.bundesbank.de/en/publications/reports/annual-and-environmental-reports ECB publication "Statistical Treatment of the Eurosystem's International Reserves" (2000), available at: https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/other/statintreservesen.pdf ECB publication "European Union balance of payments/international investment position statistical methods" (2023),available at: https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pubbydate/2023/html/ecb.bopiipbook202310~d2c47838a5.en.html In addition to gold holdings and the Reserve Position in the Fund, only those liquid claims denominated in foreign currency that the Bundesbank has on non-euro area residents are part of its reserves. The Bundesbank's claims on residents of other EMU member states, regardless of the currency, are part of other external assets. The reserve assets must: (i) be under the effective control of the Deutsche Bundesbank; and (ii) refer to highly liquid, marketable and creditworthy non-euro area currency-denominated claims on non-residents of the euro area, as well as gold, special drawing rights and the reserve position in the IMF. Deposits with banks located in Germany and/or in other euro area Member States are therefore not considered as part of the official reserves. Reserve assets are compiled on a gross basis without any netting of reserve-related liabilities (with the exception of those reserve assets included in the sub-item of financial derivatives, which are recorded on a net basis). All reversible gold transactions are treated as collateralized loans and recorded accordingly in the international reserves statistics. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification of the International Reserves complies with the revised International Reserves and Foreign Currency Liquidity - Guidelines for a Data Template of the IMF, the BPM6 and the ECB recommendations on Eurosystem reserve assets. Data are disseminated in millions of Euro on total gross international reserves, based on the accounts of the Deutsche Bundesbank. Germany applies in its national presentation the euro-area residency criterion. Therefore, the data include the foreign-currency-denominated claims on non-euro area residents only. Data are disseminated on the following: Gross official international reserves broken down by major components: monetary gold; foreign exchange assets (securities, deposits); SDRs; and the reserve position in the Fund. Other foreign currency assets. Predetermined short-term net drains on foreign currency assets. Contingent short-term net drains on foreign currency assets. Memo items. Scope of the data Institutional coverage: Deutsche Bundesbank and general government. Instrument coverage: Official Reserves and other Foreign currency-denominated claims on non-residents of the euro area, predetermined short-term net drains on foreign currency assets and contingent short-term net drains on foreign currency assets The Deutsche Bundesbank currently has no financial derivatives contracts that are settled in foreign currencies. 2.4.1 Valuation Data on international reserves are taken from the Bundesbank’s Accounting and Payments System Divisions. From 1999 onwards, the value of both the gold holdings and the foreign currency holdings is based on market valuation. The reserves stocks are valued by the External Statistics Division on a monthly basis, by using the end-of-period market prices and exchange rates. The valuation is based on market prices by using the closing mid-market prices at the end of the appropriate period. Monetary gold is valued at the market price used by the ECB. The conversion of foreign currency-denominated assets into euro is based on the mid-point foreign exchange rate on the last day of the reference period. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Reserve assets are compiled on a gross basis without any netting of reserve-related liabilities. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs For compiling the Official Reserves, the Reserves Template and the reserves transactions of the balance of payments statistics, data are mainly used from two data sources, i.e. the Payments System Division, which acts as a back office settling market operations and the Accounting Division. The detailed data from the Payments System Division are used for separating valuation changes from changes due to transactions, for a geographical allocation of the transactions, and for valuating the stocks on a monthly basis. These data are reconciled with the Daily Accounting Balance Sheet of the Bundesbank. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data The two main data sources (Accounting Division and Payments System Division) are reconciled in detail on a monthly basis. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points In the External Statistics Division, which is also responsible for disseminating the International Reserves data, a separate unit responds to users’ requests. The Bundesbank press office, which also maintains a central email account for queries addressed to the Bundesbank, forwards any Reserves related questions to this unit. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The Monthly Report provides an overview of all Bundesbank publications. All relevant statistical publications containing data and methodological notes are also listed on the Bundesbank’s balance of payments website. Publications and time-series downloads as well as email-based requests are free of charge. Orders for regular subscriptions can be placed with the Bundesbank’s press service.
