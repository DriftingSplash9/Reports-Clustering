 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical Framework : Compilation of individual categories of gross external debt is largely in accordance with sixth edition of the Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) and External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (External Debt Guide) and ESA 2010 Manual on Government Deficit and Debt. In what BPM6 is concerned, Romania as EU member state applies ECB Guideline ECB/2011/23 and Commission Regulation n.184/2005. Historical data back to 2008 have been converted and published in the statistical section of the NBR website. The dissemination of external debt statistics is currently made under BPM6 format and methodology. Gross external debt stocks are compiled on a monthly basis and are presented under the following categories: for each four sectors (central bank, general government, deposit taking corporations except the central bank, other sectors) data are broken down by original maturity (short- and long-term) and by debt instruments (debt securities, trade credits and advances, loans, currency and deposits and other debt liabilities, SDR allocations). Data on inter-company lending (liabilities) under direct investment relationships is separately identified by type and by maturity, debt liabilities of direct investment enterprise to direct investors, debt liabilities of direct investors to direct investment enterprise and debt liabilities between fellow, short and long term. Gross external debt is monthly calculated and disseminated breakdown by main institutional sectors and subsectors, structures which are used for compiling national accounts, fiscal notification and QEDS (Quarterly External Debt Statistics) tables. With respect to WB – QEDS tables Romania is disseminating all prescribed, encouraged and supplementary items, except S.3.2 table short term external debt statistics is calculated on original and remaining maturity, the latter being obtained as a sum of short-term debt stock on original maturity and long term debt repayments due within 12 months.. Definition: Gross External Debt of Romania is the outstanding amount of those actual current and not contingent, liabilities that require payments of principal and/or interest by the debtors at some points in the future and that are owed to non-residents by Romanian residents. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: NBR compiles and disseminates monthly statistical data on long-term debt broken down by: - Debtors (public, publicly guaranteed debt and private debt) -Creditors (official or private financing sources) - Institutional sectors (general government, central bank, deposit taking corporations except the central bank and other sectors) and their subsectors - Currencies - Maturity (between 1 and 5 years, more than five years) - Economic activities - Sectors (public and private sectors) - Type of instruments (debt securities, loans, trade credits and advances, currency and deposits, other liabilities, SDR allocations, direct investment – intercompany debt) NBR also compiles monthly the outstanding of short-term debt on original maturity which represents the amount of short-term liabilities owed to non-residents: short-term loans and commercial liabilities, debt securities instruments, intercompany loans, non-residents' deposits and other liabilities. The short-term external debt is broken down monthly by institutional sectors, instruments and currency. The structure of short-term debt on remaining maturity is calculated monthly, by main institutional sectors and instruments. Arrears. Arrears of principal and interest are no longer recorded as short-term debt; they are included in long term stocks. Classification by type of debt instrument. External debt data is classified by type of external debt instruments as set out in the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) and the 2013 External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users. 1) Deposits of nonresidents with domestic banks: Short-, medium- and long-term deposits of nonresidents with domestic banks are included . 2) Instruments included under "other debt liabilities": The item "other debt liabilities" is composed of clearing accounts and dividends distributed and not yet paid, or any other liability which classifies for debt except the generic instruments (IFRN quarterly survey) 3) Trade credits owed to nonresidents: Trade credits and advances position includes short / long term commercial liabilities (IFRN quarterly survey) Criteria used for determining whether a liability is a debt instrument or not: The criteria used for determining whether a liability is a debt instrument or not is the future requirement to make payments—principal and/or interest; therefore, it excludes equity capital, financial derivatives, open credit lines and other contingent liabilities." External debt liabilities of households and small private enterprises: External debt liabilities of households and small private enterprises are included under "other sectors. Liabilities of nonfinancial (both public and private) corporations, households and individual belong to "other sectors" together with liabilities of financial corporations other than monetary financial institutions: insurance corporations, pension funds, other financial intermediaries. Liabilities of (both public and private) depository corporations other than central bank: commercial banks, savings banks, credit unions or cooperatives belong to "deposit taking corporations, except the central bank". Financial leases with nonresidents: Financial leases with nonresidents are treated as loans and included in the gross external debt position. Intercompany lending between entities in a direct investment relationship : Intercompany lending between entities in a direct investment relationship is separately identified in the total external debt position and broken down by three main categories, debt liabilities of direct investment enterprise to direct investors, debt liabilities of direct investors to direct investment enterprise and debt liabilities between fellow. Maturity classification: Data is disseminated by maturity on an original maturity and on remaining maturity basis . Reclassification: Major reclassifications are/would be clearly identifiable through table footnotes . Sector attribution of external debt liabilities: External debt liabilities are attributed to the sector of the immediate borrower. Sector classification of external debt liabilities: External debt liabilities are classified under the sector category of the debtor and not of any guarantor. But in case when a guarantee has been called more than 3 years and Romanian authority paid the installments of the debtor, the institutional sector has been changed and reclassified as government sector's liabilities. Sector disaggregation: Data regarding short/long term external debt of" other sectors" breakdown by 3 subsectors (financial corporations other than monetary institutions, nonfinancial corporations, households and NPISH) is available. Data disaggregation into public sector external debt and publicly guaranteed private sector debt: Data can be dissagregated into public and publicly guaranteed debt (DMFAS) in case of long-term external debt. Scope of the data Coverage of external debt liabilities : Resident institutional units are defined accordingly with: - BPM6 concepts of economic territory and center of economic interest. -External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (External Debt Guide) and ESA 2010 Manual on Government Deficit and Debt. Geographical coverage : Only data of residents operating on the territory of Romania with nonresidents is included. Exceptions to coverage For portfolio investment, data coverage is partial since the information from external custodians is not totally available. For improving the coverage some methods are applied, it's the case of domestic bonds where the difference between issued bonds and holdings of residents are allocated to non-residents, IMF-CPIS (Liability annexes) geographical and sector breakdown being used for the compilation of the data. Households' liability transactions are partially covered, since ITRS was abandoned by the end of 2016. New data sources to improve household sector position estimates shall be deemed in the near future. Currently, the real estate mirror data exchanged between European countries on an annual basis, regarding total holdings of real estate by non-residents in Romania and total holdings of real estate by residents abroad were added to the FDI stock data starting with 2020. Unrecorded activity Some households' liability positions, SPEs. 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to unit of account Conversion to unit of account The stock of external liabilities denominated in foreign currency and domestic currency are converted into Euro and US dollars using the official exchange rate as of the last working day of reporting month, while the drawings and payments of principal and interest are converted using the transaction's day exchange rate. Valuation: The stock of external liabilities is recorded at face value and market price (Quarterly External Debt Statistics tables). Outstanding positions are based on amounts received or paid, adjusted to reflect exchange rates at the end of the reporting period. Dividends due to payments but unpaid are also included in external debt. In case of portfolio investment where the information is available the positions are all assessed at the market prices. Market prices for foreign instruments and domestic bonds issued on external capital market are uploaded into the local system from Centralized Securities Database (CSDB) whereas Bucharest Stock Exchange supplies monthly information about domestic instruments. 2.4.2 Recording basis The distinction between short-term and long-term is based on the original not on the residual maturity of the liability. The short-term debt includes all liabilities with original maturity of one year or less than one year, the medium-term debt includes liabilities with original maturity between 1 and 5 years and long-term debt includes liabilities with original maturity more than 5 years. The external debt position is recorded on accrual principle. The basis of recording outstanding positions is accrual principle while transactions are recorded when change of ownership occurs, in line with BPM6 recommendations. Valuation of tradable instruments: External debt data include debt securities valued at nominal values and market price (Quarterly External Debt Statistics tables) 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures All external debt is recorded on a gross basis, separate from any related asset component. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs FDI debt stocks are supplied based on FDI annual and quarterly surveys data. The data collected is fully consistent with BPM6 methodological standards. Provisional monthly information (available at t+45 days) is estimated based on historical data and revised once the survey results become available. The results are cross-checked with administrative sources (balance sheets), with long-term external debt statistics via debt management financial analysis system (DMFAS). Foreign direct investment includes (the paid-up capital and the reserves related to a non-resident investor holding at least 10 percent of the voting power or the subscribed share capital of a resident enterprise) loans between the investor or the group to which the investor belongs and the direct investment enterprise. Moreover, FDI includes loans from non-resident enterprises whose voting power or equity participation in the resident enterprise is less than 10 percent, but which belong to the same group as the direct investor in the resident company (fellow enterprises). Permanent debt between affiliated financial intermediaries (banks, non-bank financial institutions) is no longer considered as direct investment in external debt. Portfolio investment statistics is compiled on a security-by-security basis. The stocks and flows are recorded at market prices. On the liability side of the portfolio investment, the monthly information related to Romanian securities comes from various sources such as Romanian Stock Exchange, the Ministry of Finance, MFIs and IFs direct reporting, NBRs' Market Operations and Payments Departments. On the assets side of the portfolio investment the data about the securities issued by non-residents are extracted from the securities database called CSDB (Centralised Securities Database) managed at the level of ECB. Each month, information related to foreign instruments, provided by CSDB, such as issuer country, issuer sector, type of instrument, currency, amount issued or amount outstanding is used in compilation of portfolio investment. The market prices of both Romanian securities traded on external markets and non-resident securities are also obtained from CSDB. Data about stocks of held instruments are collected monthly through direct surveys. The reporting agents are the MFIs, IFs, FVCs, ICs and PFs on their behalf and the custodians on behalf of their clients, other than the MFIs, IFs, FVCs, ICs and PFs. Flows included in portfolio investment statistics are derived as a difference between current and previous stocks. Price changes, exchange rate changes and other changes are also compiled at the level of National Bank. Data about debt securities is collected security by security (with or without ISIN codes). They are used for classification of financial instruments part of portfolio investment. Reporting gaps exist for Romanian debt instruments purchased by non-residents that are undertaken with external custodians. These gaps (total issued amount minus total held reported instruments) are currently included in external statistics. The breakdown by countries and institutional sectors is derived based on alternative statistics such as CPIS. Back data were collected starting with December 2012, whereas the securities statistics have been available since July 2014. The security-by-security database provides liability debt securities input, including the related income. The data sources for external debt position are: Security by security data received from credit institutions, investment funds and money market investment funds, insurance corporations, pension funds, custodian residents, Clearstream related to all types of issuances abroad and in hands of resident entities (SHDB) Short/ long term trade credits – IFRN quarterly survey Short term loans – IFRN quarterly survey Short/long term deposits – balance sheets data (for deposit taking corporations except the central bank sector) medium- and long-term loans- external debt database (DMFAS) Insurance corporations' balance sheets supply quarterly info on short/long term liability side instruments inter-company loans – the results of annual and quarterly FDI surveys which are compared to external debt database for long term loans (DMFAS) · Banks' balance sheet data (an extra annex introduced starting in the middle of 2010) related to the stock of externalized loans to the private parent bank and BOP flows · For SDR allocations and other debt liabilities there is balance sheet information related to the stocks · Central bank balance sheet (short term deposits) Other liabilities (IFRN survey) 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording ED is broadly consistent with BPM6 manual, adjustment procedures exist to ensure that source data complies with the coverage, classification, and valuation and timing conventions. Direct, portfolio and other investment survey items are designed to adhere to BPM6 guidelines. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Overall, the source data for IIP/ED compiling is timely. In some cases (FDI, IFRN) where data is available with a time lag, preliminary estimates are made considering the results of previous surveys. Deadlines for respondents are mandated by Regulation no. 4/2014 regarding statistical reporting requirements, with the subsequent and notices of the NBR for the quarterly surveys. Respondent timeliness is monitored on an ongoing basis. 3.2.1 Source data assessment 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Direct investment : Data is compiled based on the results of the annual/quarterly surveys on inward direct investments starting with 2003, and on outward direct investments starting with 2006 (directional principle); the outcome of surveys gives information for all types of flows (transactions and other changes) as for stocks of debt instruments. The same as for quarterly FDI. Other investment : Data for medium- and long-term loans of deposit taking corporations except the central bank sector supplied by DMFAS system (external debt database), nowadays being assimilated to deposits in case of liabilities, are checked with banks' balance sheet data on a monthly basis. Externalized financial credits by resident credit institutions to their parents are added to the total liabilities, based on the information supplied by them. The information on SDR allocations comes from NBR balance sheet being monthly updated with the exchange rate. Insurance corporations' balance sheets supply quarterly info on short/long term liability side instruments. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures In case when external debt is contracted by Romanian government and the funds are onlent to other resident companies, the government contracting the debt is recorded as debtor and not the company to which these funds are onlent. The externalized credits by the resident banks to their private parent banks have been identified and added to the gross external debt. Monthly flows and stocks of this loan category are updated based on the banks' reports. The data are not adjusted for seasonal patterns. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results All data submitted by companies in surveys or through administrative sources are subject to verifying procedures. Where available, data is also confronted with balance sheet information of the various financial subsectors or information gathered from the financial press. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Quarterly data is periodically revised according to the Harmonized European Revision Policy in terms of timing and in depth of revisions, in line with IIP trending.
