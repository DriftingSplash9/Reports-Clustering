 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions National accounts are prepared in accordance with SNA 1968 methodology. SNA 1993 has not yet been implemented. The concepts and definitions used in preparing the national accounts are documented in the methodology paper for the interim base for the national accounts. The IHSI prepares estimates for gross domestic product (GDP) at current prices and constant prices by production sectors of economic activity and category of expenditure. However, the estimates for each type of activities at current prices are not disseminated. The available constant price statistics are the aggregates for the overall supply and demand equation and the gross domestic product (GDP) by branch of activity. GDP at current prices is determined first by using the production approach based on the production accounts for the branches that show production in sources and intermediate consumption in uses, with value added as an accounting balance. The GDP is equal to the sum of values added + (taxes net of subsidies). The table of sources and uses is also used on the basis of the equation: GDP + Imports = FC + GFCF + Exports (on the assumption that changes in stocks are nil). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The nomenclature by product/branch of activity used in national accounts is the International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC) and the Central Product Classification: U.N. Handbook. Scope of the data The scope of the national accounts statistics covers SNA 1968 accounts. The data used to prepare national accounts are national in scope. Annual estimates of GDP are calculated from both the production and the expenditure approaches. Exceptions to coverage Informal sector activities are not explicitly estimated. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuations are conducted on the basis of producer prices and purchasers’ prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis Recording basis: National accounts transactions are recorded on a cash basis. Recording period: Calendar years. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The IHSI has a list of enterprises which includes information from the Chamber of Commerce and Industry, the Ministry of Economy and Finance (General Directorate of Taxes (DGI) and the Ministry of Commerce and Industry (MCI)). These enterprises account for over 70 percent of the DGI’s tax base. These enterprises are primarily managed by the Tax Management and Control Unit (UGCF), a branch of the DGI which handles large taxpayers. The IHSI has undertaken a number of data-collection activities. The benchmark surveys are as follows: Survey on household budgets Survey on industrial production Survey on public transport Survey on informal sector activities (in progress) Furthermore, the results of the population survey have provided information which will be used to change the base for the national accounts. There is no updated directory of enterprises which could be used as a sampling base for statistical surveys. The IHSI uses the Business License Tax records of the General Directorate of Taxes (DGI) and the professional registration card (CIF) records of the Ministry of Commerce in order to establish a database of enterprises to meet national accounts needs. The data used to prepare GDP by branch of activity using the production approach are obtained from the following institutions: Agriculture: Ministry of Agriculture, Natural Resources, and Rural Development. Manufacturing: the Tax Management and Control Unit (UGCF). Construction and Public Works: Office of Occupational Accident, Health and Maternity Insurance (OFATMA), General Customs Administration (AGD). Energy: the electricity utility, Electricité d’Haïti (Ed’H). Water: the water supply utility, Centrale Autonome Métropolitaine d’Eau Potable (CAMEP) Nonmarket services: Ministry of Economy and Finance, Bank of the Republic of Haiti (BRH). Market services: BRH, Ministry of Commerce and Industry (MCI). GDP data by expenditure category come from: Bank of the Republic of Haiti General Customs Administration General Directorate of Taxes. Data sources for GDP at constant prices by branch of activity and/or by expenditure category are virtually the same as for GDP at current prices by branch of activity. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Administrative recorded data do not always meet the methodological requirements of national accounts (SNA 1968). However, the IHSI uses comparative sources to obtain information that meets methodological requirements more closely. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness GDP by branch of activity: Agriculture: data available 3 months after the end of the reference year. Manufacturing: 4 or 5 months after the reference year. Construction: 3 or 4 months after the reference year. Energy: 3 months after the reference period. Water: 3 or 4 months after the reference year. Nonmarket services: 2 to 3 months after the end of the fiscal year. BOP: 3 months after the end of the fiscal year. Commercial services: 3 to 6 months after the reference year. GDP by expenditure category: 3 months after the reference year. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Periodic revisions of national accounts estimates make it possible to verify the quality of source data by checking the data sources used. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques First, compilers prepare the source-use balances for products at current prices and then, for source-use balances at constant prices, compilers multiple the quantities for the current year by the corresponding price for the base year (1986/87). 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The accounts preparation process is essentially based on data from the BRH balance of payments. At this stage, BRH staff who prepare the BOP make a number of adjustments in order to reflect actual conditions as closely as possible. Statisticians compiling the national accounts accordingly use the adjusted foreign trade data that are published by the BRH in the balance of payments. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Preliminary results are compared with data from other independent sources in order to decide whether or not to validate those results. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses National accounts results are not considered final until after the third year. They are subject to revisions during the preceding years. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Generally speaking, revision studies are used internally to improve the quality of information. They are not disseminated. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Non-confidential statistics are also disseminated on request—e.g., information with regard to the level of detail in the data.
