 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: The United Kingdom (UK) Balance of Payments (BoP) data are based on the definition defined in the sixth edition of the IMF's ‘ Balance of Payments Manual ' (BPM6) and the System of National Accounts 2008 (SNA08). Definition: The UK balance of payments is a statistical statement that summarises transactions between UK residents and non-residents during a period. It consists of the goods and services account, the primary income account, the secondary income account, the capital account, and the financial account. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification system: The UK balance of payments are based on the BPM6 classification. Scope of the data Transactions coverage: The UK balance of payments data covers all sectors' transactions with the rest of the world. Geographic coverage: The UK's economic territory is defined to include the territories lying within its political frontiers and territorial seas and in the international waters over which it has exclusive jurisdiction. Oil and gas: All petroleum, natural gas and related products are included in merchandise trade, including those from the UK Continental Shelf. Oil imported for processing and exported after processing without change of ownership is reflected in import and exports data. Investment income and financial transactions of outward and inward investing oil companies is included in foreign direct investment. Imports and exports of services by UK companies in the oil and gas sector are included under the following categories, sea transportation, pipeline transportation, merchanting, operational leasing and engineering services. Exceptions to coverage Transactions coverage: transactions of UK households are not fully covered by existing sources and methods. Geographic coverage: The UK offshore islands - Jersey, Guernsey and the Isle of Man - are classified as non-residents to the UK as they are subject to their own monetary, fiscal authorities and have their own tax systems. Estimation of FISIM: Exports and imports of FISIM are included within financial services and excluded from investment income. Unrecorded activity Estimates are made for smuggling and fraudulent trading activity. 2.4.1 Valuation Survey respondents are asked to provide market prices when available. For the most part, the price recorded in the Foreign Direct Investment survey will be that which is recorded in the transactors' accounts or in the administrative records. This is likely to be a close approximation of the market price. Market prices are more readily available for portfolio investment. Conversion to unit of account: Transactions originally denominated in foreign currencies need to be converted to pounds sterling using market rates of exchange prevailing at the time of the transaction. Survey respondents are asked to convert transactions at the mid-point of the buying and selling exchange rates at the time of the transaction, although in practice the actual rate will vary according to what is available. Exports and imports of petroleum, natural gas and related products are valued fob. The adjustments for the cost of freight and insurance on oil and gas imports are projected from data formerly supplied by the Department of Trade and Industry. Foreign direct investment transactions by oil companies are reported at cash or exchange values. Levels of investments are requested at ‘own funds of book value'. Merchanting services related to oil and gas are collected on a gross basis, valued using the actual transaction cost. 2.4.2 Recording basis Recording of income: Income data are collected on an accrued basis on ONS and Bank of England survey forms, but dividends are recorded as of the date they are paid. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs These data are predominantly derived from surveys conducted by the Office for National Statistics and the Bank of England, as well as directly reported data from other government departments (HM Treasury, HM Revenue & Customs) and from other suppliers such as the Bank for International Settlements and the European Investment Bank. Data for trade in goods are derived from information collected by HM Revenue & Customs. Goods: The basic sources of data on trade in goods are customs declarations (Single Administrative Documents) for trade with the rest of the world. This is also true for petroleum and natural gas and related products (other than gas and electricity through pipelines to or from the EU). HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) collect information relating to the trade in Natural Gas and Electricity directly from the pipeline and grid operators. The methodology records the physical flow of Natural Gas and Electricity between the UK and its EU trading partners. Value data are estimated using the relevant market prices for gas and electricity. Services: Transportation: Freight payments on imports are derived by deducting surveyed estimates of UK operators' freight earnings on imports from the total which is based on freight rates and import tonnages matrixes. Other transport estimates are mainly based on balance of payments surveys to transport operators and ports surveys, together with information from a survey of passengers. The sources for UK transport operators cover those from the oil and gas sector. Travel: Estimates are based primarily on the International Passenger Survey, which seeks information on expenditure from samples of non-residents visitors leaving the UK and of UK residents returning from abroad. For package tourists, estimates of the transport element are deducted from the reported total package costs. Other services: Other services are based on administrative data for government exports and imports, together with a range of quarterly and annual BoP surveys to both financial and non-financial institutions, which include companies from the oil and gas sector. Primary income: Compensation of employee: Data are derived from the International Passenger Survey previously, together with information from foreign embassies and military bases in the UK and government administrative data. Estimates for investment income are largely based on quarterly and annual surveys undertaken by the ONS and the Bank of England. Direct investment income: Earnings from direct investment are collected in annual and quarterly surveys to UK enterprise groups with foreign subsidiaries, affiliates and branches and UK groups with a foreign parent. Portfolio investment income: Earnings of UK residents on foreign securities are generally collected via surveys to UK enterprises, although private non-financial corporation (PNFC's) earnings are estimated by applying appropriate rates of return to surveyed asset levels. Foreign earnings on UK equity are derived from Stock Exchange data on dividend payments, while foreign earnings on UK debt securities are largely estimated from total interest paid and balance sheet information. Other investment income: Earnings and payments on loans and deposits are collected via Bank of England surveys of UK monetary financial institutions and ONS surveys of securities' dealers. Estimates for other sectors earnings and payments are largely estimated from levels of such assets and liabilities. Reserve assets: Interest received is recorded within the Exchange Equalisation Account by the Bank of England. Secondary income: Government administrative sources are used, together with information from partner countries on household transfers and survey information on insurance premiums and claims. Capital Account: Government administrative sources are used for general government capital transfers. Financial account: Direct Investment: Estimates of direct investment are mainly derived from quarterly and annual inquiries by the ONS and the Bank of England. Limited information on property transactions are available. Portfolio Investment: Outward investment in foreign securities are derived from quarterly and annual inquiries to monetary financial institutions, insurance companies and pension funds and other financial intermediaries. Estimates of portfolio investment transactions of private non-financial corporations are derived from a quarterly inquiry of asset levels. Estimates for household sector foreign investments are based on the ONS Wealth and Assets Survey. Inward investment in UK equity are derived from the results of a portfolio investment survey, adjusted to take account of total levels of foreign investment in shares as indicated by the results of the annual share ownership surveys. Foreign transactions in British Government stocks measured from banking statistics and other Bank of England sources, in bonds and notes issued by UK MFI's and other sectors have been obtained by assuming that any net transactions in UK securities not attributable to the domestic sectors are attributable to foreign residents. Estimates of foreign transactions in money market instruments are derived from ONS and Bank of England inquiries and from information supplied by the UK's Debt Management Office. Financial derivatives: Estimates for financial derivatives are currently unavailable except for net settlement receipts/payments on UK banks' interest rate swaps which are supplied by the Bank of England. From 2010, estimates of transactions by security dealers are included. These are collected by ONS surveys. Other investment: Information on banks' loans and deposits are supplied by the Bank of England. Estimates of securities' dealers deposits abroad are derived from their asset levels reported to the ONS. Other sectors' loans and deposits are based on counterpart information obtained from the Bank for International Settlement (BIS). Information is also obtained from administrative data. Reserve assets: Information is provided by the Bank of England. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Goods: customs data are adjusted to move the data to a BPM6 consistent basis (i.e., where both exports and imports are valued free on board, or fob). In addition, a number of coverage adjustments are made to the customs data to move to BPM6 definitions. Adjustments are made to include purchases of second-hand ships, repairs to ships and aircraft, goods procured in ports and industrial gold, but exclude goods not changing ownership. Estimates of smuggled alcohol, tobacco, and illegal drugs are included in the trade in goods estimates. HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) collect information relating to the trade in Natural Gas and Electricity directly from the pipeline and grid operators. This removed the need for individual companies to submit declarations for these goods. The methodology records the physical flow of Natural Gas and Electricity between the UK and its EU trading partners. Value data are estimated using the relevant market prices for gas and electricity. Adjustments have been made in the past for rigs and production platforms entering/ leaving the territory for one year or more. They are not necessary at the present time. Services: Data on transport services are adjusted to ensure consistency with the fob-valuation of trade in goods. Purchases and sales of oil and gas from UK and foreign bunkers respectively are deducted from the disbursements elements of imports and exports of transportation services and added to imports and exports goods. These data are sourced from surveys to UK transport operators for imports and from information on total sales from UK bunkers for exports. Primary income: Primary income earned is compared with outstanding stocks to ensure rates of return are valid. BoP primary income flows are also confronted within the context of the national accounts dividends and interest matrix. Financial Account: Direct investment quarterly and annual sample results are adjusted to reflect population totals. Imputations are made to cover transactions with banks outside the BIS reporting area. Estimates are included in the account to reflect shares received by UK or foreign residents in "share for share" mergers and acquisitions. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Seasonal adjustment: X12ARIMA is used to seasonally adjust current account items where appropriate. Financial account items are not adjusted. Geographical detail: Annual and quarterly breakdowns of the current account items are published. These data are available by current account component for sixty-seven countries and geographic zones annually and for the EU, USA, Canada, Japan, Switzerland, Brazil, China, India, Hong Kong, Russia and the Euro-area quarterly. For the financial account, geographic detail is only available for direct investment flows. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Goods: Verification is undertaken in a number of ways. For instance, individual results are checked for plausibility and aggregates are validated through the supply/use framework. Services: Survey results are checked for plausibility by comparison with previously reported figures at both the individual and aggregate level. In addition, aggregates are validated through the supply/use framework. Primary income: Primary income earned is compared with outstanding stocks to ensure rates of return are valid. BoP primary income flows are also analysed within the context of the national accounts dividends and interest matrix. Secondary income: Government transfers are cross-checked with national accounts government expenditure figures. Financial Account: Flows and levels are reconciled in both BoP and national accounts framework. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions are published quarterly in the Quarterly Balance of Payments Statistical Bulletin and UK Economic Accounts, giving information on the scale and reason for revision. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Revisions are published quarterly in the ‘Quarterly Balance of Payments Statistical Bulletin' and ‘UK Economic Accounts' giving information on the scale and reason for revision.
