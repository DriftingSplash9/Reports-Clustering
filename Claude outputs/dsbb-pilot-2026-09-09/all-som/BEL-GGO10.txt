 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Statistics are consistent with the European System of National and Regional Accounts ( ESA 2010 ) regarding definitions, accounting rules and classifications ( Regulation (EU) No. 549/2013 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 21 May 2013 and Commission Regulation (EU) No. 220/2014 of 7 March 2014). ESA 2010 is fully consistent with SNA2008 and so broadly in line with the Government Finance Statistics Manual 2014 ( GFSM 2014 ). However, ESA 2010 and GFSM 2014 differ in some points. For example, Financial Intermediation Services Indirectly Measured (FISIM) and own-account capital formation are included in Belgian accounts. This is in accordance with ESA 2010, but not with GFSM 2014—which would exclude them. In addition, provisions in the field of Government Finance Statistics follow Eurostat's Manual on Government Deficit and Debt and " A Guide to the Statistical Treatment of PPPs ". These manuals aim at a fully harmonized implementation of ESA 2010 for Government Finance Statistics in the European Union. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification of units into the general government sector and its subsectors is in accordance with ESA 2010 regulation. The general government sector consists of four subsectors, namely central government, state government, local government, and social security funds. The sector classification of units is reviewed continuously. A list of units classified in the government sector is published online on the National Accounts Institute website and updated twice a year. The categories of revenues covered in the table are taxes, social contributions, grants, and other revenue. The categories of expenses are compensation of employees, use of goods and services, interest, subsidies, social benefits, and other expenses. The consumption of fixed capital is also mentioned, as well as the net acquisition of non-financial assets. The net acquisitions of financial assets and net incurrence of liabilities are allocated between domestic and foreign origin. The coverage and evaluation of these categories are in line with ESA2010/SNA 2008, so broadly in line with GFSM 2014 definitions. 2.2.1 Scope The classification of units into the general government sector and its subsectors is in accordance with ESA 2010 regulation. Scope of the data The scope is consistent with internationally accepted standards, guidelines, or good practices. The scope is consistent with ESA 2010. Government finance statistics cover the four general government subsectors: central government, state government, local government, and social security funds. These subsectors consist of main units and extra-budgetary units. The general government sector includes all institutional units which are non-market producers whose output is intended for individual and collective consumption and financed by compulsory payments made by units belonging to other sectors, and institutional units principally engaged in the redistribution of national income and wealth. Institutions belonging to the sector of general government are not identified by their legal form but are classified according to qualitative and quantitative criteria as defined in the methodological manuals. More explanation on the sectorization of the general government' units can be found in the CIS application on the National Bank of Belgium's website . The non-financial transactions cover general government revenue, expenditure, and net lending/net borrowing. The data on financial operations cover transactions in financial assets and liabilities of the general government sector. Exceptions to coverage Nihil. Unrecorded activity Nihil. 2.4.1 Valuation Flows are measured according to their exchange value, i.e., the market prices, as laid down in the ESA 2010. The value of transactions in foreign currency is converted into national currency at the current exchange rate at the time of the transaction. 2.4.2 Recording basis Flows are recorded on an accrual basis as laid down in ESA 2010. For financial transactions, the accrual principle is applied by using the financial instruments ‘other accounts receivable/payable'. Accrued interest is recorded under the corresponding financial instrument. For non-financial transactions, accrual adjustments are made, when appropriately accrued source data is not available (tax revenues, transfers). 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Grossing/netting procedures are consistent with internationally accepted standards, guidelines, or good practices. As laid down in ESA 2010, the approach is gross recording, apart from the degree of netting which is inherent in the classification themselves. Netting is implicit in various transaction categories (e.g., changes in inventories). Similarly, the financial account record increases in assets and in liabilities respectively on a net basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs For the compilation of the non-financial accounts, the main data source used is the Economic Grouping, which is a report according to a codification based on the economic nature of revenues and expenditures in the government budget. The Economic Grouping is compiled by the budget authorities (at the federal level and at the level of communities and regions) and is transmitted to the secretariat of the Base documentaire générale / Algemene Gegevensbank (General Documentary Base), set up by the cooperation agreement of October 1st, 1991, between the State, the Communities, the regions, and the Community Commissions. The Economic grouping covers the budgetary transactions of the main state (at the central and state government levels). All entities use the same economic classification for the reporting. Other data sources are provided by the Federal public service (FPS - ministry) Finances for most taxes, by the Federal public service Budget for the detailed expenditure and by other administrative bodies for transactions of the social security funds. For the compilation of the financial accounts , the building blocks reporting, developed by the NAI in the framework of the general Documentary Base, is composed of purpose-built templates for collection of financial data. This statistical survey provides information on the stocks (with multiple valuations) and flows for the various ESA2010 instruments on an instrument-by-instrument basis. The collection of data is executed by the administrations themselves and transmitted to the NAI. The reporting template is designed to ensure full consistency of opening/closing positions and flows during the reporting period as well as, if possible, the counterpart information. The reporting period is on a quarterly basis for the main states (at the central and state governments levels) and on an annual basis for other units belonging to the general government sector, except for the building blocks reporting on shares which is also on an annual basis for the main states. For local government data , the compilation of the financial accounts relies on indirect data sources available at an infra-annual frequency, while the non-financial accounts are derived from budget reporting and financial statements (annual basis). 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The Economic grouping and the building blocks reporting have been designed, developed, and adapted to meet the definitions, accounting rules and classifications of the ESA 2010 requirements. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness On a quarterly basis, the Economic grouping information is available 30 days after the end of the quarter. The quarterly financial data transmitted via the building blocks reporting are available 45 days after the end of the reference period. The annual financial data are available 45 days after the end of the reference period for currency and deposits, debt securities, loans, and transactions in shares, and on 15 August for other ESA instruments and stocks of shares. The time lag for most data sources is shorter than the requirements for the transmission to Eurostat and ECB and for the publication deadlines. When this is not the case, reasonable estimates can be made. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data are closely monitored, in collaboration with the General Documentary Base. Single point of contact for statistical purpose (SPOC) has also been set up in each government to facilitate the transmission of statistical information. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Sound statistical techniques are used in dealing with the data sources. The Belgian government accounts are compiled in accordance with the methodology of the European system of national and regional accounts (ESA 2010). Some values are distributed into quarters if only annual information is available, or in different categories if only a total is given, by using adequate statistical techniques. If source data are not available on time (especially for the most recent quarters), estimations are used. The estimated data are replaced by the actual data when they become available. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Time adjustments (e.g., for taxes), smoothing (e.g., for subsidies), consolidation adjustments, etc. are used when suitable. Consolidation means that the following distributive transactions between two units belonging the same sector or subsector are eliminated: the interests paid within public administrations the current transfers within public administrations the capital transfers other than capital taxes within administrations. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Plausibility checks and controls are carried out at all stages of the production process. Statistical discrepancies in intermediate data are investigated. Statistical discrepancies which show up in the intermediate data are traced back to the primary source data to identify possible reasons and to solve the problem. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Statistical discrepancies in intermediate data are assessed and investigated. Statistical discrepancies which show up in the intermediate data are traced back to the primary source data to identify possible reasons and to solve the problem. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The discrepancy between financial and non-financial transactions is regularly assessed and analyzed for possible improvement. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses In each March and September publications, annual and quarterly data are aligned, and revisions are analyzed to improve future processing. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses In April and October, a note with the major methodological changes is available, if any. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished statistics are normally not disseminated, but some requests can be taken into consideration. The requests can be addressed at Governmentfinance.Na@nbb.be . 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Contact points are published. A dedicated mailbox is also available ( Governmentfinance.Na@nbb.be ). 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs Catalogs and calendars of publication, requirements for source data, etc. are available on the website of the National Bank of Belgium .
