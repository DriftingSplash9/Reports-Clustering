 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The overall conceptual framework, concepts and definitions used to compile the foreign trade statistics are in broad conformity with guidelines outlined in the BPM5. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification and sectorization used for the foreign trade statistics are in broad conformity with guidelines outlined in the BPM5 and are derived from data received from National Statistics Institute (INE) which follows the U.N. recommendations: products are classified using the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System; countries are classified using the ISO codes. Scope of the data The scope of foreign trade statistics is broadly consistent with internationally accepted standards, guidelines, or good practices outlined in the BPM5. All resident-nonresident transactions as specified in the BPM5 are covered in the foreign trade statistics. Exceptions to coverage There are no transactions of enterprises that process goods for manufacture, goods for processing and repairs on goods, hence hey are not identified. The BM does not make seasonal adjustments or calculate volume and price indices. Unrecorded activity The Bank of Mozambique does not record the volume of smuggled merchandise. 2.4.1 Valuation The foreign trade statistics reflect the value denominated in specific currencies at which the goods were traded, but are converted and presented in U.S. dollars. 2.4.2 Recording basis In principle, foreign trade statistics are recorded at the moment that they physically cross the border or when information about progressive change of ownership of capital goods is available. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Exports and imports of goods are recorded on gross basis in line with the recommendations of the BPM5 . 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The foreign trade statistics are reported by the Directorate General of Customs (DGA) (on diskette with hardcopy backup duly coded by product category, origin and destination of the merchandise and customs value) to the National Statistics Institute (INE), where they are processed electronically; the INE sends them to the Bank of Mozambique (BM) – Economic Research and Statistics Department (DEE) in the form of summary tables broken down by major commodity and by country of origin and destination. The BM and INE validate and adjust electronic foreign trade statistics through information obtained: (i) directly from major private merchandise export and import enterprises through specially designed surveys; (ii) from several ministries. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data drawn from surveys are consistent with the definitions, scope and classifications, and time of recording of the foreign trade statistics. Administrative records used to compile foreign trade statistics provide reasonable approximations of the methodological requirements of the balance of payments. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The monthly foreign trade data is provided on quarterly basis. Data collection and processing timetables are adequate to meet timeliness and periodicity for disseminating the balance of payments statistics. Respondents are made aware of the deadlines set for reporting and the BM employs rigorous follow-up procedures to ensure the timely receipt of respondents' data (e.g., by contacting the respondent). 3.2.1 Source data assessment Sources of foreign trade statistics are regularly assessed in sense that cover all top export and import enterprises through the use of surveys and administrative sources apart from the Customs database. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Statistical techniques employed conform to sound statistical procedures. Adjustments to unit records are made only when clearly warranted (e.g., unusual values are not replaced or modified unless clearly required), and can be identified in datasets. Sound estimation techniques are employed to adjust data for missing observations in statistical collections. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures For BOP purposes, the import value provided by the INE is converted from a c.i.f. basis to an f.o.b. basis by means of an estimative process, which, by agreement, assumes that 9 percent represents freight and 1 percent insurance. This adjustment accounts for the difference between the foreign trade values published by the INE and the BM. Undervaluation of imports is estimated based on the volume of foreign exchange purchases and sales reported by exchange bureaus. There is no any estimate made of overvaluations of exports. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Intermediate results are validated against a wide range of data sources, including, for example data received from several government departments. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Data on freight earnings are in process of assessment in relation to the value/volume of the imports flows and surveys to the main importers. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Staff involved in producing balance of payments data monitor developments in the errors and omissions item and seek to understand them, e.g., by cross-checks between entries in the current, capital, and financial accounts. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses A review of foreign trade statistics for a particular quarter is made during the compilation of the immediately following quarter (which occurs 50 days after the end of each quarter). The revision studies and analyses are used internally and externally to inform statistical processes. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses At the time of dissemination the revision studies and analyses are published to inform the users of foreign trade statistics about any update that may be done to the preliminary data. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request In addition to the statistics routinely disseminated, other general statistics are made available upon request and customized tabulations can be provided to meet specific requests.
