 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions IIP statistics is the balance sheet of a country's external financial assets and liabilities, which comprises financial items consisting of claims on nonresidents, liabilities to nonresidents, monetary gold, and SDRs. Compilation follows reconciliation format, encompassing the end-of-period outstanding amount as well as financial transactions, price changes, exchange rate changes and other volume changes occurred during the period, all of which affects the level of assets and/or liabilities. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The IIP, by convention, is classified into assets and liabilities with the difference between the two representing the country's net position. Consistent with the standard components of the financial account in the balance of payments, the components are broken down by functional category, where assets are divided into direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives, other investment, and reserve assets while liabilities are divided into direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives, and other investment (i.e., standard components as stated in the sixth edition of the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual - BPM6 ). Scope of the data Coverage of external financial assets and liabilities: The IIP covers all institutional sectors and all investment items as listed in the IIP template. Geographical coverage: IIP statement covers Thai residents' claims on and liabilities to residents of all other countries. Data for main IIP items are also compiled and disseminated with country breakdown. Exceptions to coverage Not applicable Unrecorded activity Transactions of individuals and households may not be fully captured. 2.4.1 Valuation Outstanding amount are valued at market prices, or the closest approximation. Conversion to unit of account: The exchange rate used is the mid-rate between bid and offer rates quoted by commercial banks for the last day of the reference month. This mid-rate applies to all data on stocks of external assets and liabilities reported in foreign currencies. 2.4.2 Recording basis Recording basis: The rule applied is based on the same notion as the recording of balance of payments transaction. That is, transaction is recorded when "change of ownership" is evidenced, and consequently reflected in the position at the end of the reference period. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Outstanding amount of assets and liabilities are reported independently by data providers, on gross basis, not netted out. Gross flows are reported only for a few components if feasible for data providers. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Direct Report Direct report and Administrative Data Direct report on financial statement relating to international investment position Direct report on securities investment by non-resident and Thai investors through custodians, brokers and sub brokers Direct report on financial statement of government and state-owned enterprises International transaction Reporting System (ITRS) Surveys Quarterly surveys on external debt and lending of private non-bank sector Quarterly and Annually surveys on IIP Annually surveys on Method of Payments and Outstanding Trade Credits 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Overall IIP : Thailand's IIP follows conceptual framework specified in the BPM6 . IIP is disseminated in millions of USD, showing the components of external assets and liabilities position, which can be classified as follows: 1. Direct investment (abroad or in Thailand); 2. Portfolio investment (equity and debt securities); 3. Financial derivatives; 4. Other investment (trade credits and advance, loans, currency and deposits, and other accounts receivable/payable); and 5. Reserve assets. IIP is classified by sector: general government, central bank, other depository corporations and other sectors. International reserves: consists of monetary gold, Special Drawing Rights (SDRs), reserve positions in the Fund, and foreign exchange assets. The data are solely obtained from the BOT's balance sheet. Monetary gold and securities are adjusted to the current market price on a weekly basis. All foreign currency-denominated assets are then converted to USD equivalent using mid-rate at the end of the reference period. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data from quarterly surveys (external debt survey, and preliminary IIP survey) are one-quarter lagged, while annual surveys (IIP survey based on audited financial statement, and trade credit survey) are eight-month lagged. 3.2.1 Source data assessment 100% coverage for IIP data obtained from public and banking sector, and approximately 80% coverage for non-bank sector. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Use of threshold is adopted for selection of sampling entities, comprising major corporate with net asset value (NAV) exceeding THB 1,500 million and THB 1,000 million for inward and outward investment respectively. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Breakdown by country of non-resident counterparts and business sector are available. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Validation rules are implemented to check consistencies of data both within the survey form and with other sources such as stock prices, bond prices, administrative data on registered equity, and the ITRS. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Banking sector : Position as reported in the datasets for financial institution supervision is used for cross-checking with survey responses. Non-bank sector : Population survey data are sourced from financial statment obtained from the DBD. Extrapolation technique is imposed on the data from quarterly sampled survey to gross up the results, Direct reports : Outstanding amount and flows are reconciled at company level. Surveyed units are instructed to classify claims on and liabilities to counterparties with and without direct investment relationship. Data are validated against the balance sheet information attached to the survey form (if available). Survey on external debt : Change in the outstanding amount are cross-checked against ITRS data, and other doubtful records are confirmed directly with data providers. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs BOT utilizes an in-house computer software to detect for plausible errors/inconsistencies. The investigation is done at record level for each provider's reports. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses The DMD monitors the direction and magnitude of revisions and studies the effect of revisions on selected items and periods. Documentation on major revisions is maintained for internal use, with detailed information on causes of revision. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses The public is informed of major changes in methodology at the time the change is introduced through a press release and a notice placed on the BOT's website. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request In addition to IIP statistics disseminated on the BOT's website, other data series can be made available upon request. Unpublished (but non-confidential) specialized tabulations can be provided. Users are informed of the relevant contact information (division email), where they can reach for more information/special data requests. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The DMD publishes contact information (division contact email) for each data series to submit inquiries. The DMD commits to responding to all requests within three working days. The assistance provided to data users is regularly monitored through surveys of users. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The DMD provides a list of disseminated statistics, documents, and other services, which are updated on an annual basis. Products are available free of charge.
