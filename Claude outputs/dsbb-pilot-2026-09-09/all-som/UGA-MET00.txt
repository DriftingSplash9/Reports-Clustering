 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Concepts and definitions follows internationally accepted standards as outlined in IMTS concepts and definitions, Rev. 4., IMTS compilation manual 2010 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification based on international standards, e.g. Commodities classified according to HS Codes, SITC and BEC Scope of the data Covers formal trade cleared by customs authorities and informal/unrecorded cross border trade collected through surveys. Exceptions to coverage Goods procured in ports like aircraft parts and goods obtained under financial lease. Unrecorded activity Informal Cross Border Trade (ICBT) which is significant and contributes about 14-25% of total merchandise exports. 2.4.1 Valuation Imports – CIF prices ***Imports at FOB prices requires involvement and sensitization of stakeholders (Customs and Clearing agents) for proper disaggregation of the components Exports – FOB prices 2.4.2 Recording basis Recording done at the time of launching the customs declaration form. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Done according to international standards. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources include: Customs data-general imports and exports data Commodity authorities(complementary).-coffee, tea, cotton, fish, tobacco Informal cross border trade survey-Records all goods that cross the borders but are not recorded by customs 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording All definitions, scope, classification and valuation follows internationally accepted standards. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data available on monthly basis and 15 days after the end of the previous months. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Data sources are regularly assessed under International Trade Statistics Committee and data reconciliation meetings and reports sent to individual compilers about proposed improvements/revisions. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Statistical techniques in data computation are in line with international standards and guidelines laid down in IMTS concepts and definitions. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Informal cross border trade data collected in accordance to IMTS guidelines and estimations based on sound methodology developed by national statisticians. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Data from customs is assessed for accuracy and investigated for statistical discrepancies with data from other sources especially commodity authorities for exports. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Data reconciliation is based on available recommended national data sources to iron out data discrepancies. In some cases, mirror statistics comparison is also used to assess the reliability and accuracy of the data under reconciliation studies sponsored by EAC/COMESA. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses We undertake data reconciliation and verification at national levels and where possible, data revision is undertaken and results published in national publications. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Any revised data is disseminated to the public and a foot note provided in the publication about the revisions made. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request -Non-confidential statistics are made available to the public by express permission of the Executive Director UBOS.
