 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical Framework: The data are published quarterly on the BCRP's website ( http://www.bcrp.gob.pe ) and are broken down by sector (general government, monetary authorities, banking sector, and other sectors), type of instrument, and original maturity (short-/long-term). Definitions: The external debt position represents total liabilities generated by the financial relations maintained by Peru with external creditors. The data is compiled in accordance with the methodology described in the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) . This manual is consistent with 2003 External Debt Statistics: Guide for compilers and users (2003 EDS Guide). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: External debt is estimated essentially in conformity with the international standards contained in the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) . Scope of the data Coverage of external debt: The estimates seek to cover all of Peru's foreign liabilities, together with all related transactions. All the institutional sectors are covered. Geographic coverage: The geographic coverage is that of the national economic territory. 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to unit of account: External debt is recorded in U.S. dollars. Transactions denominated in other currencies are converted separately at the prevailing exchange rate at the time of the transaction. Data on the balances of liabilities presented by the financial corporations for monetary accounts purposes are valued at the exchange rate prevailing on the pertinent date. The Superintendency of Banking and Insurance (SBS) publishes the exchange rates daily. Valuation: All of the debt is at book value. 2.4.2 Recording basis The time of recording is the same as in the pertinent books; in other words, the balances of debt securities recorded relate to liabilities actually constituted. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources: External public debt data are derived from the accounting records of the Ministry of Economy (MEF) and COFIDE. Medium- and long-term private external debt data are taken from quarterly surveys of external debt and foreign investment carried out on a representative sample of firms. The short-term debt balances of the financial sector are based on the reports sent by the financial institutions to the BCRP for monetary accounts purposes. The balances for the nonfinancial sector are derived from the survey of external debt and foreign investment. As regards private sector bonds and similar instruments as well as private sector loans, the records are taken primarily from the quarterly surveys of external debt and foreign investment applied quarterly to approximately 850 enterprises. Different data sources are used to identify these transactions (public regulatory bodies, specialized communications media). The balances relate to those recorded by private enterprises on their books, normally in nominal terms. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The debt balance may vary, owing to factors in addition to the change in external financing, such as the inclusion of late interest, the write-off of balances, repurchase operations and debt swaps, and changes in interest rates. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Only the central government can guarantee financing to financial and nonfinancial public enterprises.
