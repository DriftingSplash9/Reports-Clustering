 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: Data are compiled in accordance with the IMF guidelines set forth in the sixth edition of the IMF’s “ Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual” ( BPM6 ). Organization of the data follows the instructions by the ECB , which are presented in the Guideline of the European Central Bank (ECB/2011/23, amended by ECB/2013/25). Additional methodological guidelines followed: The IMF’s “International Reserves and Foreign Currency Liquidity: Guidelines For A Data Template” (Guidelines for a Data Template) ECB Guideline “Statistical treatment of the Eurosystem’s international reserves” (Oct 2000) ( Guideline ) Concept, Definition, and Relationship to international guidelines: The reserve assets must: (i) be under the effective control of the BoE and (ii) refer to highly liquid, marketable and creditworthy non-euro area currency-denominated claims on non-residents of the euro area as well as gold, special drawing rights and the reserve positions in the IMF of Estonia. Deposits with banks located in Estonia and/or in other euro area Member States are therefore not considered as part of the official reserves. Reserve assets are compiled on a gross basis without any netting of reserve-related liabilities (with the exception of those reserve assets included in the sub-item of financial derivatives, which are recorded on a net basis). All reversible gold transactions are treated as collateralized loans and recorded accordingly in the national reserves statistics. According to the euro area concepts, reverse repos are classified as collateralized deposits and they are included under currency and deposits instead of within other reserve assets. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: Gross international reserves are broken down by the following components: convertible foreign currency (securities, currency and deposits), reserve position in the IMF, SDRs, and gold. Other foreign assets comprise the following components: securities, financial derivatives, deposits, loans, and gold. Relationship to international guidelines: Data are compiled using the definitions and requirements contained in the IMF's " International Reserves and Foreign Currency Liquidity: Guidelines for a Data Template (Guidelines for a Data Template) , " Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual " , 6th Edition (BPM6) and ECB Guideline "Statistical treatment of the Eurosystem's international reserves". Scope of the data International reserves and foreign currency liquidity data covering all transactions with the rest of the world are disseminated in millions of euros (EUR). Data include the following categories: Institutional coverage: Data cover the official reserves of Eesti Pank. Instrument coverage: Foreign assets in nonconvertible currencies are excluded. Financial derivatives: Financial derivatives are covered on a net basis. Transactions coverage: No transactions are excluded. Geographical coverage: No geographic regions are excluded from recording. Exceptions to coverage No exceptions. Unrecorded activity No unrecorded activities. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: International reserves are valued at prevailing market prices. Frequency of calculation and revaluation: Reserve assets are calculated on a monthly basis. Foreign currency assets and gold, included in reserves, are revalued monthly at the market price prevailing on the last business day of the month. 2.4.2 Recording basis Accrual principle. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Reserve assets are compiled on a gross basis without any netting of reserve-related liabilities (with the exception of those reserve assets included in the sub-item of financial derivatives, which are recorded on a net basis). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources: Eesti Pank. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Data are compiled in accordance with the IMF guidelines set forth in the sixth edition of the IMF's "Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual" (BPM6). 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Source data timeliness meets the SDDS requirements. Data are released with the timeliness T+5 banking days. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The quality of the data is ensured by cross checking the data with the balance sheet of Eesti Pank. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques No adjustments are applied to accounting records. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Statistical adjustments: None. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Data are verified and cross-checked with the regular accounting data of Eesti Pank. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data n/a 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs In case of discrepancies, the raw data are investigated. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses The annual Quality Report was compiled by BoE until the year 2013 (available on the Bank's website only in Estonian).
