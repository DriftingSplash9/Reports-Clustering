 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical Framework: Defined and compiled according to the methodological requirements of the “European System of Accounts 2010” (“ESA 2010”) and “System of National Accounts 2008” (“SNA 2008”).Definition: The definitions and concepts of the Bulgarian national accounts for all aggregates and balancing items closely follow the ESA 2010 and SNA 2008. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification system: The output and income approaches by activity are compiled according to the Classification of Economic Activities (CEA - 2008) which is consistent with NACE Rev. 2. NACE, Rev.2, Classification of Products by Economic activities (CPEA 2008) and the List of Products of the European Communities (PRODCOM) are applied directly as national classifications. The Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP) is used for household expenditures, the consumer price index (CPI), and the retail trade survey. The Classification of Functions of Government (COFOG) is used for individual and collective government expenditures. Institutional units, sectors and transactions are classified according to ESA 2010. Scope of the data Unit coverage: The national accounts in principle cover the economic activity of all Bulgarian resident units in conformity with the "SNA 2008" and "ESA 2010". Residency is established after 12 months in accordance with the requirements of ESA 2010. Transaction coverage: The Bulgarian national accounts cover all non-financial and financial transactions. Geographic coverage: National accounts cover the whole territory of Bulgaria, including embassies in other countries and free trade zones. Unrecorded activity Undercoverage of gross output is particularly noted for small private sector units, production for own final use and construction. The exhaustiveness adjustments covering non-response, informal activities, economically hidden transactions, income in kind, labor force survey and iliegal activities adjustment are incorporated in the annual and quarterly accounts on regular basis. 2.4.1 Valuation The valuation rules used for recording flows and stocks are in accordance with the ESA 2010. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: Accrual accounting is followed for all accounts; accrual estimates are made for cash basis sources such as administrative records of general government units as adjustments are included. Recording period: Calendar quarter. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The compilation of the quarterly GDP estimates by the production approach follow the structure of the annual one, the only difference being the level of breakdown and the less detailed data sources. GDP estimates contain FISIM allocation by institutional sector treated as users of financial services as FISIM allocation is included into corresponding GDP aggregates.The main statistical surveys conducted by the NSI and used for quarterly national accounts purposes are as follows:Sources of data are statistical surveys, annual reports of economic units and administrative data. Data sources for production approach: Statement of revenue and expenses, including statistical annexes; Statement of manufacture and sales of industrial production; Receipts of construction enterprises by type of construction; Wholesale and retail sale by groups of goods; National energy balance; Balance sheets of about 130 product groups consumed in the process of production activity of enterprises and divisions; Economic Accounts for Agriculture and Natural balance sheet of agricultural production; Annual report on employees, salaries, wages and other labour costs; Sample surveys on specific entities in institutional framework of the economic system: Survey of household budgets; Business surveys; Survey of private farms; Annual reports: Annual Report of non-financial corporations; Annual Report of the non-profit organizations; Annual Report of the banking institutions; Annual Report of insurance companies and pension funds; Annual Report, other financial intermediaries; Administrative data and balance accounts: Consolidated reports on implementation of state budget and balance accounts on budget units; Customs data; Tax data; Balance of Payments Data sources for expenditure approach: Household Budget Survey (HBS) is a sample survey. The survey uses a two-stage scientific random sampling technique: first, selection of population census areas, and second, selection of households. Institutional households are not included in the sample. The general population from which the sample is formed comprises all households in the country. Institutional households are not studied by the HBS. The unit of observation is each randomly chosen ordinary household irrespective the number of members and their material and personal status. Since 2002 the number of observed households is 3000 and since 2010 - 3060 per quarter. All households are included in the survey on a voluntary basis. The HBS sample is self-weighting, i.e. all households have an identical probability of selection. Households with similar characteristics substitute households that refuse to participate. A special investigation was undertaken to confirm that this procedure did not introduce any bias into the sample. The HBS collects information on monetary expenditure on food products, nonfoods, services, income from wages and salaries, social security benefits, households own production, etc. Since 1999, households’ expenditures are classified according to COICOP; Quarterly report on investment of enterprises. The exports and imports of goods valued free on board and calculated on the basis of monthly customs declarations. The External Trade Statistics Division of the NSI provides data at the 8-digit level of the Combined Nomenclature, which has a transition key to the national classification of products and NACE, Rev.2. The same figures are included in the BOP. Quarterly Balance of Payments compiled by the BNB on the basis of the IMF Balance of payments Manual, 6-th Edition used for estimation the imports/export of services. Data sources for income approach: The income approach is compiled in parallel with the production approach using information from the survey for activity of enterprises from non-financial sector, Quarterly report on persons employed, hours worked, wages and salaries and Labour force survey. Adjustments for income in kind and people working without labor contracts are made. Labor Force Survey (LFS), used to estimate hidden and self-employed activities. The methodology of the survey has been developed according to the International Labor Organization recommendations and usual international practice. The survey covers a sample of 19 500 noninstitutional households in the whole territory of the country. The reference period is one calendar week (the week before the survey). The data are collected from a two-stage cluster sample of the population aged 15 years and over. Quarterly report on persons employed, hours worked, wages and salaries and other labor costs. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Production approach: The activity breakdown of the quarterly GDP estimates is based on the 2-digit level of NACE, Rev. 2. Conceptual and exhaustiveness adjustments are made. They include corrections for holding gains or loss coverage of working force, income in kind, underreporting. The calculation of agricultural gross output is supplemented by figures for household primary and secondary production and consumption. These are based on estimates of areas under cultivation and livestock estimates produced by region. Intermediate consumption includes estimates of the value of seeds, fertilizer, livestock feed, power, and repairs. Output used for own intermediate consumption is excluded from both output and intermediate consumption. For other economic activities, various methods are used to supplement the standard reports and surveys to arrive at estimates for total output and intermediate consumption. The imputed rent of owners’ occupied dwellings is evaluated by the stratification approach on the basis of number of dwellings and market rent. Consumption of fixed capital is estimated by the perpetual inventory method for non-market activities. Consumption of fixed capital for market activities is derived from depreciation figures reported in the financial reports. The constant prices are obtained through double deflation, output and intermediate consumption are deflated separately, following the ESA 2010 recommendations. The indexes are Paasche type using average previous year prices as price base. The output of the industrial branches is deflated at the 2-digit level of NACE, Rev. 2. The deflator presents a combination of domestic PPIs and export UVIs. Agricultural output at constant prices is estimated using direct valuation of quantities in the previous year prices; intermediate consumption is derived by deflation. The relevant business statistics divisions supply the deflators for trade, transport, and communications. The market output of the other services is calculated at constant prices using the corresponding CPIs. The trade margin (output) is calculated at constant prices using double deflation. Combined CPI, PPI, and UVI indices are used as deflators for purchases by the wholesale and retail trade, adjusted for change in inventories. The output of non-market services and construction at constant prices is the sum of the deflated value added and intermediate consumption. The wages and salaries index for each industry is used for value added. In the case of intermediate consumption, a suitable price index is applied to each of its components. The implicit deflator for domestic demand is used to deflate financial intermediation services indirectly measured (FISIM).The volume measures of net taxes on products are estimated by applying the base year tax rates to the volume of the transactions that are subject to each tax or subsidy. Expenditure approach : The estimate of GDP by final expenditure is compiled independently from the production data. Since 1996 the estimates are in accordance with the national concept and in compliance with the ESA 2010. In some cases the estimated values of some specific transactions from the production side are applied directly to the related categories of final demand, imputed rent of owner occupied dwellings, production of households for own final use, and own account construction of residential buildings. Some of the data from the demand side are equally important in determining the adjustments for exhaustiveness applied to the production figures¾for example the HBS information about expenditure on food is the basis for adjustments to the data for the relevant manufacturing industries and retailers. In some cases the custom statistics for imports and exports of goods are used to adjust the production figures obtained from business statistics. Household final consumption expenditure: Since 1997, household final consumption expenditure is based on the 4-digit level of the COICOP. NSI uses various sources to build up the estimates of household final consumption expenditure. Balance of payments data are used to make adjustments for expenditure by tourists as required by the national concept. A comparoson model between HBS and retail trade data is established in general the the HBS is used for non-food products. Branch statistics data are used for other items such as services, including consumption of water, electricity, and heat energy. Retail trade data have also been presented in accordance with COICOP since the year 2000. COICOP classes are adjusted for the purchases of businesses, income in kind, and household production for own final consumption. NPISH final consumption expenditure: The final consumption expenditure of NPISHs is derived from the corresponding gross output figures, adjusted for the revenues from sales. Government final consumption expenditure: The government final consumption expenditure is calculated by subtracting the value of sales, which are included in the intermediate consumption or in household final expenditure, from the gross output of general government. The distinction between final and intermediate consumption is based on detailed information from the state budget. The activity detail of the National Classification of the Functions of the State Budget provides the information necessary for the individual/collective split of consumption expenditure. Gross fixed capital formation: GFCF is measured as expenditure on the acquisition of fixed assets (the actual expenditure on construction activity, improvements of existing assets, cost of delivery and installation of fixed assets, and progress payments by customers), less the disposal of existing fixed assets. The commodity flow approach is used for cross-checking the consistency of the GFCF data for construction, machinery, and equipment. Data on sales to the domestic market of those branches producing investment goods and information from customs statistics on imports of investment goods are also used. Changes in livestock used for production are derived from the agricultural balances. Expenditure on acquisition of assets by households is estimated on the basis of information on the number of permissions for individual construction of dwellings. Changes in inventories: Measurement of the changes in inventories are based on quarterly information. From the enterprises sector survey “Financial Results”. Data are available in value terms for the stocks at the beginning and at the end of the period. Changes in inventories for enterprises are derived as the difference between the inventory levels at the beginning and end of each quarter and are adjusted for holding gains/loss. Imports and exports of goods and services: The exports and imports of goods are valued free on board and are calculated on the basis of customs declarations. The data on exports and imports of services cover construction, transport, communication, financial and insurance services, operating leasing, culture, science, sports, maintenance of embassies, etc. This breakdown is provided by the BOP. Since 2001, the BOP provides the figures not only in US dollars but also in Euro and BGN Levs, based on actual transaction exchange rates. Constant prices: The household final consumption expenditure estimates at constant prices are derived by applying the relevant CPIs for the corresponding COICOP items at the 3-digit level. The CPIs used are chain-linked, Paasche type indices covering the whole territory of the country. The final consumption expenditures of general government and NPISHs are deflated with the implicit output indices of the corresponding activities.GFCF is obtained at constant prices by applying appropriate Paasche price indices¾the implicit construction output deflator, domestic PPIs, and import UVIs for investment goods; and the implicit deflator of the output of agricultural productive goods. Depending on the average stock-holding period the value of inventories by type and by branch is estimated at average previous year prices using specific monthly or quarterly price indices. The exports and imports of goods are deflated with the corresponding Paasche type UVIs. The price index is calculated from the average prices of selected commodities for each 2-digit NACE, Rev. 2 class. The import of services is deflated with the harmonized CPI of EU-15. Exports of services are derived at constant prices by applying the corresponding national CPI. The income approach : The income approach is compiled in parallel with the production approach, which means it does not lead to an independent GDP estimate. It is presented separately for the public and private sector following the same level of disaggregation as the production account. Estimates are made for the compensation of employees, other taxes and subsidies on production and imports and consumption of fixed capital. The operating surplus/mixed income is derived as a balancing item. For compensation of employees, reports of enterprises are supplemented by information obtained through labor force surveys; estimates for compensation in kind are made especially for employees in transport, communications, mining and quarrying, and catering activities. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Seasonal adjustment: Main GDP aggregates are seasonally adjusted and published. The model used is Tramo/Seats. The seasonal adjustment model is applied to the component of the quarterly time series (direct approach). 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Reconciliation: Reconciliation of accounts is based on the interrelation of the accounts within the system. To a great extent, the balancing of the data in the methodological framework of accounts is related to the process of ensuring the exhaustiveness of GDP measures. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Reconciliation: Many discrepancies in supply and demand by industry arise from informal and underground activities. These particularly affect manufactory, wholesale and retail trade. Comparisons of directly measured flows with those implicit in the Household Budget Survey may lead to adjustments in output, income, and consumption. Reconciliation between the production and final expenditure approaches is carried out in this manner. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Reconciliation: The officially published quarterly data for GDP are based on the production and expenditure approaches. The estimates are fully balanced - the additional item for unbalanced sums in the structure of final use components – statistical discrepancy is equal to zero. The revised annual data at t+9m are followed by the respective reconsilation procedute in quarterly data. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Data are available through a fax service on request in Bulgarian/English for a fee.
