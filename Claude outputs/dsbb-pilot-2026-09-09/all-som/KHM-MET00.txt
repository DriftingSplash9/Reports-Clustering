 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization CED classifies data according to the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS) of the Customs Co-operation Council, with the seventh and eighth digit extensions for national purposes. Scope of the data On the export side, the most important tax-free components of general merchandise trade are informal exports of timber and rubber, exports (principally garments) under the Generalized System of Preferences (GSP), agricultural produce (principally fish and rice), and informal re-exports to neighboring countries. On the import side, estimates are made for the following goods that enter duty-free: imports by government, commodity and project-aid imports, capital goods imports related to direct investment enterprises, and raw materials imports by export-oriented businesses. Unrecorded activity However, there is still a significant amount of unrecorded (informal and illegal) trade not covered by the CED data Informal trade estimates based on irregular inquiries are made for a range of imported goods, especially petroleum products, vehicles, electronic and white goods, construction materials (steel and cement) , and food and beverages. Demand-side estimates, using data from the NIS household surveys and national accounts database, are also used in compiling these under-coverage estimates. Goods for processing and goods for repair are not separately identified in the statistics. These are included with other commodities imported and exported. Estimates of goods procured in ports by carriers are made on the basis of information provided by the various airlines and shipping companies . 2.4.1 Valuation Imports are valued on c.i.f. basis, and exports on f.o.b. basis. For conversion of the customs import data to f.o.b. valuation, freight and insurance are estimated to be 7.2 percent and 0.8 percent respectively of the c.i.f. value of imports. Periodic studies have been conducted to ensure that these percent ratios are still valid. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The NBC compiles data on general merchandise trade from a number of sources. Monthly statistics of imports and exports of goods, which are subject to import duty or export taxes, are collected from the Customs and Excise Direction (CED). To these are added NBC's estimates of tax-free exports and duty-free imports, including informal trade. CED publishes merchandise trade data on a six-monthly basis. CED has commenced providing annual exports and imports by country and 2-digit commodity data to NBC from the beginning of 2004. However, there is still a significant amount of unrecorded (informal and illegal) trade not covered by the CED data. On the export side, estimates of informal timber exports are based on partner country information and partial indicative data collected from the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries and irregular inquiries at Customs border points. Tax-free rubber and agricultural produce exports are estimated from data collected from the Ministry of Agriculture. The Ministry of Commerce provide d data on GSP exports up to 2004, which were cross-checked with Customs GSP exports data. Since 2005 GSP exports data are collected directly from CED, which are cross-checked with GSP exports data recorded by MOC. Re-exports are derived by applying the estimated shares of particular commodities to the imports of those commodities , as CED cannot provide the data separately. The estimated breakdown for formal exports is reconciled to total CED recorded exports. On the import side, data on government imports are derived from government accounts maintained by the Ministry of Economy and Finance. Estimates of commodity aid imports and imports financed by project aid are based on international aid statistics compiled by the Council for the Development of Cambodia (CDC). Estimates of imports by direct investment enterprises and by export-oriented businesses are based on import approvals granted by the CDC. The estimated breakdown for formal exports is reconciled to total CED recorded imports. Informal trade estimates based on irregular inquiries are made for a range of imported goods, especially petroleum products, vehicles, electronic and white goods, construction materials (steel and cement) , and food and beverages. Demand-side estimates, using data from the NIS household surveys and national accounts database, are also used in compiling these under-coverage estimates.
