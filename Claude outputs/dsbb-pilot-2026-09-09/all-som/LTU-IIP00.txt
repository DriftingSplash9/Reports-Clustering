 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The concepts and definitions of the IIP of Lithuania are based on the conceptual framework of the international standards recommended by the sixth edition of the IMF Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6), European System of Accounts (ESA 2010), methodological requirements of Eurostat’s (BoP Vademecum), Guideline ECB/2011/23 (Recast) on the statistical reporting requirements of the European Central Bank in the field of external statistics, OECD Benchmark Definition of Foreign Direct Investment (Fourth Edition 2008), Regulation (EC) No 184/2005 (Recast) of the European Parliament and of the Council of 12 January 2005 on Community statistics concerning balance of payments, international trade in services and foreign direct investment. Definition: The International Investment Position is a statistical statement that shows at a point in time the value of financial assets of residents and the liabilities to non-residents. The net IIP (the stock of external financial assets minus the stock of external liabilities) shows the net worth of economy and the net lender/borrower status of an economy. The position at the end of a specific period reflects financial transactions, valuation changes, and other adjustments, all of which affect the level of assets or liabilities, that occurred during the period. The foreign assets component includes direct investment abroad, portfolio investment, and other investment and official reserve assets. The data on assets and liabilities positions are broken down further by sector and show the position as at the end of the reference period. The IIP data are used to calculate gross external debt. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification: The IIP components are classified according to the sixth edition of the IMF Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6), European System of Accounts (ESA 2010), methodological requirements of Eurostat’s (BOP Vademecum), Guideline ECB/2011/23 (Recast) on the statistical reporting requirements of the European Central Bank in the field of external statistics, OECD Benchmark Definition of Foreign Direct Investment (Fourth Edition 2008), Regulation (EC) No 184/2005 (Recast) of the European Parliament and of the Council of 12 January 2005 on Community statistics concerning balance of payments, international trade in services and foreign direct investment (for the IIP data reporting to the Eurostat and ECB purpose). The IIP is classified by functional category, financial instrument, institutional sector, and maturity. Scope of the data Position coverage: The data cover the entire economy's financial assets and liabilities positions with the rest of the world. 2.4.1 Valuation Position of external financial assets and liabilities are valued at market values for traded instruments and at book values (nominal costs) for another instrument. Since 2015 data on positions are compiled in euro and positions denominated in other currencies are converted into euro at the rates set by the LB prevailing at the time of position. 2.4.2 Recording basis Recording basis: The position data in the IIP are recorded taking the data from quarterly surveys. It means, that position at the end of previous quarter is the beginning position of the quarter under review. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Coverage of external financial assets and liabilities: in the IIP of Lithuania, external positions in financial derivatives are recorded on a gross basis 3.1.1 Source data collection programs IIP compilation by the LB is based on the statistical data collected from different data sources and reporting agents – statistical reports, statistical registers, administrative sources, data collected by using surveys, data from the LB accounting, data published by international financial organizations, data published by other countries, mass-media, data obtained by the one-off request from economical entities and government institutions in Lithuania and from colleagues abroad. A part of statistical reporting forms is collected by the LB directly from national economic entities, and some portions of these data are gathered by the State Data Agency. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Direct investment The main data sources in compiling foreign direct investment are: (i) a quarterly survey of enterprises involved in direct investment, conducted by the State Data Agency (TUI-01); (ii) foreign Loans Register of the LB is used for calculation of other capital (loans related to FDI); (iii) the annual data provided by the State Data Agency on real estate sold to nonresidents; (iv) the daily data on prices of equity and debt securities issued in the Baltic states provided by NASDAQ CSD exchanges; (v) the monthly data on listed companies provided by the Central Securities Depository. Furthermore, the Central Securities Depository provides data reported monthly on how securities are distributed among Lithuanian economic entities and non-residents (in total and broken down by country and type of investor‘s sector) as reflected in the accounting of intermediaries in public securities trading; (vi) the quarterly survey of cross-border transactions of commercial banks, conducted by the LB (B-09-01); (vii) the information from annual financial statements is used for calculation the FDI equity capital. FDI quarterly and annual surveys of non-banking sector are conducted by regional NSI's office. Quarterly reporting data from non-banking sector are collected by sampling survey of economic entities. Entities are selected according either to their level of representation in a specific activity or to their scope of transactions with non-residents. Quarterly reports submit 1.9 thousand of non-banking sector entities. Annual FDI data are collected by census survey of 3.4 thousand of non-banking sector entities. Annual FDI data are submitted by 15 May and final FDI data for the accounting year (including quarterly data revision) are compiled in September. By using quarterly surveys the LB directly collects FDI data from commercial banks. Moreover, the LB uses FDI data provided by Central Securities Depository. The LB compiles final FDI data using all aforementioned data sources (individual FDI data from NSI's initial FDI database are also available). FDI data is broken down by a geographical area and economic activity (using NACE codes corresponding to the economic activities used in the Eurostat/OECD codification for economic activity groups), except the adjustments due to exchange rate changes, adjustments due to price changes, reclassification and other adjustments (these adjustments are reported for the total FDI, without geographical breakdown). Portfolio investment is understood as foreign investment involving less than 10% of voting rights and no extensive powers in corporate management decisions. Portfolio investment covers positions in equity and debt securities. Portfolio investment is broken down further by instrument, sector, country (since the first quarter of 2004) and maturity. Some 90% of portfolio investment data are now based on security-by-security information. The primary data sources are: (i) the monthly security by security data from the custodians and directly from MFI's on the distribution of securities by holders (including government securities; (ii) the monthly survey of collective investment and money market funds conducted by the LB (KIS-01), which includes data on cross-border transactions of these funds; (iii) information from the Ministry of Finance on the government's cross-border transactions in debt securities and information on debt securities issued by central government; (iv) the monthly security by security data from the Central Securities Depository on the distribution of securities by issuers (including government securities); (v) the monthly survey of cross-border transactions of commercial banks, conducted by the LB (B-09-02); (vi) the daily data on prices of equity and debt securities issued in the Baltic states provided by NASDAQ CSD exchanges; (vii) the monthly data on securities by issuers obtained from the ECB' Centralized Securities Database (CSDB); (viii) the quarterly SHS third party holdings on euro-area securities holdings. (ix) the quarterly survey of insurance companies conducted by the LB which includes data on cross-border transactions of these funds. Financial derivatives: The sources of data are as follows: (i) the quarterly survey of cross-border transactions of banks conducted by the LB (B-09-01) and reports provided by pension and collective investment funds; (ii) the monthly reports (Balance Sheet data) of MFI's sector; (iii) the Balance Sheet data, received from the Financial Accounting Information System (FAIS) of the LB; (iv) the quarterly survey of cross-border transactions of government sector conducted by the Ministry of Finance; (v) the monthly data of cross-border transactions of banks about the realized profit/loss on financial derivatives; (vi) the quarterly survey of cross-border transactions of collective investment funds (KIS-01 report). Financial derivatives are accounted for when they are created or sold, or when other actions provided for thereunder are affected. Purchase (sale) transactions are accounted for at the market value. Transactions are recorded on a gross basis: transactions in assets are recorded separately from those in liabilities. Included are foreign exchange purchases (sales), interest rate, precious metals and liability related financial derivatives transactions (forwards, swaps, futures, and options). Other investment: The sources of data are as follows: (i) the quarterly survey of cross-border transactions of non-bank enterprises conducted by the State Data Agency (F-06) is used for calculation the trade credits and, currency and deposits items; (ii) the quarterly surveys of cross-border transactions of banks conducted by the LB (B-09-01); (iii) information from the Ministry of Finance on government loans received (and repaid) on behalf of the state and guaranteed by the government and information on loans received from non-residents directly by local government; (iv) the monthly survey of non-bank enterprises conducted by the LB (B-09-04). (v) the Foreign Loans Register of the LB, which includes foreign loans received without state guarantee by legal entities of the Republic of Lithuania and of loans granted to foreign economic operators. (vi) the data on external operations from the balance sheet of the LB. (vii) information from BIS Quarterly Review about stocks on currency and deposits with foreign banks. Breakdowns are available by sector, instrument, country (since the first quarter of 2004) and maturity. Reserve assets Official reserve assets – highly liquid, marketable, and creditworthy foreign assets controlled by the LB that may be used for direct financing of the payment's imbalances and other similar purposes. The data on reserve assets are compiled in accordance with the methodology set out both the BPM6 and ECB' Operational Guideline for the Data Template on International Reserves and Foreign Currency Liquidity. Starting with the January 2015, the claims on non-euro area residents denominated in convertible foreign currencies (other than the euro), monetary gold, SDR and reserve position in the IMF are classified as official reserve assets. The LB external assets that cannot be classified as reserve assets are recorded in the data item ‘Portfolio Investment', ‘Financial Derivatives and Employee Stock Options' or ‘Other Investment' under IIP. The data are available from the LB records. The Balance Sheet of the LB received from the Financial Accounting Information System (FAIS) of the LB is the major source for accounting of reserves assets. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Source data timeliness are stated in the Resolution No. 241 of 24 December 2009 of the Board of the LB on the approval of the procedure for submitting to the LB the statistical information and data necessary to compile the BOP and IIP. Order No. V2010/(1.7-0202)-02-42 of 25 February 2010 of the Chairman of the Board of the LB on the approval of terms for submitting to the LB the data needed to compile the BoP, IIP and official international reserves. This resolution states the kind of statistical data to be provided by the various ministries, public institutions and economic units and the required frequency and timeliness. This resolution also states that BoP and IIP data must be provided to the ECB, Eurostat and the IMF according to the required timetable, which is published on the LB website. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Stocks of external assets and liabilities are presented under the standard components. The change in stocks of standard components reflects transactions (i.e., flows), revaluations (changes in exchange rates, prices) and other adjustments (e.g., reclassifications, corrections) that occurred during the reference period. Direct investment: Data from the primary sources are reconciled and adjustments for coverage, classification and timing are made. The data on equity capital for listed enterprises are recorded at market values and at book value. The data on equity capital for unlisted enterprises are recorded at book value. FDI data in the BoP is broken down by geographical area and by economic activity (using NACE codes corresponding to the economic activities used in the Eurostat/OECD codification for economic activity groups). Since 2007, FDI data include private non-business real estate investment. Since the beginning of 2009, quarterly data (flows and stocks), collected on inward and outward FDI also cover positions between resident and nonresident fellow enterprises. Portfolio investment: Reconciliation of data from these sources and similar (to the FDI) adjustments on coverage, classification, timing, and valuation are made. Since 2006, compiling portfolio investment, data from ECB’ Centralised Security Data Base are being used. For compilation purpose type of security, country and sector of issuer attributes are used, and for cross-checking purpose price and currency of security issued attributes are used. Other investment: Estimations for the trade credits as well as for the other items (loans, deposits) are not made. Data (positions, transactions, exchange rate, other correction adjustment) on these items are obtained from quarterly surveys. Data on deposits with and loans from foreign banks are supplemented with the BIS data. Reserve assets: Reserve assets are compiled in accordance with the gross concept, without any netting with monetary authorities’ liabilities. Reserve assets are valued at prevailing market prices. Gold holdings are revalued on the last business day of each month at market price at the London bullion market. Holdings of foreign exchange (cash) are revalued on a currency-by-currency basis on each business day at the official exchange rate of that day. Securities are revalued at market price on the last business day monthly. Value of securities and deposits at the reference date includes accrued interest. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Additional detail: geographical detail on foreign direct investment assets and liabilities position data, geographical detail, and currency composition on official borrowing (debt securities and loans) and official international reserves position data are available. Since 2010 quarterly reporting of FDI data from non-banking sector are collected by sampling survey of economic entities. Entities are selected according either to their level of representation in a specific activity or to their scope of transactions with non-residents. Other: Seasonal adjustment of data is not applied. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results When preparing the IIP all components of respective item data are checked in terms of internal consistency and consistency within a respective period. Data obtained additionally are compared with respective data of other statistical sources. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses After the compilation annual data, quarterly IIP data are revised accordingly. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Information on contact points for statistical information compiled by the LB is published on the LB website https://www.lb.lt/en/statistics-contacts .
