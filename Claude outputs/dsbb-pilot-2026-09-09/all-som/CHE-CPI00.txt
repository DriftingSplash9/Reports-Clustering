 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Definition: Average measure of pure change in prices of goods and services bought for the purpose of consumption by the resident households in Switzerland. Concept and definitions follow internationally accepted standards: (CPI Manual 2020) - Consumer Price Index Manual: Theory and Practice 2020 (ILO, IMF, OECD, Eurostat, United Nations, and the World Bank), available on the following webpage: Consumer Price Index Manual - Concepts and Methods | 2020 (ilo.org) More information on the HICP: Overview - Harmonised Indices of Consumer Prices (HICP) - Eurostat (europa.eu) Legislation of the HICP: http://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/hicp/legislation 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification system: As defined by the international product nomenclature COICOP (Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose). At the detailed level, ECOICOP (5-digit level) is applied both for the CPI and the HICP: https://showvoc.op.europa.eu/#/datasets/ESTAT_European_Classification_of_Individual_Consum ption_according_to_Purpose_version_2_%28ECOICOP_2%29/data Scope of the data Population coverage: All private households residing permanently in Switzerland. CPI: all private households residing permanently in Switzerland. HICP: private and institutional households. Expenditures coverage: CPI: Expenditure of resident households, inside the economic territory and abroad (National concept). HICP: Expenditure of resident and non-resident house­holds, inside the economic territory (Domestic Concept). Geographic coverage: All of Switzerland; sample of 11 regions nationwide. Item coverage: All goods and services bought by the reference population for the purposes of consumption; expenditure on direct taxes, cash gifts, gambling and illegal activities are excluded; the CPI and the HICP have 13 main groups, more than 400 published index series and more than 1100 survey positions. Exceptions to coverage See chapter 2.2.1.1 Imputed rents for owner-occupied dwellings are excluded from the HICP but included in the CPI. Homes are included in the HICP but excluded from the CPI. Unrecorded activity See chapter 2.2.1.1 2.4.1 Valuation Types of prices: Cash prices; on an acquisition basis; unconditional subsidies and discounts are reflected; sale prices recorded if temporary reductions; money off coupons, loyalty cards, free gifts are ignored; rents net of electricity, water, heating charges and caretaking. 2.4.2 Recording basis Most prices are collected once a month during the first two weeks of the month. Fuel and a part of gasoline prices are collected twice a month (at the beginning and at mid-month). Other gasoline prices are collected based on data transmission. Food and near food prices from the most important retailers are collected for the 2 first weeks of the month using scanner data. In and out of season months are defined for fruits, clothing and some other products. Prices of some services are collected on a quarterly basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Sources of weights: The Household Budget Survey conducted by the Swiss Federal Statistical Office (FSO) during January-December T-1 among 3'000 private households of all socio-economic groups. Time period of current weights: December T-1. Frequency of weight updates: The Consumer Price Index is designed as a relative chain index which is reweighted annually in December. Item selection: The selection of products is purposive; within item specification, price collectors, in cooperation with sales outlets representatives, select products with significant market share. With scanner data the best selling items per survey position are selected Outlet selection: Purposive; Sale outlets are selected in each of the 11 CPI regions nationwide to price each of the CPI products. The selection is based on the popularity of the outlet (volume of sales), consistency and completeness of the range of products, permanency of outlet, cooperativeness of price informant, geographical location and type of outlet. Sample sizes: On average, approximately 100'000 individual prices are collected each month, from around 5400 sales outlets in the 11 regions nationwide. Moreover, for certain indices, aggregated data are collected for index calculation. Price collection methods: The price collection methods are manifold: About 27'000 prices are collected from retail sales outlets and service establishments by the means of tablet computers; five major retailers currently deliver scanner data for food and near food prices; some major retailers with a central pricing policy supply prices directly to the FSO using electronic forms; other methods include price collection by internet or email, printed forms (eg residential rents), catalogues or telephone. Item/variety specification: The FSO supplies general variety specifications to price collectors while the collectors themselves record detailed descriptions covering characteristics such as brand, size, fabric/material, etc. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Timing of price observation: Most prices are collected once a month during the first two weeks of the month. Fuel and a part of gasoline prices are collected twice a month (at the beginning and at mid-month). Other gasoline prices are collected based on data transmission. Food and near food prices from the most important retailers are collected during for the 2 first weeks of the month using scanner data. In and out of season months are defined for fruits, clothing and some other products. Prices of some services and rents are collected on a quarterly basis. Definitive data is disseminated monthly according to SDDS requirements. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Missing prices: If temporarily unavailable, the last price collected is carried forward (for max. 2 periods); if permanently unavailable, a replacement is selected. Quality differences: Small quality changes are not taken into account. Prices are adjusted for substantial quality change in determined positions where quality differences can be quantified. Products considered as totally non comparable are linked into the index. Replacement items: Price collectors select products with significant market share and if possible the same quality; quality is defined in terms of item characteristics listed in the product description; price collectors consult retailers on these issues if necessary. If scanner data are used, replacement items are selected on the basis of turnover. New items: The selection of items is being reviewed continuously and rules are established to introduce new products. Seasonal items: Fresh fruit, vegetables and clothing prices are collected for pre-specified months, the last available prices are carried forward for months with no collection; weights are held constant throughout the year for all types of fruit and vegetables. Seasonal Items HICP: Follows the Commission Regulation no. 330/2009 of 22 April 2009, art. 2.3 on the treatment of seasonal products using a counter-seasonal estimation for the fruits, the vegetables and the COICOP group 3. Owner-occupied housing: Owner-occupied housing is taken into account using the rental equivalence method in the CPI; an index for imputed rents for owner occupied housing is published. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Reference period for the CPI: December 2025 = 100. Reference period for the HICP: 2025=100. Method used to combine basic price observations: Separate indices are calculated for each index position, region and type of outlet. The indices represent the geometric mean of price ratios between the current and base period. Formula for aggregation: Index positions are aggregated to several levels using the Lowe formula and the fixed-weights proportional to annually reweighted consumption expenditures. The national index represents the weighted arithmetic average of indices relating to the different types of outlet s and regions nationwide (11 regions nationwide). Method used to update weights for price change: T-1 weights are adjusted for price change with December T-1 base prices. Linking method: Historical series are constructed at all levels; when changes to the basket are introduced, double price collections (old basket and new basket) are carried out in December T-1 to provide linking factors. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Verification procedures: Collectors are asked to provide explanations for large variations (eg "clearance sales"); FSO staff carry out monthly controls on various individual prices . Automatic validation is carried out at the FSO; invalidated prices and replacements are checked by staff. Compilers and statisticians analyse every index aggregate to ensure movements are consistent with information about commodity and retail markets. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses CPI / HICP revisions occur every 5 years and aim at updating statistical surveys and methods. They do not entail correction of published results.
