I. Analytical Framework, Concepts, Definitions, and Classifications
 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The national accounts framework is largely based on the "System of National Accounts 1993"("1993 SNA). GDP is compiled from both the production and expenditure approaches. GDP by the production approach is total output at basic prices cost less intermediate consumption at market (purchasers’) prices. Producer units are grouped according to principal economic activity. GDP from the expenditure approach is the sum of final uses of goods and services in the total economy, less the value of imports of goods and services.  Private final consumption expenditure is derived as a residual. The business portion of financial intermediation services is allocated entirely to intermediate consumption and excluded from GDP. The national accounts data for St. Vincent and the Grenadines are compiled and disseminated by the Economic Statistics section of  the Statistical Office in collaboration with the Eastern Caribbean Central Bank (ECCB), which has a role in developing methodologies.  2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Economic activities are classified using the International Standard Industrial Classification of All Economic Activities, revision 3.1  (ISIC Rev3.1). The 15 activities covered differ from the ISIC Rev 3.1 presentation by combining some sectors. They comprise the categories shown in parentheses: Agriculture, Forestry and Fishing (A-B) Mining and quarrying (C), Manufacturing (D), Electricity and water (E), Construction (F), Wholesale and retail trade (G), Hotels and restaurants (H), Transportation, Storage & Communication (I), Financial Intermediation (J), Real estate, Renting and Business Activities (K), Public Administration, Defence & Social Security (L), Other Community, Social and Personal Activities (O), Activities of Private Household  with Employed Persons (P).   General government final consumption expenditure is classified according to classification of functions of government (COFOG).     Expenditure on Gross Domestic Product is classified as: Final consumption expenditure ( Private and Government) Gross Capital Formation  ( Gross Fixed Capital Formation inclusive of  changes in inventories) Exports of Goods and Services less Imports of Goods and Services 

II. Scope of the data
 Scope of the data The national accounts cover the entire territory of St. Vincent and the Grenadines. The national accounts data cover in principle the transactions of all resident institutional units within the economy and with the rest of the world. GDP is compiled and disseminated in millions of Eastern Caribbean dollars, by the Statistical Office at current and constant (2006) prices. The reference period is the calendar year. There is a data series starting from 2000. All economic activities performed in St. Vincent and  the Grenadines are covered. Commodity flow methods are used for activities where there is no direct estimation. GDP is compiled and disseminated by both the production and expenditure approaches. The basic data sources include National Accounts Establishment Survey (NAES), government accounting records and other administrative records. More details on the data sources are given in the national accounts comprehensive framework, including Gross Fixed Capital Formation. Gross National Income (GNI) is derived by adding net  incomes from abroad to GDP, from the Balance of Payments (BOP), prepared by the Statistical Office in collaboration with the ECCB. Gross National Disposable Income (GNDI) is derived by adding net current transfers from abroad to GNI, from BOP. Saving is derived as the difference between GNDI and the sum of private and government consumption. For further details on the BOP, see the Comprehensive Framework and Data Categories and Indicators for the External Sector. Unrecorded activity Informal activities are estimated indirectly using commodity flow methods. No adjustments are made for illegal activities. 

III. Accounting Conventions
 2.4.1 Valuation   Output measured by industrial activity is recorded for St. Vincent and the Grenadines at basic prices. Intermediate consumption is measured at market (purchasers’) prices. Exports and imports are valued at free on board (f.o.b.) prices. Data are published by industrial activity at basic prices. GDP by final use is valued at market prices, which is equivalent to the "1993 SNA" purchasers’ price valuation concept. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: Most transactions are recorded on an accrual basis from the source data. An exception is the general government income and expenditure data, which are recorded on a cash basis. Recording period: Data are compiled for the calendar year. However, a number of enterprises compile accounts on a fiscal accounting year. Their annual data are included in the national accounts for a given calendar year if their fiscal year begins before July 1 of that year. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Data is recorded on a gross basis . 

IV. Nature of the Basic Data Sources
 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Production approach: The principal sources for estimating GDP by type of economic activity are: Annual  national accounts survey of establishments Financial statements from establishments Administrative data from the Inland Revenue Department Government accounts and other administrative data National Insurance Services The annual national accounts survey covers all establishments. The sources of specific industrial categories are as follows: Agriculture : Data on  production,  prices and inputs for bananas are obtained from the St. Vincent Banana Growers Association financial statements and merchandise trade data.  Data on production and prices for other crops are derived from  the Ministry of Agriculture. The external trade statistics also provides data on imported inputs used for non-banana crops. Due to unavailability of data, estimates of output and intermediate input for forestry. Data on livestock production, chicks, eggs and local poultry meat are collected from the Ministry of  Agriculture.   Data on the output of forestry is obtained from estimates of household consumption of charcoal using benchmark estimates  from 2001 Census.  Data on quantity landed and prices are obtained  for fishing from the Fisheries Division, Ministry of Agriculture. Mining and quarrying : There is no mining activity. Estimates of output and intermediate consumption for quarrying are derived as a annual National Accounts survey of establishment  and other administrative source. Manufacturing : Value of output and cost of  intermediate inputs  obtained primarily from the annual National Accounts Establishment Survey (NAES) and a monthly industrial production survey. Electricity and water : Output and intermediate inputs for electricity and water are obtained from the financial statements of the Central Water and; Sewerage Authority and St. Vincent Electricity Services, Ltd., as part of the annual NAES. Construction :  Data is obtained using the commodity flow approach. Data on imports of building materials are obtained from external trade statistics. Domestic production of construction material is obtained from a survey of industrial production. Other data are derived from the Consumer Price Index on prices for construction material. Wholesale and retail trade : Data on output and intermediate inputs are estimated from data obtained from the annual NAES of wholesale/retail establishments and from administrative records.   Hotels and restaurants : Output and  cost of intermediate inputs are estimated from data obtained from annual NAES and administrative records.   Transport, Storage and Communication :  Road transport - data on the number of vehicles registered obtained from the Inland Revenue Department.  Air transport data are obtained from annual NA and other administrative records.    Data on supporting and auxiliary transport services are derived from annual National Accounts survey and other administrative records.  Communication: Data  derived from the annual NAES from the telecommunication company. For  postal services  data are obtained from the annual National Accounts Survey.   Financial Intermediation : Data for banking are obtained from the annual NAES and an Eastern Caribbean Central Bank commercial banking reports. Data for insurance is compiled from insurance returns collected for regulatory purposes by the Registrar of Insurance.  Data for activities auxiliary to financial services are obtained from the annual National Accounts establishment survey and other administrative sources.   Real estate, Renting and Business services : For owner occupied dwellings and rented dwellings data  are derived from benchmark data from 2001 census and other administrative records. The annual National Accounts establishment survey  other administrative records  are used for the sub categories of renting of machinery and equipment; computer related services and business  services.   Public Administration, Defence &  Compulsory Social Security :  Data  for  public administration are obtained from  government accounting records . The annual National Accounts establishment survey is the data source for the Compulsory Social Security.   Education:   Data  on cost components are obtained from government accounting records  for the public component and from the annual National accounts  establishment survey and other administrative records  for the private component. Health & Social Work : Data  on  cost components are obtained from government accounting records  for the public component and from the annual National accounts  establishment survey and other administrative records  for the private component. Other community, social  & personal services : Estimates  are based on data obtained from annual National Accounts Establishment Survey and administrative records. Private Household with employed persons:  The data is obtained from the National Insurance Services on gross wages and salaries. Expenditure approach: The main sources for estimating GDP by the expenditure approach are: Government accounts and other administrative data External Trade statistics Balance of Payments statistic Private final consumption: This component of expenditure is determined as the residual of GDP at market prices, using the production method, less Government final consumption, Gross capital formation, and net exports of goods and services. As change in inventories is not separately estimated, this method effectively includes the inventory change component of capital formation in private consumption. Government final consumption : Consumption of general government services is defined as output less the value of commodity and non-commodity sales. The data are obtained from government accounting records of revenues and expenditures.   Gross fixed capital formation : Capital formation in transport and other equipment is compiled using the commodity flow method from external trade statistics on imports of capital goods, as there is no domestic production of these goods. Construction is compiled via a commodity flow approach, as in the treatment of the construction industry in the description of the production approach to compiling GDP above.   Changes in Inventories: As noted under private consumption, there is no separately identified estimate for this item in expenditure on GDP.   Exports and imports of goods and  services: These data are taken directly from the Balance of Payments compiled by the Statistical Office and ECCB.  See the comprehensive framework and categories and indicators for the External Sector for details on the compilation of net exports of goods and services. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data  are available on an annual basis. 

V. Compilation Practices
 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Production approach: Coverage adjustments: Various methods are used for the treatment of missing values of output and intermediate consumption for individual establishments in the National Establishment Survey and other sources. Among these are estimating current intermediate consumption on the basis of its past share in an establishment’s output, estimating output on the basis of the past ratio of output to selected cost data, extrapolating the most recent past observed level of output, intermediate input, and value added on the basis of growth rates observed either in the past or currently in reported data for similar establishments. Commodity flow approach to output estimation is used for construction to limit potential coverage problems from incomplete survey coverage of informal and/or non-reporting establishments. Estimation of current price data : Value added (GDP) by industry is the value of output less intermediate consumption. Agriculture : Data on output and intermediate consumption for bananas are obtained from the St. Vincent Banana Growers Association financial statements and merchandise trade data. Estimates of output and intermediate consumption for other crops are derived from data obtained from the Ministry of Agriculture. The external trade statistics also provides data on imported inputs used for non-banana crops. Due to unavailability of data, estimates of output and intermediate input for forestry. Data on livestock production, chicks, eggs and local poultry meat are collected from the Ministry of  Agriculture.   Data on the output of forestry is obtained from estimates of household consumption of charcoal using benchmark estimates  from 2001 Census. Fishing output and intermediate consumption are derived from the Fisheries Division, Ministry of Agriculture. Mining and quarrying : There is no mining activity. Estimates of output and intermediate consumption for quarrying are derived as a annual National Accounts survey of establishment  and other administrative source. Manufacturing : Output and intermediate consumption are obtained primarily from the annual National Accounts Establishment Survey (NAES) and a quarterly industrial production survey. Electricity and water : Output and intermediate consumption for electricity and water are obtained from the financial statements of the Central Water amp; Sewerage Authority and St. Vincent Electricity Services, Ltd., as part of the annual NAES. Construction : This sector is estimated using a commodity flow approach based on imports of construction material and domestic production.  Output is measured as the total cost of inputs obtained from external trade statistics on imports of construction materials, production survey data on locally produced construction materials, and the wages of construction labor. Value added is computed as a ratio of measured output. The ratio of value-added to output is estimated from a sample of construction companies. Wholesale and retail trade : The output and intermediate consumption is estimated from data obtained from the annual NAES of wholesale/retail establishments and from administrative records.   Hotels and restaurants : Value of output and intermediate consumption are estimated from data obtained from annual NAES and administrative records.   Transport, Storage and Communication : Value of output and intermediate consumption of road transport is estimated using the number of vehicles registered obtained from the Inland Revenue Department, and data from a benchmark survey conducted by the Statistical Office to derive annual revenue and intermediate consumption for each category of the following transportation activities: commuter and tour buses, taxis, self-hire vehicles, and freight trucks and vans. Output and intermediate consumption for sea transport are estimated using data from the annual NAES and administrative records. Air transport output and intermediate consumption are obtained from the same source.    Data on supporting and auxiliary transport services are derived from annual National Accounts survey and other administrative records.  Communication: Output and intermediate consumption for communications is derived from the annual NAES from the telecommunication company. The value of output and intermediate consumption for Post Office is obtained from the annual NAES.   Financial Intermediation : The output for banking is measured in GDP by economic activity as net interest and other  income.  FISIM is allocated to intermediate consumption and final users based on the stock of loans and deposits. insurance returns collected for regulatory purposes by the Registrar of Insurance.  Data for activities auxiliary to financial services are obtained from the annual National Accounts establishment survey and other administrative sources.   Real estate, Renting and Business services : Output and value added of owner occupied dwellings and rented dwellings are derived from benchmark data from 2001 census and other administrative records. Estimates of output and intermediate consumption are derived from the annual  National Accounts establishment survey  other administrative records for the sub categories of renting of machinery and equipment; computer related services and business  services.   Public Administration, Defence &  Compulsory Social Security :  Data on the value of output and intermediate consumption are obtained from  administrative records for public administration and  the annual National Accounts establishment survey for the Compulsory Social Security. Value added is computed  as the compensation of employees obtained from government accounting records for central government and local government. Data for National Insurance Scheme is compiled from annual NAES.   Education:   Data on cost components are obtained from administrative records for the public component and from the annual national accounts establishment survey and other administrative records  for the private component on out and intermediate consumption. Health & Social Work : Data on cost components are obtained from administrative records for the public component and from the annual National accounts  establishment survey and other administrative records  for the private component on out and intermediate consumption. Other community, social  & personal services : Estimates of value of output and intermediate consumption  are based on data obtained from annual National Accounts Establishment Survey and administrative records. Private Household with employed persons:  The data is obtained from the National Insurance Services on gross wages and salaries. Estimation of constant price data :  Several methods are used for compiling constant price data, depending on the industry. In the first method, constant price value added is computed by deflating the value of output and intermediate inputs for bananas, fishing and livestock. The second method  is deflating the value of output by a price index  and in the case of wholesale and retail trade the trade margin, then deriving a volume  index of the deflated output to extrapolate the constant price for other crops; mining and quarrying; construction; wholesale and retail trade; restaurants; communication, financial intermediation and  FISIM.  For public administration, defence and compulsory social security( includes central government, local government and social security); education; health and social work a weighted index of wages and salary is used to deflate the current prices. Thirdly, constant price estimates were derived by extrapolating the base year value with a volume index for manufacturing; electricity and water; real estate, renting and business services; other community services; hotels; transport (road , air , sea and other supporting transport services). Expenditure approach: Coverage adjustments: There are no adjustments in the sources and estimates of expenditure on GDP not already mentioned under the production approach. Estimation of current price data: Private consumption is determined as the residual of GDP using the production method less government consumption, gross capital formation, and exports less imports of goods and services. It thus effectively includes changes in inventories. Gross fixed capital formation comprises investment in equipment and structures. Equipment investment is estimated via a commodity flow method principally using data on imports, and structures investment is the gross output of the construction industry. There are no adjustments to equipment investment to include set-up costs. Exports and imports o f goods and services derive from the balance of payments estimates. Reconciliation: The estimates from the production and expenditure approaches are not independently determined as private final consumption is calculated as a residual and reconcile by definition. Exports and imports of goods and services used in the calculation of GDP and net  incomes from abroad used in the calculation of GNP (GNI) are taken from the Balance of payments, and therefore reconcile with those data by definition. Frequency of major revisions : A comprehensive revision of methodology and changes in concepts and classifications took place in 2010. The principal areas of improvement were in the methodologies used for compiling GDP for fishing, livestock, construction , FISIM and the other economic activities. There is a revised  time series available for the period 2000-2010, with the base year 2006 =100. Base year and update cycle: The year of  reference for constant price series is 2006; in principle, it is updated on a 10 year cycle.
