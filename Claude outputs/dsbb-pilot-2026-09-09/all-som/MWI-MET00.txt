 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The NSO compiles trade statistics in accordance with the recommendations of the UN "International Trade Statistics, Concepts and Definitions". 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The NSO compiles trade statistics in accordance with the recommendations of the UN "International Trade Statistics, Concepts and Definitions". Goods are classified mainly by the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS) of the Customs Co-operation Council. In addition to HS, goods are also classified by Standard International Trade Classification (SITC). SITC classifies goods mainly for statistical purposes. The office also classifies good by Broad Economic Category. This refers to classification of goods due to end use. Imports are valued at cost, insurance and freight (c.i.f.) and exports at free on board (f.o.b.). Values of goods expressed in foreign currency are converted to Malawi Kwacha using the prevailing weekly average exchange rates. Imports are classified by country of origin and exports by country of destination. Scope of the data Data from almost 90% of the ports are transmitted to NSO (Foreign Trade section) electronically. as a direct consequence of the Automated System for Customs Data (ASYCUDA). Bills of entry from the non-computerized ports are sent to NSO and are keyed in using Eurotrace software. The office performs various processes such as verification of commodity codes to ensure quality control before the data is published. Exceptions to coverage Some goods are excluded from trade statistics. These goods are: passenger baggage, diplomatic goods, monetary gold, motor vehicles imported on a triptych, goods in transit, etc. For sometime, NSO had not been compiling Trade Indices. However, in 2000, the Foreign Trade section began compiling quantum as well as unit price trade indices from 1994 onwards, with 1994 as the base year. The base has since been changed to 2000.
