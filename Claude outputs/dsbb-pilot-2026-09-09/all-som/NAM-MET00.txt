 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions International Merchandize Trade Statistics refers to a specialized multipurpose domain of official statistics concerned with the provision of data on the movements of goods between countries and areas. The NSA is the custodian and is responsible for the compilation of IMTS. The legal basis for the compilation of IMTS is the statistics act 9 of 2011. The IMTS methodology, scope and presentation are in conformity with the International Merchandize Trade Statistics: Compilers Manual, Revision 1 (IMTS 2010-CM) of the United Nations Statistics Division. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The ability to identify and compile details of the commodities in an internationally consistent way for various customs, statistical and analytical purposes and trade negations is by making use of commodity classifications. For the international merchandize trade statistics, both trade flows are primary classified according to the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS). The data is also available in other classifications such as: Standard International Trade Classification (SITC), Broad Economic Category (BEC), Central Product Classification (CPC), and International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC). 2.2.1 Scope The ability to identify and compile details of the commodities in an internationally consistent way for various customs, statistical and analytical purposes and trade negations is by making use of commodity classifications. For the international merchandize trade statistics, both trade flows are primary classified according to the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS). The data is also available in other classifications such as: Standard International Trade Classification (SITC), Broad Economic Category (BEC), Central Product Classification (CPC), and International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC). Scope of the data The scope of International Merchandize Trade Statistics is defined by the general and specific guidelines, The NSA follows these guidelines as recommended by the UN statistics division. In principle all transactions at and above customs threshold are covered. The transactions covered in trade statistics are those of Namibia's residents with the rest of the world. All goods transaction which adds to or subtract from the stock of material resources of Namibia by entering (imports) or leaving (exports) its economic territory are covered. The geographical territory of Namibia includes Export Processing Zones, the flow of goods between the EPZ and residents of Namibia is also covered in trade statistics. Exceptions to coverage ade transactions below customs threshold are not covered under official trade statistics but rather under informal trade statistics. Unrecorded activity Goods in transit and goods smuggled into or out of the country are not included in the coverage of trade statistics by the NSA. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation of imports and exports are all in Namibia dollars (NAD), but the data is also available in three major currencies such as: USD, EUR and GBP. Moreover, exports are valued at free on board (FOB), while imports are valued at cost, insurance, and freight (CIF). Imports at FOB is also available. 2.4.2 Recording basis Goods are recorded at the time when they enter or leave the economic territory of the country as recommended by the World Customs Organization (WCO). 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The NSA follows the IMTS 2010 guideline. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The International Trade Statistics data are sourced from NAMRA. The data are collected using Customs declaration forms filed by clearing agents using the ASYCUDA system. On the first working day of every month, NAMRA gather all the data of the previous month from all the customs border posts and offices and aggregate them as monthly files. The NSA then get the data files from NAMRA between the 1st and 5th of every month. The Agency also get the exchange rate of the previous month from South Africa Reserve Bank (SARB). 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Customs and Excise is mandated to promote the security and facilitation of international trade, transport and people and harmonize Customs procedures. The data are collected from Customs declaration form that the clearing Agents files with Customs using the ASYCUDA system. The data for the previous months are gathered from different customs offices and border post on the first day of the month. The data is shared with the NSA between the 1st and the 5th of every month. The data normally comes in a text format together with control files. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness NAMRA gathers all the data of the previous month from all the customs border posts and offices on the first working day of every month and share the files with NSA within the first week. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Face-to-face meetings and virtual communications are undertaken for the explanation of the methodology involved in data collection and in understanding the variables of interest. Companies are also contacted to shed light on what transpired when there are outliers in the dataset or when more clarity is needed. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Customs officials verify the data before assessing the goods at the time of recording. Capturing of data is automated via an SQL based database well known as ASYCUDA system. The NSA request for the SAD500 forms for further verification when it is needed. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Trade NSA does not do any adjustments or transformation to the data but request NAMRA to do corrections on their system and update the information. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The validation of trade data take place during the uploading and analysis phases. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Data are well investigated by the trade experts during the analysis phase before publication. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Unusual trends are thoroughly attended to by the trade experts who then follow up with NAMRA office and the traders too. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Trade statistics are revised on a monthly basis due to amendments and these revisions are clearly included in the monthly trade bulletins. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses No revision studies have been carried out to date as no revisions to IMTS has been done. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Provided on request to users (Ad- hoc request)
