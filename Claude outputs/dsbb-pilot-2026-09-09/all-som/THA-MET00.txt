 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Definition : The conceptual framework and classification structure of merchandise trade published by the BOT broadly corresponds to that recommended in the International Merchandise Trade Statistics: Concepts and Definitions 2010 (IMTS 2010) . Relationship of national practices with international or regional guidelines : General merchandise data are derived from international trade statistics, compiled by the Customs Department. The customs data record physical movement of goods across the Thai customs frontier. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification : The BOT classifies merchandise trade statistics by product group (PG) defined by the BOT; country of origin (for imports) and destination (for exports); and Standard International Trade Classification (SITC) based on 11-digit Harmonized System code implemented by the Customs Department. Scope of the data Transaction coverage : Data cover exports and imports above the Customs' reporting thresholds. That is, export transactions of less than THB 10,000 (or THB 50,000 for jewelry and ornaments) and import transactions of less than THB 1,500 shipped via postal services (except express courier services) are excluded. Geographical coverage : Data cover exports to and imports from all countries outside the Thai "economic territory". Exceptions to coverage Not applicable Unrecorded activity Unrecorded activities : Shuttle trade, smuggling activities, and transactions below the reporting threshold implemented by the Customs are excluded Unrecorded activities : Shuttle trade, smuggling activities, and transactions below the reporting threshold implemented by the Customs are excluded from the statistics. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation : Exports are recorded at f.o.b. prices, while imports are recorded at c.i.f. prices as declared to the Customs Department. Data are converted from original currencies to Thai baht equivalent using the Customs' announced monthly exchange rates. The amount in THB equivalent is then coverted to USD equivalent by the BOT, using Customs announced monthly exchange rates (bid rate for exports, and offer rate for imports). 2.4.2 Recording basis Recording basis : Customs data record exports at the time at which goods are loaded on to the carrier. Imports are recorded on the release date. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Data are recorded on a gross basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs General merchandise trade data are sourced from administrative records from the Customs Department. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Data are based on customs declarations collected by the Customs Department, classified both by product and by country. Type of data collected : Data collected include the value, quantity, weight, unit of quantity, mode of transport, counterpart country, HS code, etc., both for exports and imports. Basis of country data : Country classification is based on country of origin (for imports) and country of destination (for exports). 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Customs data are made available to the BOT and other compiling agencies on a monthly basis, with timeliness of approximately 18 days. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data are regularly monitored and validated in the compilation process. Unusual figures are investigated and confirmed with source agencies to ensure data quality. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Validated data are aggregated and classified by product group and country. Disseminated tables are also validated against figures announced by other compiling agencies (e.g. Ministry of Commerce) to ensure full consistency. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Seasonal adjustment: Seasonally adjusted series in the form of 12 month- or 6 month- or 3 month-moving average are produced to serve internal analytical tasks only. Geographical detail: Data classified by country of origin/destination are available, with breakdown by major trading partners disseminated on the BOT's website. Commodity detail: Data are broken down by commodity group based on 1-digit SITC and PG. Exports and imports of major products are disseminated on the BOT's website. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Please refer to 3.2.1. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Please refer to 3.2.1. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Statistical coverage is regularly assessed to detect for possible under- / over-recording. Identified gaps would be corrected if deemed significant. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses The DMD monitors the direction and magnitude of revisions and studies the effect of revisions on selected items and periods (including seasonal effects). Documentation on major revisions is maintained for internal use, with detailed information on causes of revision. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses The public is informed of major changes in methodology at the time the change is introduced through a press release and a notice placed on the BOT's website. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request In addition to the merchandise trade statistics and indices disseminated on the BOT's website, other data series can be made available upon request. Unpublished (but non-confidential) specialized tabulations can be provided. Users are informed of the relevant contact information (e-mail addresses and contact numbers), where they can reach for more information/special data requests. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The DMD publishes names and contact information of staff responsible for each data series who can be contacted by e-mail and telephone. The DMD commits to responding to all requests within three working days. The assistance provided to data users is regularly monitored through surveys of users. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The DMS provides a list of publications, documents, and other services, which are updated on an annual basis. Products are available free of charge.
