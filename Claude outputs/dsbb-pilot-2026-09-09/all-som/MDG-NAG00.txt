 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Madagascar’s national accounts are prepared in accordance with the U.N.’s SNA68 . 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The industrial classification used for annual estimates is an adaptation of the International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC rev. 2), reflecting circumstances in Madagascar. For example, and considering the difficulty of disaggregating the data on the agricultural and industrial activities of certain production units, an agroindustrial sector was created. Similarly, all activities of enterprises located in free trade areas are classified as a single industry. Scope of the data Geographic Coverage: The national territory of Madagascar, plus Malagasy territorial enclaves abroad and excluding foreign territorial enclaves in Madagascar. Transaction Coverage: All transactions involving goods and services. Unit Coverage: All units resident in Madagascar, including those operating in free trade areas. GDP estimates by industry are calculated for the following three sectors: primary, secondary, and tertiary. More detailed value added estimates are available for 31 industries. A balance sheet is prepared reflecting GDP and imports on the one hand, and consumption (government, private), gross investment (public, private), and exports on the other. Domestic saving is also calculated. No institutional sector accounts are prepared. These values are calculated in billions of Malagasy francs (FMG), at current prices and at 1984 prices. More complete accounts were prepared in 1995. Based largely on SNA93 , they reflect three GDP approaches: production, expenditure, income. The summary tables published are the Resources and Uses Table (30 products), the Integrated Economic Accounts Table (including financial transactions), and the account for the total economy. The results were used for modeling, particularly for parameterization of the calculable general equilibrium model. Unrecorded activity Activities constituting tax fraud, illegal activities (drug trafficking, prostitution). 2.4.1 Valuation The valuation methods use producer prices for production and acquisition prices for expenditure. Imports are valued c.i.f. and exports f.o.b. 2.4.2 Recording basis The time of recording is consistent with accrual basis accounting. Central government revenue and expenditure data come from the Comprehensive Treasury Operations (OGT) table and are recorded on a cash basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs There are various data sources: data on units of government are administrative; those on private sector activities are provided by the private transistors themselves, estimated by the statistics institute (trade, transportation, etc.), or estimated by the agency responsible for statistics (certain primary sector data are estimated by the statistics unit of the Ministry of Agriculture). Production approach: Agriculture: the data come from the statistics unit of the Ministry of Agriculture, Livestock, and Fisheries and are estimated by conducting a simplified annual survey based on the 1985 agricultural census. Livestock and fisheries : the statistics on livestock and fisheries are estimated and provided by the statistics unit of the same ministry. The fisheries statistics are compiled from administrative reports filed by formal maritime fishing operators and using estimates for other types of fishing. Forestry-hunting: the data are estimated on the basis of the statistics provided by the Ministry of Water and Forests. Energy: the data on electricity and water come from the company responsible for the production and distribution of water and electricity, and the data on petroleum products from the Malagasy Hydrocarbons Office (OMH). Secondary sector: the sources used are the quarterly Industrial Production Indices and the semiannual economic surveys conducted by INSTAT. Construction and public works : estimates based on Public Investment Program indicators and construction permits. Transport : fuel and electricity consumption. Hotels – bars – restaurants: INSTAT conducts semiannual economic surveys of the hotel industries. Services to businesses and households: banking services estimated on the basis of bank risk indicators; number of vehicles for insurance services; information from the economic survey for other industries. Noncommercial services : data from the Comprehensive Treasury Operations table. Taxes and duties come from the Comprehensive Treasury Operations (OGT) table and from the accounting records of local governments. Expenditure approach: International trade: imports and exports come from the international trade unit of INSTAT. Gross capital formation: private investment data are obtained from statistics on construction permits, and public investment data are provided by the Ministry of Economy, Finance, and Budget. Changes in stocks are assumed to be zero. Final consumption: Private consumption is obtained as a residual, and government consumption is calculated using data from the Ministry of Economy, Finance, and Budget. The data collected to calculate the consumer price index are used to produce estimates at 1984 prices. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques For value added estimates for each industry, it is assumed that the annual growth of value added is the same as the growth of production, and that formal sector growth is the same as informal sector growth. The volume and price share of imports and exports is determined directly using the quantities and prices provided by the international trade statistics. Private and public investment are deflated with the price indices for capital goods and construction materials. Government consumption is deflated with the indices for the paper and energy industry.
