 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions I.i.p. statistics are compiled in accordance with the standards set forth in the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6) and the OECD Benchmark Definition of Foreign Direct Investment (4th edition). Benchmark Definition of Foreign Direct Investment (4th edition). Furthermore, specific compilation recommendations from relevant international bodies are observed. In particular, the ECB and Eurostat ( Guideline ECB/2011/23 of 9 December 2011, as amended, and related " European Union balance of payments/international investment position statistical methods " ("b.o.p. and i.i.p. book"), and Commission Regulation (EU) No 184/2005 of 12 January 2005 as amended). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification / sectorization used to compile Cyprus's i.i.p. is essentially the one set in the BPM6 and ESA 2010. For institutional unit definition, the general principles on institutional units (i.e. the centre of economic interest, decision-making autonomy, and complete set of accounts) are applied. SPEs and artificial subsidiaries are treated as follows: The statistical coverage of SPEs is in line with the conclusions of the Eurostat/ECB/OECD 2013 Task Force on head offices, holding companies and SPEs as well as the recommendations of the final report of the relevant IMF's Task Force. Artificial subsidiaries, defined as entities which: Are wholly owned by a parent corporation. Lack the ability to act independently from their parent corporations and may be subject to restrictions on their ability to hold or transact assets held on their balance sheets. Are treated as an integral part of the parent, i.e. not as a separate institutional unit and their accounts are consolidated with those of the parent, unless they are resident in an economic territory different from that of the parent. Regarding geographical allocation, the general principles laid down in the BPM6 on country allocation are applied. In cases where the counterpart country is not known, the "not allocated" code is used. For the allocation to institutional sectors, the CBC and Cystat are cooperating closely to create a "Common Registry" for statistical purposes and to allocate units into institutional sectors for the compilation of external statistics and national accounts. In Cyprus, monetary authorities, other monetary financial institutions (MFIs), government, captive financial as well as non-financial SPEs (ship-owning companies) account for the vast majority of financial cross-border activity. Captive financial institutions form part of a relatively important institutional sector in Cyprus. They are categorised under sector S127, which mainly consists of SPEs, as defined in relevant IMF's Task Force. More precisely, an enterprise is usually considered as an SPE if it meets the following criteria: It is resident in an economy i.e. it is a formally registered and/or incorporated legal entity recognized as an institutional unit, with: no or little employment up to maximum of three employees1, no or little physical presence and no or little physical production in the host economy. It is directly or indirectly controlled by non-residents. It is established to obtain specific advantages provided by the host jurisdiction with an objective to: grant its owner(s) access to capital markets or sophisticated financial services; and/or isolate owner(s) from financial risks; and/or reduce regulatory and tax burden; and/or safeguard confidentiality of their transactions and owner(s). It transacts almost entirely with non-residents and a large part of their financial balance sheet typically consists of cross-border claims and liabilities. 2.2.1 Scope The classification / sectorization used to compile Cyprus's i.i.p. is essentially the one set in the BPM6 and ESA 2010. Scope of the data In principle, i.i.p. statistics produced by the CBC cover all financial stock positions between residents and nonresidents. Concerning residency definition, the general principles on residency, as laid down in the BPM6 manual, are applied. Special purpose entities (SPEs), with the exception of shipping companies for which a special regime is applied (economic ownership), are considered residents if registered/incorporated in Cyprus. Exceptions to coverage In practice, constraints on data sources prevent full coverage for i.i.p. statistics in some area as follows: External transactions (except travel) and financial stocks of resident households may not be fully covered although ongoing efforts aim to achieve this such as making use of mirror data. The recording of real estate abroad (especially for households) has room for improvement, especially for cases where investments in counterpart countries are not captured through mirror data. External transactions and financial stocks on equity, debt securities and financial derivative holdings of residents may not be fully covered in the case where the custodian is a non-resident entity. The CBC uses alternative sources of information to capture this data, such as their audited financial statements and mirror data. The market value of equity securities holdings are calculated using the market price prevailing at the beginning and end of the reporting period. For non-listed entities, if market value is not available, opening and closing positions are, in general, based on the method "own funds at book value (OFBV)". The exceptions to this are (i) when the data are collected through financial statements, in which case equity assets are recorded at the book value as depicted in the company's balance sheet and (ii), non-listed equity securities collected through the "Securities Holdings" Survey (SHS) – here the reporters are instructed to report them based on a comparable stock or based on the net asset value of the company (non-listed equity securities through the SHS concern only a small minority). Debt securities are reported as follows: Debt securities collected through the SHS survey as well as debt securities issued by residents are linked with the CSDB and therefore are recorded at market value. In case debt securities from SHS are not listed (this concerns only a very small minority), then the companies report them based on: the net present value of the expected stream of future payments/receipts associated with the securities, or for unlisted securities, the price used to value securities for accounting or regulatory purposes, or for deep discount or zero coupon securities, the issue price plus amortisation of the discount, or for debt instruments issued at a premium, the issue price less the amortisation of the premium. Debt securities holdings collected directly from the reporting entities through the "External Financial Statistics" (EFS) survey as well as through their financial statements are recorded at the price depicted in their financial statements. Income on dividends , interest and rents are reported at the price at which they are entered in the reporting entity's or the non-resident entity's books. Profits are calculated based on the "Current Operating Performance Concept" and reported on a group basis. Real estate acquisition is valued at the price used for the purchase of the property (i.e. the transaction price). If the real estate is subsequently sold, the sale is considered a withdrawal of direct investment and is recorded on the basis of the sale price. This treatment applies to all types of immovable assets, whether or not the assets are used for productive purposes. Regarding real estate stocks, the acquisition price along with the acquisition date are linked with a real estate price index in order the stock valuation to reflect the price prevailing at the end of each period. Loans and deposits are valued in nominal terms. Trade credits and other receivables/ payables are collected based on their balance sheet value (this concerns data collected through the EFS survey, BSI, financial statements). 2.4.2 Recording basis In general, the Cyprus i.i.p. statistics are compiled and recorded according to the accrual principle. Particularly: Equity and securities holdings and financial derivatives are recorded when the assets are produced or when change of ownership takes place, i.e. on the trade date (in line with BPM6). Loans and deposits are reported when they are recognised in the accounts of the reporting entities. Real estate holdings are reported when change of ownership takes place. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Grossing and netting procedures are consistent with BPM6 recommendations. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The compilation of Cyprus's i.i.p. is based, primarily, on direct reporting by resident entities. The data collected is supplemented by surveys conducted by other organisations, relevant administrative sources, annual financial statements and estimates. I.i.p. statistics rely mostly on actual data (either collected on a monthly or on a quarterly basis), except in some cases where data are only available on an annual frequency (mainly data extracted from audited financial statements of resident entities). The CBC applies a correction/estimation procedure for non-response whenever relevant reports are sent late or are of poor quality. The extent to which this procedure is used is limited (in terms of value) since the CBC staff is making considerable efforts to collect the required data from "big players" within the prescribed deadlines. As regards sampling and calibration, there is a complete coverage by existing data sources for a number of sectors, namely, MFIs (S122), insurance companies (S128) and investment funds (S124). For the remaining sectors, although the target is to include all relevant companies, at least the companies that constitute the substantial majority of each sector are covered by our existing data sources. A comprehensive description of the data collection system for the i.i.p. is given below. Direct investment Data on direct investment is collected through the following sources: The CBC's quarterly "EFS" survey, which collects information on stocks, transactions and income from investments (although this is a quarterly survey, for small companies (i.e. companies with COPC less than 1m and positions less than 10m), this run on an annual frequency). The survey collects data not only for the FDI category but for the other categories of financial account, namely, portfolio investment, financial derivatives and other investments whenever those are not available from other sources. This gives the possibility to gather comprehensive data for each specific company. (For deviations please refer to 2.2.1.2. and 2.4.1.). 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Broadly speaking, the data sources for compiling the i.i.p. are timely. In cases where data are not available within a certain time lag, preliminary estimates are made. The timiliness is especially an issue for data collected from financial statements as this is done on an annual basis and with a delay (mainly has to do with SPEs data). 3.2.1 Source data assessment The questionnaire forms used in the surveys are designed by the CBC and are revised to reflect developments in international standards and accounting standards. The CBC also regularly revises the reporting instructions to reporting entities for the i.i.p. reporting. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Corrections and adjustments on the reported data are made only when required or when confirmed with the reporting entities. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data A reconciliation of the stocks, transactions and flows is performed on a quarterly basis (for reserve assets is performed on a monthly basis). The behavior of back series is assessed against current related series. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Comparisons are made using mirror data. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses I.i.p. quarterly and annual data are available on the CBC's website , usually within five working days after the dissemination to the ECB and Eurostat. The initially released data are subject to revisions. The revision policy of the CBC includes three types of revisions: The routine/regular revisions, which concern quarterly and annual data and occur twice a year as follows: In March, whereby monthly and quarterly data of the previous year (t-1) are revised, and In September, whereby monthly, quarterly and annual data of the previous three years (t-1, t-2 and t-3) as well as the current year's (t) data are revised. The first revision of the provisional data of the first quarter in year t is published when data for the second quarter of year t are published (that is, in each September of year t). Subsequently, a first revision of data of the second and the third quarter of year t along with a second revision of the first quarter of year t is published when the data of the fourth quarter of year t is published (that is, each March in year t+1). Regarding annual data, a first revision of the provisional data of year t is made in September of year t+1 then the second revision, in September of year t+2 and then the third and final revision, in September of year t+3. When revised annual data is published, revisions are made on quarterly data as well. The major revisions, which may be regular, usually every five years, and aim to incorporate results of changes in basic data sources (this is currently practically done from 2008 onwards, which was the year in which Cyprus joined the euro area). The ad-hoc revisions, which originate from methodological changes or special events and usually take place in the year in which they appear. Further information and/or clarifications on external data produced and published, can be obtained from the EE&GF Section of the Statistics Department electronically through mail at bop@centralbank.cy . 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses The CBC analyses differences between the first assessments and revised figures on an annual basis when quality indicators are calculated in the context of the Eurostat’s and ECB's Annual Quality Report. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Non-confidential statistics that have not been published are available to users upon request, free of charge. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs All relevant statistical publications containing i.i.p. data are listed on the CBC’s website.
