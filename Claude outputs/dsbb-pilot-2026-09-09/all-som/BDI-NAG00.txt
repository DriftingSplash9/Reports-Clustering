 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Burundi's GDP and national accounts are generally compiled and disseminated by the Institute of Economic Statistics and Studies of Burundi (ISTEEBU). The analytical definitions, concepts, and framework are based on the System o f National Accounts 1993 ( SCN 93) . This task is performed by the Business Statistics and National Accounts Section in the Department of Economic and Financial Studies and Statistics. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Two main classifications have been established for Burundi: activities and products. The economic activities of the units of production are classified in accordance with the third revision of the International Standard Industrial Classification of All Economic Activities (ISIC, Rev. 3). There are 21 sectors with ramifications in various subsectors. The institutional sectors used are those in SCN 93 . This classification facilitates the production of data on the major productive sectors, namely, the primary, secondary, and tertiary sectors, to which the informal sector is added. The subsectors of these sectors are as follows: Primary sector - Food crop cultivation - Agriculture for export - Forestry, logging, ancillary services, and harvesting - Livestock farming - Fishing and fish farming Secondary sector - Extraction - Agri-food industries - Manufacturing industries - Other manufacturing and rendering activities - Electricity, gas, and water production and distribution - Construction Tertiary sector 1. Tradable sector - Commerce and vehicle repairs - Transportation and ancillary business, communications - Financial, banking, and insurance business - Other tradable services 2. Nontradable sector - Central government - Autonomous administrations (including social security agencies) - Local governments The classification of products (73 positions) is consistent with the ISIC classification and the Central Product Classification (CPC). The classification of operations is as recommended in SCN 93 The institutional sectors of the Burundi economy are determined in accordance with the classification of sectors in SCN 93 . Scope of the data Geographic coverage The national accounts cover the national territory of Burundi. Operations covered The activity of nonresident units (diplomatic enclaves and international agencies) is not covered. The activity of Burundi's embassies abroad is covered. Informal production is covered. Exceptions to coverage Because of a lack of information, the expenditure of Burundi residents abroad is not included in imports. Likewise, the expenditure of persons not resident in Burundi (tourists and business people) is not included in exports. Unrecorded activity Illegal transactions are not estimated. 2.4.1 Valuation All transactions in goods and services are recorded at market price; production, at basic price; domestic expenditure, at purchase price; and imports and exports, at their f.o.b. value. Recording periods correspond to calendar years. Exports are recorded at their f.o.b. value, that is, at their value upon exit from the territory, excluding transportation and insurance abroad. Imports are recorded on a c.i.f. basis, that is, at their value upon entry into the territory, including shipment costs to the border (insurance and freight). 2.4.2 Recording basis In principle, all transactions are recorded on an accrual basis without any adjustments for the source data available on a cash basis (as in the case of certain government data, for example). Production is estimated at ex factory price, or ex field price in the case of agriculture, and therefore excludes levies on products. In the case of production for own account, valuation is based on cost price. All indirect levies (excluding those on products) are included. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The data sources used for the national accounts cover all economic statistics. These are: Statistics from sectoral ministries (agriculture, stock farming, fishing, forestry) Price collection (ISTEEBU) Operating and capital budget (budget law) Annual report on the accounts Financial reports of administrative public institutions Financial reports of mayors' offices, local governments, INSS, and MFP Financial reports of financial institutions (banks and insurance companies) Survey of commercial and industrial enterprises COGERCO (cotton management company) report Customs statistics Balance of payments statistics prepared by the BRB Survey of development indicators Survey 123 Survey on fishing production costs Survey on the typological study and costs of rural construction Survey on production costs and expenditure of private schools and clinics 1990 general population and housing census Fiscal reporting table (TOFE) Balance of payments EBC 90 3.2.1 Source data assessment 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Once the sources have been assembled, the basic data are coded on the basis of various classifications. These source data are then input through the ERETES module, which is a national accounts compilation tool. This helps establish supply-use balances by product, sub-sectoral accounts, and summary tables such as the supply-use table and the table of integrated economic accounts. These work tables facilitate data consistency verification and validation. The components of the supply-use balance for each product are used to establish the consistency between supply (production and imports and levies on the products covered to take account of basic prices) and demand (intermediary consumption, end consumption, [gross] fixed capital formation, exports, and changes in stocks). Production Market production consists of: Sales of goods and services, valued in the income and expenditure accounts in the national chart of accounts; Stored production (finished products, manufactured locally during the accounting period and stored with the producer or distributor); Commerce, equal to the sum of the distribution margins by product rather than to their turnover; Insurance service, equal to the difference between the premiums acquired and the indemnities attributable to changes in the technical reserves; The banks, FISIM, equal to the difference between the interest received and the interest paid, rather in the production of services (remuneration of the various services to customers); Rents or building services, equal to the total rent for offices and housing (excluding land rent); Domestic services to households (remuneration of household personnel). nonmarket production consists of: Subsistence agriculture production (a large portion of agricultural production); nonmarket services, the production of which is equal to the production of government departments and equal to their operating cost, that is, intermediate consumption, compensation of personnel, repayments, and indirect taxes; Rents for owner-occupied housing; Construction for own account (self-construction); Gross capital formation for own account (works performed by enterprises themselves, including research); Advantages in kind provided free of charge by employers to their wage-earners. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results A technical validation workshop is held during the final phase of compilation of the accounts. Participants in this workshop are the [ISTEEBU] and the other producers of macroeconomic statistics as well as the various statistical source providers. The objectives of this seminar include ensuring the consistency of the national accounts with the other sets of statistics. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Not applicable, as national accounts statistics are not revised. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses 5.1.5 Dissemination on request
