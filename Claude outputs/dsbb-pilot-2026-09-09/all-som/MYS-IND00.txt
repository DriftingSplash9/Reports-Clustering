 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Concept: Measure of change, in real terms, of the volume of industrial commodities. Definition: Measure of change, in real terms, of the monthly production of industrial commodities compared to a base year period. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The classification of establishments is according to the Malaysia Standard Industrial Classification (MSIC), 2008 ver 1.0 which adhere to the International Standard Industrial Classification of All Economic Activities (ISIC), Rev. 4, with modification for local activities. The commodity classification is based on the Malaysia Classification of Products by Activity (MCPA) 2009 which is based on the United Nations Central Product Classification (CPC) with some adaptations for local products. The industrial production index is compiled based on the guidelines of International Recommendations for The Index of Industrial Production, United Nations 2010 Scope of the data Industrial coverage: Mining, Manufacturing and Electricity Sectors. Product coverage: Selected manufacturing and mining commodities that are important within the respective industries. They may refer to both principal and secondary outputs of establishments. Whilst, the electricity sector covers the generation of electricity by plants licensed to generate as well as to sell electricity. 2.4.2 Recording basis Data are collected on monthly basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs i. Manufacturing Primary: Production data are obtained from the Monthly Manufacturing Survey, which covers a representative sample of manufacturing companies from different industries. Secondary: Production data for Crude palm oil, Refined palm kernel oil and Palm kernel oil industries are obtained from secondary data. ii. Mining Production data for Crude oil and Natural gas are obtained from secondary data. iii. Electricity Data on generation of electricity are obtained from secondary data. Nature of indicators used: Production quantity. Nature of weights: Industry value added from National Accounts statistics. Source of weights: Manufacturing and Mining Census 2015, Annual Gross Domestic Product Period of current index weights: Calendar year 2015. Frequency of weight updates The weights of industries are being updated once in five years. The weights used for the computation of the IPI have been derived as follows: a) Sector level: The weights are in proportion to the Mining, Manufacturing and Electricity sector’s respective contribution to the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) of Malaysia at current prices 2015; and b) Group and Industry level: The weights are based on their proportion to the total census value added of the selected Groups and Industries in 2015. Within an industry, the weights of products have been determined based on their proportion to the sales value of that industry. Product selection The products are selected based on their contribution to value added at industry level. Unit selection These establishments are selected using probability sampling method at industry level. Sample sizes The survey covers 251 industries out of a total of 259 industries. Sample frame Sample frame is updated constantly to ensure the up datedness and accuracy of the sampling frame. Data collection methods Questionnaires are mailed to sampled establishments, followed by phone calls and personal visits by enumerators in each branch office for non-response cases. Data recorded in paper forms are input into computer for further processing. Respondent also may response through Monthly Manufacturing e-Survey (Online Electronic Questionnaire). 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Coverage of Manufacturing industries The IPI covers 245 industries out of a total of 259 industries. A well-structured questionnaire is adopted in the Monthly Manufacturing Survey to collect the required data. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The Monthly Manufacturing Survey is conducted on a monthly basis. Selected respondents are required to complete data submission for a reference month within 4 weeks after the end of that reference month. 3.2.1 Source data assessment A response rate of over 80% is achieved for each month. Adequate arrangements are made to follow up those non-response and refusal establishments, e.g. experienced enumerators are deployed to re-visit the refusal establishments with a view to persuading them to co-operate in the survey. A detailed instruction manual is available to ensure coherence in data collection by enumerators. Enumerators are also required to carry out on-the-spot checking for key variables during the data collection process, so that major inconsistencies and significant changes in reported data could be verified with respondents immediately. To ensure the quality of the data collected, an elaborate fieldwork quality control system is established. To further safeguard the quality of the collected data, completed questionnaires are subject to manual and computer editing processes through checking against a set of well-defined validation rules. Query cases are verified by enumerators with the relevant respondents. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Procedures for non-response: Statistical procedures have been applied for non-response cases. Computation of lowest level indices: Weighted arithmetic average of long-term relatives at the product level. The industrial production index is a weighted arithmetic average of quantity ratios or relatives. The index is built up from the individual commodity level to the industry (5-digit) level, which in turn is aggregated to the Group (3-digit) level. The overall index is the weighted average of the Group indices. Aggregation: The Laspeyres formula is used at all levels. Reference period: 2015 = 100. Introducing new units and products: The products will only be included in the following base year. Major establishments that commence operation after the base year are added to the sample of establishments from the commencement month. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Nature of weights: Sales values in aggregating products, value added in aggregating group (3-digit) and industries (5-digit) and Gross Domestic Product in Division/Sector (1-digit) level. Period of index weights currently used: Reporting period of the Census of Manufacturing and Mining industry. The preferred reporting period for the census in respect of a reference year is the 12 months ending December. Respondents whose accounting year differed from calendar year, the report should according to the accounting or financial year covering the major part of the reference year. Frequency of weight updates: Once in 5 years when an Economic Census are conducted. Seasonally adjusted indices: Data are seasonally adjusted at Sector Level. Verification of data: Data in time series, if necessary, are verified by contacting the reporting establishments. Verification of processing: The automated compilation system has built-in features to ensure that computation at all levels is performed correctly according to the specified formula. Calculations are reviewed by statisticians with adequate statistical analysis. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Intermediate results derived for each month are cross-checked against the historical time series data at detailed breakdown level. Any significant changes from the past trend are investigated. Where applicable, they are also checked against other relevant statistical indicators showing the prevailing economic conditions and performance of the Manufacturing sector in Malaysia. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Reported production information is subject to thorough checking and detailed validation checks by computer before tabulation. Such checking includes completeness of entries, consistency among data items and creditability of reported data. Dubious cases are followed up by telephone enquiry or by field verification visit. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs New weights compiled are compared with weights of the previous base year. Industry groups and products with abnormal changes in weights are further studied to ensure the accuracy of the weights. References to the publications of the UN and other statistical authorities in setting estimation and aggregation formulae are used. The computing system for compiling industrial production indices was fully tested before implementation thus securing the reliability of the compilation processes. Manual checking of aggregation of selected products and industry groups' indices is also performed. Industry groups with changes inconsistent with those of their component industries are further analysed. References are made to other relevant statistics such as those on domestic exports to help identify any irregularities during the compilation processes. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revision policy: The data published would be updated based on the latest available figure and would be reported in the following month’s publication. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Requests for additional statistics which are not available from the existing tabulations will be considered on a case-by-case basis having regard to such factors as the nature of the request, data relevance and precision, and data confidentiality. Where applicable and in consultation with users, customised tabulations will be provided at a charge under the cost recovery principle. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Details of contact for enquiries, including phone number, fax number, email address and office address, are clearly given in the survey report " Index of Industrial Production, Malaysia" and on DOSM website ( https://www.dosm.gov.my ). The timeliness of response to enquiries from the public is regularly monitored. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs The availability and price of the print version of the survey report Index of Industrial Production, Malaysia are publicised in DOSM website ( https://www.dosm.gov.my ).
