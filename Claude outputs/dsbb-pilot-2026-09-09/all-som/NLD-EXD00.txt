 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The Dutch balance of payments, international investment position, external debt, direct investment and portfolio investment statistics closely follow internationally accepted standards and guidelines (BPM6, ESA2010). DNB has close communication lines with international organizations (IMF, ECB, Eurostat, and OECD) to discuss the interpretation of methodological standards. In particular: The current, capital, and financial accounts of the balance of payments statement are defined according to the guidelines of the BPM6. The current and capital account balance is equal to the financial account balance (except for net errors and omissions). In constructing the balance of payments statement, a double-entry system is applied as a basic principle, and the net residual is embedded in the net errors and omissions item. A clear distinction is made between the income component and the goods and services components. The financial account provides for a separate recording of transactions in assets and transactions in liabilities, except for financial derivatives. The data are split up in twelve categories under five main headings: Current account: goods, services, primary income and secondary income; Capital account: capital transfers and gross acquisitions/disposals of nonproduced nonfinancial assets; Financial account (excluding official reserves): direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives and employee stock options and other investment; Reserve assets; Net errors and omissions. Just like the conceptual framework, concepts and definitions used to compile the balance of payments, international investment position, external debt, direct investment and portfolio investment for the Netherlands are in conformity with BPM6 guidelines. This also means that resident institutional units are defined as units that have a center of economic interest in the Netherlands. Resident institutional units are defined as units that have a centre of economic interest in the Netherlands, which is in accordance with standard principles. The place of incorporation - the jurisdiction of the laws that govern the creation and continued existence of the entity - is in the Netherlands in principle the same as the place of registration at the Dutch Chamber of Commerce (Kamer van Koophandel, or KVKKvK). All enterprises in the Netherlands are obliged by law to be registered at the KvK. For construction enterprises that operate as unincorporated establishments outside the Netherlands, the one-year guideline is applied flexibly together with other considerations (e.g., maintain a complete set of accounts, pay income taxes to the host country, etc.). The same applies to construction inside the Netherlands. International organizations and supranational authorities are not considered residents of the Netherlands or any other national economy and are distinguished separately (i.e., they have their own "country" code). Units of general government established abroad, e.g., Dutch embassies, military bases are considered to be resident of the Netherlands, and vice versa. In general, the one-year principle is applied to persons that for balance of payments purposes are no longer considered to be a Dutch resident, such as when they intend to work in a foreign country for more than one year. The Dutch balance of payments records the economic transactions between resident economic entities with non-residents during a particular period. Conceptually, the time of recording a transaction corresponds to a change in ownership. In practice, a transaction in goods is recorded at the clearing by customs, in which trade statistics are used as a source. Services are recorded at the moment they are invoiced. For portfolio investment and financial derivatives, preference is given to record a transaction on the trade date. The 10 percent rule for distinguishing direct investment from portfolio investment is applied strictly to promote international comparability (as required by BPM6). In other investment, the ESCB convention is followed that the issue of euro banknotes by the Dutch central bank is classified as an asset or liability vis-à-vis the other central banks within the Eurosystem, depending on whether Dutch issues are lower or higher than allocated to the Netherlands via the Eurosystem's ‘Banknotes Allocation Key'. Another (usually compensating) asset or liability is booked depending on whether euro banknotes held by Dutch residents are higher or lower than the amount issued by the Dutch central bank. Because external debt, direct investment and portfolio investment data are also compiled by DNB and are derived from the same reconciliation model as the balance of payments, they are fully consistent with the international investment position and in line with the recommendations of BPM6. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization In the compilation of the b.o.p./i.i.p. statistics the sector definition and delimitation are generally compliant with the definitions in BPM6. Information on the classification and sectorisation of legal entities is recorded in the ‘Algemeen Bedrijvenregister', a statistical business register operated by Statistics Netherlands and jointly maintained with DNB. Scope of the data In principle, all resident-nonresident transactions and positions as specified in BPM6 are covered in the balance of payments statistics and related external statistics. Exceptions to coverage Debt forgiveness of sectors other than the government, as a part of capital transfers, is not covered. Long-term construction projects are classified as construction services instead of foreign direct investment. Unrecorded activity Informal and illegal activities are recorded by Statistics Netherlands in the national accounts and these data are adopted in the balance of payments. 2.4.1 Valuation In general, all transactions are valued at market prices. Imports and exports are converted into f.o.b. prices. Monetary gold is valued at market prices. Financial instruments traded on markets are mostly valued by using prices from the ECB's Centralised Securities Database (CSDB). Non-tradable financial instruments are valued at nominal value. Reporting entities are instructed that direct investment transactions and stocks must be reported at market value. However, the guidelines to the report form offer respondents freedom to fall back on nominal value or historic costs if no information on market value is available in the financial administration. If no actual market prices are available for the valuation of transactions, substitute measures are taken. For example, the value of goods in barter trade is determined by customs. However, no corrections are made for the valuation of transactions between affiliated enterprises. Respondents must submit their reports in thousands of euros. The reporters should convert foreign currency transactions using the actual exchange rate or the reference rate on the transaction day. When net changes in foreign currency deposits/accounts are reported, the average exchange rate during the reporting period can be used. For this purpose, respondents can use the exchange rates that are published on the DNB website, which are updated monthly. Banking transactions in the financial account are derived from information on the banks' balance sheet. First, the end-of-month position data are valued in the currency in which they were originally denominated. The changes of position are calculated in the original currency. Consequently, these changes are converted into euro using the average exchange rates for the period concerned. The value of the transactions excludes valuation and other changes. 2.4.2 Recording basis The principle governing transactions and their time of recording is change of ownership. With respect to goods and services transactions, approximations are used in accordance with BPM6. Dividends are recorded on the dates declared payable and reinvested earnings on direct investment are attributed to the periods when earned. Loan disbursements are entered in the accounts at the moment of actual payment. Loan repayments are not entered when due, but when they are actually made. Interest income is recorded on an accrual basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures In principle, no netting is taking place. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs DNB is responsible for compiling the balance of payments and related external statistics, such as external debt, IIP, CPIS and CDIS. DNB works very closely with Statistics Netherlands. Statistics Netherlands collects data for goods and service and is also responsible for the non-financial sectors (S.11, S.13, S.14 and S.15). DNB is responsible for data collection of the financial sectors. Data are collected on a quarterly or annual basis. The detailed quarterly reports are imposed on sample populations that represent the majority of the total population. Annual reports are imposed to entities with a smaller contribution to the BoP and IIP. The annual reports contain less detailed information and are used to gross up results to a national level. Only listed equity in direct investment is collected on a monthly basis. Data of individual sectors are integrated as part of sector accounts. These integrated data are included in the CDIS. The data provided by Statistics Netherlands for the goods account comprise intra- and extra-EU trade. This information is adjusted for the c.i.f/f.o.b margin. After the adoption of the Intra-Community Trade Statistical System (Intrastat), the quality of trade statistics is generally considered to be of lower quality. For this reason, and also to avoid breaks in time series and for methodological reasons, Statistics Netherlands' National Accounts Department makes corrections to trade source data. These corrections are also adopted by DNB to ensure consistency between the balance of payments and the rest-of-the-world account. Statistics Netherlands is the prime source for quarterly data on international trade in services with a breakdown in conformity with the BPM6 standard components. The reporting population comprises 650 large enterprises and a sample of 5000 enterprises from the group of small and medium-sized enterprises. The latter provide reports with less geographical detail. The results are grossed up to a national level. Statistics Netherlands collects data on travel using a hybrid system: travel debits come from the continuous holiday survey among Dutch households and travel credits come from the lodging and accommodation survey among enterprises in the Netherlands. Government services and insurance and pension services are covered by already existing sources. Furthermore, Statistics Netherlands compiles quarterly data on compensation of employees, other primary income and on some items that belong to the secondary income and the capital account, partly based on estimates. For a few services series (FISIM, buying & selling spreads) responsibility is shared between Statistics Netherlands (estimation of the aggregates) and DNB (estimation of geographical detail). DNB collects fully reconciled data for the financial sectors on financial transactions and positions (domestic and cross-border), and additionally related income on a quarterly basis by means of its direct reporting system. This also applies to Statistics Netherlands for S.11. For portfolio investment (and listed equity in Direct Investment) DNB collects data on a monthly basis for all domestic sectors. The DNB survey system provides a full coverage of financial account transactions, investment income and the IIP. An exception to this are the transactions F2 (deposits) and F4 (loans) for deposit taking corporations ( S.122) and money market funds (S.123). The source for these transactions is the BSI (Balance Sheet Items) survey, which does not provide a fully reconciled overview of stocks and transactions. For the purposes of BoP and other macro-economic statistics, flows are in this case imputed from the changes in outstanding amounts taking into account exchange rate or other changes. Most reporting requirements include a full reconciliation between flows and stocks including a reconciliation of accrued interest. The "reconciliation model" implies that all changes between the opening and closing positions of the reporting period need to be explained. The changes are generally broken down into gross transactions, changes in market prices, exchange rate changes and other changes in volume (e.g., write-offs). Portfolio investments are almost completely reported on a security-by-security basis whereby the ISIN code system is used as security identifier. Only a very limited number of reporting agents prefer to return aggregated portfolio reports. Direct investment and other financial transactions of MFIs have been integrated in the reports for money and banking statistics. To that end, the reporting requirements for these statistics have been extended to allow for the derivation of flows. Moreover, investment income related to these financial instruments has been included in the reports. Data on reserve assets are provided by the accounting and securities departments of DNB, which also use the direct reporting system to transmit this information. The coverage of financial and non-financial entities is regularly checked against the statistical business register. Given that this register contains the full business register as maintained by the Dutch Chamber of Commerce ("Kamer van Koophandel" or KvK), and that all Dutch companies are legally required to register at the KvK, this provides a robust check for the adequateness of sectoral coverage. Registers of supervised entities such as banks, insurers, pension funds and investment funds provide additional data sources to ensure coverage. Maintaining an up-to-date reporting coverage is relatively challenging for the captives sector (S.127), given its fast-changing population. As additional measures to ensure coverage, DNB requires all new SPEs to register at DNB, and public data sources are regularly monitored to ‘scout' potential major international transactions. With respect to the services data compiled by Statistics Netherlands, all services with the exception of travel, government, insurance and pension and financial services are based on a direct reporting method (for the large enterprises) and a survey for the small and medium sized enterprises. The large enterprises are required to provide full information on all required detail and also more geographical detail than the smaller enterprises. Sampling methods for smaller enterprises are based on settlements data from the previous reporting system and readily available international payments data from banks. Travel, government goods and services n.i.e., and insurance and pension services are covered by already existing sources. Data on financial services are obtained through an estimation method based on direct reporting from the MFIs to DNB, which shares these data with Statistics Netherlands. DNB receives from the Dutch Land Registry monthly administrative data regarding the purchases and sales of Dutch real estate by nonresidents with full geographical detail. Statistics Netherlands uses data from several administrative sources (customs, VAT register, and foreign visitors) as input for various estimates regarding current account items. Data from money transactions offices in combination with migration statistics and statistics on economic growth (GDP per capita) are used as a source for the estimation of personal transfers by migrants. For the calculation of savings deposits held abroad by the Dutch resident household sector, an estimation method based on the exchange of information within the framework of the European tax savings directive is used. The financial press is monitored closely for information on international transactions that need to be recorded in the balance of payments. This holds in particular for direct investment transactions. Within the EU information on large FDI transactions and positions is exchanged between member states via the so-called FDI Network. DNB uses information that is received from this network to check reported information and to possibly detect missing transactions. The Statistics Department holds periodic meetings with business organizations to identify new developments or wishes that need to be taken into account in the compilation system. DNB has introduced a new harmonized quarterly survey for financial institutions starting 2022 Q1. This reporting framework will be covering both domestic and cross-border transactions and positions and will be servicing the national accounts, balance of payments/IIP as well as sectoral statistics. Reporting institutions will have to provide a complete and balanced balance sheet data and a profit and loss account, with a breakdown by the country and sector of the counterparty if relevant. For transactions and positions in securities, with exception of shares held as direct investment, a new monthly survey has been introduced starting July 2020. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Goods: External trade statistics are compiled by Statistics Netherlands on the basis of customs declarations and Intrastat for extra- and intra-EU trade, respectively. These data are available within six weeks. Both intra-EU and extra-EU trade statistics are compiled according to the country-of-consignment principle. The c.i.f/f.o.b. margin is calculated by Statistics Netherlands through a particular f.o.b. delivery terms method. To the extent customs declarations include f.o.b. delivery terms, these are applied as a proxy to calculate the margins for country groups. The same method is used to calculate the margins for intra-EU trade, based on the customs declarations from non-EU European countries which are adjusted for the average distances from the Netherlands. The reconciliation of international merchandise trade data with balance of payments data on goods requires extensive coordination with the compilers of trade statistics and of the rest-of-the-world account. The necessary adjustments to the data are documented. Data included in the balance of payments are consistent with data in national accounts. Services: Data on international trade in services are collected by Statistics Netherlands on a quarterly basis. Large entities report full detail and geography. In addition, a larger number of small and medium sized enterprises reports with less detail. The results are grossed up to a national level. Data included in the balance of payments are consistent with data in national accounts. Primary income: Investment income is collected through DNB's direct reporting system, as an integrated part of the reconciliation model. Dividends are recorded as of the date they are declared. All interest income is recorded on an accrual basis. Earnings on foreign direct investment (FDI) equity participations are collected quarterly, starting 2022 Q1 (before annually). Income on direct investment debt instruments, like on all other interest-bearing debt, is reported on an accrual basis. Income on real estate liabilities is estimated by applying a gross yield on the reported stocks. This yield is published by ROZ (Raad voor Onroerende Zaken), an institute which collects information on transactions and rate of return on real estate in the Netherlands. In the collection system, no difference in treatment is made between normal shares and shares in collective investment institutions. Distributed income from shares in collective investment institutions is treated as dividend income and is recorded when paid. Retained earnings of collective investment institutions is estimated on a quarterly basis. Finally, quarterly data on compensation of employees are estimated by Statistics Netherlands from several labor statistics and other primary income is estimated by Statistics Netherlands within the framework of national accounts. Secondary income: Data on secondary income from the government are collected by DNB through monthly reports from a limited number of government agencies. Data from money transactions offices in combination with migration statistics and statistics on economic growth (GDP per capita) are used as a source for the estimation of personal transfers by migrants. Quarterly data on other secondary income, in particular social security and other current transfers, are supplied by Statistics Netherlands, using various sources. Data included in the balance of payments are consistent with data in national accounts. Capital account: Data on capital transfers from the government, including non-produced, nonfinancial assets of the government, are collected through monthly direct reports from a limited number of government agencies, supplemented by a quarterly report on capital transfers provided by the European Commission. Data on migrants' transfers, related to portfolio investment, are collected through monthly direct reports of the custodian banks. Quarterly estimates are made by Statistics Netherlands for inheritances (based on inheritance tax) and for change of residency of bank accounts (based on number of emigrants and immigrants). Private sector nonproduced, nonfinancial assets are collected by Statistics Netherlands as part of their quarterly survey on international trade in services. Data included in the balance of payments are consistent with data in national accounts. Direct investment: The data on direct investment are collected through the general direct reporting system, i.e. the reconciliation model. Information is collected on equity investments, equity-related investments and various types of inter group assets and liabilities. Data on stocks of loans and other forms of investment capital are mainly collected on a quarterly basis. Geographical breakdowns are based on the residency of the direct cross-border debtor or creditor (first-shot principle). Financial derivatives contracted between affiliates, excluding the MFI sector, are not identified separately and are included in financial derivatives (in line with BPM6). Direct equity investment and other investments of MFIs are integrated in monthly money and banking statistics. Most reporters report on the basis book value or net asset value. Data on real estate investments within the Netherlands are based on information from the Dutch Land Registry. Portfolio investment: The data on portfolio investment assets are collected through the general direct reporting system, except for holdings of households which are collected from custodians. For data on portfolio-investment liabilities the so called residual approach is used: foreign holdings of resident securities equal total resident issues minus domestic holdings. Data collected from the reporting agents relate to economic ownership. Reporting by ISIN code is not compulsory, although a large part of the data is reported in this way. When investments are reported without the ISIN code, reporting agents have to submit a full reconciliation of stocks, flows, and revaluation, including interest accrued for debt, on a monthly basis. Reporters have to submit all data classified by country and sector of the issuer and by type of instrument (equity, long-term and short-term debt instruments). When reporters use security-by-security reporting, stocks are reported as number of shares for equity or nominal value for debt. The market value of stocks, price and exchange rate changes, and interest accrued is calculated by the compilation system. The system also classifies the data by country and sector of the issuer. The transformation of the data reported by ISIN code is based on data from a securities database. The database, which is updated using information from the Centralised Securities Database of the ECB, contains information on ISIN code and other security identification codes, type of security, that is, by ESA2010 instrument classification, outstanding capital (number and value of shares/debt), issue date and price, monthly price information including its source, interest (fixed/floating, current coupon rate), and the issuer (name, sector, country of residence). Financial derivatives and employee stock options: The data on financial derivatives are collected through the general direct reporting system, i.e., the reconciliation model. Information regarding contracts executed by nonresidents on the domestic derivative exchange is also collected from resident banks and clearing members. Reporting agents must submit a full reconciliation of stocks, flows, and revaluation on a monthly basis. Revaluations do not need to be broken down further into price and exchange rate changes. The data are reported by instrument: options (including warrants), futures (variation margins), forward rate agreements, interest rate swaps (only swaps where no principals are exchanged), cross-currency interest rate swaps, outright forwards and foreign exchange swaps (forward legs only), and all other over the counter (OTC) contracts. For exchange-traded derivatives, the geographical allocation is made according to the country where the exchange is based or, in the case of a contract traded on a Dutch exchange, the country of residency of the client. OTC contracts are allocated to the country of the actual counterpart. No information is available for transactions for assets and liabilities, only net figures on transactions are available. Cross-border employee stock options are (as far as known) uncommon in the Netherlands. Data are not collected. Other investment: The data on other investments of non-banks are collected through the general direct reporting system, i.e., the reconciliation model. Information on the sector of the foreign counterparts is requested on the report forms for both assets and liabilities. Other investments of MFIs are integrated in monthly money and banking statistics. Flow data on other investment are derived from monthly stock data on the balance sheet information. No data on short-term trade credits are collected in the survey system. For the calculation of positions held abroad by the Dutch resident household sector, an estimation method based on the exchange of information within the framework of the European tax savings directive is used. Reserve assets: The data on securities and financial derivatives are collected through the general direct reporting system. Data on monetary gold, receivables from the IMF, and currency and deposits are derived from information from DNB's monthly balance sheet, broken down by currency, country, and sector. Valuation of gold at market prices is done on a monthly basis. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The timeliness is mainly determined by international requirements of the ECB, Eurostat and IMF. A calendar is published annually by DNB, which contains all transmission and production deadlines to meet these requirements. With Statistics Netherlands a document with mutual agreements and deadlines for the exchange of data is updated yearly. The deadline for reporting agents has been set in such a way that DNB has sufficient time for analysis and compilation, but gives reporting agents also sufficient time for their reporting obligations. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The reporting tool for reporting agents has various validation checks with the aim to receive as clean and plausible input as possible. Most source data are only assessed on a macro level, using self-developed tools to identify for example outliers. In case of outliers, the underlying micro details are examined. Major events are confirmed with respondents, and records maintained on the confirmation. When possible, balance of payments data are verified with external administrative sources. Balance of payments and related statistics (IIP, external debt, CPIS and CDIS) are fully aligned with the rest-of-the-world account. These statistics are completely consistent with each other. This means that all data are integrated in one framework. The magnitude and direction of corrections made on ‘source data' in the integration phase are monitored. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Data on both stocks, transactions, exchange rate changes, price changes and other changes in volume are collected simultaneously through a system of direct reporting. This allows for the checking of the reconciliation. The reporting tool for reporting agents has various validation checks with the aim to receive as clean input as possible. Data are grossed up, if necessary. In case of non-reporting data are imputed. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Intermediate results are validated on a macro level, using tools for example to easily identify outliers. In case of outliers, the underlying micro details are examined. Major events are confirmed with respondents, and records maintained on the confirmation. Balance of payments and related statistics (IIP, external debt, CPIS and CDIS) are fully aligned with the rest-of-the-world account. These statistics are completely consistent with each other. This means that all data are integrated in one framework. The magnitude and direction of corrections made on ‘source data' in the integration phase are monitored. Data on stocks, transactions, exchange rate changes and flows, as well as ex revaluations and other changes are collected simultaneously. This allows for checking the full reconciliation between stocks, transactions and other changes. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data As a consequence of the allocation of tasks between both institutions, Statistics Netherlands is primarily responsible for the quality of a considerable part of the current account data. By means of several consultative structures, the quality of the intermediate data is assessed. In principle, the supplier is responsible for the assessment of the source data. Statistics Netherlands, for example, regularly assesses data on freight earnings in relation to the value and volume of the trade flows. DNB checks data collected for statistical purposes with data collected for supervisory purposes. Reported financial flows are reconciled with changes in the stock data collected for external debt and the international investment position. This is an essential feature of the Dutch direct reporting system. Data on investment income are assessed in relation to corresponding stock data in the final stage of the monthly compilation cycle, and also after the revision of quarterly data, when staff performs an economic analysis, on which the quarterly reports to the Board of Directors and statistical articles on the DNB website are based. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Balance of payments and related statistics (IIP, external debt, CPIS and CDIS) are fully aligned with the rest-of-the-world account. These statistics are completely consistent with each other. This means that all data are integrated in one framework. The magnitude and direction of corrections made on ‘source data' in the integration phase are monitored. On a quarterly basis an overview is made of net errors and omissions over a longer period, in total and by sector, in order to monitor developments. Findings may induce broader studies to identify the causes of these developments. Bilateral discrepancies within the EU are dealt with in an ECB/Eurostat taskforce. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions cannot be avoided. On one hand, some reporters exceed deadlines or have to resubmit their report, on the other hand the methodology compels compilers to make initial estimates. Monitoring of revisions is considered to be a normal part of the current compilation process. Studies of the magnitude and direction of revisions over a given period of time are part of the set of quality indicators of the ECB's and Eurostat's biennial quality reports. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses The ECB and Eurostat are informed about major revisions through regular procedures. In addition, the general public is informed about major revisions through articles on the DNB website, which assess and explain the revisions (for example, with the introduction of BPM6 in November 2014). A detailed analysis of differences between revised and preliminary data is only performed for internal analysis. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Detailed data can be disseminated on request. Before dissemination it is checked whether the confidentiality of the data is secured. These requests can be made known by sending an e-mail tot the e-mail address that is stated on the DNB website. Questions about the statistics can be asked in the same way. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The DNB website provides contact information ( https://www.dnb.nl/en/contact-faqs/ ). Questions are usually answered within a few days. Assistance to users is monitored and reviewed regularly. When questions appear to be asked more often, they will be added to the FAQ section on the website ( https://www.dnb.nl/en/statistics/frequently-asked-questions/ ). 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs All statistical news releases and articles are published on the DNB website ( Statistical news (dnb.nl) ). All statistical products and services are provided free of charge.
