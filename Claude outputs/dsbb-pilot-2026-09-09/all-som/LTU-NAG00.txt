 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: ESA 2010 rules. Definition: GDP by production approach. GDP at market prices is the sum of the value added of all industries at basic prices, which is derived as a difference between output and intermediate consumption, plus taxes, less subsidies on products which are not allocated to industries. GDP by expenditure approach. GDP is the sum of the final uses of goods and services by resident institutional units (final consumption expenditure of households, general government and non-profit institutions serving households; gross capital formation: gross fixed capital formation, changes in inventories, and acquisitions less disposals of valuables), plus exports, minus imports of goods and services. GDP by income approach. GDP is as the total of all incomes earned in the process of producing goods and services plus taxes on production and imports less subsidies. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification systems: Enterprises are classified by kind of economic activities according to NACE (Rev. 2). On the expenditure side, government output and expenditure are classified according to the Classification of the Functions of the Government (COFOG), while individual consumption expenditure according to the Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP 2018). Expenditure on fixed capital formation is classified by classification of assets according to the ESA 2010. Sectorization: Quarterly National accounts data by institutional sectors are produced only for the General Government, the Rest of the World and total economy sectors. Scope of the data Transactions coverage: Coverage includes all transactions determined by ESA 2010 as lying within the production boundary. Geographical coverage: Coverage includes the entire economic territory of the country coinciding with the customs territory. Exceptions to coverage Unrecorded activity Unrecorded activity: Estimates of unrecorded transactions are made to compensate for under-coverage and under-reporting and amount to 9.9 percent of GDP in 2024. Estimates of illegal activities like smuggling of tobacco, alcohol and drugs, and sexual services are covered. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: Output and gross value added are estimated at basic prices; intermediate consumption and gross fixed capital formation at purchasers' prices; changes in inventories at basic prices (goods for resale entering the inventories /withdrawn from inventories are valued at the purchasers' prices) and household final consumption expenditure at purchasers' prices. Current prices estimates are produced for GDP by the expenditure, income, and production approaches. Chain-linked volumes are produced for expenditure components and for GDP and its components from the production approach. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: Transactions are recorded on accrual basis (including, also government revenue and expenditure). Recording period: Calendar quarters. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Main National Accounts aggregates are recorded gross (Gross Domestic Product, Gross Value Added, Gross National Income, Gross Fixed Capital Formation), i.e., before deduction of provisions for the consumption of fixed capital. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Production approach: The GVA by production approach at current prices are derived using multiple regression models along with time series methods. For estimation of market producers' GVA by industries, additional different explanatory indicators of the respective activity – regressors are used: GVA with a time lag, VAT declarations data, production, or turnover from Short-term business statistics (STS), number of employees in full time units, average gross earnings etc. To ensure the quality of the estimates the following criteria and analysis are employed: models' accuracy criteria (determination coefficient, graphical analysis, significance of parameters, analysis of errors) are checked, expert analysis. For certain activities and for non-market producers' specific sources are used: Agriculture: for agricultural companies, enterprises, as well as for farmers' and family farms, the quarterly information on total agricultural production, intermediate consumption and inventories is estimated based on agricultural statistical surveys. Mining and quarrying; manufacturing; electricity, gas, steam, and air conditioning supply; water supply; sewerage, waste management and remediation activities: the source of information on non-market part of waste collection, treatment, and disposal activities; materials recovery activity – financial statements of enterprises. Information and communication: the information on non-market output - state budget data. Financial and insurance activities: the main sources are the financial statements of central bank, commercial banks, insurance companies, pension funds, investment management and financial brokerage companies provided by the LB. For other financial intermediaries these data are obtained from the comprehensive quarterly surveys carried out by the SL. Professional, scientific, and technical activities; administrative and support activities: the source of information on non-market part of scientific research and development activity – reports on the revenues and expenditures of the state and local budgets provided by the Ministry of Finance. Public administration and defense; compulsory social security; education; human health and social work activities: the national accounts estimates are derived from the quarterly reports on the state and local budgets and from the reports of the social security funds and other extra-budgetary funds. Quarterly statistical surveys and financial statements are used for public enterprises. Quarterly financial statements based on public sector accounting and financial reporting standards (PSAFRS) from VSAKIS database of the Ministry of Finance are used for public health care and public higher education institutions. Arts, entertainment and recreation, repair of household goods and other services: for the public sector the source of information – reports on the revenues and expenditures of the state and municipal budgets. The main source for the sector of non-profit institutions serving households S.15 is estimated data on the economic and financial activity of membership organisations (political parties, trade unions and associations), which are estimated using administrative data (financial reports provided to Center of Registers, State Social Insurance Fund Board data, State Tax Inspectorate data). Quarterly estimates are based on mathematic-statistical approaches, when annual data are split into quarters using the DEMETRA and ECOTRIM software. Expenditure approach: Household final consumption expenditure: the estimates are based on the combination of data from various statistical sources, such as surveys on retail trade and services, the quarterly level of information from the latest Household Budget survey (HBS). In addition, some administrative data and other statistical sources are used. The quarterly estimates are derived based on 13 consumption categories. Final consumption expenditure of NPISHs: the administrative data sources are used to estimate income and expenditure of NPISHs. Quarterly estimates are produced by splitting annual t–1, t–2 data using ECOTRIM software and forecasted for the current year quarters using JDEMETRA+ software. Final consumption expenditure of general government: these data are derived from the quarterly reports of the Ministry of Finance on the expenditure of central and municipal budgets. Additionally, quarterly reports on the State Social Insurance Fund, the Compulsory Health Insurance Fund, Guarantee Fund and Long-term Work Benefits Fund, the extra-budgetary funds, and few other units (included in general government) are used. Quarterly financial reports are used for public health care and public higher education institutions. The commodity flow approach is the primary method used to calculate gross fixed capital formation. The gross fixed capital formation supply model mainly captures investment in construction as well as machinery and equipment and is used for quarterly evaluation. Changes in inventories: quarterly data of the changes in inventories are estimated as balancing item, which is calculated as difference between GDP estimated by production approach and expenditure approach components. Acquisitions less disposals of valuables: the commodity flow method is used to estimate valuables. Exports and imports of goods and services: data are based on the balance of payments (BoP), which is compiled by the Bank of Lithuania (LB) in accordance with methodological requirements of the BPM6. Income approach: Compensation of employees: estimates are derived from annual Structural business statistics (SBS) survey data and quarterly data are derived from the quarterly survey of earnings. Operating surplus/mixed income – the balancing item. Taxes less subsidies on production: the information is obtained from the Ministry of Finance. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Definitions, scope, and valuation of source data are based on the international bookkeeping standards and European Regulations. International and European classifications are used as well. Time of recording is a calendar quarter. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The timeliness is stated according to meet timeliness and periodicity for dissemination of national accounts statistics. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Quarterly data are always checked and reconciled with annual data. Verification of the reliability of quarterly data is done using different data sources and administrative records. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Base year and update cycle: Chain-linking was introduced in 2006 in the quarterly and annual national accounts to measure the development of economic aggregates in volume terms. The aggregates are linked in the chain with currently introduced reference year of 2021 based on data calculated in previous year's prices. The changes between consecutive periods are linked together ("cumulated") to form chained volume measures using the previous year weights that show changes in a time series perspective. Annual overlap techniques are used for linking the quarterly elements of the chain. Chain-linking of volume measures in annual and quarterly national accounts is stipulated in the European Parliament and the Council Regulation (EU) No 549/2013 on the European System of National and Regional accounts – ESA 2010. Production approach: Volume estimates for market producers' GVA by industries are derived using multiple regression models along with time series methods. Additional different explanatory indicators of the respective activity – regressors are used: GVA with a time lag, VAT declarations data, production or turnover from STS, number of employees in full time units, average gross earnings etc. Estimated growth rates of GVA are cross-checked with the volume indices of corresponding activities published by the STS. Implicit deflators are cross-checked with available price indices. To ensure the quality of the estimates the following criteria and analysis are employed: models' accuracy criteria (determination coefficient, graphical analysis, significance of parameters, analysis of errors) are checked, expert analysis. For certain activities and for non-market producers' volume indices of output are used: Agriculture: quarterly volume index of agricultural production is calculated comparing production of different agricultural products in reporting quarter against the production in corresponding quarter of previous year, evaluated at producer prices of corresponding quarter of previous year. Mining and quarrying; manufacturing; electricity, gas, steam, and air conditioning supply; water supply; sewerage, waste management and remediation activities: non-market GVA is extrapolated using output volume index, which is formed using wages and salaries and employment indicators. Information and communication: non-market GVA is extrapolated using output volume index, which is formed using wages and salaries and employment indicators. Financial and insurance: GVA is extrapolated using volume indices of output, which are formed using wages and salaries and employment indicators. Professional, scientific and technical activities; administrative, and support activities: non-market GVA is extrapolated using output volume index, which is formed using wages and salaries and employment indicators. Public administration and defense; compulsory social security; education; human health and social work activities: non-market GVA is extrapolated using output volume index, which is formed using wages and salaries and employment indicators. Arts, entertainment and recreation, repair of household goods and other services: non-market GVA is extrapolated using volume indices of output, which are formed using wages and salaries and employment indicators. Expenditure approach: Household final consumption expenditure: the corresponding HCPIs (Harmonized indices of consumer prices) are used for deflation of data at current prices on expenditure by purposes. Final consumption expenditure of NPISHs: implicit deflator of output of industry 94 (GDP estimation by production approach) is used for deflation. Final consumption expenditure of general government: implicit deflators of non-market output of corresponding industries are used for deflation of data at current prices on individual and collective consumption expenditure of general government. Gross fixed capital formation: the estimates at previous year prices are mainly derived through deflation of values at current prices. The appropriate Construction input price indices (CIPIs), PPIs, Import price indices (IPIs), Service producer price indices (SPPIs) and CPIs are used for the components of gross fixed capital formation except of the cultivated biological resources. Volume indices are used for the estimation of the cultivated biological resources at previous year prices. Changes in inventories: quarterly data of the changes in inventories at previous year prices are estimated as balancing item, which is calculated as difference between GDP estimated by production approach at previous year prices and expenditure approach components. Acquisitions less disposals of valuables: PPI for 32.12 (NACE Rev. 2), IPI for 32 (NACE Rev. 2) are used for the estimations at previous year prices. Exports and imports of goods and services: total EPI and total IPI are used for the estimation of exports and imports of goods at previous year prices for the t–1, t–2 years. Annual exports and imports of goods at previous year prices for the year t–3 are estimated by products (CPA at two digit-level) using appropriate EPI's and IPI's for deflation of values at current prices. Quarterly estimates are benchmarked to annual data. Weighted average of price indices for exported services and weighted average of price indices for imported services are used to deflate the values at current prices on exports and imports of services. Appropriate implicit deflators of output by production approach adjusted by changes of exchange rate are used as price indices. Data on exported and imported services by kind of services are used for the weights. Income approach: no measures in real terms. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Seasonal adjustment: The seasonally adjusted quarterly GDP data since 1995 are disseminated. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Reconciliation: GDP calculations by production and expenditure approaches are the main approaches in LNA. The value of GDP by expenditure approach is balanced with the result of GDP estimated by production approach. During the balancing process, components from GDP production and expenditure approaches are adjusted. The income approach is not an independent approach since operating surplus and mixed income are derived as the residual between GDP estimated and balanced via the production and expenditure approaches and other components of the income approach. The quarterly national accounts estimates are adjusted to add up to the annual figures using the pro-rata method. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Summary Tables (in Lithuanian and English) can be sent by e-mail, free of charge, to all interested parties at the time the news release is issued, on request to the contact person. The non-confidential disaggregated quarterly data are also available upon request at the time the quarterly data is published. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points The information on contact points is presented in the news releases, Database of Indicators and Predefined tables. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs Terms and conditions for subscription, as well as a catalogue of statistical publications, are published on the SL's Internet website https://vda.lrv.lt/en/ .
