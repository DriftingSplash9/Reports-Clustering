 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions International trade statistics are compiled by the Institute of Economic Statistics and Studies of Burundi (ISTEEBU) and then forwarded to the Research Department of the Bank of the Republic of Burundi (BRB), which publishes them, in millions of Burundi francs. Imports and exports are generally presented in terms of both volume and c.i.f. value in the case of imports and in terms of f.o.b. Bujumbura in the case of exports. The classification used is that contained in the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS). The residence principle applied is that of the Balance of Payments Manual , 5th edition (BPM5). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The product classification used is that of the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System (HS), 2002 version, approved by the World Customs Organization (WCO) and adopted by the East African Community (EAC) of the Customs Union, which was put into effect on January1,2005. Data are classified by: Destination activities in the case of imports (consumption, infrastructure, and production) and product type in the case of exports (primary, manufactured, and other); and Country of origin in the case of imports and country of destination in the case of exports. Scope of the data International transactions encompass all transactions between Burundi residents and nonresidents, including electricity imports. They also include cross-border trade, which is estimated; but a pertinent survey is required to confirm the estimate. Exceptions to coverage Unrecorded activity Cross-border trade is included in total imports but remains an estimate. 2.4.1 Valuation Imports are valued at their cost, insurance, and freight (c.i.f.), and exports, on a free-on-board (f.o.b.) basis. 2.4.2 Recording basis In principle, imports and exports are recorded at the time when goods cross the border or are cleared from customs. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Foreign trade data are recorded on a gross basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The aggregated foreign trade statistics are compiled on the basis of customs data, including for coffee since July2009 (and data from the Coffee Regulation Authority ARFIC, previously), and reports on sales submitted by the Tea Office of Burundi (OTB). 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The use of the HS and the crossing of the border as identifying the time of recording are adequate for the compilation of foreign trade statistics and correspond to an intentionally [sic] recognized practice.
