 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The definition of international reserves and official reserves confirm broadly to the 6th edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6); where the "Reserve assets" are defined as those external assets that are readily available to and controlled by the monetary authorities for meeting balance of payments financing needs, for intervention in exchange markets to affect the currency exchange rate, and for other related purposes. Those, foreign assets held by SAMA that do not qualify for inclusion in the official reserves are excluded from reserves. The residency criteria followed in compiling official reserves data is in line with the BPM6 methodology guidelines. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The principles underlying the classification of financial instruments in Saudi Arabia's monetary statistics are based on legal characteristics and the liquidity of financial instruments. The classification of financial instruments is, notwithstanding the differences in terminology used, generally consistent with the MFSM recommendations. The following groups of financial instruments are identified in the data compiled by SAMA: (i) monetary gold and SDR, (ii) currency and deposits (iii) marketable securities, (iv) bills, (v) loans and advances, (vi) overdrafts, and (vii) shares. 2.2.1 Scope The principles underlying the classification of financial instruments in Saudi Arabia's monetary statistics are based on legal characteristics and the liquidity of financial instruments. The classification of financial instruments is, notwithstanding the differences in terminology used, generally consistent with the MFSM recommendations. The following groups of financial instruments are identified in the data compiled by SAMA: (i) monetary gold and SDR, (ii) currency and deposits (iii) marketable securities, (iv) bills, (v) loans and advances, (vi) overdrafts, and (vii) shares. Scope of the data The reserves cover SAMA's foreign assets transactions and their positions with nonresidents. Exceptions to coverage No exception of coverage exists. 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to Unit of Account Data are compiled in millions of SRls. Valuation SAMA accounts are compiled on the cost valuation basis for holdings of all securities other than shares and other equities. Where no observable market price is available for a security, accepted accounting standards for fair value are applied. Assets and liabilities dominated in U.S. dollar are converted using the fixed exchange rate of Riyal against US dollar. Foreign currency denominated assets and liabilities other than the US dollar converted at the market exchange rate at the end of the reference period. According to the Royal Decree No. 38, monetary gold is valued at the fixed price of 0.20751 grams for one Saudi Riyal (equivalent to about US$35 per ounce) in SAMA's accounting balance sheet and analytical accounts. However, in the official reserve template, the monetary gold is valued at market price. In addition, in the balance sheet of SAMA the quantity of gold in ounces is provided in a footnote, which allows users to calculate the value of monetary gold at market price. 2.4.2 Recording basis SAMA accounts, including data on official reserves, are recorded on cash basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures In line with the general principles of the MFSM, the accounts of the central bank are collected and compiled on a gross basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The reserves data source data falls under the Financial Affairs General Department responsibility concerning for SAMA's monthly financial accounts, that make the required data available via a fully computerized system. Monetary and Financial Statistics Department of the Data and Statistics General Department (DSGD) is responsible for compiling the analytical accounts of the central bank in line with the standard presentation recommended by international practices. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Reserve Assets are the external assets available and controlled by SAMA. It includes monetary gold, SDRs, reserve position with the IMF, foreign exchange, deposits with banks abroad, and investment in foreign securities. These data are obtained from SAMA's balance sheet. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The arrangements of the flow of the data between SAMA's related departments for compiling the official reserves provides for the timely receipt of data by the DSGD, ensures the adherence to the monthly timeliness of dissemination of the data. SAMA's accounting balance sheet is available three days after the end of the reference month 3.2.1 Source data assessment All source data used are assessed to check the consistency with international guidelines, concepts and methodologies. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques No adjustments are made to the source data. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Complementary data are obtained from the Investments Department for the purpose of SDR valuation, and detailed data with international organization. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Reserve data are validated with the IMF-related accounts available in the IMF's website and in its International Financial Statistics (IFS) publication. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Rarely, discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs are found because the monitored and audited SAMA's balance sheet is the only source. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions of released data as a whole are made within five quarters to provide data quality and authenticity. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points All SAMA publications and the website identify DSGD's contact points for enquiries by mail, telephone, facsimile, and by e-mail.
