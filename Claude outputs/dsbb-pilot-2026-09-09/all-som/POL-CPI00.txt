 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Definition: Average measure of change in the prices of goods and services bought for consumption purposes by households in Poland. Relationship with international guidelines: Data are compiled in accordance with the Practical Guide to Producing Consumer Price Indices 2009 and CPI Manual Concepts and Methods 2020. As the CPI methodology follows to a great extent the HICP methodology, data is also compiled in accordance with the HICP Methodological Manual 2024 (an updated version of the 2018 edition). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification system: Before 1999: national classification of expenditures applied for Household Budget Survey. From 1999 onwards: United Nations Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP), adapted for the needs of HICP (COICOP/HICP); 4-digit classification. From 2014: a more detailed 5-digit European Classification of Individual Consumption according to Purpose prepared by Eurostat (ECOICOP). From 2026: 5-digit European Classification of Individual Consumption according to Purpose version 2 (ECOICOP v2 = COICOP 2018) 2.2.1 Scope Classification system: Before 1999: national classification of expenditures applied for Household Budget Survey. From 1999 onwards: United Nations Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP), adapted for the needs of HICP (COICOP/HICP); 4-digit classification. From 2014: a more detailed 5-digit European Classification of Individual Consumption according to Purpose prepared by Eurostat (ECOICOP). From 2026: 5-digit European Classification of Individual Consumption according to Purpose version 2 (ECOICOP v2 = COICOP 2018) Scope of the data Population coverage: All private households residing permanently in Poland, excluding institutional households and foreign households. Geographic coverage: Entire area of the country. Prices are collected in 207 price survey regions. The region may be a small town or a part of a large city (e.g. a district). A purposive selection of regions was based on the size of population anddensity of a shop network. Item coverage: All goods and services bought within the domestic territory by the reference population for the purposes of consumption. Expenditures for cash gifts, life insurance, imputed rents, illegal activities are excluded. There are about 1,400 representative items observed (of which around 400 centrally). Collection is spread over between the 5th and 22nd day of each month (note: due to the coronavirus pandemic for some months the quotation period was modified to maintain, to an extent possible, a similar number of price quotations). Prices of goods and services are collected once a month. Fruit and vegetables are priced twice per month. More frequent data collection than twice a month is applied to some categories (e.g. some transport services).There are about 210 thousand prices collected in the field each month (note: during the coronavirus pandemic with higher than usual share of imputations). Exceptions to coverage Population coverage: excluding institutional households and foreign households. Item coverage: expenditures for cash gifts, life insurance, imputed rents, illegal activities are excluded. 2.4.1 Valuation Types of prices: Prices reflect those that consumer pays on the day of the survey, taxes e.g. value added tax and excise tax. Discounts are taken into account. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Item selection: Purposive. Selected items should represent price movement of the commodity or service group and should be available for reasonable length of time. Number of representative goods and services per group depends on its weight and variability of price movement in the group. A list of representative items is updated annually. Selection of products/varieties is also purposive. Within item specification, price collectors, in cooperation with shop assistants, select products with asignificant market share. Item and product selection is based, besides price collectors observations, also, among others, on different analyses and expertise, written information from respondents taking part in the Household Budget Survey, as well as on data obtained directly from data providers. Outlet selection: Price collection is done in various types of outlets, such as those of middle size and common type, specialized in certain assortments, departments stores, supermarkets, big trade centres, and also pharmacies, kiosks and permanent open-air market-places. Prices are not collected in temporary sales points. Sample sizes: On average, there are about 210 thousand prices collected by price collectors (note: during the coronavirus pandemic with higher than usual share of imputations) collected each month in 207 price survey regions. Price collection methods: Price collectors are equipped with electronic devices to register prices in most retail outlets. Some prices (i.e. centrally determined) are collected using price lists available online (sometimes also by phone). Internet is included as one of the sources of information on prices (including web-scraping). In addition, transactions data, obtained directly from data owners, is being used (further works are being carried out in this regard). Note: due to the coronavirus pandemic the Internet and phone calls became main methods of price collection during lockdown or increased risk periods as the price collectors were not allowed to collect prices directly in the quotation points due to health and life protection reasons. Item/variety specification: TheGUS supplies general specifications for most of the items to price collectors (e.g., women's jacket), who record detailed descriptions covering characteristics, such as brand, size, fabric/material, etc. However, fairly narrow descriptions for some items are also applied. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording 800x600 Normal 0 21 false false false PL X-NONE X-NONE MicrosoftInternetExplorer4 /* Style Definitions */ table.MsoNormalTable {mso-style-name:Standardowy; mso-tstyle-rowband-size:0; mso-tstyle-colband-size:0; mso-style-noshow:yes; mso-style-priority:99; mso-style-parent:""; mso-padding-alt:0cm 5.4pt 0cm 5.4pt; mso-para-margin:0cm; mso-para-margin-bottom:.0001pt; mso-pagination:widow-orphan; font-size:10.0pt; font-family:"Times New Roman","serif";} CPI data is published monthly, till the 15 th day of each month after the reference month and the index production process is so organized as to abide by this deadline. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Timing of price observation: Collection is spread over between the 5th and 22nd day of the month (note: some exceptions due to the coronavirus pandemic). Prices of goods and services are collected once a month. Fresh fruit and vegetables are priced twice a month. More frequent data collection than twice a month is applied to some categories (e.g. some transport services). Sources of weights: The CPI weights are derived from average annual expenditures primarily taken from the monthly Household Budget Survey based on a sample of approximately 30,000 households per year (using the method of monthly rotation with a quarterly cycle). Some adjustments for expenditures on alcoholic beverages, tobacco and catering are made using retail trade data and national accounts estimates. Time period of current weights: Previous calendar year. Frequency of weight updates: Annually. Timetable including all stages of index production process (from data collection through control to index production and publication) is prepared annually for internal use. CPI publication dates are available on the Statistics Polandwebsite . 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Sources of weights: The CPI weights are derived from average annual expenditures primarily taken from the monthly Household Budget Survey based on a sample of approximately 30,000 households per year (using the method of monthly rotation with a quarterly cycle). Some adjustments for expenditures on alcoholic beverages, tobacco and catering are made using retail trade data and national accounts estimates. Computation of lowest level indices: Individual relatives are first calculated for each price quotation in each survey region using the current month price and the previous month price. The national indices of each item and of elementary group are calculated using a geometric mean. Aggregation Laspeyres formula is applied for calculating price indices to higher levels of aggregation using weights from previous year. Alignment of expenditure and base period: Weights are updated annually. The reference period for current weights is the previous calendar year. Linking reweighted index to historical index: Historical series are constructed using a chain method. Due to the introduction of the COICOP classification in 1999, historical series are available only for the total CPI and for some narrow commodity and service groups. The information on the introduction of the more detailed 5-digit ECOICOP classification from2014 is available on theStatistics Polandwebsite. The information published is on the more detailed weights as well as on the differences between the data for the period 2010-2013 already published and the revised data according to the ECOICOP classification. The information on the introduction of 5-digit ECOICOP v2 (COICOP 2018) classification from 2026 is available on the Statistics Poland website. Reference periods: Previous period (monthly, quarterly, semi-annual, annual indices) Corresponding period of previous year (monthly, quarterly, semi-annual indices) December of previous year (monthly, quarterly indices) Cumulative corresponding period of previous year (monthly indices) In addition, for analytical purposes, data with the following reference periodsis published (calculated with the chain linking method with the use of indices with previous period=100 rounded to 1 decimal place): 1998=100 (monthly indices) e.g. 2010=100, 2015=100(annual indices) Treatment of missing prices: When a price observation is temporarily unavailable in a given month, its price is imputed. If permanently unavailable, a replacement is selected. Price collector is asked to find another item meeting the specification (note: there were more missing prices during 2020 and 2021 due to the coronavirus pandemic). Adjustments for quality differences: Quality parameters of products surveyed are carefully observed to ensure their comparability. Each change in their descriptions is considered. If it is assessed not to be any substantial quality change then the price change is recorded as affecting the index 1:1. Introducing new items: The list of representative items is updated annually based on information gathered by price collectors and the GUS/RSO central staff during the year. The RSOs are asked around the middle of the year to send to theGUS their suggestions for changes. If their justifications for introducing new products on the list (or, conversely, deleting existing ones) are convincing and there are more such cases in the country, the proposal is considered at the annual meeting of theGUS and RSO staff around October. New candidate products must represent a significant (and increasing) share in the turnover for the relevant group. Although it is recommended that the RSOs should keep the list of chosen products stable during the year, replacements are necessary during the surveyed year if a new product appears on the market with a significant share of turnover or a current product disappears from the market.Additional meetings areorganised if necessary (e.g. in 2025 several meetings were organised to discuss changes needed in the context of the introduction of the new COICOP 2018 classification). Seasonal items: According to the Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2020/1148 of 31 July 2020 also in CPI if a product is "out-of-season" the price will be imputed forward each month using the average price movement of the "in-season" products (geometric average) using counter-seasonal or all-seasonal estimation. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Owner-occupied housing (OOH): Not included in the CPI. Since the data for the 2nd quarter of 2014 (sent to Eurostat in the 3rd quarter of 2014) Statistics Poland is compiling quarterly OOH indices on a regular basis. Back data for the period 2010-1st quarter 2014 has also been compiled. OOH indices are compiled according to the Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/1470 of 17 July 2023. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Verification of prices: Collectors verify all price changes and are required to provide explanation for unusual movements. Special software is used for this purpose. Statisticians analyse unusual price movements and compare them with external information, such as market observation results, trade statistics and producer price statistics. Verifications of processing: Compilers, analysts and statisticians check all stages of index compilation. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs To counteract possible errors or mistakes, controls are implemented at all stages of the survey, from the regional price collectors to the GUS office, and any unusual price movements are investigated. Price collectors are supervised and continuously controlled by the staff of the Regional Statistical Offices. This also includes random checks of prices and products at outlets after regular price collection. A controller checks whether the product observed is found on the outlet shelf and if its quality is the same as described in the form, and also if its price was correctly registered by the price collector (note: during the coronavirus pandemic lockdowns or increased risk periods the controls were done only in-house). There is special software for the automatic control of codes, sale units and price range. If the discrepancy between the last month's price and the current month's price exceeds an indicated range, the software requires further checking to be carried out and comments are sent to the Price Statistics Centre of the RSO in Opole. Any replacements have to be commented upon and explained. Then the necessary additional information from all the RSOs is gathered by the Price Statistics Centre in Opole, responsible for the prices control on the country level. The final control takes place in the Price Statistics Centre of the Statistical Office in Opole, which also takes into consideration prices of comparable products recorded in different survey regions and suspected data are explained by RSOs by phone or by email. Also minimum and maximum prices are analysed and large differences explained. In addition, the final inspection is carried out in the GUS during the calculation of the index. At this stage the prices of comparable products recorded in other regions are taken into consideration. Uncertain data raising suspicion are explained with the participation of regional statistical offices. Apart from the expert analysis of the indices, the GUS Prices and Services Department has at its disposal IT program designed for verifying the accuracy of the calculated data. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses The CPIs are not subject to revision. The exception are the CPIs computed for the month of January of each year. Data for the month of January are published as preliminary data and later are subject to revision due to introduction of the annually updated weight system, which is based on the structure of households' expenditure (excluding own consumption) from the year preceding the one under the survey. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Provisional data for January is replaced with final data (published in March). The provisional data for January is available on the Statistics Poland website (News releases). 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Data unpublished and non-confidential is disseminated to all the users on request.
