 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The CSO compiles the merchandise trade statistics broadly according to the guidelines recommended in International Merchandise Trade Statistics (IMTS2): Concepts and Definitions 2010 , published by the United Nations. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Harmonized System 2012 is used for recording traded commodities. Published merchandise trade statistics show classifications based on different criteria. Trade is classified between transit trade and other trade; between normal trade and border trade; between trade done by government and trade done by private sector. Both export and import data are classified by type of commodity, as well as by country. Scope of the data All import and export data passing through Customs are covered. These include government and private exports and imports on trade account, gifts, aids, and parcel posts. Sales of goods, whether foreign or national, to all foreign trading ships are included in export statistics. Starting November 1988, when the border trade was opened, the data includes border trade. Export and import data are inclusive of re-exports and imports for re-exports. Exceptions to coverage Illegal trade is currently not included in trade-in-goods estimates. Also, military and diplomatic imports are not included. 2.4.1 Valuation All imports are valued at the Myanmar customs border on c.i.f. basis. Customs authorities adjust any imports declared f.o.b. to c.i.f. by using the historical adjustments of a fixed 20 percent for sea and 5 percent for air freight. Exports are valued on f.o.b. basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Re-exports and imports for re-exports are not netted from the merchandise trade data. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Trade-in-goods data are based on import and export declarations made to Myanmar Customs authorities. Gas is Myanmar’s major export. Data on gas exports are based on Ministry of Energy information on volumes of gas transferred by pipeline to China and Thailand and on the price of exported gas.
