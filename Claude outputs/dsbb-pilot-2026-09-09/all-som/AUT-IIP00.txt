 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: The framework of Austria's IIP is broadly consistent with the standard laid down in the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6) and the ESA2010 . A detailed description about Austria's BOP/IIP can be found in the BOP/IIP book published by the ECB: http://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/pdf/EUbopintlinvposstmeth.pdf?7205cc78a323d45227b89a5716c38 e52 Definition: The IIP provides information on stock positions of Austria's external financial assets and liabilities at the end of the period. The international investment position (IIP) represents a statistical statement of the claims (assets) of Austria on the rest of the world and the liabilities to the rest of the world. It is a balance sheet statement, i.e. the asset and liability items (shown separately) contain no off-balance-sheet items or forward transactions. Direct investment: In line with the definitions set forth in BPM6, direct investment covers equity, reinvested earnings and "other capital". The 10% rule is strictly applied. The cross-border acquisition and disposal of real estate also falls in the direct investment category. From 2011 onwards aspects of BPM6 and Benchmark Definition 4 of the OECD (extended directional principle) are also used to allocate the data of other capital between fellow enterprises to direct investment abroad or direct investment in the reporting economy. Before 2011 most of these loans between fellow enterprises have been recorded under other investments 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification system: Austria's IIP is compiled in accordance with the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6) and the ESA2010 . Assets and liabilities are broken down into direct investment, portfolio investment, financial derivatives, other investment and reserve assets. Sector details as requested in BPM6 are available for all functional categories; however, such details are published for all functional categories except FDI. The sectoral breakdown of foreign direct investment is shown by a separate publication on FDI. A geographical attribution of the data is available for all partner countries, but due to confidentiality reason not all countries will be published. Scope of the data Institutional coverage: The IIP fully covers the government sector, banks and (financial and non-financial) corporations to the extent that certain reporting thresholds are exceeded. Specifically for the household sector but not limited to estimates are made based on mirror data and administrative data records in order to close the potential gap for households. Sectoral coverage: Sector details as requested in BPM6 are available for all functional categories. The sectoral breakdown of foreign direct investment is shown in a separate publication. Geographic coverage: A geographical attribution of the data is available for all partner countries with the exception for portfolio investment liabilities, but due to confidentiality reason not all countries will be published. 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to unit of account: data reported in foreign currencies are converted to local currency (Euro) at the end of the period exchange rates. Valuation: Assets and liabilities are mostly valued at market prices which holds true for most of portfolio investment data and reserve assets. For direct investment in listed enterprises market values are also used, while unlisted enterprises are valued at own funds at book values. Special Purpose Entities (SPEs) are often valued on an ad-hoc basis using available data from balance sheets and transaction prices in order to achieve symmetry between assets and liabilities. For other investment nominal values are the main source of valuation. 2.4.2 Recording basis Recording basis: According to the BPM6 the accrual principle is applied. However, reported income on loans and depostits can be on a cash basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The main data sources are the foreign direct investment survey, the security-by-security reporting of resident custodians, the direct reporting of "other investment", and the balance sheet of the central bank. IIP data are collected within the general reporting system. The coverage and concepts are the same as those relating to quarterly and annual data included in the BOP financial account. Reconciliation is done on a micro level as far as possible (e.g. security-by-security for portfolio investment). Direct reporting system: Austria has switched to a "direct reporting system" (surveys) for IIP (as well as BOP) on January 1st 2006. The closed settlement system used before ended on December 31st 2005. Pure stock data are collected on portfolio investment positions. Pure stock data are collected for reserve assets and other investment as well. A securities database is used for compiling security-by-security data on both portfolio investment stocks and flows with geographical and sectoral detail. Financial derivatives are stocks directly reported. Direct investment: Equity stocks for current periods and quarterly stocks are extrapolated by using transaction data. Other capital (mainly loans between affiliates) data collection is integrated in the reporting system for "Other investment" (see below). Portfolio investment: A comprehensive and reliable compilation system is used for portfolio investment stocks (and flows). The system is based on data collection on a security-by security basis using the International Securities Identification Number (ISIN). Based on the reported ISIN code in connection with the securities database, all necessary IIP (and BoP) classifications (by financial instrument, sector, and country) are made. Depository institutions (banks) report monthly on their own and on their customers deposits (stocks and flows) without any reporting threshold. Investment funds report monthly on their own deposits (stocks and flows) without any reporting threshold, too. Information on repos and reverse repos is part of the reporting requirements. The information from banks is supplemented by compulsory reports by non-banks (corporations and households) on securities which are held outside resident depository institutions, which means securities held with non-resident custodians or in own custody. Reporting by non-banks is required quarterly, for all securities held on deposits abroad if the stock exceeds Euro 5 mio. The reported information on securities held outside resident depository institutions is enriched with data on Third Party Holdings in the Euroarea from the Securities Holdings Statistics (SHSS) as well as estimations for securities held outside the Euroarea based on data on deposits from the BIS. Financial derivatives: Specific reports on stocks are collected on a quarterly basis from enterprises which make up their balance sheet according to IAS/IFRS, if assets or liabilities exceed Euro 1 mio at the end of the reporting period. Reports from banks with specific supervisory reporting obligations are collected as well. Special reports are requested from the government sector. Other investment: Data on "other investment" are based on monthly reports by banks and non-banks. Both report on stocks and possible write-offs are broken down by country and by currency. For non-banks there are separate reports for Trade credits and for "other instruments" (loans and deposits). For each of the two reports a separate threshold of Euro 10 mio of foreign assets or foreign liabilities applies. If the threshold is exceeded, a full report is required. The reports are broken down into assets and liabilities as well as into counterparts within or outside the enterprise group (affiliates and third parties). Reserve assets: are based on internal reports of the Oesterreichische Nationalbank. It should be noted that Austria is part of the Euro area and therefore data on reserve assets should be interpreted in the context of a currency union only. Data for the Euro area as a whole are published by the European Central Bank. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Direct investment: Data on direct investment assets and liabilities are compiled in accordance with the asset-liabilities-principle indicated in the BPM6 . For other capital between fellow enterprises a change in the treatment has taken place in 2011. In accordance with BPM6 (extended directional principle) almost all capital between fellows is treated as direct investment and no longer as other investment. In addition the allocation of these loans to inward/outward foreign direct investment is based on the ultimate beneficial owner of the Austrian enterprise (i.e. a loan granted by an Austrian company to a fellow enterprise abroad will be recorded under direct investment in the reporting economy if the Austrian company is majority owned by non-residents). This change in recording produces a break in direct and other investments in the time series of 2011. Reinvested earnings and reinvested earnings from indirectly controlled entities are also included in position data. Reconciliation adjustments between flows and stocks pose a problem for direct investment in particular, because book values are primarily used for stocks and market values for flows. Data based on book value are used in line with the requirement of the ECB for a high level of comparability of the data among the Euro-Area countries. Flows and stocks are regularly reconciled on the level of data collection and for analytical uses on an aggregated level. To the extent that cumulated flows are used (real estate and remaining provisional data after six months), market valuation can be largely assumed for direct investment stocks. As soon as final results of surveys are available (T + 18 months), only book values are used for direct investment, with the exception of real estate components and listed companies. In the case of direct investment, stocks of real estate are largely compiled by using cumulated flows (approximately 3-4 percent of overall assets and liabilities of direct investment stocks). Some real stock data are available for investments of real estate funds. Valuation effects for real estate stocks are also continuously incorporated using price developments derived from the BIS property price index. As the final results of direct investment surveys only become available after 18 months, accumulated flows are used for computing provisional estimates of direct investment assets and liabilities with a lag of six to nine months. Such estimates are computed by accumulating flows (including reinvested earnings) of the reference year on the stocks of the previous reference year. For the final version of direct investment stocks (T + 18 months), the pure stocks data from the survey (also including reinvested earnings) are used. Portfolio investment: Stocks data on portfolio investment include accruals (calculated on a security-by-security basis). Interest rate components derived from discount prices and redemption at higher prices are included in the accruals calculation and therefore in the stocks. This mechanism was especially designed for zero-coupon bonds, but it also works for all other kinds of short and long-term securities. Stocks and flows are closely reconciled. Exchange rate and price adjustments are calculated security-by-security, using information from the securities database (mainly quotations and exchange rates). Other adjustments (due to reclassifications between sectors and instruments) are also identified automatically in this context. The remaining differences are calculated as residual adjustments. Portfolio investment stocks are valued at market prices. Financial derivatives: Financial derivatives stocks are pure stocks data based on direct reports by banks and non-banks. These data are derived from valuations made in context of IFRS or supervisory requirements. Derivatives securities with ISIN codes are valued at market prices using the security-by-security-system. Other investment: Other investment assets and liabilities are based on nominal values. Other investment reports by banks and non-banks are almost exclusively based on stock reports broken down by currency and country. Exchange rate conversion is made by the compiler, not by the reporting agents. Flows are derived from stocks. In addition, other investment is enriched by estimations regarding cross-border loans and deposits of domestic households as well as financial and non-financial corporations vis-a-vis non-domestic banks. Reserve assets: Data on reserve assets are prepared by the Accounting Department of the OeNB in special monthly reports. These reports comprise stocks, transactions, and all kinds of adjustments. The data are taken directly from the accounting database. Discrepancies between accounting principles and BOP/IIP concepts are seen as being insignificant. All positions are valued at market values using closing market prices for gold and closing mid-market exchange rates. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Direct investment stocks (including reinvested earnings) are available on basis of a survey with a time lag of T + 18 months. Stocks of real estate are largely compiled by using cumulated flows. Real estate funds report assets abroad with a time lag of 1 month. Additionally, since the final results of direct investment surveys only become available after 18 months, accumulated flows are used for computing provisional estimates of direct investment assets and liabilities with a lag of six to nine months. Such estimates are computed by accumulating flows of the reference year on the stocks of the previous reference year. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Reconciliation of flows and stocks is made at the level of each functional category broken down by domestic sector.
