 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The data are compiled in accordance with the methodology set out in the sixth edition of the IMF's "Balance of Payments Manual (BMP6)" . Definition: The international investment position is the balance sheet of the stock of external financial assets and liabilities of Hungarian residents to the rest of the world outstanding at end of quarter. Analytical framework: broad categories of data under 2 main headings (assets and liabilities): (a) foreign direct investment (equity and investment fund shares, debt instruments), (b) portfolio investment (equity and investment fund shares, debt securities), (c) financial derivatives and employee stock options, (d) other investment (broken down by original maturity: short term, long term), (e) reserve assets. As a supplementary information gross and net external debt, with and without debt securities, with and without national currency denominated government securities is provided. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification system: Standard classification of the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6). Direct investment: There are 2 main subcomponents within the FDI: (1) assets and (2) liabilities, both with a symmetric further breakdown: (a) equity and investment fund shares and (b) debt instruments. Portfolio investment: The classification follows the standard components of the BPM6: within the assets and liabilities (1) equity and investment fund shares, (2) debt securities. Financial derivatives and employee stock options: No further breakdown is available. Other investment: The main headings within other investment are (1) assets and (2) liabilities with a symmetric, short-term (1 year or less) and longterm (over 1 year) classification (on the basis of original maturity) as a further breakdown. Reserve assets: the subitems within reserve assets are monetary gold, SDRs, the reserve position in the IMF, currency and deposits, securities and other reserve assets. The separate subitems are published from January 1998 in USD and in EUR in separate tables on the MNB's website. 2.2.1 Scope Classification system: Standard classification of the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6). Direct investment: There are 2 main subcomponents within the FDI: (1) assets and (2) liabilities, both with a symmetric further breakdown: (a) equity and investment fund shares and (b) debt instruments. Portfolio investment: The classification follows the standard components of the BPM6: within the assets and liabilities (1) equity and investment fund shares, (2) debt securities. Other investment: The main headings within other investment are (1) assets and (2) liabilities with a symmetric, short-term (1 year or less) and longterm (over 1 year) classification (on the basis of original maturity) as a further breakdown. Reserve assets: the subitems within reserve assets are monetary gold, SDRs, the reserve position in the IMF, currency and deposits, securities and other reserve assets. The separate subitems are published from January 1998 in USD and in EUR in separate tables on the MNB's website. Scope of the data Stock of external financial assets and liabilities of Hungarian residents to the rest of the world outstanding at end of quarter. From January 2006 on a quarterly basis the MNB has also been compiling and publishing the balance of payments and international investment position statistics which include SPEs, to enable the analysis of international data by mirror statistics. However, from the perspective of analysing the external developments of Hungary, the balance of payments and international investment position excluding SPEs are more fit to purpose. Both sets of statistics are available for users on the MNB's website. In its publications and reports, the MNB analyses the data excluding SPEs. In 2020, under the leadership of the IMF, a new internationally approved SPE definition was elaborated. This SPE definition provides a wider coverage of the range and the activity pursued by special purpose entities, which means a majority or decisive control instead of an exclusive relationship with the rest of the world, and the monitored activity is not limited to passive financial activity. In 2021, the earlier SPE definition applied in Hungary has been revised with retroactive effect due to the implementation of the SPE definition approved by the IMF BOPCOM. Geographic coverage: No geographic regions are excluded from recording. Exceptions to coverage 2.4.1 Valuation Conversion to unit of account: End of period data reported in foreign currencies are converted to domestic currency (Hungarian forint) at end-quarter exchange rates. Valuation principles: Direct investment: With regard to unlisted enterprises’ equity, the valuation is based on the own funds at book value (OFBV) principle, i.e. as it is stated in the subsidiaries’ balance sheet: for outward FDI in the balance sheet of the nonresident enterprise and for inward FDI in the books of the resident enterprise. Regarding listed companies’ equity assets and liabilities valuation is at market value. Portfolio investment: Since 2008 stocks are valued at market price. Other investment: Since 2008 stocks are valued at market price, while trade credits are also reported by the economic agents. Reserve assets: Data are the end-month outstanding positions in the book of the MNB, no any other foreign asset is classified as a reserve asset than the MNB's liquid external assets meeting the criteria set in the BPM6. 2.4.2 Recording basis Direct investment: Annual survey is based on financial statements prepared according to the Hungarian accounting principles. Due to the specialty of the Hungarian accounting principles, the value of the FDI equity stock has to be increased by the amount of dividend declared. IIn the case of equity stakes in the form of Foreign direct investment in Hungary, grossing up is applied according to the corporate tax return database (TÁSA), if the foreign ownership is at or above 10%, but the enterprise has not submitted an annual questionnaire. Concerning foreign direct investment abroad a low threshold is applied. Portfolio investment: The stocks of the interest-bearing instruments are increased with the amount of interest recorded but not actually paid. Other investment: The stocks of the interest-bearing instruments are increased with the amount of interest recorded but not actually paid. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Hungary has switched to a direct reporting system on January 1st 2008. Direct investment: The primary source of information is the direct reporting of the economic entities on their outstanding positions. Portfolio investment: The primary source of information is the securities statistics which compiles monthly data on security-by security basis using the International Securities Identification Number (ISIN). As a supplementary source data on bills receivable/payable and participation not embodied in securities is collected within the questionnaires on other investments (monthly and quarterly). Financial derivatives and employee stock options: The primary source of information is the direct reporting of the economic entities on their outstanding positions. Since 2008 there are reported stocks for all sectors (except households). Other investment: The primary source of information is the direct reporting of the economic entities on their outstanding positions. Reserve assets: The primary source is the Magyar Nemzeti Bank's report on end-month outstanding positions for SDR, RPF and all those foreign exchange external assets (except securities) which are recorded in its books and are readily available for direct financing of payments imbalances, for indirectly regulating the magnitude of such imbalances through intervention in exchange markets to affect the forint exchange rate, and/or for other purposes. Data on securities is derived from the securities statistics. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The source data characteristics from various reporters are as follows: The most important data providers have to report monthly. These economic entities are designated by the MNB, their reporting is compulsory. They have to send reports on transactions and stocks of FDI, other investment, financial derivatives and employee stock options, furthermore transactions on stock of securities. The remaining data suppliers are obliged to report their data on a quarterly and annual basis, depending on pre-defined (in the MNB decree available) thresholds. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The timeliness of the source data is as follows: monthly direct reports: 10 working days after the end of the reference month; quarterly direct reports: 10 working days after the end of the reference quarter; annual direct reports on FDI: 30 June after the reference year; securities statistics: monthly, approximately 40 days after the end of the reference month 3.2.1 Source data assessment Formal computerized checks of individual reports are built into the data reception process. All the consistency requirements within one report and between different reports of a single reporting entity are clearly identified in the instructions attached to each reporting form. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Direct investment: In statistics published before the processing of data derived from annual direct investment questionnaires (which are filled in after the adoption of balance sheets and profit and loss account) estimates for corporate profitability and the distribution of income substitute the data reported with delay. In the case of equity stakes in the form of Foreign direct investment in Hungary, grossing up is applied according to the corporate tax return database (TÁSA), if the foreign ownership is at or above 10%, but the enterprise has not submitted an annual questionnaire. Concerning foreign direct investment abroad, a low threshold (26 thousand euro) is applied. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Plausibility checks are carried out on both individual reports and aggregate levels. At both these levels, time series analysis is an essential tool for this purpose. Consistency checks with other statistics are also carried out at an aggregate level. Should there be any inconsistency at this level, there is a feedback to the individual report level and the reporting entity is asked to clarify the reason for the inconsistency. Staff closely monitor the scale of errors and omissions and seek to minimize them by carrying out cross-checks between current, capital and financial accounts. The behaviour of the BOP statistical time series is routinely assessed against related series, e.g. financial flow data are validated with the corresponding stock of external debt data, and reported data on investment income are assessed in relation to the corresponding stock in the IIP statistics. Information reported in the financial press is used to verify high-value FDI and other transactions. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished and non-confidential data can be obtained upon request.
