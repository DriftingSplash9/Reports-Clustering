 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical Framework: The National Accounts of the Kyrgyz Republic are compiled in accordance with the main concepts, categories, concepts of accounts, and other methodological principles of the System of National Accounts 2008. Definition: GDP at market prices by the production method is defined as the sum of gross value added produced by resident producers plus net taxes (excluding subsidies) on products. By the expenditure method, GDP is defined as total final expenditure at purchasers' prices plus gross accumulation and net exports of goods and services. GDP by the income method is defined as the sum of compensation of employees, gross profit and gross mixed income, as well as taxes on production and imports excluding subsidies. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification systems: Since 1990, the All-Union Classifier of Branches of the National Economy (OKONKh) has been used to estimate GDP using the production method. Since 2000, the State Classifier of Types of Economic Activity (GKED) has been used. Since 2010, the State Classifier of Types of Economic Activity, 3rd revision (GKED-3), based on the international classifier NACE 2, has been used. When calculating GDP from the expenditure side, the Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (CICP), the Classification of Functions of Government (COFOG), the Classification of Goods and Services (GSCS), and the Standard International Trade Classification (SITC) are used. Scope of the data Coverage of transactions: Covers all transactions defined in the 2008 SNA. Geographical coverage: Covers the entire territory of the Kyrgyz Republic. Unrecorded activity Unrecorded activity: A number of statistical indicators are estimated for the ''unobserved'' part of the economy, including illegal activity. 2.4.1 Valuation Time of recording: Most transactions are recorded on an accrual basis, which is consistent with the general principle of the 2008 SNA, except for current expenses and revenues of government bodies, which are recorded on a cash basis. Cost assessment: Output is measured at basic prices. Intermediate and final consumption are recorded at market prices. Gross fixed capital formation is recorded at market prices. Non-market production is measured at current costs with the addition of data on changes (increases, decreases) in wage arrears. Imports and exports of goods and services are measured at FOB prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis Most transactions are recorded on an accrual basis, which is consistent with the general principle of the 2008 SNA, except for current expenses and revenues of government bodies, which are recorded on a cash basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Expenditure method: Final consumption expenditure is calculated based on retail turnover data with the addition of restaurant data, taking into account the volume of purchases on the unorganized market, expenditure on market services of the population and production for own consumption (production of agricultural products and services for living in one's own home), expenditure on individual and collective consumption of government bodies and non-profit organizations serving households. Income method: The income method of calculating GDP is developed only on an annual basis. The sources are indicated above, in Section IV. Compensation of employees includes wages and employers' deductions for social security. They are taken into account on the basis of accrued amounts. Wages cover all types of earnings in cash and in kind, regardless of the sources of financing. Taxes on production and imports include taxes on products and other taxes on production. Subsidies consist of current gratuitous and non-refundable payments that the state provides to enterprises in connection with the production, sale or import of goods and services. Gross operating surplus and gross mixed income include the portion of value added that remains with producers after expenses have been deducted. Gross mixed income generated in the "household" sector is also included. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Production method: Gross domestic product by the production method is calculated at market prices. Its volume is obtained by summing up the gross value added created in industries (at basic prices) plus net taxes (less subsidies) on products and imports. Gross value added is a production indicator, which is defined as the difference between output and intermediate consumption for the relevant type of activity (industry). The output indicator is determined on the basis of industry statistics (industry statistics; agriculture; construction; trade; transport and communications, etc.) and enterprise statistics, and its content is presented in accordance with the production boundaries adopted in the SNA. The assessment of GDP in prices of the previous year is based on volume indicators calculated in prices of the previous year.The main methods of estimating GDP and its components in comparable prices are the extrapolation method. For individual types of economic activity, the extrapolation method is used using indices of physical volume or number of employees. The deflation method mainly covers market services (CPI components). Quarterly data in average annual prices are obtained by deflation with established quarterly price indices by types of economic activity. Expense method:Final consumption expenditures are calculated using retail turnover indicators, taking into account the volume of purchases in the unorganized market, expenditures on market services for the population, as well as the production of agricultural products for their own consumption and services for living in their own homes, expenditures on individual and collective consumption by government agencies and non-profit organizations serving households. Retail turnover data and the volume of paid services to the population are adjusted for the cost of goods and services not used in final consumption by households. Residents' expenditure abroad is included in final consumption expenditure of households, non-residents' expenditure in the domestic economy is excluded (according to balance of payments data). The data on gross fixed capital accumulation are based on indicators on investments in fixed assets, design and engineering surveys for capital construction, as well as data on the number of livestock of the main herd. The volume of investments in fixed assets is also estimated based on the cost of research and development (R&D) results. The GDP estimate using the expenditure method in previous year's prices is calculated by summing up its components estimated in previous year's prices using deflation and extrapolation methods: household final consumption indicators at current prices are deflated using the consumer price index (CPI); state final consumption indicators (individual and collective) are extrapolated by indices based on the gross output of the relevant industries in the account production; indicators of imports and exports of goods and services - deflation of price indices for exports and imports of goods; Indicators of gross fixed capital accumulation are indicated by indices of prices for capital investments, indices of import prices for machinery and equipment. The change in inventories of working capital is calculated based on data from statistical reports of enterprises and livestock balances for peasant (farm) and subsidiary farms of the population. The cost of production as a result of price changes during the time they are in inventories, i.e. the impact of price changes (holding profit), is excluded from the cost of changes in inventories. Income Method: The revenue method of calculating GDP is being developed only on an annual basis. Compensation of employees includes wages and salaries and employers' social security contributions. They are recorded on an accrued basis. Wages and salaries cover all types of earnings in cash and in kind, regardless of the source of funding. Taxes on production and imports include taxes on products and other taxes on production. Subsidies consist of current gratuitous and non-refundable payments that the government makes to enterprises in connection with the production, sale or import of goods and services. Gross profit and gross mixed income include the portion of value added that remains with producers after deducting expenses related to employee salaries and net taxes on production and imports. The gross mixed income generated in the "household" sector is also included. Frequency of significant revisions: Preliminary annual GDP estimates two weeks after the end of the reporting year. These GDP estimates are refined on the basis of annual statistical reporting and are published in October-November of the month following the reporting year in the publication "Socio-Economic Situation of the Kyrgyz Republic". The full set of indicators of the system of national accounts is published in the annual collection "National Accounts of the Kyrgyz Republic", in December of the calendar year. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Reconciliation: For quarterly and annual estimates of GDP, the production method is considered the primary method. The difference between GDP by the production and expenditure methods is taken into account in the accounts as a statistical discrepancy in preliminary calculations and is smoothed out in revised calculations. Seasonal adjustment: No seasonal adjustments are made. None
