 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The MFS compilation follows the concepts and definitions of the MFSMCG, which is the internationally accepted standards for compiling MFS. However, some additional breakdowns are required to improve the presentation of assets and liabilities by counterpart sector and by type of financial instruments. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The compilation of the MFS for the CBS and ODCs follows the account classification by counterpart sector and by type of financial instruments set forth in the MFSMCG , which is the internationally accepted standards for compiling MFS. However, some accounts do not have adequate information to identify the exact counterpart sector and type of financial instruments. Therefore, additional data to capture these additional breakdowns are needed. 2.2.1 Scope The compilation of the MFS for the CBS and ODCs follows the account classification by counterpart sector and by type of financial instruments set forth in the MFSMCG , which is the internationally accepted standards for compiling MFS. However, some accounts do not have adequate information to identify the exact counterpart sector and type of financial instruments. Therefore, additional data to capture these additional breakdowns are needed. Scope of the data The RSD compiles monthly MFS for the CBS and ODCs, which contain data on financial positions vis-à-vis central and local governments, other depository corporations, other financial corporations, resident private sectors, and nonresidents. The depository corporations’ survey covers the accounts of the CBS and ODCs. Exceptions to coverage Data on currency in circulation are not available as U.S. dollar is used as the country’s de facto currency, while no Somali Shilling bank notes are officially recognized or recorded in the CBS’s financial statement. Therefore, broad money in the depository corporations’ survey includes only deposits of resident households, nonfinancial corporations, and other financial corporations with the commercial banks. Unrecorded activity U.S. dollar and other foreign currency bank notes in circulation are not captured in the depository corporations’ survey. 2.4.1 Valuation For the CBS data, market or fair value is the general basis for valuation of tradable financial instruments. Monetary gold is valued at market price, but the revaluation is made on an annual basis. Deposits and loans are recorded at nominal value. Commercial banks are required to adopt International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) to prepare and report data to the CBS. However, there is no external audit confirmation on usage of the accounting standards by some banks in Somalia. The CBS has issued a new guidance note and Sharia framework, covering Islamic practices that are not applicable under IFRS. It is expected that the commercial banks will implement them by the end of 2022. 2.4.2 Recording basis Somali commercial banks practice Islamic finance according to Sharia Law. Thus, no financial instrument ever accrues interest, though agreed markups are charged according to maturity, and these markups are included as part of the outstanding balance of the underlying financial instruments. For conventional financial transactions, accrual accounting is adopted, but data for the CBS and ODCs do not separately identify accrual interest. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The CBS’s monthly financial statement produced by the FAD is the data source for compiling the MFS for the CBS. There is a data sharing arrangement between the RSD and the FAD. Regarding commercial bank data, the current data collection system is adequate in terms of data frequency and timeliness. Source data reported by banks include monthly balance sheet data that are used to compile MFS. Banks are required to submit data within 21 days after the end of the reference month. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The CBS’s current financial statement is simple, reflecting the limited central bank operations carried out by the CBS at this stage. Assets are comprised of cash and gold holdings, deposits, loans, investments, miscellaneous assets, and fixed assets, while liabilities include mainly government and bank deposits and a few other types of domestic liabilities with small outstanding balances. IMF accounts, reflecting Somalia’s financial positions with the IMF were incorporated in the CBS’s financial statement from November 2020 onward. Some reserves, such as revenue reserves (retained earnings) and general and revaluation reserves, but no capital, are reported. The commercial banks’ assets include mainly foreign currency cash holdings, deposits, and loans, while liabilities include mainly customers’ deposits and shareholders’ equity. The FAD is responsible for daily validation checks to ensure that all entries in the CBS’s financial statement are accurately recorded, while the LSD is responsible for validation of commercial banks’ data. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Based on the data sharing arrangement between the RSD and the FAD, the CBS’s financial statement is provided to the RSD as soon as it is available. Banks are required to submit data within 21 days after the end of the reference month. So, in general, timelines of the depository corporations’ survey is not an issue. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The basic accounting identity underlying the financial statement is that total assets equal total liabilities, and total assets and total liabilities are the sum of their reported components. The CBS’s shares and other equity in the liability side include only retained earnings, general and special reserves, and valuation adjustments, but not funds contributed by owns. The CBS has only one shareholder, which is the Federal Government of Somalia. The Ministry of Finance is yet to provide the authorized capital to the CBS. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Source data are not adjusted for compiling MFS. Source data for the CBS and ODCs were mapped to the Standardized Report Form (SRF) 1SR for the central bank and the SRF 2SR for ODCs to derive the MFS for the CBS and ODCs, respectively, based on the MFSMCG. The mapping involved transforming data in the CBS’s and ODCs’ financial statements to the conventional categories of assets and liabilities by type of financial instruments and by counterpart institutional sector. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results The RSD is responsible for validation checks (e.g., vertical checks) after source data for a reference period are entered in the MFS compilation spreadsheets.
