 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical Framework The external debt of Colombia is calculated monthly in millions of dollars, and consolidates the data from all of the national public and private bodies at historical cost. Statistics are classified as short-term and long-term on an original maturity basis, and cover the following sectors: general government, monetary authorities, banks, and other sectors. Data is also broken down by debt instrument: bonds, loans, trade credits, and Special Drawing Rights (SDR). Statistics on Colombia's debt are consistent with the liability balances shown in the International Investment Position (IIP) and the flows reported in the Balance of Payment of Colombia. The data is compiled in accordance with the IMF's External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (2013) and Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual, sixth edition. Definition (Residency and Guides) According to the World Bank/International Monetary Fund definition, the gross external debt is the amount at any given time of disbursed and outstanding contractual liabilities of residents of a country to nonresidents to repay principal, with or without interest, or to pay interest, with or without principal. The external debt statistics include the financing obtained from nonresidents, mainly in the form of loans, trade credits, debt securities, financial leasing, and securitizations. Loans: Loans include financial debt liabilities created when a lender (creditor) lends funds directly to a borrower (debtor) in an agreement whereby the lender will not receive a transaction receipt, or that (s)he will receive a nonnegotiable document or instrument. Trade Credits: Trade credits include debt liabilities related to the direct concession of credit by suppliers in goods and services transactions and advance payments by purchasers for goods being manufactured. Debt Securities: Debt securities represent financing obtained in international capital markets through the placement of bonds and promissory notes. They also include securitizations, transactions in which securities are issued, the yield on and principal of which are paid based on a set of assets backing them Other liabilities include Special Drawing Rights and liabilities of insurance companies with their offshore counterparts. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification Debt is classified according to the standard components of the External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (2013) and Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual, sixth edition. Scope of the data The calculation of the external debt stock of Colombia (including financial leasing and securitizations) as well as the various transactions involved. The debt stock covers financial and non-financial public and private institutions. The data cover the whole national economic territory. 2.4.1 Valuation The external debt liabilities of Colombia are established on the basis of the following accounting criteria: Conversion to unit of account: Foreign currency transactions are converted unit of account following guidelines outlined in the BPM6. Transactions in foreign currency are converted using the midpoint exchange rate prevailing in the market on the transaction date. When the actual exchange rate is not available, the average exchange rates for the shortest applicable period are used Valuation: The gross stock of the external debt is always recorded in historical prices (nominal value). 2.4.2 Recording basis In general, transactions are recorded on an accrual basis, and the change in ownership is the principle governing the recognition of transactions and their time of recording. For most financial instruments bearing interest, the accrual of interest costs is recorded when interest accrues instead of when paid or due for payment. The exception is the recording of interest on external public debt, which is recorded when paid, owing to recording practices in source data. Comparative studies have been undertaken to estimate the difference between recording this interest on an accrual basis instead of a cash basis, with results indicating that the difference is minimal. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Current transactions are recorded on a gross basis: no transactions are recorded to date in the capital account. Withholding taxes are recorded gross, and taxes deemed payable by the recipient are recorded under current transfers. (For outward direct investment, income cannot be disaggregated into withholding tax and tax deemed payable.) In the financial account, transactions are recorded on a net basis, separately for the individual asset and liability components. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Public Sector: The first source is the monthly surveys of all public sector (Nonfinancial and Financial) borrowers who have obtained direct external financing. The second source is the Ministry of Finance and Public Credit, which provides information on government loans. The data on financial leasing is taken from the import statistics of the National Taxes and Customs Directorate (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales, DIAN) and from the debt records of the Foreign Exchange Department (Departamento de Cambios Internacionales, DCIN) of the Central Bank (Banco de la República). Private Sector: Nonfinancial Private Sector: The external debt statistics of the nonfinancial private sector are taken from the DCIN of the Central Bank. Financial Sector: The external debt of public and private financial entities is calculated using the weekly data reported to the Central Bank by commercial banks, financial corporations, and direct investment companies. The liabilities of insurance companies are taken from the balance sheets provided by these companies. The data on financial leasing is taken from the import statistics of the National Taxes and Customs Directorate (DIAN). 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data are generally consistent with the definition, scope, classification, and time of recording adopted by the BR. Administrative records that are used to compile external debt statistics provide reasonable approximations of methodological requirements and are reviewed for forms design. Information on external debt obtained from the debt compiling agency is generally in line with the recommendation of BPM6 . 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data collection and processing timetables are adequate to meet timeliness and periodicity requirements for disseminating the balance of payments statistics. The BR will publish third-quarter data 2 months after the end of the reference period. The BR rigorously follows up with late reporters to ensure the timely receipt of respondents’ data. 3.2.1 Source data assessment For public sector loans and external debt securities, the information in the database is validated using reports furnished by multilateral lending institutions such as the World Bank (IBRD), the Inter-American Development Bank (IBD). For cross-check purposes, information of the BIS is also used. External debt is reviewed with the Statistical Committee of Public Finances; for example, leasing transactions are cross-checked in the debt data of banks and the import data of NTCD. Financial sector liabilities are contrasted with their monthly balance sheets. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The disbursements of trade credits (liability) are estimated using DIAN's customs records of imports and the repayments are observed in the DCIN database. The data is classified by sector according to the borrowing entity reporting the data in the public and private nonfinancial sectors and the financial sector. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures On a weekly basis the staff of the BR evaluates DCIN's database registries. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Intermediate results are checked across a wide range of data sources. For private sector external debt, balance sheets (source Superintendencia de Sociedades) are used to validate estimates. On a weekly basis, the staff of the BR evaluates DCIN's database registries. In addition, the public sector external debt data can be checked against the statistics provided by multilateral lending institutions such as the World Bank (IBRD) and the Inter-American Development Bank (IDB). Also, comparisons are made with the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) banking data and reported data on deposits abroad of the nonbank sector (Locational Banking Statistics). Also, other validations are made with the institutions that compile public external debt. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data If is necessary the external debt information is confirm with borrower. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Data on selected external debt transactions is compared with data from the Inter-American Development Bank (IDB), the IMF, and the World Bank. In addition, comparisons are made with the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) banking data and reported data on deposits abroad of the nonbank sector (Locational Banking Statistics). The source data used by the BR to calculate this data include the records of resident individuals and companies with bank accounts abroad that are registered at the BR. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions to the debt external are periodically assessed. Analyses of revisions include the size of revisions. The BR staff also analyze the reasons for modification to improve the compilation process. Studies are conducted in terms of the reason for the revision, and adequate documentation is maintained on large revisions. However, analysis is not formally carried out on direction and magnitude of revisions, as well as long-term revision patterns. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Studies and analysis of revisions are made available internally to the BR, but no official studies have been published. Analysis of differences between the revised and preliminary data is not published. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request In addition to the statistics disseminated, other general statistics are made available to users upon request. Additional statistics and the instructions for obtaining them are available on the website in Excel or PDF. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Prompt and knowledgeable service and support are available to statistics users. Contact points for inquiries are provided on some statistical products (e.g., Borradores de Economía ), including e-mail contact of the authors, telephone, and fax. The website's customer service page includes a link to the Head of Economic Studies, its e-mail address, and telephone number; however, it does not explicitly mention external debt statistics or a direct contact person. The BR website prominently displays access points for clients to obtain statistical information. No formal system monitors user assistance, although an informal follow-up system operates through the Section's administrative staff. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs A catalog of all issues and articles of Revista del Banco de la República is available physical and virtually in the website. In addition, a website-accessible list is available for all documents BR produces. The Revista del Banco de la República is the only product that carries a minimal charge, and instructions on ordering it can be found on the Web or in the publication.
