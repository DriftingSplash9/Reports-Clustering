 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The overall structure of concepts and definitions follows internationally accepted standards. The framework for balance of payments statistics follows the Sixth edition of the Balance of Payment and International Investment Position Manual ( BPM6 ) . The results are produced according to the IMF's standard presentation (in Euros and in US Dollars). The concepts and definitions used are in broad conformity with those used in BPM6 . 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The sectorization follows BPM6 . Transactions are generally classified according to the standard components of BPM6 . There is currently no separate item for financial derivatives, but these instruments have not been reported in the ITRS by Macedonian financial institutions so far. An important improvement introduced in 2007 in the time series, but for the period since 1997, was that transactions for loans and trade credit between affiliated enterprises have been separately identified, and properly classified under direct investment; intercompany lending (except if both parties are financial intermediaries), instead of under other investment, as was the case previously. This also was a part of the recommendations of the ROSC mission that was successfully implemented. 2.2.1 Scope The sectorization follows BPM6 . Transactions are generally classified according to the standard components of BPM6 . There is currently no separate item for financial derivatives, but these instruments have not been reported in the ITRS by Macedonian financial institutions so far. An important improvement introduced in 2007 in the time series, but for the period since 1997, was that transactions for loans and trade credit between affiliated enterprises have been separately identified, and properly classified under direct investment; intercompany lending (except if both parties are financial intermediaries), instead of under other investment, as was the case previously. This also was a part of the recommendations of the ROSC mission that was successfully implemented. Scope of the data The scope is broadly consistent with internationally accepted standards, covering all transactions between residents and nonresidents. Among the items largely not covered in the data sources are shuttle trade, smuggling, and other illegal activity. An important improvement of the scope of data has been achieved by including transactions through accounts held abroad by residents, which have been included on gross basis. Data have been included in the BOP since 2003. Also an important improvement, thus adhering to one of the recommendations of the ROSC mission, was the introduction of reinvested earnings in the Macedonian balance of payments. A new data source has been introduced - an annual survey of foreign direct investment of enterprises. Unrecorded activity Among the items largely not covered in the data sources are shuttle trade, smuggling, and other illegal activity. Investors in foreign securities (PI-assets) different from the government, financial institutions and the National bank, currently are not covered with the main data source used (Security-by Security database). However these investments are monitored through other sources and currently the amounts are not significant. Ongoing activities are in place to establish high-quality and more comprehensive sources for reporting of these investments as well. 2.4.1 Valuation Market prices are used to value flows and stocks. Valuation principles follow BPM6 methodology. Market valuations are used in general. Market price equivalents are used for barter goods, aid goods, trade between affiliated enterprises, and goods subject to transfer pricing. A Customs Office division monitors market prices to assess market-equivalent prices for goods transactions reported at arm's length market prices. Exports and imports are valued f.o.b. Import data are collected on a c.i.f. basis and converted using a ratio derived from information on customs documents and revised each year. The data are currently published in U.S. dollars and Euros. The conversions at the individual transaction level have been made using the average daily exchange rate or, in a few cases, a ten-day average exchange rate. Many international transactions are conducted in Euros and/or are conducted with euro zone countries, so the euro is a more relevant currency for assessing international performance. 2.4.2 Recording basis Under BPM6 goods should be recorded at the time of change of economic ownership. For goods, the data source reflects the time of customs clearance. Adjustments are made for some large, identified transactions, such as acquisition of airplanes, to make the timing of the goods flow and the corresponding financial flows more consistent and on an accrual basis. Interest on debt, for the loans component, is on accrual basis, since the beginning of 2010, while interest on debt securities is on accrual basis since the beginning of 2008. The items of the balance of payments that are based on the ITRS use the time of payment for its basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures The grossing and netting procedures are broadly consistent with international guidelines. Data are published in gross terms in the current account and net terms in the capital and financial accounts. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Source data are obtained from comprehensive data collection programs that are based upon Macedonia's specific conditions. The main data sources for the Macedonian balance of payments are: the single administrative document (SAD) for the foreign trade of the Republic of North Macedonia; the system for reporting on the banks' international payment operations (ITRS); reporting system on external debt (ED) reporting system on external claims (EC) the banks' reports on the stock of assets and liabilities(the banks' report on the deposit money banks book keeping stock ); the monthly reports on stocks and flows (changes and stocks) on the accounts abroad of the residents which are not authorized banks and the stock and flow on settlement accounts(monthly reports-MR); additional NBRNM's reports on the official foreign exchange reserves stock of the Republic of North Macedonia; data from the annual direct investment survey -(VS 22/11), which resident legal entities submit to the NBRNM on annual basis and related to the investment from other countries. The annual survey for direct investments abroad (VS-11) is on a census basis, while the survey for direct investments in the reporting country, starting from reporting period 2017, was transformed from census to sample survey. quarterly reports for realized and planned financial result for the current year ( KVS); survey on debt/claims takeovers among residents and nonresidents ( POZ) quarterly reports on claims and liabilities on the basis of international commercial operations of residents ( KIPO form) reports for stocks and flows data on investments in equity and investment fund shares (VHV-1) and reports for stocks and flows data on investments in debt securities (VHV-2). The trade data from customs use the special trade system. There are no adjustments for omitted trade due to smuggling, illegal activity or shuttle trade. Customs data includes aid and direct investment supplied in the form of goods. These items are covered in the balance of payments and are valued at market-equivalent prices. The adoption of the ITRS as one of the main data sources is considered to be appropriate to North Macedonia's circumstances. The experience of private sector cooperation in surveys is patchy. The banks are good data reporters and have integrated their own recording systems with balance of payments statistics requirements. Having in mind the growth in private current transfers in North Macedonia's BOP, as well as the fact that most of it stems from the item "foreign exchange, net", in the national presentation, which in general recorded the net value of the exchange bureau transactions with the banks, in the absence of any information, in 2007, 2011,2016 and 2022, the NBRNM has initiated surveys on private transfers, conducted by independent agencies. The results from these surveys are expected to contribute in identifying some of the large inflows currently classified under private transfers. The ITRS instructions and forms state that when a single transaction represents a net of credits and debits (e.g., when a dealer both pays to and receives money from another business abroad), the gross amounts should be recorded. In the cases of credit card operators, the data are reported in gross terms in the ITRS. However, having in mind that for recording of transport, insurance, and telecommunications, where operators often have inward and outward transactions and have net settlements, the ITRS is not the most appropriate data source, a survey has been introduced recording gross flow data through settlement accounts, directly from the enterprises for these types of services. The NBRNM has instituted a survey for transactions through authorized account of residents held abroad, as the ITRS cannot record these transactions. Unauthorized bank accounts held abroad are also omitted from the ITRS, and there are no estimations for flows through these accounts in the BOP. There is no reporting threshold for the ITRS. Data on transactions of foreign embassies in North Macedonia and international organizations are obtained from the ITRS. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The source data used for the Macedonian balance of payments generally adopt definitions; scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording that are close to BPM6 standards. The ITRS and surveys are set up to provide data that follow BPM6 concepts on definitions, scope, and classifications. The timing bases for some source data for the Macedonian balance of payments differ from BPM6 . The timing of goods is according to customs clearance, which is only an approximation for the time of change of economic ownership. Other transactions measured through the ITRS are based on the time of payment, which is adopted as an acceptable approximation to an accrual basis. Some adjustments are made to data to bring them closer to BPM6 standards, such as deduction of freight and insurance on imports of goods and timing adjustments for large individual transactions. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Source data are timely. Customs data are available on CD within one month of the end of the reference month. ITRS records are provided punctually through electronic transfer, within eight days of the end of the reference period. The introduction of the ITRS electronic reporting was a major step towards reducing the processing time within NBRNM. Data on debt transactions and interest are based on a survey, and the results are provided in sufficient time for balance of payments purposes. These deadlines facilitate release of quarterly balance of payments data 60 days after the end of the reference quarter, with the perspective of further reduction of the processing period. Experience with direct surveys indicates that slow response of enterprises could be a potential problem to be monitored. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data—including censuses, sample surveys and administrative records—are routinely assessed, e.g. for coverage, sample error, response error, and non- sampling error; the results of the assessments are monitored and made available to guide statistical processes. Customs, ITRS, and NBRNM surveys all use enterprise identification numbers that allow related data to be cross-checked. There is extensive cross-checking of data among various data sources. Large and unusual ITRS transactions are also identified and checked. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Data compilation employs sound statistical techniques to deal with data sources. The ITRS check procedures include checks to avoid miscoding and misreporting. The opening and closing values of accounts of the banks are fully reconciled with transactions and other changes. Data sources are checked with any available related indicators, for example, customs data are checked with the relevant financing transactions for goods, and debt transactions in the ITRS are checked against the debt survey. Data are checked for temporal consistency. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The c.i.f / f.o.b. factor is updated annually based on import values shown on customs declarations where the contract is expressed in f.o.b. terms. The developments in the ratio are shown in a footnote to the relevant tables. No adjustments are made for cash transactions or smuggling. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Intermediate data are validated using other sources whenever possible. Newspapers and information agencies are monitored for information about large international transactions, and it is confirmed that the corresponding flows are covered in the balance of payments. Survey data are checked against ITRS records and fully reconciled. Customs data are checked with the corresponding goods transactions reported under the ITRS. Output data are examined for plausibility. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Statistical discrepancies in intermediate data are assessed and investigated. Flow data are reconciled with the relevant stock data (e.g., banks' transactions with opening and closing positions, debt transactions and stocks, direct investment transactions and stocks). Merchandise trade and associated financial flows are reviewed. The calculations are made as part of the estimation of trade credit, but fluctuations are investigated for possible data problems. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Statistical discrepancies and other potential indicators of problems in statistical outputs are investigated The net errors and omissions item is monitored. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses The NBRNM studies and analyzes the revisions as part of its data quality control process. Studies and analyses of revisions are used internally The NBRNM carefully monitors revisions of individual records. They are documented as to the cause, identified by the staff member making the amendment, and signed off by a supervisor. It is checked that the revisions flow through to aggregate results. The aggregates are also compared with data for the same month as reported in the previous month's publication. Changes are checked, to ensure that they can be explained. The revised data are denoted with a footnote that provides an appropriate explanation for the implemented changes. Significant revisions are noted in thee-mail to users who request the monthly tables. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses A statement of causes of a few important revisions is shown as a footnote to the tables. More detailed comments on the magnitude and causes of revisions are published within the press releases and provided to those users who have requested e-mail delivery explanation of the data. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request The NBRNM meets requests for additional information without a fee. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Contact points are provided in all publication formats. Prompt and knowledgeable assistance is provided. Requests are kept in a file, and timeliness is monitored (usually within two days for existing data). The NBRNM has provided lectures on balance of payments at a Faculty of Economics, as well as workshops organized within NBRNM for data users-analysts, journalists and reporters. Students are informed about balance of payments along with other aspects of NBRNM operations during field visits to the NBRNM. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs A complete list of publications produced by the NBRNM and information is available on the NBRNM website, in Macedonian and English. All publications are available free of charge on the Internet.
