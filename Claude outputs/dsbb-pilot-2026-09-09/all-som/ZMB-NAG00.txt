 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The Zambian National Accounts are compiled and disseminated by the National Accounts Branch of the Central Statistical Office (CSO). The National Accounts Branch is responsible for: Preparation of preliminary and revised estimates of Gross Domestic Product (GDP). Development of methodologies and procedures for estimating national accounts aggregates in conformity with internationally accepted guidelines and recommendations. Conducting the National Income Inquiry, which covers the service sectors. National Accounts data are expected by government and other users as a matter of routine on an annual basis. They are used in many ways, including their being basic in the preparation of the annual Budget speeches by the Ministry of Finance. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Establishments are classified according to the second revision of the International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC rev.2). The major activities covered are: 1) Agriculture, Forestry and Fishing; 2) Mining and Quarrying; 3) Manufacturing; 4) Electricity, Gas and Water; 5) Construction; 6) Wholesale and Retail Trade; 7) Restaurants, Bars and Hotels; 8) Financial Institutions and Insurance; 9) Transport, Storage and Communications; 10) Real Estate and Business Services; and 11) Community, Social and Personal Services. For external trade statistics, the classification systems used are the Standard International Trade Classification (SITC) and the Harmonized System (HS). Plans are underway to introduce the Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP), for consumer price statistics. Scope of the data Geographic Coverage: The national accounts cover the whole country. However, the data sources mainly cover the major cities where most economic activity takes place. In the rural areas, it is only Agricultural production that is adequately covered. Transaction Coverage: All transactions are within the scope of the data and are covered in principle. Unit Coverage: The national accounts cover, in principle, all resident institutional units and economic activities within the geographical boundaries of Zambia. However, informal activities are not well covered. In the case of the general government sector, imputations are made for local government because of the lack of data. On the production side, GDP estimates are disseminated for 11 major industry groupings, as well as for 23 subheadings, grouped into primary, secondary and tertiary sectors, as well as FISIM. On the expenditure side, the following major expenditure categories are compiled: Government Final Consumption Expenditure Private Final Consumption Expenditure (derived residually) Gross Fixed Capital Formation Change in Stocks Exports of Goods and Services Imports of Goods and Services Unrecorded activity No estimates are made for illegal activities. Imputations are made for local government. In addition, the industries where small-scale operations predominate, such as small-scale mining and manufacturing, construction, retail trade and other service industries, are not adequately covered, since they operate at the level of the informal sector. However, some indirect estimates are used, based on the 1993/4 Household Budget Survey. 2.4.1 Valuation Output is recorded at producer prices, while exports and imports are valued f.o.b. 2.4.2 Recording basis All transactions are recorded on an accrual basis, except for the accounts of the General Government sector, which are on a cash basis. The recording period is the calendar year for most activities. However, in agriculture the crop year runs from October to September of the following year. Production is recorded in the year in which harvesting takes place. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Gross Domestic Product by kind of economic activity The CSO maintains a Business Register (BR) or Register of Establishments from which samples are drawn for economic surveys for most of the industries. The BR is updated with information from CSO provincial offices. The benchmark year for the GDP estimates is 1994. For most sectors, value added is first compiled at constant prices and then inflated to current prices using an appropriate reflator. The wholesale price index (WPI) was used extensively until it was discontinued in 1998. Since then, the Consumer Price Index (CPI) is used as a proxy. Unless otherwise indicated, reflation of constant price estimates, using the most appropriate price index, is the method used to compile the current price estimates by industry. Agriculture, forestry and fishing Crops : Volumes of production of food and cash crops are compiled using data from the Crop forecasting and Post-harvest surveys. These two surveys are conducted by the Agriculture Division of the Central Statistical Office (CSO) in collaboration with Ministry of Agriculture and Co-operatives (MACO. Benchmark data are based on the 1994 Post-harvest survey. The current price value is obtained by multiplying the benchmark figure with Consumer price index for Agriculture. Livestock: Benchmark data are based on the 1994 Post-harvest survey. The data are extrapolated on the livestock population growth numbers. The livestock numbers are obtained from the Department of Veterinary in the Ministry of Agriculture and Co-operatives. Forestry: Data on forestry are very poor. Estimates for this sector derived indirectly by using the output from a selected sample of enterprise in the manufacture of forestry products. Fishing : Fishing data are based on fish landings/ catch data from the major lakes and rivers. The data source is the Department of Fisheries. Hunting: Hunting is not measured separately. It is included with agriculture in the benchmark estimate. Mining and Quarrying The Industrial Production Branch of the CSO conducts a quarterly survey of industrial production, which covers all the major mining and quarrying establishments. Benchmark data are based on the 1994 Census of Industrial Production. However, the price weights in the Index of Industrial Production compilation, the results of which are used in national accounts, are based on the 1980 Census of Industrial Production re-referenced to 2000 using turnover/sales as weights. The export price index of Copper and Cobalt is used to reflate the constant price estimates to obtain current price data. Manufacturing Benchmark estimates are based on the 1980 Census of Industrial Production re-referenced to 2000 using turnover/sales as weights. The quarterly survey of industrial production is used to extrapolate the benchmark estimates. Electricity and water Electricity: The statistical section of the Zambia Electricity Supply Corporation (ZESCO) collects data on production (megawatts) of hydroelectricity. Production of hydroelectricity is used to extrapolate value added for the sector. The benchmark data are from 1994. Water: Population growth is used to extrapolate the value added in this sector from the 1994 benchmark. Construction Construction activity is estimated on the basis of building materials. The main indictors are domestic and imported cement and stone quarrying. The figures for domestically produced cement are obtained from the sole domestic producer - Chilanga Cement PLC. The cement import data are derived from the import data compilation from Customs documents. Stone quarrying estimates are obtained from the Index of Industrial Production compilation. Wholesale and Retail Trade The benchmark estimates are based on the 1994 National Incomes Inquiry. Estimates for subsequent years are based on the weighted output of agriculture, manufacturing and imports of consumption goods. Data on trade margins are not available. Restaurants, Bars and Hotels For Restaurants and Bars, the change in the Index of Industrial Production for food is used to extrapolate the value added at constant prices whose base is 1994. For the current price series the Wholesale price index for food is used to inflate the estimates at constant prices. For the Hotels sub-sector, data on bed occupancy rates is used to extrapolate the 1994 benchmark estimates from the National Incomes Inquiry. This data on bed occupancy rates is obtained from the Ministry of Tourism. In order to get the Value added at current prices, the CPI (High Metro Group 1994=100) is multiplied with the constant price data. Transport, Storage and Communication Transport Road Transport: Benchmark estimates are based on the 1994 National Incomes Inquiry, and extrapolated with indicators of passengers carried and cargo hauled by the major transport companies. A combined index of passengers carried and cargo hauled is used to extrapolate the benchmark estimates. The CPI (Transport Metro High 1994=100) is used to get the current price estimate. Rail Transport: The benchmark estimates are based on the 1994 National Incomes Inquiry. Data on cargo hauled and passengers carried from the two major rail transport companies – Railway Systems of Zambia Limited and Tanzania Zambia Railway Authority (TAZARA) are used to extrapolate the benchmark estimates. The CPI (Transport Metro High 1994=100) is used to get the current price estimate. Air Transport: Passengers carried and cargo hauled by the major carriers is used to extrapolate the benchmark estimates, which were based on the 1994 National Incomes Inquiry. The CPI (Transport Metro High 1994=100) is used to get the current price estimate. Other Transport: Estimates for water transport, services incidental to transport, miscellaneous transport and storage are not included in the Zambian national accounts for the transport sector. Communication Postal Services: Changes in the number of domestic and international letter mail and parcels carried by Zambia Postal Services are used to extrapolate the benchmark estimate based on the 1994 National Incomes Inquiry. Telecommunications: Constant price estimates are based on the number of telephone calls, as reported by the major telecommunication companies. The consumer price index for transport is used to derive the current price estimates. Financial Institutions and Insurance The Labour and Employment Branch of the CSO collects data on the number of employees by sector. Constant price estimates for financial institutions are based on employment in the financial institutions and insurance sector as an indicator of constant price output and value added. The number of policies issued by major insurance companies is used as the indicator in the insurance sector. Real Estate and Business Services For the real estate component, data from the Household Budget Survey (HBS) of 1994 were used to derive the benchmark estimates, because of poor response to the National Incomes Inquiry. The extrapolator currently being used is the population growth rate. For Business Services, the change in the value added in trade is used to extrapolate the benchmark estimate computed using the 1994 National Incomes Inquiry. Community, Social and Personal Services Reports from the Ministry of Finance and National Planning provide data on expenditure by function by government. This information is used to extrapolate the public sector components by industry. No source data are currently available for the private sector component. Expenditure Approach A complete expenditure approach is not compiled. Government Final Consumption Expenditure The data are derived from Central Government expenditure. Local government expenditure is not covered directly. An imputation of a fixed percentage of Central Government expenditure is made for local government. The total CPI is used to reflate to current prices. Private Final Consumption Expenditure Benchmark estimates of private final consumption expenditure are based on the 1994 Household Budget Survey. For recent years it is obtained as a residual because of lack of data that could be used as extrapolators. Gross Fixed Capital Formation Estimates for Gross Fixed Capital Formation (GFCF) are based on the output of the construction industry and the imports of capital equipment. These two indicators are used to extrapolate the 1994 benchmark estimates. Changes in Stocks Inventory change is based on the 1994 Post-harvest Survey and the Census of Industrial Production for 1994. Changes in the volume of copper electrolytic bars were used to extrapolate the benchmark figures.. Foreign Merchandise Trade/ Exports and Imports The data are collected by the Zambia Revenue Authority (ZRA). The External Trade branch of CSO tabulates the data from the customs documents. No unit value indices are compiled. The HS and the SITC are used to classify and tabulate the data. The results are used by the national accounts to extrapolate the benchmark estimates of 1994. Foreign Trade in Services The source of data for this component is the Balance of Payments compilation by the Bank of Zambia. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Production Approach: The benchmark data at constant prices are extrapolated with indicators described in the previous section. Gross value added at factor cost is then computed as the sum of the individual industry value added estimates, less the Financial Intermediation Services Indirectly Measured (FISIM). After adding net taxes on production and imports to this total, constant price Gross Domestic Product (GDP) at market prices is obtained. To derive GDP at current prices, the individual industry value added estimates at constant prices are reflated with an appropriate reflator, usually the WPI or CPI component. The estimates of value added so obtained are then summed to obtain GDP at market prices, taking into account FISIM and net taxes on production and imports (at current prices). 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Major revisions to the national accounts are made when there is evidence that current sources and methods stop reflecting the actual economic activity. Ideally, an accounting framework such as commodity flow accounts, input-output tables or Social Accounting Matrices are the main strategies used for revision purposes. However, these data frameworks have not been officially compiled in Zambia for quite some time. Commodity flow accounts and input-output tables for the years 1994 to 1998 were compiled in 1999. Unfortunately, these tables have not been disseminated nor incorporated in the revised National accounts. In short, there are no hard and fast rules on revision and dissemination policy.
