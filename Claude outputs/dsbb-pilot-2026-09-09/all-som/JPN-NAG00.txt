 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: The data are compiled in accordance with the System of National Accounts 2008. Definition: GDP measures the total value of final goods and services produced by domestic institutional units during the period. Since 2016, Japan has followed the 2008 SNA as a general framework for compiling the national accounts statistics. The benchmark Input-output Table for 2020, released in June 2024, is broadly compiled according to the international standard’s concepts and definitions and is the basis for a benchmark updating of the national accounts data. The deviations from 2008 SNA are limited and described in detail in an appendix to the Compiling Standard of SNA in Japan ( https://www.esri.cao.go.jp/jp/sna/data/reference1/sakusei_top.html (in Japanese)) . 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The 2008 SNA is followed to classify institutional units, transactions, and other flows. JSIC (Japanese Standard Industrial Classification) is used to classify economic activity, which is broadly consistent with ISIC (International Standard Industrial Classification) Rev.4. COICOP is used to classify household consumption, and COFOG is used to classify functions of the general government. Data for final expenditures are published at a high level of aggregation, although they are compiled at a finer level. In the annual accounts, household consumption is published according to the 13 main COICOP groups and with a breakdown for durables, semi-durables, non-durables and services. Functions of government are published according to the 10 main COFOG groups and breakdowns of them. Fixed capital formation is published according to the main categories by type, by institutional sector (including a subdivision on private and public) and by activity. In the quarterly accounts, household consumption is shown as a total supplemented with separate information on imputed rent; and fixed capital formation is broken down into private residential and private nonresidential and public investment. Data for household consumption are compiled for 116 purpose classifications, both quarterly and annually, but not seen as reliable enough to be published. Four categories of Domestic Final Consumption Expenditure of Households are released both quarterly and annually. 2.2.1 Scope The 2008 SNA is followed to classify institutional units, transactions, and other flows. Scope of the data Transactions coverage: All resident household and legal entity institutional units are covered in the source data by using supply-side's commodity-flow method. Geographical coverage: The data cover the entire Japanese economy. The delineation of the constituent units of the economy is broadly in accordance with the 2008 SNA . Japan compiles the 2008 SNA tables and accounts that the ISWGNA determines as a minimum requirement for its implementation, as listed below: annual value added and GDP at current and constant prices by activity; annual expenditures of GDP at current and constant prices; annual value added components at current prices by activity; sequence of accounts for the total economy (up to balance sheet accounts) with an annual frequency; and annual rest of the world accounts. Japan also compiles the following tables (except for the quarterly accounts by economic activity) determined as recommended by the ISWGNA: quarterly expenditures of GDP at current and constant prices; quarterly accounts by economic activity; annual supply and use tables. Annual supply and use tables are compiled. In addition, annual input-output tables, consistent with the final annual national accounts data, are compiled, along with the detailed five-yearly benchmark input-output tables compiled as a joint project between ESRI and nine other ministries and agencies. Furthermore, institutional sector accounts are compiled on an annual basis, inclusive of financial flow accounts and balance sheets. Aggregated capital stock data are also compiled. The delineation of the constituent units of the economy is broadly in accordance with the 2008 SNA. As Japan's balance of payments (BOP) statistics are the source for the rest of the world account in the national accounts, the conversion rules, etc. used in the BOP are automatically included in the national accounts. In principle, the Japanese BOP follows the IMF Balance of Payments Manual , 6th edition ( BPM6) and should thus be consistent with the 2008 SNA rest of the world account. The production boundary is broadly in accordance with the 2008 SNA. Thus, the following items are within the scope for output measurement: own-account production of all goods for own final consumption, output for own-account fixed capital formation, and mineral exploration and evaluation. Illegal output sold to willing buyers is not included in the accounts. The asset boundary is broadly in accordance with the 2008 SNA . It includes the following fixed assets: Dwellings, Other buildings and structures, Machinery and equipment, Defense equipment, Cultivated biological resources, and Intellectual property products. The Intellectual property products are composed of Research and development, Mineral exploration and evaluation, Computer software and Entertainment originals. 2.4.1 Valuation GDP and its components from the expenditure side are valued at purchasers' prices; Gross fixed capital formation does not include consumption tax. Market output is valued at prices inclusive of consumption tax. The consumption tax (CT) possesses most of the characteristics of a value-added tax (VAT). This implies that the valuation system is essentially a gross VAT system. However, the gross treatment is chosen because the CT system in practice deviates from a standard VAT system. In a gross VAT system, the taxes that the purchasers can later deduct will be included in the prices, such as in the purchasers' prices for intermediate consumption and fixed capital formation and changes in inventories. In Japan, the enterprises registered to pay CT are only required to submit an annual declaration disclosing their total sales and purchases (following the schedule of their own accounting year), and this forms the basis for the actual payment of CT to the tax authority. The concepts of invoiced and deductible VAT play no role in the invoicing practices of the enterprises. In current transactions, the CT is therefore not necessarily identified, which also implies that the value concept including CT is used in all statistical reporting. This approach is called the "modified gross approach" (modified because the CT on investments is later deducted). With the gross system, the main problem in the national accounts is to have the CT removed from the final expenditure items, for which it is deductible, to obtain the correct measure for GDP at market prices from the expenditure side. In practice, this problem is related to fixed capital formation (but excluding residential buildings and most public investment) and changes in inventories. The total deductible CT from these items is posted as a negative CT on the expenditure side, with a similar negative amount as a balancing item on the production side, so that the total CT that appears as taxes on production in the national accounts will equal the government revenue from this tax. Outputs for own use are valued at equivalent market prices, and transfer prices are not usually detected. Total exports and imports are recorded on an f.o.b. basis, and the information on insurance and freight necessary to transform the imports c.i.f. is available from the estimates made to compile the BOP. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: In principle, transactions and flows are record on an accrual basis. Few government-related transactions are recorded on a cash basis due to the limited availability of source data. Transactions and flows, including work in progress, are in principle recorded on an accrual basis. The government accounts data sources are on a cash basis, as is the case in several countries. However, adjustments are made in compiling the national accounts for some important types of expenditures, for which monthly information is available. An example is medical expenditures that are recorded on an accrual basis on the assumption that the payments take place one month later than the transaction. Consumption of fixed capital in government nonmarket production is also estimated on an accrual basis. Other government related transactions are recorded on a cash basis, owing to the limited availability of source data. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Transactions between establishments within the same enterprise are in principle recorded on a gross basis. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources are continuously reviewed and broadly sufficient. Annual enterprise/establishment statistics: The Statistical Business Register is the business register of Japan, which is developed in accordance with Paragraph 1, Article 27 of the Statistics Act (Act No.53 of 2007). The results of various statistical surveys, such as the Economic Census, and the administrative records information (e.g., information on labour insurance, information on commercial and corporate registration), are integrated into the database. It is updated routinely to collect information on all establishments and enterprises, and to maintain this information up-to-date. Data for compiling annual accounts: Sufficiently detailed data are available from the "Annual Business Survey" conducted by the MIC and the Ministry of Economy, Trade, and Industry (METI). Household budget surveys: The system of household budget surveys in Japan consists of three related surveys: (1) the Family Income and Expenditure Survey (FIES), (2) Survey of the Household Economy, and (3) National Survey of Family Income, Consumption and Wealth. The first two are monthly with sample sizes of about 8,000 and 30,000 households, respectively, whereas the third is five-yearly (conducted for years ending on 4 and 9), with a sample size of about 56,000 households, and can be seen as the benchmark survey. The results of the FIES and Survey of the Household Economy are so timely that they can be used in the first preliminary version of the quarterly accounts. The survey of the household economy is relatively new, introduced on the initiative of the national accounts compilers, to better cover those goods and services that are infrequently bought. For national accounts purposes, the information obtained from the survey of the household economy replaces what has been obtained on the corresponding items in the FIES and thus improves the overall coverage and reliability of the combined data set. Before the data are introduced into the national accounts calculations, they are further adjusted for insufficient coverage of the single person households. These data are also adjusted by means of correction factors, where the ratio of expenditure per household according to the FIES to expenditure per household according to the "national survey of family income and expenditure" is determined per item. The current quarterly expenditure obtained from FIES is multiplied by this ratio to determine the "benchmarked" amounts. The section about the specific quarterly compilation techniques explains how these data are incorporated into the compilation of the quarterly accounts. More technical details about the household surveys are given in the DQAF on the Consumer Price Index (CPI). Government finance statistics: Because statistics with good coverage for government are only available with a lag, various sources are used for compiling the national accounts data for the quarterly data and the preliminary annual data. The total estimates are derived as the sum of separate estimates for each of the cost components: intermediate consumption, compensation of employees, consumption of fixed capital, and taxes on production less sales of products. The DNA carries out a special survey on local government, for the purpose of producing national accounts. The results are mainly used for determining the cost composition, whereas the total is obtained from budget data and trend extrapolations. For central government, the DNA uses the number of government information. Major parts of central government expenditures can be estimated by means of the monthly records of the payments for medical care, nursing care, etc. The ratios of intermediate consumption, sales of products, consumption of fixed capital, and taxes on production are to a considerable extent based on constant proportions or trend extrapolations. For compiling the final data at the end of the second year after the reference year, all the reference year comprehensive data are available for both central and local government as well as for social security institutions. Thus a good basis exists for the annual benchmarking of the series on which the preliminary estimates are based. Data collections based on administrative data sources: The administrative data are used to compile national accounts data where necessary, while the use of the information such as tax record is relatively limited in Japan, due to very strict confidentiality rules in force for those agencies in possession of these data, as well as the fact that Japan's CT system does not have invoice as abovementioned. Periodic (two to five years) surveys/censuses and ad-hoc surveys are conducted: Comprehensive large-scale surveys, such as the Population Census and Economic Census for Business Activity, are conducted around every five years. The latter contains detailed data and industrial classification and legal organization for all enterprises in the country. In connection with the five-yearly Input-output table, a great number of special surveys are conducted, in particular on input structures, structure of fixed capital formation, etc. As mentioned above, the national survey of family Income, consumption and wealth,is conducted every five years. Price statistics used to derive constant price estimates are adequate: Price indices are adequate and timely for the volume estimates of the national accounts. The available price statistics are sufficient for national accounts purposes. Examples are the general price indices (CPI and Producer Price Index (PPI); see the DQAFs on CPI and producer price index or PPI) and the more specialized price indices for services (the Services Producer Price Index or SPPI), together with the price indices for exports and imports. The availability of individual price indices allows the volume estimates to be made at an approximately 400 product level in both the quarterly and the annual accounts. Data for compiling the quarterly GDP: quarterly estimates of GDP are compiled using alternative data where no direct source data are available. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Major sources are shown below. Some source data are not consistent with the definitions, classifications, and requirements of the national accounts, and they are adjusted before used in the compilation. But in some cases, and in particular for the short-term data needed for the first preliminary estimates of the quarterly accounts, timeliness is lagging. In such cases, estimates are based on approximations, related indicators, trend extrapolation, etc. using as much information as is available. 3.2.1 Source data assessment During the process of regular estimation and its review, source data from surveys are analyzed and verified. When necessary, the DNA refers to source data producing agencies. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques The supply-side's commodity flow method naturally includes adjustments for non-response and reconciliation with timely available administrative data and other sources. Expenditure approach: Quarterly aggregate : Annual estimates for the previous year are calculated, using the commodity flow method based on supply-side statistics with wide coverage; The annual estimates are broken down into quarterly ones, in principle, according to the quarterly pattern of the auxiliary series; The quarterly estimates for the previous year are extrapolated based on the quarterly changes in the auxiliary series; Real-term values are calculated from nominal values (estimated in the above-mentioned process)and deflators with the chain-linking method. Unit deflators for each commodity are estimated from CPI of the MIC, PPI and SPPI (Services Producer Price Index) of the Bank of Japan (BOJ), and other sources; Seasonal factors are adjusted. Annual aggregate: Using the commodity flow method, output, intermediate consumption, household final consumption, gross fixed capital formation, changes in inventories, and external transactions are estimated for each of more than 2,000 commodity groups provided by market producers. Outputs of services by producers of government services and by producers of private non-profit services to households are estimated separately from input cost approach. GDP is obtained by adding up estimates for these three types of producers. The price indices for each item are derived principally from CPI of the MIC, and PPI and SPPI of the BOJ. Income approach: Total factor income is estimated with the value-added method in production approach. Setting the total income as control total, receipts and payments of income by institutional sector are derived, based on Financial Statements Statistics of Corporations, settlements of accounts of governments, and other sources. In the process, current transfers to and from each institution and the disposable income of institutional sectors are obtained. Savings are estimated by subtracting separately estimated final consumption expenditure of private and public institutions from the disposable income. Production approach: V matrix, which shows outputs of commodities by economic activity, is prepared, and outputs of around 90 industry groups are estimated. The outputs of more than 2,000 commodity groups estimated with the commodity flow method act as a control total. Value added by industry and its components are estimated by subtracting intermediate inputs by industry, which are estimated separately from U matrix, from the outputs of each industry - this is known as the value-added method. Note that the double-deflation method is used to obtain real-term GDP from the production side - 1) deflate nominal outputs and nominal intermediate inputs separately, 2) subtract real-term intermediate inputs from real-term outputs, then obtain real-term value added. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures As a seasonal adjustment method for quarterly GDP, X-12-ARIMA is used. The method is applied for every set of aggregate; namely, private consumption, residential investment, non-residential investment, private inventory, government consumption, public investment, public inventory, exports and imports of goods and services. The chain-linking method has been implemented for GDP (expenditure approach) and its components from January-March 1994. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Intermediate results are checked for temporal consistency and compared to the development in other types of data. Sometimes these checks result in changes in the data. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data The commodity flow system and the supply and use framework are used to investigate discrepancies and to adjust the statistical output. During the annual estimation process, a systemic procedure exists to routinely assess the potential discrepancies in intermediate data. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs National accounts estimates are checked against several kinds of macroeconomic data. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Differences between preliminary estimates and revised ones are routinely checked. Findings are properly fed back to improve estimation processes. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses When the second preliminary quarterly estimates are disseminated, a table shows the revisions compared to the first release, and major reasons for the revisions are given with the published data. For annual estimates, major reasons for revisions are explained in the "Notice on Usage". These documents are posted also on the ESRI website. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request More detailed data than is routinely disseminated are provided upon request within the range of possibilities and resources. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Access points for clients to obtain statistical information are notified on the ESRI website.
