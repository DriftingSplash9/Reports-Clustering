 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Definitions: Receipts included in the report are classified into the following major categories: (1) budget receipts and (2) offsetting collections. Budget receipts are collections from the public that result from the exercise of the Government's sovereign or governmental powers, excluding receipts offset against outlays. Budget receipts consist mainly of tax receipts (including social insurance taxes), receipts from court fines, certain licenses, and deposits of earnings by the Federal Reserve System. Offsetting collections are from other Governmental accounts or the public that are of a business-type or market-oriented nature. The concepts and definions are consistent with the International Monetary Fund's Government Finance Statistics Manual 2014. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification System: The classification of MTS receipts and outlays is defined by the Office of Management and Budget. Scope of the data Central Government: The data cover the financial activities of the Federal Government (including the operations of the Social Security Administration) and off-budget Federal entities (primarily the Social Security trust funds and the Postal Service). The Monthly Treasury Statement of Receipts and Outlays of the United States Government (MTS) summarizes the financial activities of the Federal Government and off-budget Federal entities conducted in accordance with the Budget of the U.S. Government. It presents receipts and outlays of funds, the surplus or deficit, and the means of financing the deficit or disposing of the surplus (showing a breakdown by major group of debt instrument). 2.4.2 Recording basis Accounting basis: The information is presented in the MTS on a modified cash basis: receipts and expenditures are recorded on a cash basis; and interest on public debt (public issues) is recognized on an accrual basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Adjustment for grossing and netting of transactions: Outlays are stated net of offsetting collections (including receipts of revolving and management funds) and of refunds. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Data sources: The major sources of data include monthly accounting reports by Federal entities and disbursing officers, and daily reports from the Federal Reserve banks. These reports detail accounting transactions affecting receipts and outlays of the Federal Government and off-budget Federal entities, and their related effect on the assets and liabilities of the U.S. Government. The data are based on actual outturns and the major sources of the data include the monthly accounting reports by Federal entities and disbursing officers, and daily reports from the Federal Reserve banks. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Verification of payment data: The monthly payment activity reported by Federal entities on their Statements of Transactions is compared to the payment activity of Federal entities as reported by disbursing officers. Verification of collection data: Reported collections appearing on Statement of Transactions are compared to deposits as reported by Federal Reserve banks. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Consolidation: The data are published on a consolidated basis. The data on payments and collections are reported by account symbol into the central accounting system. The account symbol identifies the federal program agency, the length of the program spending, and the specific program purpose. In turn, the data are extracted from this system for use in the preparation of the MTS. None
