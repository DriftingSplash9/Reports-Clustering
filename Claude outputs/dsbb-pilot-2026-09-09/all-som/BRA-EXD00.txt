 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The external debt encompasses total contractual debts effectively disbursed and not yet paid, owed by residents of an economy to nonresidents, in which there is an obligation to pay principal and/or interest at some point(s) in the future. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization External debt data are broken down into General Government, Central Bank, Banks, Other Sectors and Intercompany Lending, also by original maturity (short and long-term) and by instrument (special drawing rights, currency and deposits, debt securities, loans, trade credits and advances, and other debt liabilities). 2.2.1 Scope External debt data are broken down into General Government, Central Bank, Banks, Other Sectors and Intercompany Lending, also by original maturity (short and long-term) and by instrument (special drawing rights, currency and deposits, debt securities, loans, trade credits and advances, and other debt liabilities). Scope of the data The calculation covers data on the entire short and long-term public or private external debt, for the entire national territory and all economic sectors, expressed in national or foreign currency, in the modalities of financed imports, leasing, service financing and financial loans, as well as all operations or alterations in such records, such as inflows, payments, assumptions, renewals and conversions. Exceptions to coverage Short-term commercial credits and advances are currently not covered. Unrecorded activity None. 2.4.1 Valuation Nominal value obtained from the records, except the debt securities issued by the Republic in the international market, which are valued both at nominal and market values. 2.4.2 Recording basis The external debt is registered on a cash basis. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Grossing/netting procedures are broadly consistent with BPM6.The disbursements are registered on a gross basis. All financial operations derived from a particular debt record are clearly and separately identified. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Electronic Declaratory Registry - Financial Operations Registry (RDE-ROF), Brazilian Securities and Exchange Comission (CVM), Open Market Department of BCB, Central Bank Accounting Plan of the National Financial System Institutions (COSIF), BCB´s accounting data. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data obtained from the various sources are consistent with the definitions, scope, classifications, and time of recording of the external debt statistics. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Data collection and processing timetables are adequate to meet timeliness and periodicity for disseminating the external debt statistics. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The quality and comprehensiveness of the data sources are continually analyzed. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Compilation procedures minimize processing errors such as coding, editing, and tabulation errors. Data sources are received and processed electronically through IT systems. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Data are not adjusted seasonally. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Intermediate results are checked across a wide range of data sources. BOP flows, for example, are an important tool in checking external debt stocks prior to its final release. Both results, the estimated and the compiled ones, are compared to check the consistency of the latter. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data The behavior of series is routinely crosschecked with related series and indicators. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs BCB routinely verifies debt data against data of multilateral organizations on external debt, mainly IDB and the World Bank. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses External debt data are subject to revision in case of methodology changes or factual errors. The external debt statistics are revised according to the Review Policy of the statistics (https://www.bcb.gov.br/content/statistics/methodologicalnotes_docs/ Review_Policy_of_Official_Economic_Statistics.pdf). 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses The general policy of the Central Bank of Brazil is that major changes in methodology are announced at the time they are introduced. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Statistics not routinely disseminated are made available to users upon request, following data disclosure rules. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Prompt and knowledgeable service and support are available to users. Contact points for enquiries are available on the metadata section of each statistics series available at the BCB website (www.bcb.gov.br), including e-mail addresses of the staff in charge. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs All Brazilian external debt data are available to the public on the BCB website at no charge.
