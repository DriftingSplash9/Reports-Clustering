 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: Quarterly National Accounts (QNA) are estimates of the main aggregates of annual National Accounts so they respect the same concepts. The concepts and overall structure are in accordance with ESA 2010 (European System of Integrated Economic Accounts). Methodological framework: Portuguese Quarterly National Accounts are estimated by econometric methods of temporal disaggregation. In general terms, these methods consist in relating each Annual National Accounts variable (reference variables) with one or more indicators (related indicators) available quarterly. These methods can solve simultaneously two problems in QNA estimation: distribution (quarterly estimation when annual total is known) and extrapolation (when annual total is unknown). A quasi-direct approach is followed in some specific cases (e.g. imports and exports of goods and services), since quarterly series present only minor differences with respect to the annual figures. Therefore, the general approach for compiling Portuguese QNA can be considered mixed, by using direct methods in a few specific cases and indirect methods as the main approach. Definition: The nature of economic activity is in accordance with ESA 2010 rules. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification systems: Quarterly data are aggregations of the Annual National Accounts. The classification systems used in the National Accounts are: ESA 2010 for the institutional sectors; National classification of products in accordance with the Classification of Products by Activities; National classification of activities NACE Rev.2; Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP); Classification of the Functions of Government (COFOG); and Harmonized System (HS) for international transactions. Scope of the data Transactions coverage: The coverage of the transactions is in accordance with ESA 2010 rules. Geographic coverage: The geographic coverage is the entire country of Portugal (including Madeira and Azores). Exceptions to coverage There are no exceptions to coverage. Unrecorded activity Unrecorded activity: Under reporting activities are evaluated by annual National Accounts in the context of the exhaustiveness of GNI and, due to the procedures used in quarterly accounts, they are implicitly included. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: Gross Value Added at market prices. Expenditure aggregates at purchaser's prices. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: Most transactions are recorded on an accrual basis, but some government transactions are recorded on a cash basis (with some temporal adjustments). 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Grossing/netting procedures in accordance with ESA 2010 rules. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs All source data used are compliant with ESA 2010 definitions or, alternatively, appropriate measures are applied to ensure compliance. All surveys conducted by INE are subject to appropriate statistical criteria on the selection of samples and extrapolation, meaning that results are representative of relevant universes. All indicators used are either direct volume indicators, or current prices indicators deflated by appropriate chained indices, so all indicators used in volume estimates are chained volume indicators, ensuring chained volume final estimates. Main data sources used: Information on vegetable and animal production; Economic Accounts for agriculture, forestry and fishery; Consumption of electricity; Fuel sales; Information on costs and profits of financial enterprises; Retail trade sales indices; Consumer price indices; Sales of passenger cars, of light commercial vehicles and of heavy duty vehicles; Industry production, turnover and prices indices; Index of production in construction; Balance of payments, appropriated using criteria, definitions and scope established by final Annual National Accounts; International trade statistics (nominal and deflators);; Services turnover indices; Quarterly accounts for General government sector; Sales of cement and steel; Licenses for construction; Housing construction cost indices; House Price Index Number of overnight stays; Service producer prices; Information within the scope of the electronic system for issuing invoices and automatic communication to the Tax Authority (e invoice system); Operations on the automatic teller machines of the Multibanco network; Employment data from Labour Force Survey; Monthly Statement of Earnings from Social Security and the Contributory Relation of Caixa Geral de Aposentações . 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording All source data are available in accordance with ESA 2010 rules or are adjusted by National Accounts to be in accordance with ESA 2010 rules. All information integrated in Quarterly National Accounts is in accordance with definition, scope, classification, and time of recording demanded by ESA 2010 . Related indicators used in indirect estimations. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Timeliness of source data is compatible with Quarterly National Accounts timeliness. 3.2.1 Source data assessment All source data is routinely assessed both by the units in charge of their production and by National Accounts. For sample surveys, coverage is assessed in real time and samples are analyzed and revised yearly. Results of these assessments are used to eliminate errors and distortions whenever possible, namely when changes of base occur. Final Annual National Accounts also performs routine assessments of source data by comparing several sources of information, which allows checking out for inconsistencies. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques QNA estimates are compiled at current prices and in chain-linked volumes. The concrete method of compilation depends on data availability. Estimates at current prices are first derived if indicators are available in nominal terms and then volume estimates are obtained by deflation with quarterly prices. Volume estimates are directly obtained if the indicators are available in real terms and then nominal estimates are obtained by inflating with price data. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Base year and update cycle: chain-linked volume series are referenced to 2021 and will be updated every five years. Reconciliation: The differences between GDP compiled by the production approach and GDP compiled by the expenditure approach are allocated to a "statistical discrepancy" item on the production side. Quarterly data are backward revised when the annual National Accounts are compiled, and the econometric procedure used ensures that the sum of quarters is equal to the annual final data. Seasonal adjustment: All the indicators used in the estimates are previously adjusted for seasonal patterns and calendar effects, ensuring that the final estimates are also seasonally and calendar adjusted. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Intermediate results are analyzed and validated in the light of the information directly used in the estimation, but also by using auxiliary information. This is used to validate both the source data and the preliminary results obtained by using that data. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Intermediate results are analyzed and validated in the light of the information directly used in the estimation, but also by using auxiliary information. This is used to validate both the source data and the preliminary results obtained by using that data. Adjustments are introduced whenever problems, errors or discrepancies in source are transferred to estimated values by econometric modelling. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs Magnitude of discrepancies between production and expenditure approaches are analyzed to identify potential problems in intermediate results. From that point, an investigation is initiated to identify the source of those discrepancies back to source data. Adjustments are made when considered necessary to reduce discrepancies. Final Annual National Accounts also performs routine assessments of source data by comparing several sources of information, which allows checking out for inconsistencies. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions are routinely analyzed using several statistical measures to identify and classify the magnitude, sign and possible systematic deviations of the revisions. These statistical measures for analyzing revisions are updated each new quarter. Analyzing these results enables the identification of main sources of revisions, allowing the introduction of improvements to contain revisions, namely on source data. Related information can be consulted in Statistics Portugal's revision policy . 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Whenever a revision is carried out the revised data are published in Statistics Portugal website. Data users are provided with information on revisions and any methodological changes in the national accounts press releases.
