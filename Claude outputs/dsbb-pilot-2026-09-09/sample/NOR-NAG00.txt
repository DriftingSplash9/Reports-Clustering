 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The GDP data are compiled using both expenditure and production methods. The analytical framework is based on the 2008 System of National Accounts (2008 SNA) and the European System of National and Regional Accounts 2010 (ESA 2010) . The GDP related part of the Norwegian National Accounts (NNA) is based on an integrated supply and use table framework, both for the annual and for the quarterly estimates. The 2008 SNA and ESA 2010 was implemented in 2014. Historical annual series beginning in 1970 and quarterly series beginning in 1978 are available. All aggregates are defined according to international standards (SNA and ESA). GDP measures the total value of goods and services produced from resident producers less total value of goods and services consumed as inputs in the production process, before deduction of provision for consumption of fixed capital. The production approach thus defines GDP = Output (at basic prices) - Intermediate consumption + Taxes on products, net -. The expenditure approach defines GDP = Final consumption expenditure + Gross fixed capital formation + Changes in inventories + Exports - Imports. Finally, the income approach defines GDP = Compensation of employees + Operating surplus /Mixed income + Consumption of fixed capital + Taxes on production - Subsidies on production - . 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Institutional sectors and subsectors are defined in accordance with the 2008 SNA / ESA 2010. The activity classification is based on the Norwegian Standard Industrial Classification (SN2007), which is an elaboration of the EU-standard NACE Rev.2., and is at the aggregated level identical with ISIC Rev.4. The product classification is based on EU's central product classification CPA. Final consumption expenditure is classified by purpose - household consumption by COICOP, NPISHs by COPNI and general government by COFOG. Gross fixed capital formation is cross-classified by industry, and type of capital good. The quarterly compilation system is based on aggregated versions of the detailed classifications in the annual system, e.g. approximately 60 industries and 80 products. Scope of the data Transaction coverage: All activities within the 2008 SNA production boundary are in principle covered. Geographic coverage: The GDP data cover the entire Norwegian economy. The Norwegian economic territory includes mainland Norway, the Norwegian part of the Continental Shelf, Jan Mayen and Bjørnøya. Transactions related to Norwegian controlled activities at Svalbard and Norwegian controlled or managed ships in ocean transport mostly between third countries, are also covered. Exceptions to coverage Transaction coverage: No particular transactions are intentionally excluded. Unrecorded activity Norway participates in the European project on improving coverage (exhaustiveness) of GDP. Specific adjustments are made for hidden and under-reported data whenever possible, as well as illegal activities regarding consumption of prostitution services, drugs and smuggled alcohol (spirits). 2.4.1 Valuation Output of market production and production for own final use is valued at basic prices, while non-market output (per convention) is valued as equal to total costs of production. Supplementary data for output at producers' prices are available. All intermediate and final use categories are valued at purchasers' prices net of deductible VAT. As in the Norwegian BOP, total and detailed imports are at c.i.f, and exports at f.o.b. (including an imputed export value for cost of insurance and freight included in imports and supplied by Norwegian producers). For international reporting purposes, imports c.i.f. are adjusted to f.o.b. valuation on a global basis. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: Transactions are in general recorded on an accrual basis. Taxes on income, as well as most taxes and subsidies on production and imports are on an approximately accrual basis (VAT on a full "theoretical basis"), while other parts of the government accounts are on a cash basis. Exports and imports of goods are in practice recorded when the goods cross the border as registered through customs declarations. Recording period: Calendar quarters and calendar years. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs In general, sources data are improved in coverage and quality as we move in time from quarterly to annual estimates. Below, main sources used for the quarterly output estimations are described briefly, while those later used for the annual accounts are not dealt with here. Sources regarding the Quarterly National Accounts are described in more detail in the publication Quarterly national accounts. Methods and sources of the quarterly national accounts compilations for Norway December 2013 . Production approach Main data used for agriculture are y early data at a detailed product level from the Budgeting Committee for Agriculture, where the estimates are assigned to the respective quarters the products are harvested, monthly indicators on dairy products and on meat and poultry production. For agriculture there is also detailed volume information available for intermediate consumption. Statics used for forestry are the quarterly commercial roundwood removal statistic and a quarterly producer price index for timber extraction. For fisheries , quarterly volume and price estimates are obtained from the Directorate of fisheries, whereas export figures of salmon and trout (volume and value) in the external trade statistics are the main indicators in aquaculture . For mining, manufacturing and electricity output, the monthly index of industrial production (computed for about 70 different branches) is the main volume indicator, in addition to monthly producer price indices (PPI). For extraction of crude oil and natural gas, monthly data (tons) of extracted crude oil, natural gas and natural gas liquids, respectively, are used, as well as producer price indices for various petroleum products. Monthly building cost indices and quarterly production index of building and construction are basic data for construction. Various indicators are used for the transportation industries, such as ton-kilometers for freight transport by road (by truck and by train), passenger kilometers by taxi, number of passengers transported by air, rail, etc and travelling on charter tours, gross tonnage of ships, CPI components, cost price index for road transportation and several producer price indices. Turnover statistics (based on the VAT-register-) are used for the major part of service industries. Other sources are employment figures (of this hours worked), number of licenses paid to Norwegian broadcasting, quarterly income of state-run lotteries, guest-nights in hotels, quarterly business accounts for financial services, and quarterly data from central government and local government accounts. Quarterly producer price indices are available for most service industries. Dwelling services are estimated by extrapolation, using housing stock data in square metres (applying the perpetual inventory method) and the subcomponent of CPI price index. Expenditure approach: In the quarterly accounts, for final consumption expenditure of households the most important indicator is the monthly index of retail trade. Direct volume indicators used are car registrations, oil and petrol deliveries to households and petrol stations, quarterly consumption of electricity, monthly quantities of beer and soft drinks from breweries, government accounts data on fees paid by households, output indicators for various services (see above), foreign exchange statistics on travel and various CPI price indices. For government consumption expenditure, data are available from quarterly government accounts. For gross fixed capital formation, main sources are the quarterly survey of investment expenditure that covers mining, manufacturing, electricity, oil and gas extraction and wholesale and retail trade. Other sources used for estimations of fixed capital formation is exports and imports on ships and aircraft, data from the building register covering the number of square meters which are set in production and completed during a month, car registration data and data from central and local government accounts. Data on exports and imports on goods from external trade statistics are available on a monthly basis (customs records). Data for export and import of services are available on a quarterly basis from a sample survey. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Statistical procedures regarding the Quarterly National Accounts are described in more detail in the publication Quarterly national accounts. Methods and sources of the quarterly national accounts compilations for Norway December 2013 . The Norwegian QNA is based on extrapolation of the latest final version of the annual accounts (changing every year), using quarterly/monthly indicators. In year t tthe base year will be (t-2). Supply and use framework enables the use of the commodity-flow method to reconcile sources and the production and expenditure approach. For areas where no sources are available, as for intermediate consumption in most industries, the method of fixed coefficients is used (defined as the share of output from the base year). Both constant and current prices are compiled. Below, some specific issues related to the methods are briefly described. Production approach: Output of wholesale and retail trade at constant prices is estimated from the expenditure side by applying fixed coefficients of trade margins to the commodity flows (from the base year). Current prices are also estimated based on the commodity flow approach and relevant price indices. To calculate production of government services at constant prices, the output at current prices (consisting of compensation of employees, intermediate consumption, consumption of fixed capital etc.) are mainly deflated by relevant cost price indices. Indirect volume measurement is used for some service categories used for individual consumption, as education. Expenditure approach: For household consumption expenditure of some minor services there is a lack of short-term information. In these cases trend extrapolation is used, Consumption of dwellings is consistent with the production of dwelling services. Final consumption expenditure of NPISH is calculated as a fixed share of output in NPISHs.. For gross fixed capital formation, there is no information on investments in machinery and equipment in other service industries, and thus these variables are assumed to follow output. Investment in ships is determined using imports less exports as indicator. Because of lack of information, changes in inventories are estimated as a residual (from the commodity balances). Other expenditure components or output may be adjusted if the changes in inventories and statistical discrepancies look unrealistic. VAT is calculated in the supply and use model by applying the tax rates of the base year to the commodity flows. The calculated VAT is reconciled with information from the government account on accrued VAT. Other taxes on products and subsidies at current prices are based either on monthly data from the tax authority or an estimate for the whole year, distributed equally over the quarters. At constant prices other taxes and subsidies on products follow the fixed coefficients from the base year. Base year and update cycle: The principle of annual chaining was introduced in the Norwegian national accounts in 1990. The QNA time series are reported in constant prices using the reference year of the most recent final national accounts, since this is the base year for the compilation of the current quarter. Thus, the volume measures for the series backward are presented in monetary terms by applying the annually-reweighted chain Laspeyres volume changes to the values of the reference year. The detailed annual supply and use tables are compiled in current and previous year's prices. Seasonal adjustment: The QNA data are seasonally adjusted using the X12ARIMA package. None
