 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions At the national level: The Balance of Payments (BOP) is a statistical statement summarizing transactions between res idents and non-residents during a given period. It includes the merchandise trade (MET) account, the primary income account, the secondary income account, the capital account, and the financial account. In Niger, the BOP compiled by the National Directorate of the BCEAO, in accordance with the 6th edition of the “Manual on Balance of Payments and International Investment Position (IIP); external position (IIP; PEG)” of the International Monetary Fund. At the regional level: the UEMOA Balance of Payments (regional BOP) is compiled by the headquarters of the BCEAO. It is prepared following the BPM6/MBP6 framework and covers the economic transactions of the Union's residents with the rest of the world. Thus, intra-UEMOA transactions are excluded from the regional BOP. It has, among other sources, the data from country Balance of Payments, compiled by the States and the data on reserve assets produced by the headquarters. Residents: individuals having their main centre of interest in a UEMOA member state, national civil servants posted abroad, and national or foreign legal entities for their establishments in a UEMOA member state. Non-residents: individuals having their main centre of interest abroad, foreign officials stationed in a UEMOA member state, and national or foreign legal entities for their establishments abroad. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization The different components of the Balance of Payments are developed in accordance with the format defined by the BPM6/MBP6 framework. 2.2.1 Scope The different components of the Balance of Payments are developed in accordance with the format defined by the BPM6/MBP6 framework. Scope of the data Geographic and unit coverage: The Niger's Balance of payments (BOP) covers all transactions involving residents of the national economic territory, as defined in the 2008 SCN, and non-residents. Coverage of operations: All operations are covered, in accordance with the BPM6/MBP6 framework. Exceptions to coverage No exception in coverage. Unrecorded activity Due to limitations in the current data collection system or due to their sensitive nature, certain activities are not included in the balance of payments statistics. These include: of digital trade; illegal transactions such as drug trafficking; of resident travelers' baggage. 2.4.1 Valuation Transactions are recorded in CFA francs (valued based on the exchange rate of the operation); For operations resulting from stock variations, the average exchange rate at the end of the period is used; The assessment is generally made at market price 2.4.2 Recording basis All Balance of payments (BOP) transactions are established on the basis of assessed rights, except for income, which is recorded on the basis of receipts/payments. Current and capital account transactions are recorded on a gross basis. However, for financial account transactions, they are recorded on a net basis. Thus: Merchandise trade is recorded f.o.b. (free on board); For imports, valued c.a.f. (cost, insurance, freight) in the customs statistics, which constitute the primary source of data, a c.a.ff.o.b. adjustment is necessary. This adjustment is estimated annually by the BCEAO for Niger, through the use of a coefficient, obtained on the basis of a survey on freight and customs information. This coefficient is regularly updated; Loans, borrowings, and deposits are expressed in nominal value, while direct investments are recorded at their historical value (acquisition cost) on the balance sheet and at their nominal value on the liability side, as are portfolio investments on the liability side. The titles recorded on the balance sheet are valued according to the portfolio to which they are attached (deposit, investment, trading). 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The main data sources used for the Niger's Balance of payments (BOP) are administrative statistics and surveys by the BCEAO to businesses (representative sample). The collection of data for the Balance of payments (BOP) for year N begins no later than March 31st of year N+1 and ends no later than the end of October N+1. A schedule is developed and monitored by the BCEAO headquarters. Adherence to this schedule is part of the service's performance objectives and is assessed annually. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Data collection from public and private companies is conducted through a representative sample of Niger's economic fabric (the main source). The sample is regularly updated to take into account the dynamics of business creation and closure. Other sources used are the reports of various national and international public agencies. Thus, we have the following sources: Surveys of international organizations and diplomatic missions: Questionnaires sent to non-residents in countries (foreign airlines, foreign embassies, international organizations, etc.). Government documents : External trade statistics from the National Institute of Statistics; Reports on the agricultural campaign and exports of agricultural and livestock products by the Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock. Sectoral reports from ministerial departments: the Ministry of Higher Education, the Ministry of Planning (Department of Planning and Programs), the Ministry of Finance (Investment Financing Department, Studies and Reforms Department). Reports from international organizations (UNDP, IMF, World Bank). Bank commercial situation reports: DEC and other tables. These documents allow the grouping of data from the commercial banks' DEC2000 reports, the central bank's balance sheet, or analytical tables relating to the operations of certain specific organizations: UEMOA, ASECNA, CRRAE, IRD, General Treasury, etc. For the regional balance of payments, the data are provided by the regional directorates and the BCEAO headquarters (including non-distributed assets). 3.2.1 Source data assessment In order to guarantee the quality of the data communicated by its sources, the BCEAO, in collaboration with the National Balance of Payments Committee (CNBP), organizes each year, a training seminar for respondents, on the data compilation necessary for the balance of payments and the completion of the related questionnaires. On this occasion, feedback is provided to the structures regarding the quality and inconsistencies identified in the communicated information. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Merchandise trade: The customs data, processed by the National Statistics Institute (INS-Niger), are adjusted by the West African Monetary Union (UEMOA). The most important adjustment is the transition from Cost, Insurance and Freight (CIF) valuation to Free On Board (FOB) valuation for imports, the estimation of informal trade, and the adjustment of the value of certain exports (mineral products). The UEMOA also estimates the procurement and revenue expenditures as well as the imports and exports of goods intended for processing. Services: Transport: Maritime and rail freight is determined from the BCEAO survey of companies. Road freight is estimated, in the absence of direct data sources. Air transport is determined by data from airlines represented in the country. Travel: Estimates concerning foreign tourists are based on the statistics of the Ministry of Tourism. Vacation spending by non-residents is estimated from the income of this category and the length of stay. The travel expenses of Nigerian employees for business trips abroad are obtained from the General Directorate of the Treasury and Public Accounting (DGTCP), while the expenses of Nigerian students abroad come from the National Agency for Scholarship Allocation (ANAB) and the Ministry of Higher Education for national scholarships. Other services: The data come from surveys of businesses. The DGTCP also provides public spending abroad. Financial operations: Direct and portfolio investments: Primarily determined from the BCEAO survey of companies, these investments include equity transactions, the counterpart of reinvested profits, and intra-company loans, including trade credits, for direct investments. Portfolio investments are divided into equity holdings, debt securities, and money market instruments. Other investments: Public sector liabilities notably include external public debt. The information is drawn from the Treasury balance sheet. Assets and liabilities of monetary authorities are estimated by the BCEAO headquarters and transmitted to the National Directorate. Banking assets and liabilities cover commercial banks and the National Post Office. The commitments of public administrations include, in particular, external public debt. The information is obtained from the Ministry of Finance (DGTCP, Directorate of Public Debt). The assets and liabilities of the monetary authorities are obtained from the detailed report of the Central Bank. The assets and liabilities of banks are calculated based on bank transfers (DEC2000). The processing is performed on an application called “Balance of Payments in terms of Transaction (BPT)", implemented by the BCEAO, based on the BPM6/MBP6 framework. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures The treatments or transformations performed on the data concern notably: processing of CIF imports to FOB; Adjustment to external trade data to account for cross-border flows that are not recorded. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results An internal validation mechanism is implemented by the BCEAO. The report is reviewed by the Internal Control Service, then transmitted to the Statistics Department at BCEAO headquarters, before being validated by the CNBP. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses In collaboration with the CNBP, the BCEAO conducts an annual day of dissemination of external accounts, chaired by the Minister of Finance. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Reports validated by CNBP, unpublished, may be shared upon request.
