 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions External debt statistics are compiled in accordance with the IMF methodology prescribed in the Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual (BPM6). The structure of external debt is broken down by institutional sectors, debt instruments, and maturity. The classification of debt by sector and instrument is consistent with the financial account classifications under BPM6. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization External debt is classified by resident and non-resident sectors and by maturity. Resident sectors cover: the public and private sectors (banks, corporations, households and NPISHs). Non-resident sectors are broken down into international institutions, foreign governments, other financial institutions and other creditors. External debt is shown by original maturity and remaining maturity. Liabilities Direct investment (equity and reinvested earnings, debt instruments); Portfolio investment (equity and investment fund participations, debt securities); Financial derivatives; Other investments (Currency and deposits, Loans, Trade credits and advances, Other accounts payable, SDRs). In addition, the data are classified: - by institutional sector (general government, monetary authorities, depository institutions except central banks, other sectors); - by original maturity (short term up to one year and long term); - by instrument (currency and deposits, debt securities, loans, trade credit and advances, other accounts payable) 2.2.1 Scope External debt is classified by resident and non-resident sectors and by maturity. In addition, the data are classified: - by institutional sector (general government, monetary authorities, depository institutions except central banks, other sectors); - by original maturity (short term up to one year and long term); - by instrument (currency and deposits, debt securities, loans, trade credit and advances, other accounts payable) Scope of the data The concepts, definitions, and procedures used in the compilation of external debt statistics follow, to the extent possible, the sixth edition of the IMF Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6). Gross external debt is defined as residents' external debt liabilities to nonresidents and includes principal and interest in arrears, and principal not yet due. Exceptions to coverage There are no exceptions to coverage. 2.4.1 Valuation Main valuation method for listed equity is market value, while for unlisted equity main valuation method is own funds at book value. Nominal value as the main valuation method is used for ID in the form of debt (debt securities and debt other than securities) and for loans included in “Other Investments”. For portfolio investments and reserve assets, the valuation method used for the preparation of the IIP is market value. 2.4.2 Recording basis All External Debt items are recorded on an accrual basis, except for input data derived from Balance of Payments transactions and other assessments made in the compilation of part of the direct investment and portfolio investment (liabilities). The distinction between short-term and long-term debt is based on original maturity. Long-term debt includes all liabilities with original maturities of more than one year, and short-term debt includes all liabilities with original maturities of one year or less. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The main sources of data for Andorra's external debt statistics are: - The specific survey conducted on financial institutions; - Specific survey of insurance companies. - Administrative data sources - Mirror statistics 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Source data, scope, classifications, valuation and time of recording are in accordance with the BPM6 manual. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness The data source is currently annual. 3.2.1 Source data assessment Source data are routinely evaluated. Results of surveys conducted are monitored and made available to guide statistical processes. Large transactions are monitored to ensure that they are correctly recorded. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Direct investment: source data collected through a direct survey and administrative records. Data are recorded according to BPM6 and the OECD benchmark definition of DI 4th edition. Portfolio investment: This is collected through the specific survey of financial institutions. Other investments: most of it is collected from the statistics of financial institutions. Other data sources are mirror surveys. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures In case of data needs, the statistics department performs data imputation based on different statistical techniques. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Data from financial institutions are validated through automated data validation procedures. Data entry procedures include checks to avoid miscoding and misreporting. Queries are made to reporters on unusual transactions and large values. Data sources are checked against available indicators. Intermediate results are validated using other sources whenever possible. 3.4.2 Assessment of intermediate data Potential discrepancies in intermediate data are assessed on a regular basis and the corresponding adjustments are made if necessary. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses The statistics department disseminates working papers and analyses on relevant topics in order to stimulate debate and contribute to better knowledge. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Yes, ad-hoc requests are also supplied, by sending a request through e-mail at estadistica@govern.ad
