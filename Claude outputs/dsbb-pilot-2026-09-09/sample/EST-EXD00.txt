 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework and concepts: External debt statistics follow conceptual framework of the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) and External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (External Debt Guide) . 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification system: Gross external debt is a part of the IIP and follows conceptual framework of the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) and External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (External Debt Guide) . As for sector classification, external debt data are classified into four sectors: general government, central bank, monetary financial institutions, except central bank, and other sectors. As for geographical breakdowns, external debt items are classified according to the country of the foreign counterparty (when relevant) and also have activity breakdown according to the NACE rev.2 (since 2013). Data are available on special request. However, due to confidentiality reasons, country and activity classifications for some items may not be published. Relationship to international guidelines: Notable deviations from international guidelines do not exist. 2.2.1 Scope Classification system: Gross external debt is a part of the IIP and follows conceptual framework of the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) and External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (External Debt Guide) . As for sector classification, external debt data are classified into four sectors: general government, central bank, monetary financial institutions, except central bank, and other sectors. As for geographical breakdowns, external debt items are classified according to the country of the foreign counterparty (when relevant) and also have activity breakdown according to the NACE rev.2 (since 2013). Data are available on special request. However, due to confidentiality reasons, country and activity classifications for some items may not be published. Relationship to international guidelines: Notable deviations from international guidelines do not exist. Scope of the data Data are in millions of euros (EUR) for both assets and liabilities. Positions coverage: Gross external debt statistics are based on the external liabilities recorded in the international investment position, which are debts by nature as they have to be repaid. Direct, portfolio and other investment in equity capital, reinvestment of earnings, financial derivatives (other than reserves) and employees stock options are excluded from the accounting of gross external debt. Debt creative liabilities of companies in direct investment relationship are included in gross external debt. End-of-quarter gross external debt according to IMF Special Data Dissemination Standard is presented by the following sectors: general government, central bank, monetary financial institutions, except central bank, and other sectors. Data are broken down by maturity (short- and long-term) on an original maturity basis and by debt instrument as specified in the BPM6 – SDRs (for central bank only), currency and deposits, debt securities, loans, trade credits and advances, other debt liabilities. Data on intercompany lending (liabilities) under direct investment relationships are separately identified without sector attribution and maturity. External debt liabilities are attributed to the sector of the immediate borrower, and not of any guarantor. Geographical coverage: No geographic regions are excluded from recording. Exceptions to coverage No exceptions. Unrecorded activity No unrecorded activities. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: The general principle is to use the market values. The loans and deposit positions are recorded at nominal value. Conversion to unit of account: Positions in foreign currency are converted to euros, using the exchange rate valid at the end of the reporting period. 2.4.2 Recording basis In general, the accrual principle applies in the Estonian external sector statistics for the time of recording. Data supplied by the respondents of balance of payments surveys are typically based on business accounting records, and are considered to be close approximations to the accruals principle. If bookkeeping data are not readily available, estimates may be provided. 2.4.3 Grossing/netting procedures Grossing/netting procedures are broadly consistent with the BPM6 recommendations. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Debt securities: The main sources for compiling debt securities' positions are the ESCB's Centralised Securities Database, the Estonian Securities Register (NASDAQ), reporting by the Financial Supervision Authority and credit institutions, financial reporting by Eesti Pank, the system of accounts of the general government, and the enterprise survey. Trade credit and advances: The data sources are enterprise surveys, the accounts of the general government, and data models for estimations. Loans: The data sources are enterprise surveys, reporting by credit institutions and the Financial Supervision Authority, the accounts of the general government, and the ITRS. Currency and deposits: The main data sources are reports of credit institutions and enterprise surveys.. Other debt liabilities: The main data sources are the system of accounts of the general government (government sector), financial reporting by Eesti Pank (Central Bank), reporting by credit institutions (credit institutions) and the enterprise surveys (for other sectors). Inter-company loans: Information is derived from the enterprise surveys of non-bank direct investment enterprises and credit institutions' regular reports. These data sources are supplemented by data from the ITRS by using perpetual inventory method. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording The definitions, scope, classifications, valuation and time of recording are consistent with the rules set out by External Debt Statistics: Guide for Compilers and Users (External Debt Guide) and the sixth edition of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6) . Definition: Gross External Debt of Estonia is the outstanding amount of those actual, current, and not contingent, liabilities that require payments of principal and/or interest by the debtor at some points in the future and that are owed to nonresidents by residents. End-of-quarter gross external debt according to IMF Special Data Dissemination Standard is presented by the following sectors: general government, central bank, monetary financial institutions, except central bank, and other sectors. Data are broken down by maturity (short- and long-term) on an original maturity basis and by debt instrument as specified in the BPM6 – SDRs (for central bank only), currency and deposits, debt securities, loans, trade credits and advances, other debt liabilities. Data on intercompany lending (liabilities) under direct investment relationships are separately identified without sector attribution and maturity. External debt liabilities are attributed to the sector of the immediate borrower, and not of any guarantor. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness Source data timeliness meets the SDDS Plus requirements. All external sector statistics (BOP, IIP and Gross External Debt) are published with timeliness NLT (no later than) T + 75 calendar days. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The quality of data sources and input data is monitored on an ongoing basis; the accuracy and reliablility of data sources is considered good. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Data are mostly derived from the Eesti Pank's enterprise surveys. The cut off the tail method is applied and nearly 95% of the total foreign turnover is covered. The response rate is 94–96% and special procedures are used to cover non-respondence. The surveys cover around 3,000 entities. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Seasonal adjustment: Data are not seasonally adjusted. Geographic detail: Breakdown by country and activity is available for all items (if not confidential) starting from 2013. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Automatic cross-checking procedures are built in for data collected via an e-reporting system (the enterprise survey is collected electronically). For data validation and plausibility checks, a special IT tool is used, which enables to discover outliers on micro and macro level. This tool is used also for the reconciliation of BOP flows and IIP stocks. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs As a rule, data are collected only once and later changed between data compilers; there are no consistency problems in the final data. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses The revision practice has been harmonised within the EU. Following the harmonised revision cycle, different vintages of time series are stored to facilitate revision analysis. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses The annual Quality Report was compiled by Eesti Pank until the year 2013. From 2015 it was replaced by the Quality Report compiled by the Eurostat. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request Unpublished non-confidential data can be disseminated to an ad-hoc request if this does not breach confidentiality requirements. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Further information on data is available by phone on +372 6680 719 or email at info@eestipank.ee .
