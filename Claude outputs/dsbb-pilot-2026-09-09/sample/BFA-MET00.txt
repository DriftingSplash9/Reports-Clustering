 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions The Institute of National Statistics and Demography (INSD) serves as the secretariat for the technical committee responsible for monitoring the production of external trade statistics. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Transactions in goods are classified in two catagories: Special Trade and General Trade. Special Trade includes: (a) Imports of goods, which either directly upon entry or after storage, transit, transshipment, or upon leaving bonded warehouses or free trade zones, are: declared for domestic consumption (including merchandise previously accepted under the temporary admission regime); accepted for temporary entry or inward processing; admitted into bonded plants or industrial free zones; admitted into licensed in-bond factories; re-imported after temporary export for outward processing. Goods marketed for consumption after temporary entry, upon exit from bonded plants, industrial free zones and licensed in-bond factories are not included in special trade. (b) Exports of goods which are: declared subject to simple exit procedures (whether local or consideredor considered as local); re-exported after temporary entry for inward processing; leaving bonded plants or industrial tax-free zones; leaving licensed in-bond factories; exported temporarily for outward processing. General Trade includes: (a) Imports of goods which, either directly upon entry or after storage, transit, or transshipment are: declared for domestic consumption; accepted for temporary admission; accepted for temporary entry or inward processing; admitted into bonded plants or industrial tax-free zones; admitted into licensed in-bond factories; re-imported after temporary export for outward processing. admitted into bonded warehouses or tax-free trade zones; (b) Exports of goods which are: declared subject to simple exit procedures (whether local or nationalized); re-exported after temporary admission; re-exported after temporary entry for inward processing; exported temporarily for outward processing. leaving bonded plants or industrial tax-free zones; leaving licensed in-bond factories; leaving bonded warehouses or tax-free trade zones. Goods that are transshipped or in transit are not included in either in Special Trade nor in General Trade. Scope of the data The external trade statistics record all merchandise trade between the "statistical customs territory" of Burkina Faso and every other "statistical customs territory." A "statistical customs territory" is any clearly demarcated territory under customs control. In practice, statistical territories are identified by codes. Merchandise is defined in the national tariff nomenclature, which is the Harmonized System (HS). All merchandise entering or leaving the territory, whether or not it is taxable by customs, is recorded. Merchandise is defined by its value, in CFA francs; by volume, expressed in gross weight, net weight (kilograms); by quantity (in other units): number, liters, meters, etc. Imports are posted to the country of origin in the statistics, while exports are posted to the country of destination. Exceptions to coverage The Burkinabè external trade statistics do not take into account the following trade in goods and transactions: monetary gold, securities, currency (notes and coins) and bank money, military equipment, goods in direct transit, goods moved from one bonded warehouse to another, transshipments of goods for export. used personal effects carried by travelers, effects for the personal use of members of the diplomatic corps and foreign nationals on mission in Burkina Faso, Unrecorded activity The liberalization of trade between the WAEMU countries has weakened coverage of local goods, such as agricultural products and live animals. 2.4.2 Recording basis Imports are reported c.i.f. (cost-insurance-freight) and f.o.b. (free on board), while exports are reported exclusively f.o.b. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs In sum, the processing procedure is as follows: Division IV of the Directorate General of Customs (DGD) of Burkina Faso transmits to the Market Production, Trade, and Services Unit of the INSD on electronic media, and depending on data availability, monthly files with declarations, by settlement date. After transferring these records to the external trade data management software (EUROTRACE), the monthly customs declaration files undergo a series of corrections (offsetting entry, weight check, amounts with no corresponding units, export charges above zero, taxes, etc.) and updates of the various code tables, as necessary. The corrected customs declarations are then processed and incorporated into the databases. After undergoing the various procedures, the external trade statistics are available by product, country, customs regime, mode of transportation, etc. The data that are processed and disseminated in real time are those from the ASYCUDA (Automated System for Customs Data) computerized customs offices. In terms of coverage, these data represent about 80 percent of imports and 70 percent of exports. The reports of non-computerized customs offices are processed at least six months after the date on which they are recorded. The consolidated external trade statistics (computerized offices and non-computerized offices) are only available several months after the end of the reference period. None None
