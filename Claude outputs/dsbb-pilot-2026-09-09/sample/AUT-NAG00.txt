 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: Austria's national accounts framework is set up according to European System of Accounts (ESA2010) definitions which are consistent with System of National Accounts (SNA2008) definitions. Quarterly data are broken down according to the stipulations of the European Union laid down by council regulation (EU) 549/2013 of May 21, 2013 (L174/1) and its amendments. Time series for annual and quarterly figures start from 1995 on-wards. Definition: In Austria's national accounting system, GDP is primarily calculated as the sum of gross value added of all industries -- classified according to NACE Rev. 2 -- plus taxes and less subsidies on products (which are not allocated to sectors and industries). Unintended growth by natural processes without human contribution like increase in fish or wood stock is not production 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Results are broken down according to ESA2010. Supply side is broken down by NACE Rev. 2. On the expenditure side, for regular quarterly releases, figures of final consumption expenditure are classified by private households, private non-profit institutions serving households, and general government; the advance release covers only final consumption expenditure of private households including private non-profit institutions serving households and final consumption expenditure of general government. For regular quarterly releases, gross fixed capital formation is classified by seven types according to ESA2010 (advance releases cover only the sum). For regular dissemination, quarterly national accounts data for exports and imports are separated by goods and services (again, advance estimates provide only a sum of them). 2.2.1 Scope Results are broken down according to ESA2010. Supply side is broken down by NACE Rev. 2. On the expenditure side, for regular quarterly releases, figures of final consumption expenditure are classified by private households, private non-profit institutions serving households, and general government; the advance release covers only final consumption expenditure of private households including private non-profit institutions serving households and final consumption expenditure of general government. For regular quarterly releases, gross fixed capital formation is classified by seven types according to ESA2010 (advance releases cover only the sum). For regular dissemination, quarterly national accounts data for exports and imports are separated by goods and services (again, advance estimates provide only a sum of them). Scope of the data GDP comprises all activities within the ESA2010 production boundary. Estimates for hidden but legal economy activities are included as long as they contribute to GDP. Illegal activities are integrated according to the production definitions of ESA2010 (drug production and trafficking, prostitution, smuggling of cigarettes). GDP calculations cover the whole area within Austria's national border (including the free trade zones of Jungholz and Kleines Walsertal) as well as Austrian embassies in foreign countries. Exceptions to coverage Not included are embassies of foreign countries in Austria and other ex-territorial institutions (e.g., international organizations situated in Austria). Unrecorded activity All unrecorded activities within the boundaries of the ESA2010 are included. 2.4.1 Valuation Supply side contributions to GDP are valued at basic prices. To get to GDP at market prices taxes on products (less subsidies) are added. Demand side aggregates are valued at market prices, owner-occupied housing at comparable rental values and non-market transactions consist of production cost plus a profit-surcharge in the case of own production. Tangible fixed assets include setup costs as well as non-deductable taxes. 2.4.2 Recording basis All transactions are calculated on an accrual basis. Published data relate to calendar years and quarters. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Production approach: The main database for annual national accounts is a primary survey covering NACE Rev.2 industries B to N. The survey covers about 10% of all Austrian enterprises and represents nearly 90% of total enterprise turnover. The surveys deliver data on output and intermediate consumption. Enterprise reports of large firms are used additionally. Quarterly national accounts are compiled on a monthly survey of enterprises, which provides output figures. Results for agriculture are derived from various special statistics on output volumes and prices. Administrative records are used for the calculation of production in the government sector. Expenditure approach: Private household consumption expenditure is partially derived from retail trade statistics, statistics on non-retail traded private consumption (like energy consumption), tax revenue statistics, etc. Additionally, some services like owner-occupied rents are imputed. NPISH-consumption is mainly derived from government records (health expenditure). Government consumption calculations are based on government records. Import and export values are derived from monthly INTRASTAT and EXTRASTAT statistics as well as from the balance of payment statistics. Gross fixed capital formation figures are compiled using various data sources such as investment surveys, administrative data and commodity flows. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness As at the time of setting up QNA not all source data are available in full length, statistical techniques are applied in order to extend the series. This applies to the advanced releases as well as to the regular disseminations. 3.2.1 Source data assessment The quality assessment of the source data is an intrinsic part of the time disaggregation procedure. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Production approach: Source data are supplemented by estimates concerning unit-non-response, underreporting and hidden economic activity. Missing observations are either completed by forecasting or other econometric methods. In order to calculate value added from output figures on a quarterly basis, input/output ratios are derived. In some cases, this is not done explicitly but instead by econometric methods. For some service sectors, quarterly employment figures are used for extrapolation of value added. Expenditure approach: Adjustments for underreporting and imputing for rents take place on a regular basis. Quarterly figures are calculated by econometric means with statistics like retail sales, exports and imports, production statistics, tax revenues and administrative data as input. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Production approach: Price adjustment for annual statistics is done by deflating separately output and intermediary consumption (double deflation), for quarterly figures by econometric methods implicitly. Expenditure approach: Deflation is done using CPI data, wholesale prices, energy statistics, wage increase data (for government consumption) etc. National accounts are calculated at previous year prices. Results are chain-linked with 2015 as the reference year. Seasonal adjustment: Seasonal adjustment is applied to results not corrected in order to reach the chain-linked annual totals. Seasonal adjustment of results published at t+30 and at t+2 months is based on the software package “Persephone” developed by the Methods Unit of Statistics Austria. Chain linking: QNA estimates are chain-linked by the annual overlap technique and so add up automatically to the respective annual figures. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results Production approach: Data used in the production approach are tested for plausibility with tax records or benchmark figures for individual branches (like employment). Expenditure approach: Primary data, used for compilation of the expenditure side, is tested for plausibility in the same way as for production, with emphasis on tax records. 3.4.3 Assessment of discrepancies and other problems in statistical outputs The compilation of Austria's national accounts is primarily based on the production approach. A statistical discrepancy is therefore shown on the demand side in nominal terms. In addition, an income approach is calculated with ‘gross operating surplus and gross mixed income’ as a residual. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses WIFO: At the quarterly frequency revisions studies are available (most in German language): https://www.wifo.ac.at/en/research/thematic-platforms/business-cycles-and-forecasting/nati onal-accounts/ 5.1.5 Dissemination on request After publication, further details (if disposable) are disseminated on request.
