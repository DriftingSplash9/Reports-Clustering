 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Central government debt is mostly compiled following IMF´s Government Finance Statistics Manual (GFSM 2014), with some differences: Valuation by yield curve (price curve methodology) instead of market value, which means it reflects the debt at creation and subsequent economic flows, such as accrued interest. Debt does not include other accounts payable, such as delays in the payment of goods and services. Securities issued by the treasury to the central bank portfolio are not considered, although the central bank securitized repo operations are included in the general government debt. 2.2.1 Scope Central government debt is mostly compiled following IMF´s Government Finance Statistics Manual (GFSM 2014), with some differences: Valuation by yield curve (price curve methodology) instead of market value, which means it reflects the debt at creation and subsequent economic flows, such as accrued interest. Debt does not include other accounts payable, such as delays in the payment of goods and services. Securities issued by the treasury to the central bank portfolio are not considered, although the central bank securitized repo operations are included in the general government debt. Scope of the data Domestic debt: - Securities in the market issued by Tesouro Nacional (TN), the national treasury: federal public debt bonds issued and recorded at Sistema Especial de Liquidação e Custódia (SELIC), a settlement and custody system, settled and redeemed in BRL; - Central bank repo operations (financial assets sold under repurchase agreements); - Bank debt: financing granted by financial institutions; - Agricultural debt securities (TDA): TN Securities issued to fund agrarian reform operations. - Other debt, including securitized debt. External debt: - Debt securities and debts with international organizations and foreign banks. 2.4.1 Valuation See item 2.1.1 3.1.1 Source data collection programs The majority of public internal debt in Brazil is registered at custodians such as the Sistema Especial de Liquidação e Custódia (Selic), special settlement and custody system, and theB3/Central de Custódia e Liquidação Financeira de Títulos (B3/Cetip), a custody and liquidation chamber. Those are the source for most of the securities issued by the treasury and for central bank repo operations. External debt data can be directly accessed through BCB´s own IT systems. Other part of data is obtained from diverse sources, including financial institutions statements. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Central Government Debt is published on a gross basis. Flows that represent relationships among the subsectors of general government are eliminated in the consolidation process. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses See item 4.3.1
