 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Coverage The statistics cover both the Tanzania Mainland and Tanzania Zanzibar. The Free Port located in Zanzibar is considered as part of Tanzania. The published international trade statistics is in accordance to the general trade concepts and definitions. However, data according to the special trade concept are also available. The recommendations of the UN "International Trade Statistics Concepts and Definitions" are being followed. Nomenclature of commodities are classified according to the convention on Harmonized Commodity, Description and Coding Systems or Harmonized Systems (HS) of 2007 version, approved by the World Customs Organization (WCO) and adopted by the East African Community (EAC) Customs Union that became into operation on 1 st January 2005. However, the EAC Customs Union and Tanzania in particular has adopted HS version 2012 that came into force on 1 st July 2012 . As a general rule, goods are arranged in order of their degree of processing; raw materials, un worked products, semi-finished products and finished products. For the international standard of comparability, a commodity as defined by six digits code whereby the first two digits refers to chapter, the four digits is division. The seven and eighth digit code are left for manipulation of local use. Partner countries are classified by the (International Organization for Standardization (ISO)) coding established by the UN Statistics Division. The World Trade Organization (WTO) agreement on Valuation has been adopted and its implementation commenced on January 2001. Imports are valued at cost, insurance and freight (cif) and exports at free-on-board (fob). 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Coverage Nomenclature of commodities are classified according to the convention on Harmonized Commodity, Description and Coding Systems or Harmonized Systems (HS) of 2002 version, approved by the World Customs Organization (WCO) and adopted by the East African Community (EAC) Customs Union that became into operation on 1 st January 2005. However, the EAC Customs Union and Tanzania in particular has adopted HS version 2012 that came into force on 1 st July 2012 . As a general rule, goods are arranged in order of their degree of processing; raw materials, un worked products, semi-finished products and finished products. For the international standard of comparability, a commodity as defined by six digits code whereby the first two digits refers to chapter, the four digits is division. The seven and eighth digit code are left for manipulation of local use. Partner countries are classified by the (International Organization for Standardization (ISO)) coding established by the UN Statistics Division. The World Trade Organization (WTO) agreement on Valuation has been adopted and its implementation commenced on January 2001. Imports are classified by country of origin and exports by country of the last known destination. Scope of the data The responsibility for the compilation of Trade Statistics has been mandated to the Customs and Excise Department (CED) in the Tanzania Revenue Authority (TRA) since 1996. The CED uses exclusively customs declaration forms, single bills of entry and Tanzania Single Administrative Document (TANSAD) as source of data and is able to compile consistently series from 1995 onwards. The statistics cover both the Tanzania Mainland and Zanzibar. The Free Port located in Zanzibar is considered as part of Tanzania. Exceptions to coverage Excluded from the statistics are, among other things, passengers' baggage, motor vehicles imported on a triptyque, diplomatic goods, goods in transit, and monetary gold. Importation of electricity has been included as part of trade merchandise statistics . A threshold is in place below which no detailed flows are recorded. However, these data are recorded separately and later on consolidated in aggregated form. Efforts are underway to incorporate unrecorded cross border trade data into official trade statistics. The data are recorded in the date and month the documents are registered by Customs. Unrecorded activity Coverage The data do not incorporate smuggled goods. 2.4.1 Valuation The World Trade Organization (WTO) agreement on Valuation has been adopted and its implementation commenced on January 2001. Imports are valued at cost, insurance and freight (cif) while exports at free-on-board (fob). Values expressed in foreign currencies are converted to Tanzanian shillings using the weekly average of exchange rate of the previous week submitted by the Bank of Tanzania . Shipments without invoice, such as barter and aid goods, and intra-company are recorded at the values assessed by the CED.
