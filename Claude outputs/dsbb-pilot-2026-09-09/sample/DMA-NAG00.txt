 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical Framework, Concepts and Definitions The framework for National Accounts is based largely on the "System of National Accounts, 1993" ("1993 SNA"). The annual national accounts of Dominica are disseminated in the "National Accounts Report" of the Central Statistical Office (CSO) and in the Eastern Caribbean Central Bank's (ECCB) "Annual National Account Digest". Real Estate, Renting and Business Activities 11. Public Administration, Defense and Social Security 12. Education 13. Health and Social Work 14. Other Community, Social and Personal Services 15.Private Households with Employed Persons Type of Expenditure: 1. Final Consumption a. Private Consumption b. General Government Consumption 2. Gross Capital Formation 2.1. Items of Gross Fixed Capital Formation a. Land Development b. Transport Equipment c. Other Equipment d. Construction e. Increase in Stocks 4. Exports Less Imports of Goods and Services Gross Domestic Product (GDP) at factor cost and in constant 1990 prices is estimated by the 13 major economic activities and 7 sub-activities used in deriving current dollar estimates. Other Measures: Gross National Income Gross Disposable Income Saving Gross Capital Formation Definitions: The concepts used primarily follow the "1993 SNA". GDP is compiled from both the production and the expenditure approach. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization Classification System Economic activities are classified using the International Standard Industrial Classification, Revision 3.1 (ISIC Rev 3.1). The 15 activities covered comprise: Agriculture (A) Fishing ( B), Mining and Quarrying (C), Manufacturing (D), Electricity, Gas and Water Supply (E), Construction (F), Wholesale and Retail Trade (G), Hotels and Restaurants (H), Transportation, Storage and Communications (I), Financial Intermediation (J), Real Estate, Renting and Business Activities (K), Public Administration and Defense; Social Security (L), Education (M), Health and Social Work (N), Other Community, Social and Personal (O), Activities of Private Households as Employers (P), Extraterritorial Organization and bodies (Q). Scope of the data Geographic Coverage The national accounts cover the entire economic territory of Dominica. Unit Coverage In principle, all resident institutional units operating within the economy plus their transactions to and from the rest of the world are included in the national accounts. Coverage The statistical framework follows the "System of National Accounts, 1993" ("1993 SNA"). GDP is compiled and disseminated in millions of Eastern Caribbean Dollars by the Central Statistical Office (CSO) at current and constant (2006) prices. The period of reference is the calendar year. Consistent data series begin with 2000. All resident institutional units operating within the Dominican economy and transactions to and from the rest of the world are covered. Commodity flow method is used for construction and wholesale and retail activities where reporting is a problem arising from lack of respondent cooperation and the presence of significant informal activity. GDP is compiled and disseminated by (detailing 15 categories and 32 sub-categories) the production and expenditure method. The basic data sources for compiling national accounts are National Accounts Establishment Survey, Government Administrative records and External Trade Statistics. More details on the data sources are available in the Comprehensive Framework. Gross National Income (GNI) is derived by adding net factor incomes from abroad from the balance of payments (BOP), prepared by the Eastern Caribbean Central Bank (ECCB) and the CSO. Gross National Disposable Income (GNDI) is derived by adding net current transfers from abroad from the BOP to GNI. Saving is derived as the difference between GNDI and the sum of private and government consumption. For further details on the BOP, see the Comprehensive Framework and Data Categories and Indicators for the External Sector. Unrecorded activity Unrecorded Activity (as relevant) Own-account consumption of goods and services through informal channels are not covered by the available sources of data and are indirectly estimated. No adjustments are made for illegal activities. However an informal sector questionnaire which will provide complete coverage will be administered during the 2011 Population and Housing Census. 2.4.1 Valuation Valuation: Output is measured by industrial activity in producers' prices. Intermediate consumption is valued at market prices. Data are published by industrial activity at basic prices, which is derived from output at producer's prices by subtracting taxes on products and production and adding subsidies for products and on production. Exports and Imports are valued free on board (f.o.b.) GDP by final use is valued at market prices, which is equivalent to the "1993 SNA" purchasers' price valuation concept. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: Transactions are usually recorded on an accrual basis. An exception is the government revenue and expenditure data, which are recorded on a cash basis. Recording period: Data are compiled for the calendar year. However, a number of enterprises compile accounts on a fiscal accounting year. Their annual data are included in the national accounts for a given calendar year if their fiscal year begins before July 1 of the reference period. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Production approach: · Agriculture: Output of crops is obtained from the Ministry of Agriculture. Intermediate inputs for other crops comprise of imports obtained from External Trade Statistics and the local inputs are extrapolated based on administrative records. Data on the output and intermediate input of forestry activities are based on administrative records. Fishing output and intermediate consumption are derived as direct estimates from Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries Division. · Mining and quarrying: There is no mining activity. Estimates of output and intermediate consumption for quarrying are obtained from the National Accounts Establishment Survey, (NAES) · Manufacturing: Output, value added and intermediate consumption are obtained primarily from the NAES. · Electricity and water: Direct estimates of output, intermediate input, and value added for Electricity and Water are obtained from the financial statements of the Dominica Water and Sewage Company Ltd and Dominica Electricity Services Ltd as part of the NAES. · Construction: Output is measured by the commodity flow approach using construction materials data from the external trade statistics, production survey data on locally produced construction materials, and the wages of construction labor. The value of trade and transport margins on inputs which is included in the computation of output prices is estimated from ad hoc surveys of building contractors and quantity surveyors. Value added is computed, as a fraction of measured output. The ratio of value-added to output is estimated from a sample of construction companies. · Wholesale and retail trade: The value-added is estimated on the basis of the annual accounts obtained via NAES and is supplemented by information obtained from the Inland Revenue. · Hotels and restaurants: Output of accommodation services in current and constant prices are estimated from data on number of tourists in paid accommodation, average length of stay, and average daily expenditure obtained from Visitor Exit Surveys while constant prices are estimated by deflating using Consumer Price Index. · Transport: Output and value added for Sea Transport, Air Transport, Road Transport are directly estimated using data from the Annual National Accounts Establishment Survey. · Communications: Output and value added for communications sector are derived using data from the NAES Establishment Survey and the Government Accounts from the Treasury Department, Ministry of Finance and Planning. · Banks and insurance: Data for banking are obtained from the NAES and from the ECCB's commercial banking survey. Data for insurance come from the NAES and is supplemented by the Insurance Returns collected for regulatory purposes by the Registrar of Insurers, and the ECCB regional survey of foreign insurance companies. · Real Estate, Renting and Business Activities: Value added is computed by extrapolation based on a benchmark survey. · Public Administration, Defense and Social Security: Value added comprises of compensation of employees, which is obtained from Government accounting records, the financial statement of the Dominica Social Security and the NAES covering local government. Education: data are derived from public accounts and private educational institutions Health and Social Work: data are derived from public records and NAES. · Other services: Estimates for professional and business services, private health, education, and entertainment activities are based on data received from a variety of sources including the Dominica Social Security, the Inland Revenue Department of the Ministry of Finance and Planning and the NAES. Expenditure approach: · Private final consumption: This component of expenditure is determined as the residual of GDP at market prices using the GDP derived from the production method, less government final consumption, Gross capital formation, and net exports of goods and services. · Government final consumption: Consumption of general government services is defined as output less the value of commodity and non-commodity sales and own-account capital formation. The source data are government budgetary and administrative records. . · Gross Fixed capital formation: Capital formation for transport equipment and other equipment is compiled from customs sources using a commodity flow approach, as there is little domestic production of these items. Construction is compiled via a commodity flow approach, as in the treatment of the construction industry in the description of the production approach to compiling GDP above. Land development is compiled based on data obtained from the administrative records of the Ministry of Finance, Industry and Planning (MOFIP). · Increase in stocks: Increase in stocks (change in inventories) is estimated based on the NAES, Income Tax Returns and extrapolation. · Exports and imports of goods and non-factor services: These data are taken directly from the Balance of Payments compiled by the ECCB and the CSO. See the comprehensive framework and categories and indicators for the External Sector for details on the compilation of net exports of goods and services. Discrepancies: No discrepancy between the production and expenditure approach as the expenditure component for private final consumption is derived as a residual. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Production approach: · Coverage adjustments: Various methods are used for the treatment of missing values of output and intermediate consumption for individual establishments in the NAES and other sources. Among these are estimating current intermediate consumption on the basis of its past share in an establishment's output, estimating output on the basis of the past ratio of output to selected cost data, extrapolating the most recent past observed level of output, intermediate input, and value added on the basis of growth rates observed either in the past or currently in reported data for similar establishments. · Estimation of current price data: Value-added (GDP) by industry is the difference between output and intermediate consumption. For banana crops, quarrying, electricity and water, air and sea transport, post and communications, and banking and insurance, output and intermediate consumption are compiled from accounting records sampled in the National Establishment Survey or obtained from administrative sources. The value added of banks is measured as net interest and other income. There are insufficient data at present to allocate this output to intermediate and final consumption. Consequently, aggregate intermediate consumption includes payment of net interest income to a notional industry. For general government, value added comprises compensation of employees. For manufacturing, the level of output and intermediate consumption are estimated from the annual NAES. For construction, output is estimated using a commodity flow method, as the sum of production costs for materials and labor unique to the industry, plus trade and transport margins for those items. For non-banana crops, fishing, hotels and restaurants, and road and sea transport, output is produced from estimates of quantity produced multiplied by average price. For construction, wholesale and retail trade, hotels and restaurants, and road transport (in non-benchmark years), value added is estimated as the product of an estimated ratio of value added to output from a sample survey, either the annual NAES or an ad hoc or special collection, multiplied by estimated total output for the industry. For Real Estate and Housing, output and intermediate consumption are estimated by using NAES ratios based on Census data on housing and construction starts along with movements in the Consumer Price Index. · Estimation of constant price data: Constant price value added is computed by deflating current price value added by a price index of commodities for telecommunications, construction, insurance and general government. In the second method, constant price value added is computed by extrapolating a reference year current price estimate of valued added by a volume index of physical indicators for agriculture, manufacturing, electricity and water, hotels and restaurants, transport, and banks. Expenditure approach: · Coverage adjustments: There are no adjustments in the sources and estimates of expenditure on GDP not already mentioned under the production approach. · Estimation of current price data: Private consumption is determined as the residual of GDP (which was derived from the production method), less government consumption, gross capital formation, and exports less imports of goods and services. Gross fixed capital formation comprises investment in equipment and structures. Equipment investment is estimated via a commodity flow method principally using data on imports, and structures investment is the gross output of the construction industry. There are no adjustments to equipment investment to include set-up costs. As noted in Nature of Basic Data Sources above, exports and imports of goods and services derive from the balance of payments (BOP) prepared by the ECCB and CSO. As private consumption is calculated as a residual, the estimates from the production and expenditure approaches are not independently determined and reconcile. Exports and imports of goods and services used in the calculation of GDP and net factor incomes from abroad used in the calculation of GNI are taken from the BOP, and therefore reconcile with those data by definition. Base year and update cycle: The year of reference for constant price series is 2006 and is updated on a 10-year cycle. None
