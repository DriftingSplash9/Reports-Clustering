 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Analytical framework: Statistics Estonia compiles national accounts data according to the concepts, definitions, classifications and accounting rules of the European System of Accounts (ESA 2010). GDP is compiled using three approaches: the production, expenditure and income approach. Definition: GDP at market prices from the production side is defined as the sum of gross values added of resident producers plus net taxes on products. From the expenditure side, GDP is defined as the sum of total final expenditure at purchaser’s prices, gross capital formation, exports of goods and services less imports of goods and services. GDP by income approach is the sum of compensation of employees, consumption of fixed capital, net operating surplus or mixed income and taxes linked to production and imports less subsidies. However, net operating surplus and mixed income are derived as residual values. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization National accounts calculations are based on the following classifications: The estimates of output, intermediate consumption, gross fixed capital formation and consumption of fixed capital are compiled according to the Estonian Classification of Economic Activities (EMTAK 2008.3.0) which is in conformity with NACE Rev. 2. The estimates of household final consumption expenditure are broken down by 146 commodity groups according to the Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose (COICOP 2018) (at the 4-digit level). The calculation of the exports and imports of goods is distributed into 40 subdivisions at the 2-digit level according to Statistical Classification of Products by Activity in the European Economic Community (CPA). The division between individual and collective goods and services is made on the basis of Classification of the Functions of Government (COFOG). COFOG level II government functions are devided into 69 different functional groups. Scope of the data Transactions coverage: Includes all transactions determined by ESA 2010 as lying within the production boundary. Geographical coverage: Includes the entire economic territory of the country. Unrecorded activity GDP includes estimations of exhaustiveness. Adjustments for exhaustiveness are made for transactions that are not directly observed by regular statistical surveys or administrative sources. The estimates of exhaustiveness are made in four institutional sectors: non-financial corporations sector (S.11), financial corporations sector (S.12), households sector (S.14), and NPISH sector (S.15). The overall adjustment of exhaustiveness encompasses adjustments for: non-registered underground economy (N1); illegal activities (N2); misreporting of economic indicators (N6); other GDP under-coverage (N7). 2.4.1 Valuation In the case of market production and production for own final use, output is valued at basic prices, while intermediate consumption is valued at purchaser's prices. Non-market output is valued at total production costs. According to ESA 2010 methodology, general government whole output is valued as the sum of intermediate consumption, compensation of employees, consumption of fixed capital and other taxes on production. There are no other subsidies on production for government non-market producers. Non-profit institutions serving households are also non-market producers and output calculations are made on the basis of costs. Household final consumption expenditure and acquisitions of fixed assets are valued at purchaser's prices. Exports and imports of goods and services are recorded at FOB-value. During the accounting period, changes in stocks are valued at current average prices.Changes in inventories of stocks of finished goods and work-in-progress are valued at basic prices, while changes in inventories of materials and supplies and goods intended for resale are valued at purchasers' prices. There are two kinds of adjustments made to the data on inventories provided by source statistics.Firstly, mark-up adjustment for operating surplus is made to arrive inventories of finished goods and work-in-progress at basic prices. Secondly, the effects of holding gains that are not part of production are eliminated from changes in the inventories of finished goods, work-in-progress, materials and supplies and goods intended for resale. 2.4.2 Recording basis Time of recording: Transactions of goods and services are calculated on an accrual basis. Valuation of taxes is made on an accrual basis (time-adjusted cash method). International transactions are recorded as at the time the goods enter/leave the economic territory as documented in the corresponding customs declarations. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Production approach: Agriculture, hunting and forestry: Incorporated agricultural producers are covered by short-term statistics (STS) and sampled by using the Statistical Profile. International and European classifications are used as well. Time of recording is on accrual base. 3.3.1 Source data statistical techniques Adjustments to source data: Adjustments for exhaustiveness are made for transactions that are not directly observed by regular statistical surveys or recorded in administrative sources. Estimates of exhaustiveness for the quarterly and annual national accounts are made in four institutional sectors: · I. non-financial corporations sector (S.11); · II. financial corporations sector (S.12); · III. households sector (S.14); · IV. NPISH sector (S.15). In Estonia, the following five types of non-exhaustiveness exist: · N1: Producers engaged in legal activities who should be registered but do not do it in order to avoid tax and social security obligations, minimum wage requirements, health and safety regulations, etc.; · N2: Producers who avoid registration because they are engaged in illegal activities; · N3: Producers who are not obliged to register because they have no market output (typically households engaged in the production of goods for own consumption or own fixed capital formation); · N6: Misreporting by producers, usually under-reporting of gross output and/or over-reporting of intermediate consumption; · N7: Other statistical deficiencies, because data are incomplete, not collected, not directly collectable or incorrectly handled by statisticians during processing and compilation. 3.3.2 Other statistical procedures Compilation of GDP at current prices Production approach: Value added is calculated as the sum of sales at basic prices less goods purchased for resale, information from PSFS, output for own final use, changes in the inventories of finished goods and work-in-progress that are adjusted to eliminate holding gains on inventories, minus intermediate consumption of goods and services. The output of collective and individual services by the general government and non-profit institutions is estimated as the sum of intermediate consumption, compensation of employees, consumption of fixed capital and other taxes on production. Value added is calculated as the above total less intermediate consumption – the calculations are carried out in full for the sake of transparency. The output of non-financial corporations is calculated at basic prices and includes: Sales of enterprises (net); Purchased goods and services for resale (-); Changes in the inventories of finished goods at basic prices (+); Changes in the inventories of work-in-progress at basic prices (+); Own account products (+). Product subsidies, income from operating costs, targeted financing, information from the PSFS system, own-account production of computer software, mineral exploration, research and development (R&D), some other extras (information about branches, etc.) and holding gains are added to the calculation of output. The intermediate consumption of non-financial corporations is calculated at basic prices. The total intermediate consumption includes also holding gains and losses, artistic originals, vehicles registration fees, research and development, the non-observed economy. Financial intermediate services indirectly measured (FISIM ) is added to intermediate consumption. The value added of the general government at current prices is estimated based on total production costs as the sum of compensation of employees, consumption of fixed capital (CFC) and other taxes on production. For calculating the CFC in the general government sector, the simplified perpetual inventory method (PIM) is used. It is based on the application of depreciation and discards functions and involves the estimation of capital stocks. The year 1995 is used as the starting point for gross stock and CFC estimation assets the time series of gross fixed capital formation that is used as an input in the PIM is available starting from 1995. In order to get CFC estimates at current prices, capital stocks are revaluated into replacement prices by using the changes in relevant price indices. The same indices are used to get CFC estimates at current prices, since the quarterly estimation is based on gross capital stocks at previous year's prices. Linear depreciation method is used for assets with long service lives and geometric depreciation method for assets with short service lives. Other taxes on production payable by the general government sector include: taxes on land, pollution resulting from production activities, specific exploitation of water resources, business and professional licenses. The output of the general government sector at current prices is estimated as the sum of value added and intermediate consumption. Agriculture, hunting, forestry and fishing: Agricultural output is calculated using average per-hectare crop yields, which are grossed up using land use data to determine output volumes, which are then multiplied by average prices to calculate output at current prices. Livestock output is based on quarterly sales data and surveys of the number of livestock. At present, account is not taken of work-in-progress and changes in inventories when calculating agricultural output. The output of agricultural holdings is estimated as follows: the total area of small holdings is equal to the total territory of Estonia less the area not used for agricultural purposes less the area used by agricultural enterprises and farms larger than one hectare. The breakdown of the use of these small holdings, in terms of agricultural products and yields, is assumed to be the same as that of larger enterprises. The fishing data of households is based on the administrative data of the Tax and Customs Board (the tax declarations on business income of sole proprietors) and HBS. Mining and quarrying: Explained above Manufacturing: Explained above. Electricity, gas and water supply: Explained above. Construction: Explained above. In addition, construction output by households is calculated based on the inputs purchased for new construction and for capital repair. Wholesale and retail trade: The output of wholesale and retail trade consists in their trade margin and is calculated as the difference between the value of sales and the cost of goods for resale. Hotels and restaurants: Explained above. Transport, storage and communication: Explained above. Financial intermediation: The output of the financial sector consists of market output, non-market output of the central bank, FISIM, own-account produced software and income in kind (ie for the private use of business cars). Real estate: Explained above. Transport: Explained above. Public administration and defence: Explained above. Education: Explained above. Health and social work: Explained above. Other services: Explained above Expenditure approach: Household final consumption expenditure: The systematic tabular approach is used for compiling the HFCE of Estonia. The same set of analytical tables is used for annual and quarterly estimates. It gives the possibility of using the same framework for all HFCE estimates and ensures better comparability between data. HFCE estimates are broken down by 146 commodity groups (at the 4-digit level of COICOP 2018). The individual consumption expenditure includes both purchased goods and services for own consumption. Adjustments are made for wages and salaries in kind, purchases and sales outside the retail trade system, consumption of occasional and temporary activities, intentionally non-registered units, sole proprietorships and illegal activities. Additions are made to take into account the consumption of people living in institutions. Final consumption expenditure of the general government and NPISHs: The government final consumption expenditure at current prices is estimated on the basis of total output value, from which market output, output for own final use and payments for non-market output are excluded and social transfers in kind related to expenditure on products supplied to households via market producers are added. The final consumption expenditure of the NPISH sector is calculated as output of the sector (the sum of intermediate consumption, compensation of employees, consumption of fixed capital and other taxes on production) minus its market sales, research and development produced for own final use and own account software. The sources for final consumption estimations of NPISHs are the same as for the production approach. After output is calculated according to the production method, sales of goods and services, research and development produced for own final use and own account software are subtracted and final consumption expenditure is received. In the case of NPISHs, the main part of the sales of services consists of sporting services, adult education services and renting. The figures of the sales of goods and services are derived on the basis of the data from the latest available survey of non-profit organisations. Gross fixed capital formation: Quarterly and annual estimates of GFCF at both the current and previous year's prices are estimated by five institutional sectors, activity level A87, by type of fixed assets (15). GFCF includes acquisitions of new and existing tangible assets during the accounting period, including major repairs and land improvement, less disposals of tangible assets. Fixed assets acquired under financial leasing contracts (in the total value of capital goods) as well as own-account construction and research and development and major repairs are included in GFCF. Acquisitions less disposals of intangible assets, including own-account produced software, are also taken into account. Changes in inventories: According to ESA 2010, inventories of materials and supplies and goods for resale are valued at purchasers' prices, while inventories of finished goods and work-in-progress are valuedat basic prices.Thus, adjustments need to be made for enterprises' data on the inventories of finished goods and work-in-progress through determining the production costs based on operating surplus. Operating surplus is calculated by using the ratio of production costs to the sales of the previous year by activity. According to ESA 2010, entries and withdrawals of inventory are valued at the time the entries and withdrawals are made. Holding gains arise from changes in prices during the period they are held in inventory, therefore, the lag between the time they are produced/acquired and the time they are disposed/used must be excluded. Exports and imports of goods and services: Exports and imports of goods and services at current prices are based on two main data sources – foreign trade statistics and BoP(balance of payments). Foreign trade statistics are compiled by Statistics Estonia based on two parallel data collection systems: Intrastat and Extrastat. Intrastat data are based on statistical declarations which collect data on the exchange of goods with EU Member States. Extrastat data are dervied from customs declarations which cover the exports and imports of goods with non-EU Member States. The BoP is compiled by Eesti Pank. The methodological basis for the compilation of the BoP is the IMF Balance of Payments Manual 6. In national accounts, estimates of the exports and imports of goods follow the same concepts as the Balance of Payments. The exports and imports of goods at current prices in national accounts correspond to the BoP data. The exports and imports of services are classified according to the categories of the BoP. Exports of goods are valued at f.o.b. prices that include the value of goods,the cost of insurance and transportation to the border of the exporting country and is therefore compilant with the ESA 2010 definitions. The f.o.b. value is equivalent to the purchasers' price. In foreign trade statistics, the imports of goods are valued at c.i.f. prices, which include the value of goods,the transportation and insurance costs up to the importing country. The c.i.f. value corresponds to the basic price as import duties and excise taxes on imports are not included in the value. For the imports of goods, correctionfor the additional delivery costs to the importing country's frontier is made, as Statistics Estonia publishes exports as well as imports of goods at f.o.b prices. Compilation of GDP at the previous year's prices. NA volumes are published as chain-linked series at reference year 2020 prices for each of the NACE groups. Currently, consumer price, producer price, construction price, agriculture, export and import price indices are used upon a deflation. In Estonia, the Laspeyres indices ( the value of basket of goods available in year 0, related to the original basket of year 0) have been used in calculations at the previous year's prices. National accounts at the previous year's prices are calculated for both the production and expenditure bases. Production-based accounts are calculated by double deflation. It means that output and intermediate consumption are deflated separately, and value added at the previous year's prices is derived as the difference between output and intermediate consumption at the previous year's prices. The preferred approaches for market output are deflation product-by-product, separate deflation of domestic and export sales used an appropriate producer price index (PPI) or service producer price index (SPPI). If the appropriate PPI or SPPI required for deflation is not available, alternative types of indicators are applied – e.g. consumer price index (CPI). The preferred method for estimating intermediate consumption at the previous year's prices is deflation product-by-product and separate deflation of domestic and imported intermediate consumption. While official actual intermediate consumption's price indices required for the deflation of intermediate consumption are currently not available from price statistics, the same price indices as for deflation of output are used. The taxes and margins at previous year's prices for each product are calculated by applying the previous year tax and margin rates on the intermediate consumption at previous year's prices (i.e. the volume projection method is used) The output of non-market activities (general government sector, NPISH, financial sector and imputed rents) is calculated as the sum of production costs (input method), where inputs are deflated at the product level or extrapolated by a volume indicators. Upon the deflation of the value added components of the government sector, the special case of physical volume index, i.e. the change in the number of employees, is applied. AOL has been selected since: it is more practical for the Laspeyres volume measures in national accounts, it meets the time consistency criterion, i.e. the annual sums of quarterly chain-linked figures equal the corresponding chain-linked annual figures, it ensures uniformity with the techniques already used by the majority of EU Member States. The variables of chain-linked volumes are non-additive (except for the reference year and the year following the reference year). Due to the lack of detailed information for the households sectors, the outputs of the non-financial enterprises sector and households sector are deflated together and their intermediate consumption is also deflated together. For NPISHs, compensation of employees and taxes on production are deflated by the overall CPI. Consumption of fixed capital is deflated by CFC deflators derived from GFCF estimates. Intermediate consumption is deflated at the product level, using corresponding indices such as the consumer price index, producer price index of industrial output, import price index, the CPI of foreign partners. Expenditure approach: Household final consumption expenditure: HFCE is calculated at the 4th level of COICOP groups and the same commodity detail is used for deflation. Prices are the purchaser's prices, including VAT. The consumption of households' own production is valued at basic prices. For deflation, the consumer price indices (CPIs) and producer price indices (PPIs) (the latter is used for the deflation of the consumption of households' own production) are used. For narcotics and prostitution, values are received directly from the Police and Border Guard Board. For the expenditures of resident households in the rest of the world, the weighted CPI for the main destination countries for tourism is used. For non-residents' expenditures on the economic territory, the same CPIs are used as for the expenditures of households living in Estonia. The final consumption expenditure of the general government and NPISHs: For the estimation of the final consumption expenditure of the general government, output, output for own final use, sales and social transfers in kind are deflated separately. Output estimation methods are described above. Social transfers in kind and sales of general government are deflated at the level of product using the appropriate price index. FISIM is deflated separately and then added to the estimation. For NPISHs, compensation of employees and taxes on production are deflated by the overall CPI. Consumption of fixed capital is deflated by CFC deflators derived from GFCF estimates. Intermediate consumption and sales are deflated at the product level, using corresponding indices like the consumer price index, producer price index of industrial output, import price index, the CPI of foreign partners. Gross fixed capital formation: Current price estimates for domestically produced machinery and equipment are deflated using the PPI of machinery and equipment, and another deflator is used for imports. Construction estimates are deflated using the total construction price (cost) index. GFCF price indices differ neither by institutional sector nor by economic activity. In the case of dwellings, the constant price series of new constructions is calculated using the area of new constructions and the previous year's average price of one square metre in new apartments. The constant price series of renovated constructions is calculated using the area of renovated constructions and the previous year's average price of one square metre of major repairs. The costs of ownership transfer are deflated using the price index of other housing acquisition related services. Changes in inventories: Inventories are deflated by products as follows: Materials and supplies – the relevant PPI except for fuel where the CPI is used; Work-in-progress – the relevant PPI; Finished goods – the relevant PPI, Goods for resale – the CPI for goods is used. The exports and imports of goods are deflated by the groups of the BoP, but ‘general merchandise' is distributed into 40 subdivisions using 2-digit CPA codes. This results in a more detailed and accurate deflation. For goods, mainly specific export and import price indexes are used. If there is no relevant export or import price index available, the relevant producer price index is used, if appropriate. For ‘net exports of goods under merchanting' and exports and imports of ‘non-monetary gold', the main export or import price index is used. The exports of services are deflated by the groups of the BoP. The exports of services are deflated using mainly the domestic CPIs. For ‘Manufacturing services on physical inputs owned by others', the main producer price index is used. Other transport services include, for example, the maintenance and cleaning of transportation equipment and other support services provided by imports, airports and other terminal facilities, therefore the average of the CPIs for transport services and other services in respect of personal transport equipment is used for the deflation of that entry. ‘Operational leasing services' is deflated by using the average of the CPIs for the manufacture of machinery and equipment, manufacture of electrical and optical equipment and manufacture of motor vehicles, trailers and semi-trailers. For ‘Maintenance and repair services', the average of the following CPIs is used: maintenance of dwellings, repair of household appliances, maintenance of transport equipment and maintenance of audio-visual, photographic and information processing equipment. The imports of services are deflated by using the weighted average of CPIs and currency exchange rates of the most important trade partners according to the statistics of Eesti Pank (imports of services by country). Both the calculations at current prices and volumes after chain-linking are adjusted. . 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions of the QNA data can be divided into three groups: Regular revisions Major regular (benchmark) revisions and Major occasional revisions In principle, the regular revision of the national accounts data in Estonia is in compliance with the EU revision policy for national accounts data. The revision of the principal quarterly national accounts indicators is carried out regularly based on the Structural Business Survey (SBS), the results of which are published 21 months after the end of the year subject to revision. In addition, as the Balance of Payments and government accounts of the previous year are revised as well, the QNA of the first quarter of the current year have to be revised. The revised figures are published in August. The final regular revision of the national accounts figures is based on the supply and use tables (SUTs). The SUT is published in t+36 months in December. The revised QNA figures based on the SUT are published in t+45 months. Both major regular and major occasional revisions are carefully planned and the revised figures are published in the statistical database of Statistics Estonia at the end of August. The public is informed early enough about the upcoming major revisions via regular QNA-focused press releases. The revisions are analysed regularly, but only a summary of the results is published. 4.3.3 Dissemination of revision studies and analyses Both major regular and major irregular revisions are carefully planned and the revised figures are published in the statistical database of Statistics Estonia in August. The public is informed early enough about the upcoming major revisions via regular QNA-focused press releases. The revisions are analysed regularly, but only a summary of the results is published. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request More detailed or other unpublished non-confidential data are available on request. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Information on contact points is provided in each publication and news release of Statistics Estonia.
