 2.1.1 Concepts and definitions Compilation of merchandise trade generally follows the method recommended in the United Nations publication International Merchandise Trade Statistics: Concepts and Definitions, (New York: United Nations, 2010). The text can be found on the United Nations' website Items which are not taken into consideration in the compilation of merchandise trade statistics follow the recommendations of the United Nations manual. They include: bank notes, coins and stamps, personal effects, empty containers, samples, transshipment, and items (including imports by individual households) below the threshold value of 3000 rupees that are exempted from import duty. 2.3.1 Classification/sectorization For the quarterly bulletin, exports are tabulated by domestic exports, and re-exports. For major domestic exports (goods of national origin), value and quantity are published for seven principal categories: (1) Frozen and fresh fish; (2) Canned tuna; (3) Other processed fish; (4) Fish Oil and Fats; (5) Sea Cucumber; (6) Animal feed; and (7) Medicaments and Medical Appliances. The published tables show value of exports by country of destination for the principal commodity categories. Further commodity detail is published in the annual Statistical Abstract. Re-exports are published by (1) Re-exports to ships and aircraft and (2) Other re-exports. (Re-exports are defined as goods exported in the same condition as imported or after undergoing minor operations which leave them essentially unchanged.) Commodity data for imports, exports and re-exports are published both by Harmonized System (2 digit level) http://wtocentre.iift.ac.in/DOC/Harmonised%20System%20Nomenclature.pdf and SITC rev 3 classification which can be accessed through the UN website: http://unstats.un.org/unsd/cr/registry/regcst.asp?Cl=14 Values of imports are also published by country of origin. 2.2.1 Scope For the quarterly bulletin, exports are tabulated by domestic exports, and re-exports. Scope of the data Data covers the entire Seychelles economy, as recorded on Seychelles customs declarations. The time lag to receive raw data from customs is 7 days after the reference month. Exceptions to coverage Construction and other heavy equipment imported temporarily, to be re-exported within twelve months, are excluded. Aircraft spare parts purchased abroad are also excluded. Imports of gold to be used in jewelry and other manufacturing are not included in imports data. 2.4.1 Valuation Imports are valued c.i.f. Exports and re-exports are valued f.o.b. 3.1.1 Source data collection programs Source data are provided to NBS online by the Customs Division using the Electronic Government System. NBS applies edit checks to unit records. Exports data are supplemented with information obtained by NBS direct from major exporters. News reports of imports of major items are followed up to ensure that they are included in merchandise trade statistics. 3.1.2 Source data definitions, scope, classifications, valuation, and time of recording Imports and exports are published by c.i.f. and f.o.b. valuations, respectively. Goods are valued at the date of lodgment of the customs declaration, with imports reported in foreign currencies converted to Seychelles rupees using the official rate of exchange at that time. 3.1.3 Source data timeliness A dissemination calendar is available on the webpage. 3.4.1 Validation of intermediate results NBS applies monthly average price of products to data where Weight or Supplementary Quantities are not plausible. 3.5.1 Revision studies and analyses Revisions are done periodically. 5.1.5 Dissemination on request This is done routinely to assist the business community in making their forecasts and government in preparation for special foreign and diplomatic missions. 5.3.1 Dissemination of information on contact points Assistance is also given to stakeholders who experience difficulty in accessing and downloading the publications from the website. 5.3.2 Availability of documents and services catalogs A list of all NBS Publications is indicated in the annual Seychelles in Figures booklet
